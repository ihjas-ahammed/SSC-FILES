1 Theorems with amsthm

1.1 Defining and using a theorem

The preamble defines environments with \newtheorem{name}{Title}. In the body the environment is used like any other:

\newtheorem{theorem}{Theorem}[section]       % numbered within sections
\newtheorem{lemma}[theorem]{Lemma}           % shares the theorem counter
\newtheorem{definition}[theorem]{Definition}
\newtheorem{remark}[theorem]{Remark}

\begin{theorem}[Pythagoras]\label{thm:pyth}
In a right-angled triangle with legs $a, b$ and hypotenuse $c$,
$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$.
\end{theorem}
Theorem 1.1 (Pythagoras)

In a right-angled triangle with legs a, b and hypotenuse c, a2 + b2 = c2.

Lemma 1.2

Every theorem in the same counter as Theorem 1.1 is numbered in sequence with it.

Definition 1.3

A triangle is right-angled if one of its angles equals 90°.

Remark 1.4

The theorem numbers include the section number because of the optional argument [section] in \newtheorem.

\begin{proof}
Let the right angle be at $C$. Place a square of side $a+b$ around the
triangle; comparing areas gives the result. \qedhere
\end{proof}
Proof.

2 Boxes

\fbox draws a frame around its contents. \framebox takes an optional width and alignment. \makebox sets a fixed width without a frame. \parbox makes a fixed-width paragraph in a line.

\noindent
\fbox{Boxed text}\quad
\framebox[4cm][c]{Centred in 4\,cm}\quad
\makebox[3cm][r]{Right in 3\,cm}\\[1em]
\fbox{\parbox{0.7\textwidth}{A framed paragraph. The \texttt{parbox}
lets the text wrap inside a box of fixed width, for key results or
warnings.}}

3 Floating figures

A figure environment places the picture at a suitable location and numbers it. Here the picture is drawn with TikZ so that the example needs no image file. With an external picture, use \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{file.png} (graphicx).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1.2]
    \draw[->] (-0.3,0) -- (3.3,0) node[right] {$x$};
    \draw[->] (0,-0.3) -- (0,2.6) node[above] {$y$};
    \draw[thick, blue] (0,0) .. controls (1,2.2) and (2,2.2) .. (3,0.4);
    \draw[dashed] (0,0) -- (3,0.4);
    \node[below right] at (3,0.4) {end};
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \caption{A curve from the origin, with its chord drawn dashed.}
  \label{fig:curve}
\end{figure}

Figure 1 shows the curve. The caption sits below the figure; \caption before \label is required so that the reference shows the figure number.

4 Cross references

4.1 Labels and the three reference commands

A label is placed right after the item it names, inside its environment or heading. Then:

  • \ref{label} gives the number: Section 1.
  • \pageref{label} gives the page: page 1.
  • \eqref{label} gives the equation number in brackets (for equations): (1).
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass}
  F = ma
\end{equation}
F = ma (1)

Equation (1) above is referred to by \eqref{eq:mass}.

4.2 Smart references with hyperref

With hyperref loaded, \autoref{label} writes the word for you and makes the reference a clickable link: see Figure 1 and Theorem 1.1.

4.3 Rules for labels

  • Labels are case-sensitive and must be unique in the document.
  • Prefixes such as sec:, fig:, tab:, eq:, thm: keep them readable.
  • After adding or moving a label, compile again (twice in total) so that the references are updated.
Compile twice. \ref, \pageref and \listoffigures need a second pass before they show the right numbers.

5 Footnotes

A footnote is written with \footnote{...}, which numbers it and places the text at the bottom of the page.1 Inside a table or a minipage, use \footnotemark in the text and \footnotetext after the table.

1 The footnote is typeset at the bottom of the page where it appears, not at the end of the document.

6 Presentation conventions

6.1 Front matter and back matter

A presentable document has, in order: a title block, an abstract, a table of contents, lists of figures and tables, the body, and then references and appendices. The sectioning commands fill the body. \appendix changes the later sections to letters.

6.2 Spacing and emphasis

  • Put one blank line between paragraphs; do not use \\ for that purpose.
  • Use \emph for the first use of a term and \textbf sparingly.
  • Keep display maths and figures close to the text that discusses them.
