1. Fonts and text styling

Font commands change the look of a piece of text:

CommandResult
\textbf{bold}bold
\textit{italic}italic
\emph{emphasis}emphasis (italic inside italic text becomes upright)
\texttt{typewriter}typewriter
\textsf{sans serif}sans serif
\textsc{small caps}small caps
\underline{underlined}underlined

Use \emph for meaning and \textit for pure styling. Commands can be combined: \textbf{\textit{both}}, which gives both.

1.1 Font size

Sizes are set with declarations. A declaration applies until the end of the enclosing group (a pair of braces or an environment):

{\small This is small text.}
{\Large This is large text.}

Sizes, from small to large: \tiny, \scriptsize, \footnotesize, \small, \normalsize, \large, \Large, \LARGE, \huge, \Huge.

This is small text.    This is large text.

2. Spacing and page layout

2.1 Paragraph spacing

\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}     % no indent at the start of paragraphs
\setlength{\parskip}{0.6em}     % vertical gap between paragraphs
\noindent                       % suppress indent for one paragraph

2.2 Manual spacing

\vspace{1cm} adds vertical space and \hspace{2cm} adds horizontal space. \\ forces a line break and \newpage forces a page break.

2.3 Margins

The geometry package sets margins in one line, as in the source file of these notes:

\usepackage[margin=2.5cm]{geometry}

2.4 Headers, footers and page numbers

The fancyhdr package gives control over the running header and footer, with L, C and R positions. These notes use:

\pagestyle{fancy}
\fancyhf{}                          % clear everything first
\fancyhead[L]{Left text}
\fancyhead[R]{\thepage}
\renewcommand{\headrulewidth}{0.4pt}

A preview of the resulting running header:

MAT5FS112 – Module II3

Page numbering style: \pagenumbering{roman} (i, ii, iii…) or \pagenumbering{arabic}. \thispagestyle{empty} removes the header from one page.

4. Rows and columns: tabular

The tabular environment sets rows and columns. The argument gives one letter per column: l (left), c (centre), r (right), and p{width} (wrapped paragraph of given width). Cells are separated by & and rows end with \\.

\begin{tabular}{lcr}
Item   & Mark & Grade \\
\hline
Algebra & 18  & A     \\
Real    & 14  & B     \\
\end{tabular}

Rendered:

ItemMarkGrade
Algebra18A
Real14B

4.1 Rules: \hline and \cline

\hline draws a full-width horizontal line. \cline{2-3} draws a line only under columns 2 to 3. Vertical lines come from | in the column specification.

\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|}
\hline
Name & Physics & Maths \\
\cline{2-3}
Asha & 80      & 75    \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

Rendered:

NamePhysicsMaths
Asha8075

4.2 Spanning columns: \multicolumn

\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|}
\hline
\multicolumn{3}{|c|}{Semester V marks} \\
\hline
Name & Physics & Maths \\
\hline
Asha & 80      & 75    \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

Rendered:

Semester V marks
NamePhysicsMaths
Asha8075

4.3 Professional tables with booktabs

The booktabs package uses \toprule, \midrule, \bottomrule and avoids vertical lines, which gives cleaner results. \cmidrule{2-3} draws a partial rule.

\begin{tabular}{l r r}
\toprule
Module & Units & Min.\ marks \\
\midrule
I   & 4 & 10 \\
II  & 3 & 10 \\
III & 4 & 10 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

Rendered:

ModuleUnitsMin. marks
I410
II310
III410

4.4 Fixed-width columns with p{}

Use p{width} when a cell has a paragraph of text. The array package adds m{width} (vertically centred) and b{width} (bottom aligned).

\begin{tabular}{p{3.5cm} p{8cm}}
\toprule
Command & Purpose \\
\midrule
\verb|\hline| & Draws a full horizontal rule across the table. \\
\verb|\multicolumn| & Merges several cells in a row into one. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

Rendered:

CommandPurpose
\hlineDraws a full horizontal rule across the table.
\multicolumnMerges several cells in a row into one.

5. Tables as floating objects

A table placed in a table environment floats: LaTeX moves it to a suitable place and numbers it. The caption and the label let the text refer to it.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Marks awarded in the LaTeX internal examination}
  \label{tab:marks}
  \begin{tabular}{lcc}
    \toprule
    Student & Assignment & Viva \\
    \midrule
    Student A & 9 & 8 \\
    Student B & 7 & 9 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Rendered:

StudentAssignmentViva
Student A98
Student B79
Table 1: Marks awarded in the LaTeX internal examination
Placement. As Table~\ref{tab:marks} shows, the marks are recorded in two parts. The placement specifier [htbp] means: here, top, bottom, or on its own page. The ~ before \ref is a non-breaking space that keeps “Table” and the number together.
