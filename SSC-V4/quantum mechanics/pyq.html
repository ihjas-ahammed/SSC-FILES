1.1 The Schrödinger Equation & 1.2 The Statistical Interpretation

CONCEPT c.1.1.2THEORY · DERIVATION
The Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation

State the Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation for a particle of mass $m$ in a potential $V(x,t)$. Explain the role of the imaginary unit $i$, and show how the equation connects to de Broglie matter waves.

Solution & Invariants

$$i\hbar \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial t} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2 \Psi}{\partial x^2} + V(x,t)\Psi$$

The factor $i$ on the left side rotates $\Psi$ in the complex plane ($e^{-i\omega t}$), converting exponential dissipation into oscillatory phase rotation and quantum interference. Applying the operators $E \to i\hbar \partial/\partial t$ and $p \to -i\hbar \partial/\partial x$ to a plane wave $\Psi = A e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$ directly reproduces the classical energy relation $E = \frac{p^2}{2m} + V$.

CONCEPT c.1.2.1DEFINITION · BORN'S RULE
Born's Probability Density and Interval Detection

State Max Born's statistical interpretation of the wave function $\Psi(x,t)$. What is the probability $P_{ab}$ of locating the particle between $a$ and $b$ at time $t$?

Solution

$$P_{ab}(t) = \int_a^b |\Psi(x,t)|^2 dx = \int_a^b \Psi^*(x,t)\Psi(x,t) dx$$

The wave function is a probability amplitude; its modulus squared $\rho(x,t) = |\Psi(x,t)|^2$ is a probability density with dimensions of $1/\text{Length}$.

Common Trap: Do not confuse probability density $\rho(x)$ with probability $P$. Density can exceed 1 at a point; only the integrated probability is bounded between 0 and 1.

1.3 Probability Theory & 1.4 Normalization

GRIFFITHS PROBLEM 1.46 MARKS · LEVEL 3
Triangular Wave Packet Normalization and Statistics

At $t = 0$, a particle is described by the triangular wave packet: $$\Psi(x,0) = \begin{cases} A \frac{x}{a}, & 0 \le x \le a \\ A \frac{b-x}{b-a}, & a \le x \le b \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$ (a) Normalize $\Psi(x,0)$ to determine $A$. (b) What is the probability of finding the particle to the left of $a$? (c) Calculate $\langle x \rangle$.

Worked Solution

(a) $\int_0^b |\Psi|^2 dx = A^2 [\frac{a}{3} + \frac{b-a}{3}] = A^2 \frac{b}{3} = 1 \implies A = \sqrt{\frac{3}{b}}$.

(b) $P(x < a) = A^2 \frac{a}{3} = \frac{a}{b}$.

(c) $\langle x \rangle = \frac{2a + b}{4}$.

CONCEPT c.1.4.2THEORETICAL PROOF · 5 MARKS
Preservation of Normalization in Time

Prove that if a wave function is normalized at $t = 0$, the Schrödinger equation guarantees it remains normalized for all future times: $\frac{d}{dt}\int_{-\infty}^\infty |\Psi(x,t)|^2 dx = 0$.

Proof

$$\frac{d}{dt}\int |\Psi|^2 dx = \int \left(\frac{\partial \Psi^*}{\partial t}\Psi + \Psi^* \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial t}\right) dx$$

Substitute $\frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial t} = \frac{i\hbar}{2m}\frac{\partial^2 \Psi}{\partial x^2} - \frac{i}{\hbar}V\Psi$. Because $V$ is real, the potential terms cancel: $$\frac{\partial |\Psi|^2}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[\frac{i\hbar}{2m}\left(\Psi^* \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial \Psi^*}{\partial x}\Psi\right)\right] = -\frac{\partial J}{\partial x}$$

Integrating across $(-\infty, \infty)$ yields $[-J]_{-\infty}^\infty = 0$ because $\Psi \to 0$ as $x \to \pm\infty$.

1.5 Momentum & Operators

CONCEPT c.1.5.4THEORY & FLUX
Probability Current Density and Continuity Equation

Define the probability current density $J(x,t)$ and state the quantum continuity equation. What is $J$ for a plane wave $A e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$ and for a purely real spatial wave function $\psi(x) \in \mathbb{R}$?

Solution

$$J(x,t) = \frac{\hbar}{2mi}\left(\Psi^* \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial x} - \Psi \frac{\partial \Psi^*}{\partial x}\right) = \frac{\hbar}{m}\text{Im}\left(\Psi^* \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial x}\right)$$

Continuity: $\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial J}{\partial x} = 0$.
For a plane wave: $J = \frac{\hbar k}{m}|A|^2 = v \rho$.
For real $\psi(x)$: $J = 0$, meaning standing waves have zero net spatial probability current.

CONCEPT c.1.5.5EHRENFEST'S THEOREM · 5 MARKS
Ehrenfest's Theorem for Momentum

State and prove Ehrenfest's theorem for the rate of change of the momentum expectation value $\frac{d\langle p \rangle}{dt}$.

Solution

$$\frac{d\langle p \rangle}{dt} = \left\langle -\frac{\partial V}{\partial x} \right\rangle = \langle F(x) \rangle$$

Together with $d\langle x \rangle/dt = \langle p \rangle/m$, this demonstrates that average quantum coordinates follow Newton's classical equations of motion.

1.6 The Uncertainty Principle & IIT JAM Past Papers

IIT JAM PH 20242 MARKS · LEVEL 4
Superposition Probability Current Density

A particle of mass $m$ is described by the 1D wave function $\psi(x) = A e^{ikx} + B e^{-ikx}$. Show that the net probability current density is $J = \frac{\hbar k}{m}(|A|^2 - |B|^2)$.

Worked Solution

Substituting $\psi$ into $J = \frac{\hbar}{m}\text{Im}(\psi^* d\psi/dx)$: $$\psi^* \frac{d\psi}{dx} = (A^* e^{-ikx} + B^* e^{ikx}) \cdot ik(A e^{ikx} - B e^{-ikx}) = ik(|A|^2 - |B|^2) + \text{real cross terms}$$ Taking the imaginary part extracts $k(|A|^2 - |B|^2)$, yielding $J = \frac{\hbar k}{m}(|A|^2 - |B|^2)$. The forward current from $A$ subtracts the backward current from $B$.

IIT JAM PH 20211 MARK · LEVEL 4
Ground State Energy via Heisenberg Uncertainty

Estimate the ground-state energy of a 1D harmonic oscillator $H = \frac{p^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2$ using $\Delta x \Delta p \ge \hbar/2$.

Worked Solution

Setting $\Delta p \approx \frac{\hbar}{2\Delta x}$: $$E(\Delta x) = \frac{\hbar^2}{8m(\Delta x)^2} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2(\Delta x)^2$$ Minimizing with respect to $\Delta x$ yields $(\Delta x)^2 = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}$, giving $E_0 = \frac{1}{4}\hbar\omega + \frac{1}{4}\hbar\omega = \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega$.

CONCEPT c.2.1.1THEORY · DERIVATION · 5 MARKS
Separation of Variables and the Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation

Derive the Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} + V(x)\psi = E\psi$ from the TDSE by separating variables $\Psi(x,t) = \psi(x)\phi(t)$. Explain why the probability density $|\Psi(x,t)|^2$ is static.

Solution & Invariants

Dividing the TDSE by $\psi(x)\phi(t)$ separates time and spatial variables, equating both sides to the constant $E$. Integrating $\frac{d\phi}{dt} = -\frac{iE}{\hbar}\phi$ gives $\phi(t) = e^{-iEt/\hbar}$. The modulus squared is $|\Psi(x,t)|^2 = |\psi(x)|^2 |e^{-iEt/\hbar}|^2 = |\psi(x)|^2$, which is strictly time-independent.

CONCEPT c.2.2.2QUANTIZATION · 6 MARKS
Infinite Square Well Energy Levels & Wavefunctions

For a particle in an infinite potential well $V(x) = 0$ ($0 \le x \le a$), apply Dirichlet boundary conditions $\psi(0) = \psi(a) = 0$ to derive the quantized energy eigenvalues $E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2ma^2}$ and normalized spatial eigenfunctions $\psi_n(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{a}}\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{a}\right)$.

Solution

Inside the well $\psi''(x) = -k^2\psi(x)$ where $k = \frac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}$. Boundary condition $\psi(0) = 0 \implies \psi(x) = A\sin(kx)$. The right wall requires $\psi(a) = A\sin(ka) = 0 \implies ka = n\pi$ ($n=1,2,3,\dots$). Normalization $\int_0^a |A|^2 \sin^2(n\pi x/a) dx = 1 \implies A = \sqrt{2/a}$.

2.3 Free Particle, Wave Packets & 2.4 Probability Current

CONCEPT c.2.3.3THEORY · 4 MARKS
Phase Velocity vs. Group Velocity of Matter Waves

Prove that for a non-relativistic free particle with dispersion $\omega = \frac{\hbar k^2}{2m}$, the group velocity is twice the phase velocity: $v_g = 2 v_p = v_{\text{classical}}$.

Solution

Phase velocity: $v_p = \frac{\omega}{k} = \frac{\hbar k}{2m} = \frac{p}{2m} = \frac{1}{2}v_{\text{cl}}$.
Group velocity: $v_g = \frac{d\omega}{dk} = \frac{\hbar k}{m} = \frac{p}{m} = v_{\text{cl}}$.
Therefore, $v_g = 2 v_p = v_{\text{cl}}$. The physical particle envelope moves at the group velocity.

CONCEPT c.2.4.1CONTINUITY EQUATION · 5 MARKS
Derivation of Local Probability Conservation

Derive the quantum continuity equation $\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial J}{\partial x} = 0$ directly from the Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation, and define the probability current density $J(x,t)$.

Solution

Differentiating $\rho = \Psi^*\Psi$ and substituting $\frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial t}$ and $\frac{\partial \Psi^*}{\partial t}$ from the TDSE cancels the real potential $V(x)$. The spatial terms combine into $-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[\frac{\hbar}{2mi}\left(\Psi^*\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial x} - \Psi\frac{\partial\Psi^*}{\partial x}\right)\right] = -\frac{\partial J}{\partial x}$.

2.5 The Potential Step & 2.6 The Potential Barrier (Tunneling)

CONCEPT c.2.5.2SCATTERING · 6 MARKS
Potential Step Reflection and Transmission for $E > V_0$

Calculate the reflection coefficient $R$ and transmission coefficient $T$ for particles of energy $E > V_0$ incident on a potential step of height $V_0$. Prove that $R + T = 1$.

Solution

Matching $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at $x = 0$ yields $B/A = \frac{k_1 - k_2}{k_1 + k_2}$ and $C/A = \frac{2k_1}{k_1 + k_2}$.
$$R = |B/A|^2 = \left(\frac{k_1 - k_2}{k_1 + k_2}\right)^2, \qquad T = \frac{k_2}{k_1}|C/A|^2 = \frac{4k_1 k_2}{(k_1 + k_2)^2}$$ Summing: $R + T = \frac{(k_1 - k_2)^2 + 4k_1 k_2}{(k_1 + k_2)^2} = \frac{(k_1 + k_2)^2}{(k_1 + k_2)^2} = 1$.

CONCEPT c.2.6.2QUANTUM TUNNELING · 6 MARKS
Rectangular Barrier Tunneling and Wide-Barrier Scaling

State the transmission coefficient $T$ for a rectangular potential barrier of height $V_0$ and width $a$ when $E < V_0$. Show that for $\kappa a \gg 1$, $T \approx 16\frac{E}{V_0}\left(1 - \frac{E}{V_0}\right)e^{-2\kappa a}$.

Solution

Exact formula: $T = \left[1 + \frac{V_0^2 \sinh^2(\kappa a)}{4E(V_0 - E)}\right]^{-1}$ where $\kappa = \frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}}{\hbar}$.
For $\kappa a \gg 1$, $\sinh(\kappa a) \approx \frac{1}{2}e^{\kappa a} \implies \sinh^2(\kappa a) \approx \frac{1}{4}e^{2\kappa a}$. Inverting yields $T \approx \frac{16 E(V_0 - E)}{V_0^2} e^{-2\kappa a}$.

3.1 Hilbert Space, Dirac Notation & 3.2 Hermitian Operators

CONCEPT c.3.1.2THEORY · 4 MARKS
Inner Product, Bra-Ket Geometry and Projectors

Given $|1\rangle, |2\rangle$ orthonormal, let $|\psi\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}|1\rangle + i\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}|2\rangle$. Construct the projection operator $\hat{P}_\psi = |\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$ in matrix form and verify that $\hat{P}_\psi^\dagger = \hat{P}_\psi$ and $\hat{P}_\psi^2 = \hat{P}_\psi$.

Solution

$\langle\psi| = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\langle 1| - i\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\langle 2|$. In matrix form:
$$\hat{P}_\psi = \begin{pmatrix} 1/\sqrt{3} \\ i\sqrt{2/3} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1/\sqrt{3} & -i\sqrt{2/3} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1/3 & -i\sqrt{2}/3 \\ i\sqrt{2}/3 & 2/3 \end{pmatrix}$$ Taking conjugate transpose gives $\hat{P}_\psi^\dagger = \hat{P}_\psi$. Squaring yields $\hat{P}_\psi^2 = \hat{P}_\psi$ with $\text{Tr}(\hat{P}_\psi) = 1/3 + 2/3 = 1$.

CONCEPT c.3.2.2THEOREM · 5 MARKS
Reality of Eigenvalues and Orthogonality of Eigenstates

Prove that all eigenvalues of a Hermitian operator $\hat{A}$ are strictly real, and that eigenstates corresponding to distinct eigenvalues are mutually orthogonal.

Proof

Let $\hat{A}|\psi\rangle = \lambda|\psi\rangle$. Then $\langle\psi|\hat{A}|\psi\rangle = \lambda\langle\psi|\psi\rangle$. Since $\hat{A}^\dagger = \hat{A}$, $\langle\psi|\hat{A}|\psi\rangle^* = \langle\psi|\hat{A}^\dagger|\psi\rangle = \langle\psi|\hat{A}|\psi\rangle$, so $\lambda^* = \lambda \implies \lambda \in \mathbb{R}$.
For two states $\hat{A}|\psi_1\rangle = \lambda_1|\psi_1\rangle$ and $\hat{A}|\psi_2\rangle = \lambda_2|\psi_2\rangle$: $$\langle\psi_1|\hat{A}|\psi_2\rangle = \lambda_2\langle\psi_1|\psi_2\rangle = \lambda_1\langle\psi_1|\psi_2\rangle \implies (\lambda_1 - \lambda_2)\langle\psi_1|\psi_2\rangle = 0$$ Since $\lambda_1 \ne \lambda_2$, $\langle\psi_1|\psi_2\rangle = 0$.

3.3 Commutator Algebra & 3.4 Generalized Uncertainty

CONCEPT c.3.3.2OPERATOR ALGEBRA · 4 MARKS
Commutator of Position with Powers of Momentum

Prove by mathematical induction using the Leibniz product rule that $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}^n] = i\hbar n \hat{p}^{n-1}$ for any positive integer $n$.

Solution

Base case $n=1$: $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar$.
Inductive step: $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}^{n+1}] = [\hat{x}, \hat{p}\hat{p}^n] = [\hat{x}, \hat{p}]\hat{p}^n + \hat{p}[\hat{x}, \hat{p}^n] = i\hbar \hat{p}^n + \hat{p}(i\hbar n \hat{p}^{n-1}) = i\hbar(n+1)\hat{p}^n$. Holds for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

CONCEPT c.3.4.1DERIVATION · 5 MARKS
The Robertson-Schrödinger Uncertainty Relation

Derive the generalized Robertson uncertainty relation $\Delta A \Delta B \ge \frac{1}{2}|\langle [\hat{A}, \hat{B}] \rangle|$ using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Solution

Let $|u\rangle = (\hat{A} - \langle A\rangle)|\psi\rangle$ and $|v\rangle = (\hat{B} - \langle B\rangle)|\psi\rangle$. Then $\langle u|u\rangle = \Delta A^2$, $\langle v|v\rangle = \Delta B^2$. By Cauchy-Schwarz $\Delta A^2 \Delta B^2 \ge |\langle u|v\rangle|^2$. Decomposing $\Delta\hat{A}\Delta\hat{B} = \frac{1}{2}\{\Delta\hat{A}, \Delta\hat{B}\} + \frac{1}{2}[\hat{A}, \hat{B}]$, the real and imaginary parts yield $|\langle u|v\rangle|^2 \ge \frac{1}{4}|\langle [\hat{A}, \hat{B}] \rangle|^2$, proving $\Delta A \Delta B \ge \frac{1}{2}|\langle [\hat{A}, \hat{B}] \rangle|$.

3.5 Unitary Transformations & 3.6 Matrix Mechanics

CONCEPT c.3.5.2SYMMETRY · 5 MARKS
Space Translation Operator as a Unitary Exponent

Show that $\hat{T}(a) = \exp(-ia\hat{p}/\hbar)$ is unitary and generates spatial translations: $\hat{T}(a)\psi(x) = \psi(x - a)$.

Solution

Since $\hat{p}^\dagger = \hat{p}$, $\hat{T}^\dagger(a) = \exp(+ia\hat{p}/\hbar) = \hat{T}(-a)$, so $\hat{T}^\dagger\hat{T} = \hat{I}$. Substituting $\hat{p} = -i\hbar d/dx$, the operator exponential becomes $\exp(-a d/dx) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-a)^n}{n!} \frac{d^n}{dx^n}$, which is the Taylor expansion of $\psi(x - a)$.

CONCEPT c.3.6.3THEOREM · 5 MARKS
Simultaneous Eigenstates of Compatible Observables

Prove that if two Hermitian operators $\hat{A}$ and $\hat{B}$ commute ($[\hat{A}, \hat{B}] = 0$), they possess a complete set of simultaneous orthonormal eigenstates.

Solution

Let $\hat{A}|u_n\rangle = a_n |u_n\rangle$. Operating with $\hat{B}$: $\hat{A}(\hat{B}|u_n\rangle) = \hat{B}\hat{A}|u_n\rangle = a_n(\hat{B}|u_n\rangle)$. Thus $\hat{B}|u_n\rangle$ is in the eigenspace of $a_n$. If non-degenerate, $\hat{B}|u_n\rangle = b_n|u_n\rangle$. If degenerate, diagonalizing $\hat{B}$ inside the eigenspace yields a simultaneous orthonormal eigenbasis.

Commutator of Symmetrized Virial Operator with Position

Find the commutator $[\hat{D}, \hat{x}]$ where $\hat{D} = \frac{1}{2}(\hat{x}\hat{p} + \hat{p}\hat{x})$.

4.1 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator (Ladder Operators & States)

CONCEPT c.4.1.2ALGEBRAIC METHOD · 4 MARKS
Commutation Relation of Ladder Operators & Hamiltonian Factorization

Using $\hat{a} = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\hat{x} + \frac{i}{\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega}}\hat{p}$ and $\hat{a}^\dagger = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\hat{x} - \frac{i}{\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega}}\hat{p}$, prove $[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 1$ and express $\hat{H}$ in terms of $\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$.

Solution

Using $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar$: $$[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = -\frac{i}{2\hbar}[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] + \frac{i}{2\hbar}[\hat{p}, \hat{x}] = -\frac{i}{2\hbar}(i\hbar) + \frac{i}{2\hbar}(-i\hbar) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 1$$ $$\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\hat{x}^2 + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m\hbar\omega} - \frac{i}{2\hbar}[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = \frac{\hat{H}}{\hbar\omega} - \frac{1}{2} \implies \hat{H} = \hbar\omega\left(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$

CONCEPT c.4.1.4DERIVATION · 4 MARKS
Ground State Wave Function via Annihilation Condition $\hat{a}|0\rangle = 0$

From the lowering condition $\hat{a}\psi_0(x) = 0$, derive the normalized ground state wave function $\psi_0(x)$ of the harmonic oscillator.

Solution

$$\left(\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}x + \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega}}\frac{d}{dx}\right)\psi_0(x) = 0 \implies \frac{d\psi_0}{dx} = -\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}x\psi_0(x)$$ Integrating gives $\psi_0(x) = A e^{-\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}x^2}$. Normalizing via $\int_{-\infty}^\infty |\psi_0|^2 dx = |A|^2 \sqrt{\frac{\pi\hbar}{m\omega}} = 1$ yields: $$\psi_0(x) = \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4} e^{-\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}x^2}$$

CONCEPT c.4.1.7THEOREM · 4 MARKS
The Quantum Virial Theorem for Stationary States

Prove that for any stationary state $|n\rangle$ of a 1D harmonic oscillator, the expectation value of kinetic energy equals the expectation value of potential energy: $\langle T \rangle = \langle V \rangle = \frac{1}{2}E_n$.

Proof

Using $[\hat{x}\hat{p}, \hat{H}] = 2i\hbar(\hat{T} - \hat{V})$ and Ehrenfest's theorem $\frac{d}{dt}\langle \hat{x}\hat{p} \rangle = \frac{1}{i\hbar}\langle [\hat{x}\hat{p}, \hat{H}] \rangle = 2(\langle T \rangle - \langle V \rangle) = 0$ for any stationary state, we have $\langle T \rangle = \langle V \rangle$. Since $\langle T \rangle + \langle V \rangle = E_n$, it follows that $\langle T \rangle = \langle V \rangle = \frac{1}{2}E_n = \frac{1}{2}(n + 1/2)\hbar\omega$.

4.2 3D Separation of Variables & 4.3 The Box Potential

CONCEPT c.4.3.2DEGENERACY · 4 MARKS
Symmetry-Induced Degeneracy in a 3D Cubic Box

For a particle in a 3D cubic box of side $L$, find the energies and degeneracies of the ground state and the first three excited states in units of $E_1 = \frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}$.

Solution

Energy is $E = (n_x^2 + n_y^2 + n_z^2)E_1$ with $n_x, n_y, n_z \ge 1$:

  • Ground state $(1,1,1)$: $E = 3E_1$, non-degenerate ($g = 1$).
  • First excited state $(2,1,1), (1,2,1), (1,1,2)$: $E = 6E_1$, 3-fold degenerate ($g = 3$).
  • Second excited state $(2,2,1), (2,1,2), (1,2,2)$: $E = 9E_1$, 3-fold degenerate ($g = 3$).
  • Third excited state $(3,1,1), (1,3,1), (1,1,3)$: $E = 11E_1$, 3-fold degenerate ($g = 3$).

4.4 The 3D Harmonic Oscillator

CONCEPT c.4.4.3COMBINATORICS · 5 MARKS
Degeneracy Formula for 3D Isotropic Oscillator

Prove that the degree of degeneracy of the $N$-th energy level $E_N = (N + 3/2)\hbar\omega$ in a 3D isotropic harmonic oscillator is $g_N = \frac{(N+1)(N+2)}{2}$.

Proof

The degeneracy is the number of non-negative integer solutions to $n_x + n_y + n_z = N$. By the stars-and-bars combinatorial theorem, placing $N$ identical quanta into 3 distinct coordinate bins gives: $$g_N = \binom{N + 3 - 1}{3 - 1} = \binom{N + 2}{2} = \frac{(N+1)(N+2)}{2}$$ For $N=0$, $g_0 = 1$; for $N=1$, $g_1 = 3$; for $N=2$, $g_2 = 6$; for $N=3$, $g_3 = 10$.

5.1 & 5.2 Spherical Coordinates, Separation of Variables & Spherical Harmonics

CONCEPT c.5.1.2 & c.5.2.3THEORY & PROOF · 5 MARKS
Separation of 3D Schrödinger Equation & Spatial Parity of Y_l^m

Show that the time-independent Schrödinger equation in a central potential $V(r)$ separates into radial and angular parts with separation constant $l(l+1)$. State the spatial parity of spherical harmonics under space inversion.

Model Solution

Using $\nabla^2 = \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(r^2\frac{\partial}{\partial r}) - \frac{\hat{L}^2}{\hbar^2 r^2}$ and ansatz $\psi(r,\theta,\phi) = R(r)Y(\theta,\phi)$, dividing by $RY$ gives: $$\frac{1}{R}\left[\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^2\frac{dR}{dr}\right) - \frac{2mr^2}{\hbar^2}(V(r)-E)R\right] = \frac{\hat{L}^2 Y}{\hbar^2 Y} = l(l+1)$$ Under spatial reflection $\mathbf{r} \to -\mathbf{r}$ ($\theta \to \pi - \theta, \phi \to \phi + \pi$), the parity of $Y_l^m$ is strictly $\hat{\Pi} Y_l^m = (-1)^l Y_l^m$.

5.3 The Radial Equation & Infinite Spherical Well

CONCEPT c.5.3.1 & c.5.3.4DERIVATION · 5 MARKS
Effective Potential & s-State Ground Energy of Spherical Well

Derive the effective 1D Schrödinger equation for $u(r) = r R(r)$ and determine the ground state energy of a particle of mass $m$ in an infinite spherical well of radius $a$.

Model Solution

Substituting $R(r) = u(r)/r$ into the radial equation gives $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2 u}{dr^2} + \left[V(r) + \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2mr^2}\right]u = E u$. For $l=0$ inside the well ($r \le a$), $u(r) = A\sin(kr)$ with $u(0)=0$. At the rigid wall $u(a)=0 \implies ka = n\pi$. The ground state ($n=1$) energy is: $$E_{1,0} = \frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2ma^2}$$

5.4 The Hydrogen Atom

CONCEPT c.5.4.3 & c.5.4.6CALCULATION · 5 MARKS
Most Probable Distance vs Average Radius in Ground State Hydrogen

Using the ground state radial wavefunction $R_{10}(r) = 2a_0^{-3/2}e^{-r/a_0}$, find the most probable radius $r_{\text{mp}}$ and compare with the mean radius $\langle r \rangle$.

Model Solution

Radial probability density is $P(r) = r^2|R_{10}|^2 = \frac{4}{a_0^3}r^2 e^{-2r/a_0}$. Setting $\frac{dP}{dr} = 0 \implies 2r(1 - r/a_0)e^{-2r/a_0} = 0 \implies r_{\text{mp}} = a_0$. The expectation value is $\langle r \rangle = \int_0^\infty r P(r) dr = \frac{4}{a_0^3}\frac{3!}{(2/a_0)^4} = \frac{3}{2}a_0$. Thus, $\langle r \rangle = 1.5 a_0 > r_{\text{mp}} = a_0$ due to the long exponential tail.

6.1 & 6.2 General Angular Momentum & Ladder Operators

CONCEPT c.6.1.2 & c.6.2.2ALGEBRA & PROOF · 5 MARKS
Ladder Operators Action & Angle of Precession Cone

Using ladder operators $\hat{J}_\pm = \hat{J}_x \pm i\hat{J}_y$, prove that $\hat{J}_\pm|j, m\rangle = \hbar\sqrt{j(j+1) - m(m\pm 1)}|j, m\pm 1\rangle$. Calculate the minimum angle between $\mathbf{J}$ and the $z$-axis for $j = 1$.

Model Solution

Norm evaluation $\langle j, m|\hat{J}_\mp\hat{J}_\pm|j, m\rangle = \langle j, m|(\hat{J}^2 - \hat{J}_z^2 \mp \hbar\hat{J}_z)|j, m\rangle = \hbar^2[j(j+1) - m(m\pm 1)]$. Taking the square root with positive Condon-Shortley phase gives the result. For $j=1$, length $|\mathbf{J}| = \hbar\sqrt{1(2)} = \sqrt{2}\hbar$. Maximum projection is $m = 1$, giving $\cos\theta_{\min} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \implies \theta_{\min} = 45^\circ$.

6.4 Spin 1/2 & Pauli Matrices

CONCEPT c.6.4.2 & c.6.4.4DYNAMICS · 5 MARKS
Master Pauli Identity & Larmor Precession

State the master identity for $(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{A})(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{B})$ and describe the time evolution of a spin-1/2 particle initially along $+x$ in a magnetic field $\mathbf{B} = B_0 \hat{\mathbf{z}}$.

Model Solution

Identity: $(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{A})(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{B}) = (\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{B})I + i\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot(\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B})$. Under $\hat{H} = -\gamma B_0 \hat{S}_z$, the state evolves as $|\chi(t)\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e^{i\omega_L t/2}|\uparrow\rangle + e^{-i\omega_L t/2}|\downarrow\rangle)$, where $\omega_L = \gamma B_0$. The expectation values are $\langle S_x(t) \rangle = \frac{\hbar}{2}\cos(\omega_L t)$, $\langle S_y(t) \rangle = \frac{\hbar}{2}\sin(\omega_L t)$, and $\langle S_z(t) \rangle = 0$, representing pure precession in the $xy$-plane.

6.5 Addition of Angular Momenta & Clebsch-Gordan Coefficients

CONCEPT c.6.5.4 & c.6.5.5COUPLING · 5 MARKS
Singlet vs Triplet for Two Spin-1/2 Particles & Spin-Orbit Coupling

Construct the singlet ($S=0$) and triplet ($S=1$) states for two spin-1/2 particles and calculate the eigenvalue of $\hat{\mathbf{S}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{S}}_2$ on both.

Model Solution

Triplet ($S=1$): $|1, 1\rangle = |\uparrow\uparrow\rangle$, $|1, 0\rangle = \frac{|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle + |\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}$, $|1, -1\rangle = |\downarrow\downarrow\rangle$. Singlet ($S=0$): $|0, 0\rangle = \frac{|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle - |\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}$. Using $\hat{\mathbf{S}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{S}}_2 = \frac{1}{2}(\hat{S}^2 - \frac{3}{2}\hbar^2)$: $$\langle \hat{\mathbf{S}}_1 \cdot \hat{\mathbf{S}}_2 \rangle = \begin{cases} +\frac{1}{4}\hbar^2 & (S = 1, \text{ Triplet}) \\ -\frac{3}{4}\hbar^2 & (S = 0, \text{ Singlet}) \end{cases}$$

If two non-interacting electrons are in the state $|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle$, what is the probability of measuring total spin $S = 1$?
