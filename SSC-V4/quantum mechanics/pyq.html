1.1 The Schrödinger Equation & 1.2 The Statistical Interpretation

CONCEPT c.1.1.2THEORY · DERIVATION
The Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation

State the Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation for a particle of mass $m$ in a potential $V(x,t)$. Explain the role of the imaginary unit $i$, and show how the equation connects to de Broglie matter waves.

Solution & Invariants

$$i\hbar \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial t} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2 \Psi}{\partial x^2} + V(x,t)\Psi$$

The factor $i$ on the left side rotates $\Psi$ in the complex plane ($e^{-i\omega t}$), converting exponential dissipation into oscillatory phase rotation and quantum interference. Applying the operators $E \to i\hbar \partial/\partial t$ and $p \to -i\hbar \partial/\partial x$ to a plane wave $\Psi = A e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$ directly reproduces the classical energy relation $E = \frac{p^2}{2m} + V$.

CONCEPT c.1.2.1DEFINITION · BORN'S RULE
Born's Probability Density and Interval Detection

State Max Born's statistical interpretation of the wave function $\Psi(x,t)$. What is the probability $P_{ab}$ of locating the particle between $a$ and $b$ at time $t$?

Solution

$$P_{ab}(t) = \int_a^b |\Psi(x,t)|^2 dx = \int_a^b \Psi^*(x,t)\Psi(x,t) dx$$

The wave function is a probability amplitude; its modulus squared $\rho(x,t) = |\Psi(x,t)|^2$ is a probability density with dimensions of $1/\text{Length}$.

Common Trap: Do not confuse probability density $\rho(x)$ with probability $P$. Density can exceed 1 at a point; only the integrated probability is bounded between 0 and 1.

1.3 Probability Theory & 1.4 Normalization

GRIFFITHS PROBLEM 1.46 MARKS · LEVEL 3
Triangular Wave Packet Normalization and Statistics

At $t = 0$, a particle is described by the triangular wave packet: $$\Psi(x,0) = \begin{cases} A \frac{x}{a}, & 0 \le x \le a \\ A \frac{b-x}{b-a}, & a \le x \le b \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$ (a) Normalize $\Psi(x,0)$ to determine $A$. (b) What is the probability of finding the particle to the left of $a$? (c) Calculate $\langle x \rangle$.

Worked Solution

(a) $\int_0^b |\Psi|^2 dx = A^2 [\frac{a}{3} + \frac{b-a}{3}] = A^2 \frac{b}{3} = 1 \implies A = \sqrt{\frac{3}{b}}$.

(b) $P(x < a) = A^2 \frac{a}{3} = \frac{a}{b}$.

(c) $\langle x \rangle = \frac{2a + b}{4}$.

CONCEPT c.1.4.2THEORETICAL PROOF · 5 MARKS
Preservation of Normalization in Time

Prove that if a wave function is normalized at $t = 0$, the Schrödinger equation guarantees it remains normalized for all future times: $\frac{d}{dt}\int_{-\infty}^\infty |\Psi(x,t)|^2 dx = 0$.

Proof

$$\frac{d}{dt}\int |\Psi|^2 dx = \int \left(\frac{\partial \Psi^*}{\partial t}\Psi + \Psi^* \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial t}\right) dx$$

Substitute $\frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial t} = \frac{i\hbar}{2m}\frac{\partial^2 \Psi}{\partial x^2} - \frac{i}{\hbar}V\Psi$. Because $V$ is real, the potential terms cancel: $$\frac{\partial |\Psi|^2}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[\frac{i\hbar}{2m}\left(\Psi^* \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial \Psi^*}{\partial x}\Psi\right)\right] = -\frac{\partial J}{\partial x}$$

Integrating across $(-\infty, \infty)$ yields $[-J]_{-\infty}^\infty = 0$ because $\Psi \to 0$ as $x \to \pm\infty$.

1.5 Momentum & Operators

CONCEPT c.1.5.4THEORY & FLUX
Probability Current Density and Continuity Equation

Define the probability current density $J(x,t)$ and state the quantum continuity equation. What is $J$ for a plane wave $A e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$ and for a purely real spatial wave function $\psi(x) \in \mathbb{R}$?

Solution

$$J(x,t) = \frac{\hbar}{2mi}\left(\Psi^* \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial x} - \Psi \frac{\partial \Psi^*}{\partial x}\right) = \frac{\hbar}{m}\text{Im}\left(\Psi^* \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial x}\right)$$

Continuity: $\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial J}{\partial x} = 0$.
For a plane wave: $J = \frac{\hbar k}{m}|A|^2 = v \rho$.
For real $\psi(x)$: $J = 0$, meaning standing waves have zero net spatial probability current.

CONCEPT c.1.5.5EHRENFEST'S THEOREM · 5 MARKS
Ehrenfest's Theorem for Momentum

State and prove Ehrenfest's theorem for the rate of change of the momentum expectation value $\frac{d\langle p \rangle}{dt}$.

Solution

$$\frac{d\langle p \rangle}{dt} = \left\langle -\frac{\partial V}{\partial x} \right\rangle = \langle F(x) \rangle$$

Together with $d\langle x \rangle/dt = \langle p \rangle/m$, this demonstrates that average quantum coordinates follow Newton's classical equations of motion.

1.6 The Uncertainty Principle & IIT JAM Past Papers

IIT JAM PH 20242 MARKS · LEVEL 4
Superposition Probability Current Density

A particle of mass $m$ is described by the 1D wave function $\psi(x) = A e^{ikx} + B e^{-ikx}$. Show that the net probability current density is $J = \frac{\hbar k}{m}(|A|^2 - |B|^2)$.

Worked Solution

Substituting $\psi$ into $J = \frac{\hbar}{m}\text{Im}(\psi^* d\psi/dx)$: $$\psi^* \frac{d\psi}{dx} = (A^* e^{-ikx} + B^* e^{ikx}) \cdot ik(A e^{ikx} - B e^{-ikx}) = ik(|A|^2 - |B|^2) + \text{real cross terms}$$ Taking the imaginary part extracts $k(|A|^2 - |B|^2)$, yielding $J = \frac{\hbar k}{m}(|A|^2 - |B|^2)$. The forward current from $A$ subtracts the backward current from $B$.

IIT JAM PH 20211 MARK · LEVEL 4
Ground State Energy via Heisenberg Uncertainty

Estimate the ground-state energy of a 1D harmonic oscillator $H = \frac{p^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2$ using $\Delta x \Delta p \ge \hbar/2$.

Worked Solution

Setting $\Delta p \approx \frac{\hbar}{2\Delta x}$: $$E(\Delta x) = \frac{\hbar^2}{8m(\Delta x)^2} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2(\Delta x)^2$$ Minimizing with respect to $\Delta x$ yields $(\Delta x)^2 = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}$, giving $E_0 = \frac{1}{4}\hbar\omega + \frac{1}{4}\hbar\omega = \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega$.
