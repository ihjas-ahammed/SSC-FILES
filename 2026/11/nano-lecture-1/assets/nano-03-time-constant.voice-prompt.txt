SCRIPT:
A hot cup cools in minutes.
A hot nanoparticle?
In a flash. Why?

Picture a hot body joined to a cold one by a thin channel.
Heat flows down it, and the hot body cools.

Heat flow per second:
k a T over l.
That heat is lost by the body’s C V.
Equate and integrate:
T falls exponentially.

The time constant, tau, is l C V over k a.
Volume goes as l cubed, area as l squared.
So tau goes as l squared.
A thousand times smaller, a million times faster.

That’s why a hot AFM tip can write data.
A current heats the tip, and it writes a bit.
The tip is tiny, so it heats and cools fast, on very little power.
A P Z T sensor reads the bits.

Quick recap:
channel, flow, decay, tau, l squared, tip.

Smaller body, faster cooling.
