VOICE / STYLE:
Calm, friendly physics teacher introducing nanoscience to undergraduate students.
SLOW, unhurried teaching pace, noticeably slower than a social-media reel. Clear diction, natural, not robotic.
Pause about half a second at each line break and a full second at blank lines, so students can read the cards on screen.
Say "C D S" letter by letter. Light emphasis on key words, never shouting.
No music, no sound effects, dry studio recording.
Target total length: about 81–93 seconds.
Read the script exactly as written. Do not add an intro, outro or sign-off.

SCRIPT:
Your phone packs in billions of transistors.
How did it get so small?

Start with two prefixes.
Micro is a millionth of a metre.
Nano is a billionth.
Divide one by the other:
nano is a thousand times smaller than micro.

Now walk down the ladder.
A hair: fifty microns.
A blood cell: five microns.
Below one micron, the nano world begins.
A virus: twenty nanometers.
DNA: about two nanometers.

Best example: your phone.
Computers once filled whole buildings.
Now they fit a pocket, because chip parts shrank from micro to nano size.

So what counts as nano?
At least one dimension of one to a hundred nanometers, and properties that change with size.
Shrink a C D S crystal:
red, orange, then yellow.

Quick recap:
micro, nano, ladder, phone, range, colour.

Smaller size, new physics.
