Module 1 PYQ Practice (6 Questions • 9 Marks) Axioms, Conditioning & Bayes' Theorem
GATE DA 2024 — Q.12
1 Mark MCQ PYQ PDF p. 7
Question Statement:
Three fair coins are tossed independently. Let event $T$ be "at least two heads are obtained", and event $S$ be "at least two tails are obtained". Which of the following statements is TRUE?
(A) Events $T$ and $S$ are mutually exclusive.
(B) Events $T$ and $S$ are independent.
(C) $P(T \cup S) < 1$.
(D) $P(T \cap S) > 0$.
📖 Source Textbook: Sheldon Ross — A First Course in Probability (10th Ed.) • Section: Section 2.2 & 2.3 "Sample Space, Events & Axioms" • Cutout Link: pp. 42–64 (Cutout p. 18) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: In 3 coin tosses, the number of heads $H \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$. Event $T \iff H \ge 2 \iff H \in \{2, 3\}$. Event $S \iff \text{tails} \ge 2 \iff H \le 1 \iff H \in \{0, 1\}$. Since $\{2, 3\} \cap \{0, 1\} = \emptyset$, $T \cap S = \emptyset$, meaning the events cannot occur together ($P(T \cap S) = 0$). Thus $T$ and $S$ are mutually exclusive (and exhaustive, $P(T \cup S) = 1$). Correct option: (A).
GATE DA 2024 — Q.58
2 Marks NAT PYQ PDF p. 36
Question Statement:
Let $T$ and $S$ be events with $P(T') = 0.6$, $P(S \mid T) = 0.3$, and $P(S \mid T') = 0.6$. Compute the conditional probability $P(T \mid S)$.
📖 Source Textbook: Sheldon Ross — A First Course in Probability (10th Ed.) • Section: Section 3.3 "Bayes's Formula" • Cutout Link: pp. 82–101 (Cutout p. 51) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: $P(T) = 1 - P(T') = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4$. By the Law of Total Probability: $P(S) = P(S \mid T)P(T) + P(S \mid T')P(T') = (0.3)(0.4) + (0.6)(0.6) = 0.12 + 0.36 = 0.48$. By Bayes' Theorem: $P(T \mid S) = \frac{P(S \mid T)P(T)}{P(S)} = \frac{0.12}{0.48} = \frac{1}{4} = \mathbf{0.25}$.
GATE DA 2025 — Q.31
1 Mark NAT PYQ PDF p. 16
Question Statement:
Box 1 contains 2 white and 3 black balls. Box 2 contains 4 white and 1 black ball. Box 3 contains 3 white and 4 black balls. A box is chosen uniformly at random and a ball is drawn. If the drawn ball is white, what is the probability that it came from Box 2?
📖 Source Textbook: Sheldon Ross — A First Course in Probability (10th Ed.) • Section: Section 3.3 "Bayes's Formula" • Cutout Link: pp. 82–101 (Cutout p. 51) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: $P(B_i) = 1/3$ for $i=1, 2, 3$. $P(W|B_1) = 2/5 = 0.40$, $P(W|B_2) = 4/5 = 0.80$, $P(W|B_3) = 3/7 \approx 0.4286$. Total probability: $P(W) = \frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{5} + \frac{4}{5} + \frac{3}{7}\right) = \frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{6}{5} + \frac{3}{7}\right) = \frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{57}{35}\right) = \frac{19}{35}$. Posterior: $P(B_2 \mid W) = \frac{\frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{4}{5}}{\frac{19}{35}} = \frac{4/15}{19/35} = \frac{28}{57} \approx \mathbf{0.491}$.
GATE DA 2026 — Q.19
1 Mark MCQ PYQ PDF p. 12
Question Statement:
A non-empty subset $M$ of $\{1, 2, \dots, 2026\}$ is selected uniformly at random. What is the probability that the minimum element of $M$ is equal to 10?
(A) $\frac{2^{2016}}{2^{2026}-1}$      (B) $\frac{2^{2016}-1}{2^{2026}-1}$      (C) $\frac{1}{2026}$      (D) $\frac{2^{2015}}{2^{2026}-1}$
📖 Source Textbook: Sheldon Ross — A First Course in Probability (10th Ed.) • Section: Section 1.4 & 2.5 "Combinations & Equally Likely Outcomes" • Cutout Link: pp. 18–26 (Cutout p. 1) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: Total number of non-empty subsets of a 2026-element set is $2^{2026} - 1$. For 10 to be the minimum element of $M$: (1) 10 must be in $M$, (2) elements $1, 2, \dots, 9$ cannot be in $M$, (3) each of the remaining $2026 - 10 = 2016$ elements (from 11 to 2026) can either be in $M$ or not. There are $2^{2016}$ such subsets. Thus, probability is $\mathbf{\frac{2^{2016}}{2^{2026}-1}}$. Correct option: (A).
GATE DA 2026 — Q.47
2 Marks MCQ PYQ PDF p. 30
Question Statement:
In a corpus of 30 stories, 20 are written by Author $X$ and 10 by Author $Y$. A story is chosen at random. A specific stylistic feature occurs in $80\%$ of stories by $X$ and $40\%$ of stories by $Y$. If the chosen story contains the feature, what is the probability it was written by Author $X$?
📖 Source Textbook: Sheldon Ross — A First Course in Probability (10th Ed.) • Section: Section 3.3 "Bayes's Formula" • Cutout Link: pp. 82–101 (Cutout p. 51) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: Prior: $P(X) = 20/30 = 2/3$, $P(Y) = 10/30 = 1/3$. Likelihoods: $P(F|X) = 0.8$, $P(F|Y) = 0.4$. Total probability of feature: $P(F) = (2/3)(0.8) + (1/3)(0.4) = \frac{1.6 + 0.4}{3} = \frac{2.0}{3}$. Bayes' Theorem: $P(X|F) = \frac{(2/3)(0.8)}{2/3} = \mathbf{0.80} = \mathbf{4/5}$.
GATE DA 2026 — Q.57
2 Marks NAT PYQ PDF p. 35
Question Statement:
A disease affects $1\%$ of a population. A diagnostic test has sensitivity $P(+|D) = 0.95$ and specificity $P(-|D^c) = 0.90$. A randomly chosen person tests positive. Compute the posterior probability $P(D|+)$ that the person actually has the disease.
📖 Source Textbook: Sheldon Ross — A First Course in Probability (10th Ed.) • Section: Section 3.3 "Bayes's Formula (Diagnostic Testing)" • Cutout Link: pp. 82–101 (Cutout p. 51) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: $P(D) = 0.01, P(D^c) = 0.99$. False positive rate: $P(+|D^c) = 1 - 0.90 = 0.10$. Total positive probability: $P(+) = (0.95)(0.01) + (0.10)(0.99) = 0.0095 + 0.0990 = 0.1085$. Posterior: $P(D|+) = \frac{0.0095}{0.1085} \approx \mathbf{0.08755}$ ($\approx 8.76\%$).

Consider the statements: (I) For a Poisson random variable, the mean and variance are equal. (II) A normal random variable is symmetric about its mean. Which of the following is correct?
(A) Both (I) and (II) are TRUE      (B) (I) is TRUE, (II) is FALSE
(C) (I) is FALSE, (II) is TRUE      (D) Both (I) and (II) are FALSE

GATE DA 2024 — Q.36
2 Marks MCQ PYQ PDF p. 21
Question Statement:
A fair six-sided die is rolled repeatedly until two consecutive 6s appear. What is the expected number of rolls?
(A) 36      (B) 42      (C) 48      (D) 30
📖 Source Textbook: Sheldon Ross — A First Course in Probability (10th Ed.) • Section: Section 4.3 & 4.8 "Expected Value & First-Step Analysis" • Cutout Link: pp. 131–147 (Cutout p. 1) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: Let $E$ be expected rolls to get two consecutive 6s, and $E_1$ be expected rolls given that the last roll was a 6. By conditioning on the next roll: $E = 1 + \frac{5}{6}E + \frac{1}{6}E_1$, and $E_1 = 1 + \frac{1}{6}(0) + \frac{5}{6}E = 1 + \frac{5}{6}E$. Substituting $E_1$: $E = 1 + \frac{5}{6}E + \frac{1}{6}(1 + \frac{5}{6}E) = \frac{7}{6} + \frac{35}{36}E \implies \frac{1}{36}E = \frac{7}{6} \implies E = 7 \times 6 = \mathbf{42}$. Correct option: (B).
GATE DA 2025 — Q.39
2 Marks MCQ PYQ PDF p. 21
Question Statement:
Let $F_X(x)$ be a cumulative distribution function of a random variable $X$ with a jump discontinuity at $x = c$. Which of the following is equal to $P(X = c)$?
(A) $F(c) - F(c^-)$      (B) $F(c^+) - F(c)$      (C) $F(c)$      (D) 0
📖 Source Textbook: Sheldon Ross — A First Course in Probability (10th Ed.) • Section: Section 4.10 "Properties of the Cumulative Distribution Function" • Cutout Link: pp. 171–176 (Cutout p. 37) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: By Proposition 4.1 of Ross: for any real number $c$, $P(X = c) = F(c) - \lim_{h \to 0^+} F(c - h) = F(c) - F(c^-)$. For a continuous random variable, $F(c) = F(c^-) \implies P(X = c) = 0$. For a discrete or mixed random variable, the jump size exactly equals the probability mass at that point. Correct option: (A).
GATE DA 2025 — Q.54
2 Marks MSQ PYQ PDF p. 29
Question Statement:
Consider an infinite sequence of independent coin tosses with $P(\text{Heads}) = p \in (0, 1)$. Let $X_n$ be the indicator of Heads on the $n$-th toss. Let $S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$. Which of the following statements is/are TRUE?
(A) $E[S_n/n] = p$
(B) $\text{Var}(S_n/n) = \frac{p(1-p)}{n}$
(C) $S_n \sim \text{Binomial}(n, p)$
(D) $\lim_{n \to \infty} \text{Var}(S_n/n) = 0$
📖 Source Textbook: Sheldon Ross — A First Course in Probability (10th Ed.) • Section: Section 4.6 "Bernoulli and Binomial Random Variables" • Cutout Link: pp. 148–155 (Cutout p. 18) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: $S_n$ is the sum of $n$ independent $\text{Bernoulli}(p)$ variables, so $S_n \sim \text{Binomial}(n, p)$ (C is true). $E[S_n/n] = \frac{np}{n} = p$ (A is true). $\text{Var}(S_n/n) = \frac{1}{n^2}\text{Var}(S_n) = \frac{np(1-p)}{n^2} = \frac{p(1-p)}{n}$ (B is true). As $n \to \infty$, $\frac{p(1-p)}{n} \to 0$ (D is true). Correct options: (A), (B), (C), and (D).
GATE DA 2025 — Q.61
2 Marks NAT PYQ PDF p. 32
Question Statement:
A bag contains 5 white and 10 black balls. A sample of 3 balls is drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that exactly 2 white balls are drawn?
📖 Source Textbook: Sheldon Ross — A First Course in Probability (10th Ed.) • Section: Section 4.8 "The Hypergeometric Distribution" • Cutout Link: pp. 163–166 (Cutout p. 33) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: By the hypergeometric PMF: $P(X = 2) = \frac{\binom{5}{2}\binom{10}{1}}{\binom{15}{3}}$. Compute: $\binom{5}{2} = 10$, $\binom{10}{1} = 10$, so numerator $= 100$. Denominator: $\binom{15}{3} = \frac{15 \times 14 \times 13}{6} = 455$. Thus, $P(X = 2) = \frac{100}{455} = \frac{20}{91} \approx \mathbf{0.2198}$.

Module 3 PYQ Practice (11 Questions • 18 Marks) Continuous Distributions, Joint PDFs & Conditioning
GATE DA 2024 — Q.56
2 Marks NAT PYQ PDF p. 35
Question Statement:
Let $X \sim \text{Uniform}(1, 3)$ and $Y \sim \text{Uniform}(2, 4)$ be independent continuous random variables. Compute the probability $P(X + Y \le 5)$.
📖 Source Textbook: Sheldon Ross — A First Course in Probability (10th Ed.) • Section: Section 5.3 & 6.2 "Uniform RVs & Independent Joint Distributions" • Cutout Link: pp. 245–274 (Cutout p. 42) ↗
