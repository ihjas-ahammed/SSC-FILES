Module 1 PYQ Practice (3 Questions • 5 Marks) Vector Spaces, Subspaces & Basis
GATE DA 2024 — Q.47
2 Marks MSQ PYQ PDF p. 28
Question Statement:
Which of the following subsets of $\mathbb{R}^3$ is/are vector subspace(s)?
(A) $S_1 = \{(x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid x + 2y + 3z = 0\}$
(B) $S_2 = \{(x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid x - y + z = 1\}$
(C) $S_3 = \{(x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid xy = 0\}$
(D) $S_4 = \{(x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid x = y = z\}$
📖 Source Textbook: Gilbert Strang (4th Ed.) • Section: Section 3.1 "Spaces of Vectors" • Cutout Link: pp. 131–142 (Cutout p. 12) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: A subset $S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is a subspace if and only if it contains $\mathbf{0}$ and is closed under addition and scalar multiplication. (A) and (D) pass all axioms (homogeneous linear equations). (B) fails because $\mathbf{0} \notin S_2$. (C) fails closure under addition: $(1, 0, 0) \in S_3$ and $(0, 1, 0) \in S_3$, but $(1, 1, 0) \notin S_3$. Correct options: (A) and (D).
GATE DA 2026 — Q.22
1 Mark MCQ PYQ PDF p. 13
Question Statement:
Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix with real entries. Which of the following statements is ALWAYS TRUE regarding the four fundamental subspaces?
(A) The row space of $A$ and the null space of $A$ are orthogonal complements in $\mathbb{R}^n$.
(B) The column space of $A$ and the null space of $A$ are orthogonal complements in $\mathbb{R}^m$.
(C) $\text{dim}(\mathbf{C}(A)) + \text{dim}(\mathbf{C}(A^T)) = n$.
(D) The left null space of $A$ is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$.
📖 Source Textbook: Gilbert Strang (4th Ed.) • Section: Section 3.6 & 4.1 "Dimensions & Orthogonality of Four Subspaces" • Cutout Link: pp. 195–205 (Cutout p. 76) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: By the Fundamental Theorem of Linear Algebra (Part 2), $\mathbf{C}(A^T) \perp \mathbf{N}(A)$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$, meaning every row vector is perpendicular to every vector in the null space. Correct option: (A).
GATE DA 2026 — Q.36
2 Marks MSQ PYQ PDF p. 23
Question Statement:
Let $V$ be the vector space of all $2 \times 2$ real symmetric matrices. Which of the following subsets of $V$ form a basis for $V$?
📖 Source Textbook: Gilbert Strang (4th Ed.) • Section: Section 3.5 "Independence, Basis and Dimension" • Cutout Link: pp. 179–194 (Cutout p. 60) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: A general $2 \times 2$ symmetric matrix has the form $\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ b & c \end{bmatrix}$, depending on 3 independent parameters. Thus, $\text{dim}(V) = 3$. Any basis must contain exactly 3 linearly independent symmetric matrices that span $V$.
Module 2 PYQ Practice (3 Questions • 5 Marks) Linear Systems & Determinants
GATE DA 2024 — Q.35
1 Mark NAT PYQ PDF p. 20
Question Statement:
Let $M = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 3 & 1 & 3 \\ 4 & 3 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$. Compute the determinant $\det(M^2 + 12M)$.
📖 Source Textbook: Gilbert Strang (4th Ed.) • Section: Section 2.5 & 5.1 "Inverse Matrices & Determinant Properties" • Cutout Link: pp. 255–265 (Cutout p. 105) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: Notice that $\text{Row}_3 = [4, 3, 6] = [1, 2, 3] + [3, 1, 3] = \text{Row}_1 + \text{Row}_2$. Because the rows are linearly dependent, $M$ is singular and $\det(M) = 0$. Factoring the expression: $\det(M^2 + 12M) = \det(M(M + 12I)) = \det(M) \cdot \det(M + 12I) = 0 \cdot \det(...) = \mathbf{0}$.
GATE DA 2024 — Q.48
2 Marks MSQ PYQ PDF p. 29
Question Statement:
Consider a system of linear equations $M\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$. Which of the following statements is/are TRUE?
(A) If $M$ is a $3 \times 3$ matrix and $\text{rank}(M) < 3$, then for every $\mathbf{b}$ the system has a unique solution.
(B) If $M$ is a $3 \times 3$ matrix with $\text{rank}(M) < 3$, then there exists some $\mathbf{q}$ with infinitely many solutions and some $\mathbf{p}$ with no solution.
(C) If $M$ is a $2 \times 3$ matrix, the system can have a unique solution for some $\mathbf{b}$.
(D) If $M$ is a $3 \times 2$ matrix with $\text{rank}(M) = 2$, the system has either 0 or 1 solution for any $\mathbf{b}$.
📖 Source Textbook: Gilbert Strang (4th Ed.) • Section: Section 2.1 & 3.4 "Linear Equations & Complete Solution" • Cutout Link: pp. 166–178 (Cutout p. 90) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: For square $3 \times 3$ matrices, unique solution $\iff \det(M) \ne 0 \iff \text{rank} = 3 \implies$ unique solution for all RHS (so (A) is false). When $\text{rank}(M) < 3$, $\mathbf{q} \in \mathbf{C}(M)$ yields infinite solutions while $\mathbf{p} \notin \mathbf{C}(M)$ yields no solutions, making (B) TRUE. For $2 \times 3$ matrices, $n=3 > m=2$, so $\text{nullity} \ge 1$ (always infinite solutions if consistent, never unique, so (C) is false). For $3 \times 2$ matrices with full column rank 2, a consistent system has a unique solution and an inconsistent system has no solutions, making (D) TRUE. Correct options: (B) and (D).
GATE DA 2025 — Q.12
2 Marks MCQ PYQ PDF p. 7
Question Statement:
Let $\mathbf{u} = [1, 2, 3]^T$ and $\mathbf{v} = [4, 5, 6]^T$. Let $M = I_3 + \mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T$. What is the determinant $\det(M)$?
(A) 1      (B) 32      (C) 33      (D) 0
📖 Source Textbook: Gilbert Strang (4th Ed.) • Section: Section 5.1 & 5.2 "Properties of Determinants & Outer Products" • Cutout Link: pp. 255–279 (Cutout p. 105) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: By the matrix determinant lemma: $\det(I + \mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T) = 1 + \mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{u}$. Compute the inner product: $\mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{u} = (4)(1) + (5)(2) + (6)(3) = 4 + 10 + 18 = 32$. Thus, $\det(M) = 1 + 32 = \mathbf{33}$. Correct option: (C).
Module 3 PYQ Practice (6 Questions • 10 Marks) Orthogonality, Projections & Quadratic Forms
GATE DA 2024 — Q.49
2 Marks MSQ PYQ PDF p. 30
Question Statement:
Let $U$ be a proper subspace of $\mathbb{R}^3$, and let $M$ be the projection matrix that projects any vector in $\mathbb{R}^3$ onto $U$. Which of the following statements is/are TRUE?
(A) If $\text{dim}(U) = 1$, then $\text{dim}(\text{Null}(M)) = 1$
(B) If $\text{dim}(U) = 2$, then $\text{dim}(\text{Null}(M)) = 1$
(C) $M^3 = M$
(D) If $\text{dim}(U) = 1$, then $M^2 = M$
📖 Source Textbook: Gilbert Strang (4th Ed.) • Section: Section 4.2 "Projections onto Subspaces" • Cutout Link: pp. 217–228 (Cutout p. 25) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: Any projection matrix is idempotent: $M^2 = M$, which immediately implies $M^3 = M^2 \cdot M = M \cdot M = M$ (so C and D are true). The column space of $M$ is $U$, so $\text{rank}(M) = \dim(U)$. By the Rank-Nullity Theorem, $\dim(\text{Null}(M)) = 3 - \dim(U)$. When $\dim(U) = 2$, $\dim(\text{Null}(M)) = 3 - 2 = 1$ (so B is true). When $\dim(U) = 1$, $\dim(\text{Null}(M)) = 3 - 1 = 2$ (so A is false). Correct options: (B), (C), and (D).
GATE DA 2025 — Q.38
1 Mark MCQ PYQ PDF p. 20
Question Statement:
Let $M = I_n - \frac{1}{n} \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^T$, where $\mathbf{1}$ is the $n \times 1$ column vector of all ones. Which of the following is the rank of $M$?
(A) $n$      (B) $n - 1$      (C) 1      (D) 0
📖 Source Textbook: Gilbert Strang (4th Ed.) • Section: Section 4.2 & 8.6 "Projections & Statistics (Centering Matrix)" • Cutout Link: pp. 464–469 (Cutout p. 78) ↗
Theoretical Rationale: $M$ is the canonical centering matrix. Compute $M^2 = (I - \frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^T)(I - \frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^T) = I - \frac{2}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^T + \frac{1}{n^2}\mathbf{1}(\mathbf{1}^T\mathbf{1})\mathbf{1}^T$. Since $\mathbf{1}^T\mathbf{1} = n$, this simplifies to $I - \frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^T = M$ ($M$ is idempotent). Trace: $\text{trace}(M) = \text{trace}(I_n) - \frac{1}{n}\text{trace}(\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^T) = n - \frac{1}{n}(n) = \mathbf{n - 1}$. Rank: for idempotent matrices, $\text{rank}(M) = \text{trace}(M) = \mathbf{n - 1}$. Correct option: (B).

Question Statement:
Let $M = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & c \end{bmatrix}$. For what values of $c \in \mathbb{R}$ is the quadratic form $\mathbf{x}^T M \mathbf{x} > 0$ for all non-zero $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^2$?
(A) $c > 0$      (B) $c > 0.5$      (C) $c > 2$      (D) $c > 1$

Let $\mathbf{u}_1, \mathbf{u}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^n$ be mutually orthogonal unit vectors. Let $M = \mathbf{u}_1\mathbf{u}_1^T + \mathbf{u}_2\mathbf{u}_2^T$. Which of the following statements is FALSE?
(A) $M^2 = M$
(B) $\text{rank}(M) = 2$
(C) The non-zero eigenvalues of $M$ are both equal to 1
(D) $M$ is positive definite on $\mathbb{R}^n$ (for $n > 2$)

GATE DA 2026 — Q.65
2 Marks NAT PYQ PDF p. 39
Question Statement:
Let $Q$ be a $3 \times 3$ orthogonal matrix. Let $\mathbf{x} = [3, -4, 0]^T$. What is the Euclidean norm $\|Q\mathbf{x}\|_2$?
📖 Source Textbook: Gilbert Strang (4th Ed.) • Section: Section 4.4 "Orthogonal Bases and Gram-Schmidt" • Cutout Link: pp. 241–254 (Cutout p. 49) ↗

Question Statement:
Let $M = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}$ for $\theta \in (0, \pi)$. Which of the following is TRUE about the eigenvalues of $M$?
(A) Both eigenvalues are real and positive.
(B) Both eigenvalues are real and negative.
(C) The eigenvalues are complex conjugates with magnitude 1.
(D) One eigenvalue is 0 and the other is 1.
