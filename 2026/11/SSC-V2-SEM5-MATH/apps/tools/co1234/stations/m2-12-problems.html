The chapter 2 and chapter 3 exercises that test the syllabus items directly: proofs of convexity, membership questions, dual cones, conjugates, quasi- and log-concavity. Each answer is worked from the definitions in this module. Convex sets (Boyd ch. 2 exercises)

$C$ convex, $\mathbf x_1,\dots,\mathbf x_k\in C$, $\theta_i\ge0$, $\sum\theta_i=1$. Show $\sum\theta_i\mathbf x_i\in C$.

Induction on $k$; the definition is the case $k=2$.

$k=1$: trivial; $k=2$: the definition. Suppose true for $k-1$, and let $\theta_k\lt1$ (if $\theta_k=1$ the point is $\mathbf x_k$). Then $$\sum_{i=1}^k\theta_i\mathbf x_i=(1-\theta_k)\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac{\theta_i}{1-\theta_k}\mathbf x_i}_{\mathbf y}+\theta_k\mathbf x_k.$$ The weights $\theta_i/(1-\theta_k)$ are $\ge0$ and sum to $1$, so $\mathbf y\in C$ by the induction hypothesis; then the $k=2$ case puts the combination in $C$.

Show $C$ is convex iff its intersection with any line is convex; affine iff every line intersection is affine.

(⇒) A line is convex (affine), and intersections preserve it. (⇐) Take $\mathbf x_1,\mathbf x_2\in C$; the line $L$ through them meets $C$ in a convex set containing $\mathbf x_1,\mathbf x_2$, so it contains the segment between them; hence $C$ is convex. For affine: $C\cap L$ is affine and contains two points of $L$, so it is all of $L$.

If $C$ is closed and contains $(\mathbf a+\mathbf b)/2$ whenever $\mathbf a,\mathbf b\in C$, prove $C$ is convex.

Repeated midpoints give $\theta\mathbf a+(1-\theta)\mathbf b\in C$ for every dyadic rational $\theta=m/2^k\in[0,1]$ (induction on $k$: a point with denominator $2^{k+1}$ is the midpoint of two points with denominator $2^k$). Any $\theta\in[0,1]$ is a limit of dyadic rationals $\theta_j$, and $\theta_j\mathbf a+(1-\theta_j)\mathbf b\to\theta\mathbf a+(1-\theta)\mathbf b$, which lies in $C$ because $C$ is closed.

Show $\conv S$ is the intersection of all convex sets containing $S$.

Let $D$ be that intersection. $\conv S$ is convex and contains $S$, so $D\subseteq\conv S$. Conversely any convex $B\supseteq S$ contains every convex combination of points of $S$ (exercise 2.1), so $\conv S\subseteq B$; hence $\conv S\subseteq D$.

Find the distance between $\{\mathbf a^T\mathbf x=b_1\}$ and $\{\mathbf a^T\mathbf x=b_2\}$.

The points $\mathbf x_i=(b_i/\|\mathbf a\|_2^2)\mathbf a$ lie on the hyperplanes and the segment joining them is along the common normal. Distance $\|\mathbf x_1-\mathbf x_2\|_2=\dfrac{|b_1-b_2|}{\|\mathbf a\|_2}$.

When is $\{\mathbf a^T\mathbf x\le b\}\subseteq\{\tilde{\mathbf a}^T\mathbf x\le\tilde b\}$? When are they equal?

Containment iff $\tilde{\mathbf a}=\lambda\mathbf a$ for some $\lambda\gt0$ and $\tilde b\ge\lambda b$. (If $\tilde{\mathbf a}$ is not a positive multiple of $\mathbf a$, there is a direction $\mathbf v$ with $\mathbf a^T\mathbf v\le0$, $\tilde{\mathbf a}^T\mathbf v\gt0$, and moving far along $\mathbf v$ from a point of the first halfspace leaves the second.) Equal iff $\tilde{\mathbf a}=\lambda\mathbf a$, $\tilde b=\lambda b$, $\lambda\gt0$.

Show $\{\mathbf x\mid\|\mathbf x-\mathbf a\|_2\le\|\mathbf x-\mathbf b\|_2\}$ ($\mathbf a\ne\mathbf b$) is a halfspace $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x\le d$.

Square both sides and cancel $\mathbf x^T\mathbf x$: $-2\mathbf a^T\mathbf x+\mathbf a^T\mathbf a\le-2\mathbf b^T\mathbf x+\mathbf b^T\mathbf b$, i.e. $2(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)^T\mathbf x\le\mathbf b^T\mathbf b-\mathbf a^T\mathbf a$. $\mathbf c=2(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)$, $d=\|\mathbf b\|^2-\|\mathbf a\|^2$; the boundary is the perpendicular bisector of $\mathbf a\mathbf b$. (Exercise 2.9: a Voronoi region is an intersection of $K$ such halfspaces, a polyhedron.)

$C=\{\mathbf x\mid\mathbf x^TA\mathbf x+\mathbf b^T\mathbf x+c\le0\}$, $A\in\mathbf S^n$. (a) Show $C$ is convex if $A\succeq0$. (b) Show $C\cap\{\mathbf g^T\mathbf x+h=0\}$ is convex if $A+\lambda\mathbf g\mathbf g^T\succeq0$ for some $\lambda$. Are the converses true?

(a) $f(\mathbf x)=\mathbf x^TA\mathbf x+\mathbf b^T\mathbf x+c$ has $\nabla^2f=2A\succeq0$, so it is convex and $C$ is a sublevel set. (b) On the hyperplane, $f(\mathbf x)=f(\mathbf x)+\lambda(\mathbf g^T\mathbf x+h)^2$, a quadratic with Hessian $2(A+\lambda\mathbf g\mathbf g^T)\succeq0$; so the intersection is (sublevel set of a convex function) ∩ (hyperplane), convex. Converses are false: $n=1$, $A=-1$, $b=0$, $c=-1$: $C=\{-x^2-1\le0\}=\mathbf R$ is convex but $A\not\succeq0$.

Show $\{\mathbf x\in\mathbf R^2_+\mid x_1x_2\ge1\}$ is convex; more generally $\{\mathbf x\in\mathbf R^n_+\mid\prod x_i\ge1\}$.

$a^\theta b^{1-\theta}\le\theta a+(1-\theta)b$ for $a,b\ge0$ (weighted AM–GM, §3.1.9).

Let $\prod x_i\ge1$, $\prod y_i\ge1$, $0\le\theta\le1$. Then $$\prod_i(\theta x_i+(1-\theta)y_i)\ge\prod_ix_i^\theta y_i^{1-\theta}=\Big(\prod x_i\Big)^\theta\Big(\prod y_i\Big)^{1-\theta}\ge1.$$

(a) slab $\alpha\le\mathbf a^T\mathbf x\le\beta$; (b) rectangle $\alpha_i\le x_i\le\beta_i$; (c) wedge $\mathbf a_1^T\mathbf x\le b_1$, $\mathbf a_2^T\mathbf x\le b_2$; (d) $\{\mathbf x\mid\|\mathbf x-\mathbf x_0\|_2\le\|\mathbf x-\mathbf y\|_2\ \forall\mathbf y\in S\}$; (e) $\{\mathbf x\mid\mathbf{dist}(\mathbf x,S)\le\mathbf{dist}(\mathbf x,T)\}$; (f) $\{\mathbf x\mid\mathbf x+S_2\subseteq S_1\}$, $S_1$ convex; (g) $\{\mathbf x\mid\|\mathbf x-\mathbf a\|_2\le\theta\|\mathbf x-\mathbf b\|_2\}$, $0\le\theta\le1$.

(a)–(c) Convex: intersections of halfspaces (polyhedra). (d) Convex: for each $\mathbf y$ the condition is a halfspace (exercise 2.7); intersect over $\mathbf y\in S$. (e) Not convex in general: $S=\{-1,1\}$, $T=\{0\}$ in $\mathbf R$ gives $\{|x|\ge\tfrac12\}$. (f) Convex: it is $\bigcap_{\mathbf y\in S_2}(S_1-\mathbf y)$, an intersection of translates of a convex set. (g) Convex: for $\theta=1$ a halfspace; for $\theta\lt1$, squaring gives $(1-\theta^2)\mathbf x^T\mathbf x-2(\mathbf a-\theta^2\mathbf b)^T\mathbf x+\mathbf a^T\mathbf a-\theta^2\mathbf b^T\mathbf b\le0$, a ball (convex quadratic sublevel set).

$S$ convex. (a) $S_a=\{\mathbf x\mid\mathbf{dist}(\mathbf x,S)\le a\}$ is convex. (b) $S_{-a}=\{\mathbf x\mid B(\mathbf x,a)\subseteq S\}$ is convex.

(a) $\mathbf{dist}(\cdot,S)$ is a convex function when $S$ is convex (example 3.16), and $S_a$ is its sublevel set. Directly: if $\|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf y_i\|\le a+\epsilon$ with $\mathbf y_i\in S$, then $\theta\mathbf y_1+(1-\theta)\mathbf y_2\in S$ is within $a+\epsilon$ of $\theta\mathbf x_1+(1-\theta)\mathbf x_2$. (b) $S_{-a}=\bigcap_{\|\mathbf u\|\le a}(S-\mathbf u)$: $\mathbf x\in S_{-a}$ iff $\mathbf x+\mathbf u\in S$ for all $\|\mathbf u\|\le a$. An intersection of translates of $S$.

$\mathbf{prob}(x=a_i)=p_i$, $a_1\lt\dots\lt a_n$, $\mathbf p$ in the probability simplex. Which conditions are convex in $\mathbf p$: (a) $\alpha\le\mathbf Ef(x)\le\beta$; (b) $\mathbf{prob}(x\gt\alpha)\le\beta$; (c) $\mathbf E|x^3|\le\alpha\mathbf E|x|$; (d) $\mathbf Ex^2\le\alpha$; (e) $\mathbf Ex^2\ge\alpha$; (f) $\mathbf{var}(x)\le\alpha$; (g) $\mathbf{var}(x)\ge\alpha$; (h) $\mathbf{quartile}(x)\ge\alpha$; (i) $\mathbf{quartile}(x)\le\alpha$?

(a)–(e): each is one or two linear inequalities in $\mathbf p$ ($\mathbf Ef(x)=\sum p_if(a_i)$, $\mathbf{prob}(x\gt\alpha)=\sum_{a_i\gt\alpha}p_i$, $\mathbf E|x^3|-\alpha\mathbf E|x|=\sum p_i(|a_i|^3-\alpha|a_i|)$): convex. (f) $\mathbf{var}(x)=\sum p_ia_i^2-(\sum p_ia_i)^2$ is a concave function of $\mathbf p$, so $\mathbf{var}\le\alpha$ is not convex in general. (g) A superlevel set of a concave function: convex. (h) $\mathbf{quartile}\ge\alpha$ iff $\sum_{a_i\lt\alpha}p_i\lt0.25$: a halfspace, convex. (i) $\mathbf{quartile}\le\alpha$ iff $\sum_{a_i\le\alpha}p_i\ge0.25$: convex.

$C$, $D$ disjoint. Show $\{(\mathbf a,b)\mid\mathbf a^T\mathbf x\le b\ \forall\mathbf x\in C,\ \mathbf a^T\mathbf x\ge b\ \forall\mathbf x\in D\}$ is a convex cone.

For each fixed $\mathbf x$ the conditions $\mathbf a^T\mathbf x-b\le0$ (or $\ge0$) are homogeneous linear inequalities in $(\mathbf a,b)$: halfspaces through the origin of $\mathbf R^{n+1}$. An intersection of such halfspaces is a convex cone (it contains $\mathbf 0$ always; it is $\{\mathbf 0\}$ if no separating hyperplane exists).

Describe $\mathbf S^n_+$ explicitly for $n=1,2$.

$n=1$: $\{x\mid x\ge0\}$. $n=2$: $\begin{pmatrix}x_1&x_2\\x_2&x_3\end{pmatrix}\succeq0$ iff $x_1\ge0$, $x_3\ge0$, $x_1x_3\ge x_2^2$ (all principal minors, not only leading ones, must be $\ge0$ for semidefiniteness). For $n=3$ the conditions are $x_1,x_4,x_6\ge0$, the three $2\times2$ principal minors $\ge0$ and $\det\ge0$.

Show: (a) $K^*$ is a convex cone; (b) $K_1\subseteq K_2$ ⇒ $K_2^*\subseteq K_1^*$; (c) $K^*$ is closed.

(a) $K^*=\bigcap_{\mathbf x\in K}\{\mathbf y\mid\mathbf x^T\mathbf y\ge0\}$, an intersection of halfspaces through $\mathbf 0$. (b) A $\mathbf y$ non-negative on all of $K_2$ is non-negative on $K_1$. (c) Each halfspace $\{\mathbf x^T\mathbf y\ge0\}$ is closed and an intersection of closed sets is closed.

Find the dual cone of $K=\{A\mathbf x\mid\mathbf x\succeq0\}$, $A\in\mathbf R^{m\times n}$.

$\mathbf y\in K^*$ iff $\mathbf y^TA\mathbf x\ge0$ for all $\mathbf x\succeq0$ iff $A^T\mathbf y\succeq0$ (take $\mathbf x=\mathbf e_i$; conversely sums of nonnegative terms). $K^*=\{\mathbf y\mid A^T\mathbf y\succeq0\}$.

 Convex functions (Boyd ch. 3 exercises)

$f$ convex on $\mathbf R$, $a\lt b$ in $\dom f$. (a) $f(x)\le\frac{b-x}{b-a}f(a)+\frac{x-a}{b-a}f(b)$ on $[a,b]$. (b) $\frac{f(x)-f(a)}{x-a}\le\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\le\frac{f(b)-f(x)}{b-x}$. (c) If differentiable, $f'(a)\le\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\le f'(b)$. (d) If twice differentiable, $f''(a),f''(b)\ge0$.

(a) $x=\theta a+(1-\theta)b$ with $\theta=\frac{b-x}{b-a}$: the definition. (b) Subtract $f(a)$ from (a): $f(x)-f(a)\le\frac{x-a}{b-a}(f(b)-f(a))$; divide by $x-a\gt0$. Similarly subtract (a) from $f(b)$: $f(b)-f(x)\ge\frac{b-x}{b-a}(f(b)-f(a))$; divide by $b-x$. (c) Let $x\to a$ in the left inequality and $x\to b$ in the right. (d) By (c), $f'(b)-f'(a)\ge0$ for all $a\lt b$; so $f'$ is nondecreasing and $f''\ge0$: $f''(a)=\lim_{b\to a}\frac{f'(b)-f'(a)}{b-a}\ge0$.

$f$ convex, $\dom f=\mathbf R^n$, bounded above. Show $f$ is constant.

Suppose $f(\mathbf x)\lt f(\mathbf y)$. Let $g(t)=f(\mathbf x+t(\mathbf y-\mathbf x))$, convex on $\mathbf R$ with $g(0)\lt g(1)$. For $t\gt1$, $1$ is a convex combination of $0$ and $t$: $g(1)\le(1-\tfrac1t)g(0)+\tfrac1tg(t)$, so $g(t)\ge g(0)+t(g(1)-g(0))\to\infty$. This contradicts boundedness.

(a) $e^x-1$; (b) $x_1x_2$ on $\mathbf R^2_{++}$; (c) $1/(x_1x_2)$ on $\mathbf R^2_{++}$; (d) $x_1/x_2$ on $\mathbf R^2_{++}$; (e) $x_1^2/x_2$ on $\mathbf R\times\mathbf R_{++}$; (f) $x_1^\alpha x_2^{1-\alpha}$, $0\le\alpha\le1$, on $\mathbf R^2_{++}$.

(a) Convex ($f''=e^x\gt0$); monotone, so quasiconvex and quasiconcave; not concave.
(b) Hessian $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ indefinite: neither convex nor concave. Superlevel sets $x_1x_2\ge\alpha$ are convex (2.11): quasiconcave; sublevel sets are not convex: not quasiconvex.
(c) $\nabla^2f=\frac1{x_1x_2}\begin{pmatrix}2/x_1^2&1/(x_1x_2)\\1/(x_1x_2)&2/x_2^2\end{pmatrix}$, leading minors $\gt0$: convex, hence quasiconvex. Not concave or quasiconcave.
(d) Linear-fractional: quasilinear. Hessian $\begin{pmatrix}0&-1/x_2^2\\-1/x_2^2&2x_1/x_2^3\end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $-1/x_2^4\lt0$: neither convex nor concave.
(e) Quadratic-over-linear: convex, quasiconvex; not concave or quasiconcave.
(f) Concave (Hessian $\alpha(\alpha-1)x_1^\alpha x_2^{1-\alpha}\begin{pmatrix}1/x_1^2&-1/(x_1x_2)\\-1/(x_1x_2)&1/x_2^2\end{pmatrix}\preceq0$), hence quasiconcave; not convex for $0\lt\alpha\lt1$.

Show convex: (a) $\max_i\|A^{(i)}\mathbf x-\mathbf b^{(i)}\|$; (b) $\sum_{i=1}^r|x|_{[i]}$, the sum of the $r$ largest absolute values.

(a) Each $\|A^{(i)}\mathbf x-\mathbf b^{(i)}\|$ is a norm of an affine function, convex; the maximum of convex functions is convex. (b) $\sum_{i=1}^r|x|_{[i]}=\max\{\sum_{i\in I}s_ix_i\mid|I|=r,\ s_i=\pm1\}$, a maximum of linear functions.

Show $f(\mathbf x)=-\log\big(-\log\sum_ie^{\mathbf a_i^T\mathbf x+b_i}\big)$ is convex on $\{\sum e^{\mathbf a_i^T\mathbf x+b_i}\lt1\}$.

$g(\mathbf x)=\log\sum e^{\mathbf a_i^T\mathbf x+b_i}$ is convex (log-sum-exp of an affine map) and negative on the domain. $h(u)=-\log(-u)$ on $u\lt0$ is convex ($h''=1/u^2$) and increasing ($h'=-1/u\gt0$). Convex nondecreasing ∘ convex is convex.

$f,g$ convex, both nondecreasing (or both nonincreasing), positive on an interval. Show $fg$ is convex.

For twice differentiable $f,g$: $(fg)''=f''g+2f'g'+fg''$. Here $f'',g''\ge0$, $f,g\gt0$, and $f'g'\ge0$ (same sign). So $(fg)''\ge0$. (Without differentiability: for $x\lt y$ and $z=\theta x+(1-\theta)y$, $f(z)g(z)\le(\theta f(x)+(1-\theta)f(y))(\theta g(x)+(1-\theta)g(y))$ and this is $\le\theta f(x)g(x)+(1-\theta)f(y)g(y)$ because the difference is $-\theta(1-\theta)(f(y)-f(x))(g(y)-g(x))\le0$.)

Find the conjugates of (a) $\max_ix_i$; (c) $\max_i(a_ix+b_i)$ on $\mathbf R$ ($a_1\lt\dots\lt a_m$, none redundant); (d) $x^p$ on $\mathbf R_{++}$ for $p\gt1$ and for $p\lt0$.

(a) If some $y_k\lt0$, take $\mathbf x=-t\mathbf e_k$: $\mathbf y^T\mathbf x-\max x_i=-ty_k\to\infty$. If $\mathbf y\succeq0$ and $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf y\ne1$, take $\mathbf x=t\mathbf 1$: $t(\mathbf 1^T\mathbf y-1)$ unbounded. If $\mathbf y\succeq0$, $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf y=1$: $\mathbf y^T\mathbf x\le\max x_i$, value $0$ at $\mathbf x=0$. So $f^*=0$ on the probability simplex, $\infty$ elsewhere.
(c) $\dom f^*=[a_1,a_m]$. For $y\in[a_i,a_{i+1}]$ the supremum of $yx-f(x)$ is at the kink $x_i=-(b_{i+1}-b_i)/(a_{i+1}-a_i)$, giving $f^*(y)=-b_i-(b_{i+1}-b_i)\dfrac{y-a_i}{a_{i+1}-a_i}$: piecewise linear, interpolating $f^*(a_i)=-b_i$.
(d) $p\gt1$: for $y\le0$ the supremum is $0$ (as $x\to0$); for $y\gt0$ it is at $x=(y/p)^{1/(p-1)}$: $f^*(y)=(p-1)(y/p)^{p/(p-1)}$. $p\lt0$: for $y\gt0$ unbounded; for $y\le0$ the maximiser is $x=(y/p)^{1/(p-1)}$ and $f^*(y)=(p-1)(y/p)^{p/(p-1)}$ ($=0$ at $y=0$).

Show log-concave: (a) $e^x/(1+e^x)$; (b) $1/(1/x_1+\dots+1/x_n)$ on $\mathbf R^n_{++}$.

(a) $\log f=x-\log(1+e^x)$, $(\log f)''=-\dfrac{e^x}{(1+e^x)^2}\lt0$: concave. (b) $f=(\sum_ix_i^{p})^{1/p}$ with $p=-1$, which is concave on $\mathbf R^n_{++}$ by exercise 3.17. It is positive, and a positive concave function is log-concave ($\log$ is concave and increasing, so $\log\circ f$ is concave).

Show $f(X)=X^{-1}$ is matrix convex on $\mathbf S^n_{++}$.

By example 3.48 it suffices that $\mathbf z^TX^{-1}\mathbf z$ is convex in $X$ for each fixed $\mathbf z$. The matrix fractional function $(\mathbf x,X)\mapsto\mathbf x^TX^{-1}\mathbf x$ is jointly convex (example 3.4: its epigraph is an LMI); fixing $\mathbf x=\mathbf z$ restricts it to an affine set, which keeps convexity.
