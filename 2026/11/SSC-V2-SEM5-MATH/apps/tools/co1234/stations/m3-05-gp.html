Monomials and posynomials are not convex, so a GP is not a convex problem as written. Substitute $x_i=e^{y_i}$ and take logarithms: monomials become affine, posynomials become log-sum-exp, and the GP becomes convex. Monomials and posynomials (§4.5.1)

Monomial: $f(\mathbf x)=cx_1^{a_1}x_2^{a_2}\cdots x_n^{a_n}$ with $c\gt0$, $a_i\in\mathbf R$, on $\mathbf R^n_{++}$. Posynomial: a sum of monomials $\sum_{k=1}^Kc_kx_1^{a_{1k}}\cdots x_n^{a_{nk}}$. Monomials are closed under products, quotients and powers; posynomials under sums, products and positive scaling; a posynomial divided by a monomial is a posynomial.

 Geometric programs and their convex form (§4.5.2–4.5.3)

GP: minimise $f_0(\mathbf x)$ s.t. $f_i(\mathbf x)\le1$, $h_i(\mathbf x)=1$, with $f_0,\dots,f_m$ posynomials and $h_i$ monomials; implicitly $\mathbf x\succ0$.

Other forms reduce to this: $f\le h$ with $h$ a monomial ⇔ $f/h\le1$; $h_1=h_2$ (monomials) ⇔ $h_1/h_2=1$; maximise a nonzero monomial ⇔ minimise its inverse.

Examples (§4.5.4)

Frobenius norm diagonal scaling. Minimise $\|DMD^{-1}\|_F^2=\sum_{i,j}M_{ij}^2d_i^2/d_j^2$ over $D=\diag(\mathbf d)\succ0$: an unconstrained GP in $\mathbf d$.

Design of a cantilever beam. $N$ segments with widths $w_i$ and heights $h_i$: minimise total volume $\sum w_ih_il_i$ subject to bounds on widths, heights, aspect ratios $h_i/w_i$, stress $6iF/(w_ih_i^2)\le\sigma_{\max}$ and vertical deflection $y_1\le y_{\max}$, where the deflection and slope are given by recursions that keep everything posynomial. A GP.

Minimising the spectral radius via Perron–Frobenius. For an elementwise positive $A(\mathbf x)$ with posynomial entries, $\lambda_{\mathrm{pf}}(A)=\inf\{\lambda\mid A\mathbf v\preceq\lambda\mathbf v\text{ for some }\mathbf v\succ0\}$; minimising it is the GP: minimise $\lambda$ s.t. $\sum_jA(\mathbf x)_{ij}v_j/(\lambda v_i)\le1$.

A posynomial needs positive coefficients $c_k\gt0$; $x_1-x_2$ is not a posynomial, and an equality constraint must be a monomial, not a posynomial.

 Convert to convex form: minimise $x/y$ subject to $2\le x\le3$, $x^2+3y/z\le\sqrt y$, $x/y=z^2$.

GP form: minimise $xy^{-1}$ s.t. $2x^{-1}\le1$, $x/3\le1$, $x^2y^{-1/2}+3y^{1/2}z^{-1}\le1$, $xy^{-1}z^{-2}=1$. With $u=\log x$, $v=\log y$, $w=\log z$: minimise $u-v$ s.t. $\log2-u\le0$, $u-\log3\le0$, $\log(e^{2u-v/2}+e^{\log3+v/2-w})\le0$, $u-v-2w=0$.

 Is a posynomial a convex function of $\mathbf x$?

Not in general: $\sqrt x$ is a monomial and concave. After $x=e^y$, $\log f$ is convex in $\mathbf y$.
