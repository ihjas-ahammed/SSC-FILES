The dual cone $K^*$ collects every $\mathbf y$ that makes a non-obtuse angle with all of $K$. It turns generalized inequalities into families of ordinary ones: $\mathbf x\preceq_K\mathbf y$ iff $\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf x\le\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf y$ for every $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq_{K^*}0$. Minimal elements are what you get by minimising positive weighted sums. Dual cones (§2.6.1)

$K^*=\{\mathbf y\mid\mathbf x^T\mathbf y\ge0\ \text{for all}\ \mathbf x\in K\}$. It is always a convex cone (an intersection of halfspaces through $\mathbf 0$), even if $K$ is not.

 A wedge $K$ of half-angle $\alpha$ in $\mathbf R^2$ and its dual, of half-angle $90^\circ-\alpha$. At $45^\circ$ the cone is self-dual (like $\mathbf R^2_+$).

Example 2.22. Subspace $V$: $V^*=V^\perp$. Example 2.23. $(\mathbf R^n_+)^*=\mathbf R^n_+$: if $\mathbf y\succeq0$ then $\mathbf x^T\mathbf y\ge0$ for all $\mathbf x\succeq0$; if $y_i\lt0$ then $\mathbf x=\mathbf e_i$ gives $\mathbf x^T\mathbf y\lt0$. Self-dual.

Example 2.24. With the inner product $\tr(XY)$ on $\mathbf S^n$, $(\mathbf S^n_+)^*=\mathbf S^n_+$. Proof: if $Y\notin\mathbf S^n_+$ there is $\mathbf q$ with $\mathbf q^TY\mathbf q=\tr(\mathbf q\mathbf q^TY)\lt0$, and $X=\mathbf q\mathbf q^T\succeq0$. If $X,Y\succeq0$, write $X=\sum\lambda_i\mathbf q_i\mathbf q_i^T$ ($\lambda_i\ge0$): $\tr(YX)=\sum\lambda_i\mathbf q_i^TY\mathbf q_i\ge0$.

Properties. $K^*$ is closed and convex. $K_1\subseteq K_2$ ⇒ $K_2^*\subseteq K_1^*$. If $K$ has nonempty interior, $K^*$ is pointed. If $\cl K$ is pointed, $K^*$ has nonempty interior. $K^{**}=\cl(\conv K)$, so $K^{**}=K$ for a closed convex cone. Hence: $K$ proper ⇒ $K^*$ proper and $K^{**}=K$.

 Dual generalized inequalities (§2.6.2)

For proper $K$, $K^*$ is proper and defines $\preceq_{K^*}$, the dual inequality. Key facts:

$\mathbf x\preceq_K\mathbf y\iff\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf x\le\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf y$ for all $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq_{K^*}0$. $\quad\mathbf x\prec_K\mathbf y\iff\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf x\lt\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf y$ for all $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq_{K^*}0$, $\boldsymbol\lambda\ne\mathbf 0$.

Example 2.26 (alternatives for strict generalized inequalities). $A\mathbf x\prec_K\mathbf b$ is infeasible iff there is $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq_{K^*}0$, $\boldsymbol\lambda\ne\mathbf 0$, $A^T\boldsymbol\lambda=\mathbf 0$, $\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf b\le0$ (same separation argument as example 2.21 with $\mathbf R^m_{++}$ replaced by $\intr K$).

Minimum and minimal elements via dual inequalities (§2.6.3)

Minimum. $\mathbf x$ is the minimum of $S$ iff for every $\boldsymbol\lambda\succ_{K^*}0$, $\mathbf x$ is the unique minimiser of $\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf z$ over $\mathbf z\in S$.
Minimal (sufficient). If $\boldsymbol\lambda\succ_{K^*}0$ and $\mathbf x$ minimises $\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf z$ over $S$, then $\mathbf x$ is minimal.
Minimal (partial converse). If $S$ is convex and $\mathbf x$ is minimal, there is a nonzero $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq_{K^*}0$ such that $\mathbf x$ minimises $\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf z$ over $S$.

Proof of the sufficient condition. Suppose $\boldsymbol\lambda\succ_{K^*}0$ and $\mathbf x$ minimises $\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf z$ on $S$, but $\mathbf x$ is not minimal: some $\mathbf y\in S$, $\mathbf y\ne\mathbf x$, has $\mathbf y\preceq_K\mathbf x$. Then $\mathbf x-\mathbf y\in K\setminus\{\mathbf 0\}$ and $\boldsymbol\lambda^T(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)\gt0$, so $\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf y\lt\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf x$: contradiction.

The converse needs convexity and only gives $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq_{K^*}0$ (maybe on the boundary of $K^*$); a minimiser for a boundary $\boldsymbol\lambda$ need not be minimal (fig. 2.24).
