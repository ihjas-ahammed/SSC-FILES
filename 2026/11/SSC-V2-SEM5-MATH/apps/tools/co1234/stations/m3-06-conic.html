Allow vector-valued constraints $f_i(\mathbf x)\preceq_{K_i}0$ with $K_i$-convex $f_i$. The simplest case, a linear objective with an affine conic constraint, is a conic program; with $K=\mathbf S^k_+$ it is a semidefinite program (SDP), which contains LP, QP, QCQP and SOCP. Convex problems with generalized inequality constraints (§4.6)

Minimise $f_0(\mathbf x)$ s.t. $f_i(\mathbf x)\preceq_{K_i}0$, $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$, with $f_0$ convex, $K_i$ proper cones, $f_i$ $K_i$-convex. The feasible set is convex, local optima are global, and the optimality criterion of §4.2.3 holds unchanged.

 Conic form problems (§4.6.1)

Conic form: minimise $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $F\mathbf x+\mathbf g\preceq_K0$, $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$. Standard form: minimise $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$, $\mathbf x\succeq_K0$. Inequality form: minimise $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $F\mathbf x+\mathbf g\preceq_K0$. With $K=\mathbf R^m_+$ these are LPs.

 Semidefinite programming (§4.6.2)

SDP (inequality form): minimise $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $x_1F_1+\dots+x_nF_n+G\preceq0$ (an LMI, $F_i,G\in\mathbf S^k$), $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$. Standard form: minimise $\tr(CX)$ s.t. $\tr(A_iX)=b_i$, $i=1,\dots,p$, $X\succeq0$. Several LMIs combine into one block-diagonal LMI.

Embedding the others. LP: $A\mathbf x\preceq\mathbf b$ ⇔ $\diag(A\mathbf x-\mathbf b)\preceq0$. SOCP: $\|A\mathbf x+\mathbf b\|_2\le\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+d$ ⇔ $\begin{pmatrix}(\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+d)I&A\mathbf x+\mathbf b\\(A\mathbf x+\mathbf b)^T&\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+d\end{pmatrix}\succeq0$ (Schur complement).

Examples (§4.6.3)

Second-order cone programming as a conic program over a product of second-order cones $K=K_1\times\dots\times K_m$.

Matrix norm minimisation. Minimise $\|A(\mathbf x)\|_2$ (largest singular value) with $A(\mathbf x)=A_0+x_1A_1+\dots+x_nA_n$: $\|A\|_2\le s\iff A^TA\preceq s^2I\iff\begin{pmatrix}sI&A\\A^T&sI\end{pmatrix}\succeq0$. So: minimise $s$ subject to that LMI — an SDP.

Moment problems. $(\mathbf E t^k)_{k\le2n}$ of a random variable on $\mathbf R$ are exactly the vectors whose Hankel matrix $H(x_0,\dots,x_{2n})\succeq0$; bounds on $\mathbf E p(t)$ given moment bounds are SDPs.

Bounding portfolio risk with incomplete covariance information: maximise $\mathbf x^T\Sigma\mathbf x$ over $\Sigma\succeq0$ with known entry bounds — an SDP in $\Sigma$.

Fastest mixing Markov chain on a graph: minimise the second largest eigenvalue modulus of a symmetric stochastic matrix with the graph's sparsity, an SDP.

Write minimise $\lambda_{\max}(A(\mathbf x))$, $A(\mathbf x)=A_0+\sum x_iA_i$, as an SDP.

Minimise $t$ s.t. $A(\mathbf x)\preceq tI$. (Exercise 4.43.)

 Why is an LP a special case of an SDP?

A diagonal matrix is PSD iff its diagonal is nonnegative, so $A\mathbf x\preceq\mathbf b$ is the LMI $\diag(\mathbf b-A\mathbf x)\succeq0$.
