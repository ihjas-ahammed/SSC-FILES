Everything carries over when constraints are $f_i(\mathbf x)\preceq_{K_i}0$: the multiplier becomes a vector $\boldsymbol\lambda_i$ in the dual cone $K_i^*$, the product $\lambda_if_i$ becomes the inner product $\boldsymbol\lambda_i^Tf_i$. Then SDP and cone-program duals follow in a few lines. The Lagrange dual (§5.9.1)

Problem: minimise $f_0(\mathbf x)$ s.t. $f_i(\mathbf x)\preceq_{K_i}0$ ($f_i:\mathbf R^n\to\mathbf R^{k_i}$), $h_i(\mathbf x)=0$. Lagrangian $L=f_0+\sum_i\boldsymbol\lambda_i^Tf_i(\mathbf x)+\sum\nu_ih_i(\mathbf x)$, $g=\inf_{\mathbf x}L$. Lower bound: if $\boldsymbol\lambda_i\succeq_{K_i^*}0$ then $g\le p^\star$, because $\boldsymbol\lambda_i^Tf_i(\tilde{\mathbf x})\le0$ whenever $f_i(\tilde{\mathbf x})\preceq_{K_i}0$ (definition of the dual cone). Dual problem: maximise $g$ s.t. $\boldsymbol\lambda_i\succeq_{K_i^*}0$. Weak duality always; strong duality for convex problems ($f_i$ $K_i$-convex) under Slater: some $\mathbf x$ with $f_i(\mathbf x)\prec_{K_i}0$, $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$.

Example 5.12 (cone program in standard form). Minimise $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$, $\mathbf x\succeq_K0$. $L=\mathbf c^T\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf x+\boldsymbol\nu^T(A\mathbf x-\mathbf b)$, so $g=-\mathbf b^T\boldsymbol\nu$ if $A^T\boldsymbol\nu-\boldsymbol\lambda+\mathbf c=0$. Dual: maximise $-\mathbf b^T\boldsymbol\nu$ s.t. $A^T\boldsymbol\nu+\mathbf c\succeq_{K^*}0$ — a cone program in inequality form, exactly as for LP.

Optimality conditions (§5.9.2)

Complementary slackness: $\boldsymbol\lambda_i^{\star T}f_i(\mathbf x^\star)=0$. (Weaker than the scalar case: both $\boldsymbol\lambda_i^\star\ne0$ and $f_i(\mathbf x^\star)\ne0$ are possible. What remains: $\boldsymbol\lambda_i^\star\succ_{K_i^*}0$ ⇒ $f_i(\mathbf x^\star)=0$ and $f_i(\mathbf x^\star)\prec_{K_i}0$ ⇒ $\boldsymbol\lambda_i^\star=0$.)
KKT: $f_i(\mathbf x^\star)\preceq_{K_i}0$, $h_i(\mathbf x^\star)=0$, $\boldsymbol\lambda_i^\star\succeq_{K_i^*}0$, $\boldsymbol\lambda_i^{\star T}f_i(\mathbf x^\star)=0$, $\nabla f_0+\sum Df_i(\mathbf x^\star)^T\boldsymbol\lambda_i^\star+\sum\nu_i^\star\nabla h_i=0$. Necessary with zero gap, sufficient for convex problems.

For an SDP, complementary slackness $\tr(F(\mathbf x^\star)Z^\star)=0$ with $F(\mathbf x^\star)\preceq0$, $Z^\star\succeq0$ implies $F(\mathbf x^\star)Z^\star=0$: the ranges are orthogonal, so $\rank F(\mathbf x^\star)+\rank Z^\star\le k$.

Perturbation and alternatives (§5.9.3–5.9.4)

Example 5.13. For the inequality form SDP, $Z^\star$ gives the sensitivity of $p^\star$ to perturbations $F(\mathbf x)\preceq U$: $\nabla p^\star(0)=Z^\star$ (§5.6, not examined; quoted for completeness).

Alternatives with generalized inequalities. Strict: (I) $f_i(\mathbf x)\prec_{K_i}0$, $h_i(\mathbf x)=0$ and (II) $\boldsymbol\lambda_i\succeq_{K_i^*}0$, not all zero, $g(\boldsymbol\lambda,\boldsymbol\nu)\ge0$ are weak alternatives, and strong alternatives for convex problems (with the mild condition). Example 5.14 (LMI feasibility). $F(\mathbf x)=x_1F_1+\dots+x_nF_n+G\prec0$ is infeasible iff there is $Z\succeq0$, $Z\ne0$, with $\tr(GZ)\ge0$ and $\tr(F_iZ)=0$, $i=1,\dots,n$. (For the nonstrict LMI $F(\mathbf x)\preceq0$ the alternatives are only weak in general.)

Maximise $-\mathbf b^T\boldsymbol\nu$ s.t. $A^T\boldsymbol\nu+\mathbf c\succeq_{K^*}0$ (example 5.12).

 Why must the multiplier for $f_i(\mathbf x)\preceq_K0$ lie in $K^*$?

So that $\boldsymbol\lambda^Tf_i(\mathbf x)\le0$ for every feasible $\mathbf x$: for $\mathbf u=-f_i(\mathbf x)\in K$ we need $\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf u\ge0$, which is the definition of $\boldsymbol\lambda\in K^*$. This keeps $g\le p^\star$.
