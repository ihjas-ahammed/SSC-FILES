The best lower bound is found by maximising $g$ over $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0$: the dual problem, always convex. Its optimal value $d^\star$ never exceeds $p^\star$ (weak duality). For convex problems with a strictly feasible point (Slater), $d^\star=p^\star$ (strong duality). The dual problem (§5.2, 5.2.1)

$$\text{maximize}\ g(\boldsymbol\lambda,\boldsymbol\nu)\quad\text{subject to}\ \boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0.$$ A convex optimization problem (concave objective, convex constraint) whether or not the primal is convex. Optimal $(\boldsymbol\lambda^\star,\boldsymbol\nu^\star)$ are the optimal Lagrange multipliers; $d^\star$ is the dual optimal value. Implicit constraints ($g\gt-\infty$) are usually made explicit, as in the LP dual.

 Weak and strong duality (§5.2.2–5.2.3)

Weak duality: $d^\star\le p^\star$, always (from $g\le p^\star$). $p^\star-d^\star\ge0$ is the duality gap. If $p^\star=-\infty$ then $d^\star=-\infty$ (dual infeasible); if $d^\star=\infty$ then the primal is infeasible.
Strong duality: $d^\star=p^\star$. Not true in general.
Slater's condition: if the problem is convex ($f_0,\dots,f_m$ convex, $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$) and there is $\mathbf x\in\relint\mathcal D$ with $f_i(\mathbf x)\lt0$ for all $i$ and $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ (strictly feasible), then strong duality holds and, if $d^\star\gt-\infty$, the dual optimum is attained. Affine inequalities need only hold non-strictly (refined Slater).

 Examples (§5.2.4)

Least-squares solution of linear equations (min $\mathbf x^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$): dual maximise $-\tfrac14\boldsymbol\nu^TAA^T\boldsymbol\nu-\mathbf b^T\boldsymbol\nu$; Slater holds whenever the primal is feasible (only equality constraints), so $p^\star=d^\star$.

LP: strong duality holds if the primal is feasible (refined Slater); in fact it holds unless both primal and dual are infeasible.

QCQP with $P_0\succ0$, $P_i\succeq0$: $L=\tfrac12\mathbf x^TP(\boldsymbol\lambda)\mathbf x+\mathbf q(\boldsymbol\lambda)^T\mathbf x+r(\boldsymbol\lambda)$ with $P(\boldsymbol\lambda)=P_0+\sum\lambda_iP_i\succ0$, so $g(\boldsymbol\lambda)=-\tfrac12\mathbf q(\boldsymbol\lambda)^TP(\boldsymbol\lambda)^{-1}\mathbf q(\boldsymbol\lambda)+r(\boldsymbol\lambda)$. Slater: some $\mathbf x$ with all quadratic constraints strict.

Entropy maximisation: dual maximise $-\mathbf b^T\boldsymbol\lambda-\nu-e^{-\nu-1}\sum e^{-\mathbf a_i^T\boldsymbol\lambda}$ s.t. $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0$; maximising over $\nu$ analytically gives maximise $-\mathbf b^T\boldsymbol\lambda-\log\sum e^{-\mathbf a_i^T\boldsymbol\lambda}$, a GP in convex form.

Minimum volume covering ellipsoid: Slater always holds ($X=\epsilon I$ with $\epsilon$ small makes every $\mathbf a_i^TX\mathbf a_i\lt1$), so strong duality.

A non-convex problem with strong duality: minimise $\mathbf x^TA\mathbf x+2\mathbf b^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $\mathbf x^T\mathbf x\le1$ with $A$ indefinite (trust region). Dual: maximise $-\mathbf b^T(A+\lambda I)^\dagger\mathbf b-\lambda$ s.t. $A+\lambda I\succeq0$, $\mathbf b\in\mathcal R(A+\lambda I)$. Strong duality holds (a general fact for problems with one quadratic constraint).

Mixed strategies for matrix games (§5.2.5)

Player 1 picks $k$, player 2 picks $l$, player 1 pays $P_{kl}$. With mixed strategies $\mathbf u,\mathbf v$ (probability vectors) the expected payoff is $\mathbf u^TP\mathbf v$. If player 1 commits first, she solves minimise $\max_i(P^T\mathbf u)_i$ s.t. $\mathbf u\succeq0$, $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf u=1$ (an LP); if player 2 commits first, he solves maximise $\min_i(P\mathbf v)_i$ s.t. $\mathbf v\succeq0$, $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf v=1$. These two LPs are duals of each other, and LP strong duality gives the minimax theorem: $\min_{\mathbf u}\max_{\mathbf v}\mathbf u^TP\mathbf v=\max_{\mathbf v}\min_{\mathbf u}\mathbf u^TP\mathbf v$ — no advantage in knowing the opponent's mixed strategy.

Slater needs a convex problem. For non-convex problems a gap is normal (two-way partitioning). And "strictly feasible" means $f_i(\mathbf x)\lt0$ for every non-affine inequality — a boundary point does not count.

 State weak duality and prove it in two lines.

$d^\star\le p^\star$. For $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0$ and feasible $\tilde{\mathbf x}$: $g(\boldsymbol\lambda,\boldsymbol\nu)\le L(\tilde{\mathbf x},\boldsymbol\lambda,\boldsymbol\nu)\le f_0(\tilde{\mathbf x})$. Take sup over dual feasible pairs and inf over feasible $\tilde{\mathbf x}$.

 Does Slater's condition hold for minimise $x$ subject to $x^2\le0$? Is there a duality gap?

No strictly feasible point ($x^2\lt0$ is impossible), so Slater fails. $p^\star=0$. $g(\lambda)=\inf_x(x+\lambda x^2)=-1/(4\lambda)$ for $\lambda\gt0$, so $d^\star=\sup(-1/4\lambda)=0$: no gap, but the dual optimum is not attained.
