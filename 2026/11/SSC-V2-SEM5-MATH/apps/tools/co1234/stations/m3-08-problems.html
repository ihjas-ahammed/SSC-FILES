Chapter 4 exercises on the syllabus items: solving small problems by geometry or the optimality criterion, explicit LP and QCQP solutions, and reformulations as LP, SOCP, GP and SDP. Optimality and small problems

Feasible set: $2x_1+x_2\ge1$, $x_1+3x_2\ge1$, $x_1,x_2\ge0$. Give the optimal set and value for (a) $x_1+x_2$; (b) $-x_1-x_2$; (c) $x_1$; (d) $\max\{x_1,x_2\}$; (e) $x_1^2+9x_2^2$.

The feasible set is unbounded with vertices $(0,1)$, $(\tfrac25,\tfrac15)$ (where $2x_1+x_2=1$ meets $x_1+3x_2=1$) and $(1,0)$. Linear objectives: check vertices and recession directions.

(a) Values at the vertices: $1$, $\tfrac35$, $1$. Optimal set $\{(\tfrac25,\tfrac15)\}$, $p^\star=\tfrac35$.
(b) Unbounded below along $(t,t)$: $p^\star=-\infty$.
(c) $p^\star=0$, optimal set $\{(0,x_2)\mid x_2\ge1\}$.
(d) If $\max\{x_1,x_2\}\le t$ then $2x_1+x_2\le3t$, so $t\ge\tfrac13$; $(\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$ is feasible ($1\ge1$, $\tfrac43\ge1$). $p^\star=\tfrac13$, optimal set $\{(\tfrac13,\tfrac13)\}$.
(e) Try $x_1+3x_2=1$ active: minimise $x_1^2+9x_2^2$ on that line, $2x_1=\nu$, $18x_2=3\nu$ give $x_1=\tfrac12$, $x_2=\tfrac16$. Check $2x_1+x_2=\tfrac76\ge1$ ✓. Optimality criterion: $\nabla f_0=(1,3)$ is a positive multiple of the normal $(1,3)$ of the active constraint, so $\nabla f_0^T(\mathbf y-\mathbf x)=(y_1+3y_2)-1\ge0$ on the feasible set. $p^\star=\tfrac14+\tfrac14=\tfrac12$ at $(\tfrac12,\tfrac16)$.

Show $\mathbf x^\star=(1,\tfrac12,-1)$ is optimal for minimise $\tfrac12\mathbf x^TP\mathbf x+\mathbf q^T\mathbf x+r$ s.t. $-1\le x_i\le1$, $P=\begin{pmatrix}13&12&-2\\12&17&6\\-2&6&12\end{pmatrix}$, $\mathbf q=(-22,-14.5,13)$, $r=1$.

Convex ($P\succ0$: minors $13$, $77$, $100$). Criterion: $\nabla f_0(\mathbf x^\star)^T(\mathbf y-\mathbf x^\star)\ge0$ for every $\mathbf y$ in the box.

$P\mathbf x^\star=(13+6+2,\ 12+8.5-6,\ -2+3-12)=(21,14.5,-11)$, so $\nabla f_0(\mathbf x^\star)=P\mathbf x^\star+\mathbf q=(-1,0,2)$. Then $\nabla f_0^T(\mathbf y-\mathbf x^\star)=-(y_1-1)+2(y_3+1)$. Both terms are $\ge0$ on the box since $y_1\le1$ and $y_3\ge-1$. Hence $\mathbf x^\star$ is optimal. (Interpretation: $x_1$ is pushed up against $1$, $x_3$ down against $-1$, and $x_2$ is free with zero partial.)

Solve: (a) min $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$; (b) min $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $\mathbf a^T\mathbf x\le b$; (c) min $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $\mathbf l\preceq\mathbf x\preceq\mathbf u$; (d) min $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf x=1$, $\mathbf x\succeq0$ (and with $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf x\le1$).

(a) If infeasible, $p^\star=\infty$. If $\mathbf c=A^T\boldsymbol\lambda$, then $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x=\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf b$ on the whole feasible set: $p^\star=\boldsymbol\lambda^T\mathbf b$, every feasible point optimal. Otherwise $\mathbf c$ has a component in $\mathcal N(A)$ and $p^\star=-\infty$.
(b) If $\mathbf c=\lambda\mathbf a$ with $\lambda\le0$: $p^\star=\lambda b$ (attained on the boundary hyperplane; if $\lambda=0$, $p^\star=0$). Otherwise $p^\star=-\infty$.
(c) Separable: $x_i=l_i$ if $c_i\gt0$, $x_i=u_i$ if $c_i\lt0$: $p^\star=\sum_i\min\{c_il_i,c_iu_i\}=\mathbf c^T\mathbf l-\sum_{c_i\lt0}c_i(l_i-u_i)$.
(d) $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ is a convex combination of the $c_i$: $p^\star=\min_ic_i$, attained by putting all weight on the cheapest coordinates. With $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf x\le1$: $p^\star=\min\{0,\min_ic_i\}$.

Minimise $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $A\mathbf x\preceq\mathbf b$ with $A$ square and non-singular. Show $p^\star=\mathbf c^TA^{-1}\mathbf b$ if $A^{-T}\mathbf c\preceq0$, and $-\infty$ otherwise.

Change variables $\mathbf y=\mathbf b-A\mathbf x\succeq0$, $\mathbf x=A^{-1}(\mathbf b-\mathbf y)$ (one-to-one). Objective $\mathbf c^TA^{-1}\mathbf b-(A^{-T}\mathbf c)^T\mathbf y$ over $\mathbf y\succeq0$. If $A^{-T}\mathbf c\preceq0$ every term $-(A^{-T}\mathbf c)_iy_i\ge0$, minimum at $\mathbf y=\mathbf 0$: $p^\star=\mathbf c^TA^{-1}\mathbf b$. If some $(A^{-T}\mathbf c)_i\gt0$, let $y_i\to\infty$: $p^\star=-\infty$.

Formulate as LPs: (a) min $\|A\mathbf x-\mathbf b\|_\infty$; (b) min $\|A\mathbf x-\mathbf b\|_1$; (c) min $\|A\mathbf x-\mathbf b\|_1$ s.t. $\|\mathbf x\|_\infty\le1$; (d) min $\|\mathbf x\|_1$ s.t. $\|A\mathbf x-\mathbf b\|_\infty\le1$; (e) min $\|A\mathbf x-\mathbf b\|_1+\|\mathbf x\|_\infty$.

(a) min $t$ s.t. $-t\mathbf 1\preceq A\mathbf x-\mathbf b\preceq t\mathbf 1$.
(b) min $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf s$ s.t. $-\mathbf s\preceq A\mathbf x-\mathbf b\preceq\mathbf s$.
(c) min $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf s$ s.t. $-\mathbf s\preceq A\mathbf x-\mathbf b\preceq\mathbf s$, $-\mathbf 1\preceq\mathbf x\preceq\mathbf 1$.
(d) min $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf t$ s.t. $-\mathbf t\preceq\mathbf x\preceq\mathbf t$, $-\mathbf 1\preceq A\mathbf x-\mathbf b\preceq\mathbf 1$.
(e) min $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf s+t$ s.t. $-\mathbf s\preceq A\mathbf x-\mathbf b\preceq\mathbf s$, $-t\mathbf 1\preceq\mathbf x\preceq t\mathbf 1$.
In each, at an optimum of the LP the auxiliary variables equal the absolute values (or maximum) they bound, so $\mathbf x$ is optimal for the original problem and the optimal values agree.

(a) min $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $\mathbf x^TA\mathbf x\le1$, $A\succ0$, $\mathbf c\ne\mathbf 0$ (and if $A\not\succeq0$). (b) min $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $(\mathbf x-\mathbf x_c)^TA(\mathbf x-\mathbf x_c)\le1$. (c) min $\mathbf x^TB\mathbf x$ s.t. $\mathbf x^TA\mathbf x\le1$, $B\succeq0$ (and $B\not\succeq0$).

(a) Put $\mathbf y=A^{1/2}\mathbf x$, $\|\mathbf y\|_2\le1$, objective $(A^{-1/2}\mathbf c)^T\mathbf y\ge-\|A^{-1/2}\mathbf c\|_2$. So $p^\star=-\sqrt{\mathbf c^TA^{-1}\mathbf c}$ at $\mathbf x^\star=-A^{-1}\mathbf c/\sqrt{\mathbf c^TA^{-1}\mathbf c}$. If $A$ has a negative eigenvalue with eigenvector $\mathbf v$, every $t\mathbf v$ is feasible and (choosing the sign, or adding a small multiple of $\mathbf c$) the objective is unbounded: $p^\star=-\infty$.
(b) Shift $\mathbf x=\mathbf x_c+\mathbf z$: $p^\star=\mathbf c^T\mathbf x_c-\sqrt{\mathbf c^TA^{-1}\mathbf c}$.
(c) $B\succeq0$: $p^\star=0$ at $\mathbf x=\mathbf 0$. If $B\not\succeq0$: with $\mathbf y=A^{1/2}\mathbf x$, minimise $\mathbf y^TA^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\mathbf y$ over $\|\mathbf y\|\le1$: $p^\star=\lambda_{\min}(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2})\lt0$, at the corresponding unit eigenvector.

 Reformulations

Verify that $\mathbf x^T\mathbf x\le yz$, $y,z\ge0$ iff $\left\|\begin{pmatrix}2\mathbf x\\y-z\end{pmatrix}\right\|_2\le y+z$. Use it to write minimise $\sum_i1/(\mathbf a_i^T\mathbf x-b_i)$ (on $A\mathbf x\succ\mathbf b$) as an SOCP.

Squaring the right form (with $y+z\ge0$): $4\mathbf x^T\mathbf x+(y-z)^2\le(y+z)^2\iff\mathbf x^T\mathbf x\le yz$; and $y+z\ge|y-z|$ forces $y,z\ge0$. For the problem: minimise $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf t$ s.t. $t_i(\mathbf a_i^T\mathbf x-b_i)\ge1$, $t_i\ge0$, i.e. $\left\|\begin{pmatrix}2\\t_i-\mathbf a_i^T\mathbf x+b_i\end{pmatrix}\right\|_2\le t_i+\mathbf a_i^T\mathbf x-b_i$.

Express as convex problems: (a) min $\max\{p(\mathbf x),q(\mathbf x)\}$; (b) min $e^{p(\mathbf x)}+e^{q(\mathbf x)}$; (c) min $p(\mathbf x)/(r(\mathbf x)-q(\mathbf x))$ s.t. $r\gt q$; $p,q$ posynomials, $r$ a monomial.

(a) GP: min $t$ s.t. $p(\mathbf x)/t\le1$, $q(\mathbf x)/t\le1$; then $x_i=e^{y_i}$, $t=e^s$ and take logs.
(b) With $x_i=e^{y_i}$, $p(e^{\mathbf y})=\sum_ke^{\mathbf a_k^T\mathbf y+b_k}$ is convex in $\mathbf y$, and $e^u$ is convex increasing, so $e^{p}+e^{q}$ is convex in $\mathbf y$: minimise it unconstrained.
(c) Epigraph: $p/(r-q)\le t$ ⇔ $p\le tr-tq$ ⇔ $\dfrac{p}{tr}+\dfrac{q}{r}\le1$, a posynomial in $(\mathbf x,t)$ (posynomial divided by a monomial). GP: min $t$ s.t. that constraint; convex after the log change of variables.

$A(\mathbf x)=A_0+x_1A_1+\dots+x_nA_n$, $A_i\in\mathbf S^m$. Minimise $\lambda_{\max}(A(\mathbf x))$ as an SDP.

$\lambda_{\max}(A)\le t\iff A\preceq tI$. So: minimise $t$ s.t. $A(\mathbf x)-tI\preceq0$, an LMI in $(\mathbf x,t)$. Similarly the spread $\lambda_{\max}-\lambda_{\min}$: minimise $t_1-t_2$ s.t. $t_2I\preceq A(\mathbf x)\preceq t_1I$.

Explain how to solve minimise $\dfrac{\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+d}{\mathbf e^T\mathbf x+f}$ s.t. $G\mathbf x\preceq\mathbf h$ by bisection.

For fixed $t$, $f_0(\mathbf x)\le t\iff\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+d-t(\mathbf e^T\mathbf x+f)\le0$ (on $\mathbf e^T\mathbf x+f\gt0$), a linear inequality. So "is $p^\star\le t$?" is an LP feasibility problem. Keep an interval $[l,u]$ containing $p^\star$, test the midpoint, halve; after $\lceil\log_2((u-l)/\epsilon)\rceil$ tests the interval is shorter than $\epsilon$.
