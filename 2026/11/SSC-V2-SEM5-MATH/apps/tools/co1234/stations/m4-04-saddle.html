Primal and dual are the two orders of a min–max of the Lagrangian: $p^\star=\inf_{\mathbf x}\sup_{\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0}L$ and $d^\star=\sup_{\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0}\inf_{\mathbf x}L$. Weak duality is the max–min inequality; strong duality says the order does not matter, and an optimal primal–dual pair is a saddle point of $L$. Max–min characterisation (§5.4.1)

(Inequality constraints only.) $$\sup_{\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0}L(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\lambda)=\sup_{\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0}\Big(f_0(\mathbf x)+\sum\lambda_if_i(\mathbf x)\Big)=\begin{cases}f_0(\mathbf x)&f_i(\mathbf x)\le0\ \forall i\\\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}$$ so $p^\star=\inf_{\mathbf x}\sup_{\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0}L(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\lambda)$, while by definition $d^\star=\sup_{\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0}\inf_{\mathbf x}L(\mathbf x,\boldsymbol\lambda)$.

Max–min inequality (any $f$, any sets $W,Z$): $\sup_{\mathbf z\in Z}\inf_{\mathbf w\in W}f(\mathbf w,\mathbf z)\le\inf_{\mathbf w\in W}\sup_{\mathbf z\in Z}f(\mathbf w,\mathbf z)$. For $L$ this is weak duality; equality is strong duality. If equality holds, $f$ (or $L$) has the strong max–min property.

Proof (exercise 5.24): for any $\tilde{\mathbf w},\tilde{\mathbf z}$, $\inf_{\mathbf w}f(\mathbf w,\tilde{\mathbf z})\le f(\tilde{\mathbf w},\tilde{\mathbf z})\le\sup_{\mathbf z}f(\tilde{\mathbf w},\mathbf z)$. The left side does not depend on $\tilde{\mathbf w}$ and the right not on $\tilde{\mathbf z}$; take sup over $\tilde{\mathbf z}$ on the left, inf over $\tilde{\mathbf w}$ on the right.

Saddle points (§5.4.2)

$(\tilde{\mathbf w},\tilde{\mathbf z})$ is a saddle point of $f$ if $f(\tilde{\mathbf w},\mathbf z)\le f(\tilde{\mathbf w},\tilde{\mathbf z})\le f(\mathbf w,\tilde{\mathbf z})$ for all $\mathbf w\in W$, $\mathbf z\in Z$. Then the strong max–min property holds and the common value is $f(\tilde{\mathbf w},\tilde{\mathbf z})$. For the Lagrangian: if $\mathbf x^\star$ and $\boldsymbol\lambda^\star$ are primal and dual optimal with zero duality gap, $(\mathbf x^\star,\boldsymbol\lambda^\star)$ is a saddle point of $L$; conversely a saddle point of $L$ gives primal and dual optimal points with zero gap.

 The Lagrangian of exercise 5.1: along $\lambda=2$ it has a minimum at $x=2$; along $x=2$ it is constant in $\lambda$ ($f_1(2)=0$, complementary slackness), and $L(2,\lambda)\le5\le L(x,2)$. Game and price interpretations (§5.4.3–5.4.4)

Game. Player 1 chooses $\mathbf w$, player 2 chooses $\mathbf z$, player 1 pays $f(\mathbf w,\mathbf z)$. If player 1 moves first, player 2 answers with $\sup_{\mathbf z}$ and player 1 can guarantee $\inf_{\mathbf w}\sup_{\mathbf z}f$; if player 2 moves first, the payment is $\sup_{\mathbf z}\inf_{\mathbf w}f$. Moving second is an advantage (max–min inequality); with the strong max–min property it is no advantage. A saddle point is a solution (equilibrium) of the game. For $L$: $\mathbf x$ against $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0$.

Prices (tax). $\lambda_i$ is the price per unit of violation of constraint $i$ (a payment received for slack). $g(\boldsymbol\lambda)$ is the best cost an enterprise can get when allowed to violate constraints at that price. With strong duality, at the optimal prices $\boldsymbol\lambda^\star$ there is no advantage in being allowed to violate constraints: the enterprise chooses a feasible optimal point.
