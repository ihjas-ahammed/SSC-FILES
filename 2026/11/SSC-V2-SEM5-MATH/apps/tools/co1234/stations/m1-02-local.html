At a local maximum or minimum every directional derivative that exists is zero, because the slice through the point has a maximum or minimum there. That only gives candidates: a point where all partial derivatives vanish can still be a saddle. Definitions (§19.7)

Let $A$ contain the open ball $\|\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\xi\|\lt\Delta$. $f:A\to\mathbf R$ has a local maximum at $\boldsymbol\xi$ if there is $\delta\gt0$ with $f(\mathbf x)\le f(\boldsymbol\xi)$ whenever $\|\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\xi\|\lt\delta$; a local minimum if $f(\mathbf x)\ge f(\boldsymbol\xi)$ there. Roughly: a little hill, or a little valley, above $\boldsymbol\xi$.

Theorem 19.8 and corollary 19.9

Theorem 19.8. If $D_{\mathbf u}f(\boldsymbol\xi)$ exists and $f$ has a local maximum or minimum at $\boldsymbol\xi$, then $D_{\mathbf u}f(\boldsymbol\xi)=0$.

Proof. $F(t)=f(\boldsymbol\xi+t\mathbf u)$ is defined on an open interval round $0$ and has a local maximum or minimum at $t=0$. By the one-variable result (theorem 11.2) $F'(0)=0$, i.e. $D_{\mathbf u}f(\boldsymbol\xi)=0$.

Corollary 19.9. At a local maximum or minimum, every partial derivative that exists is zero (a partial derivative is a directional derivative along an axis).

 Example 19.10: candidates are not answers

$f=x_1^2x_2+x_2^3x_1-x_1x_2$. Necessary conditions:

$$f_1=2x_1x_2+x_2^3-x_2=x_2(2x_1+x_2^2-1)=0,\qquad f_2=x_1^2+3x_2^2x_1-x_1=x_1(x_1+3x_2^2-1)=0.$$

Four cases: $x_2=0,x_1=0$; $x_2=0,x_1=1$; $x_1=0,x_2^2=1$; and $2x_1+x_2^2=1$, $x_1+3x_2^2=1$ (so $x_2^2=\tfrac15$, $x_1=\tfrac25$). Candidates: $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(0,-1),(\tfrac25,\pm\tfrac1{\sqrt5})$.

$(0,0)$ is not an extremum: on $x_1=x_2$, $f(t,t)=t^3+t^4-t^2$ has a local maximum at $0$; on $x_1=-x_2$, $f(t,-t)=-t^3-t^4+t^2$ has a local minimum at $0$. A saddle point. (The book prints $f(t,-t)=t^3-t^4+t^2$; the correct expansion is $-t^3-t^4+t^2$, and the conclusion, a local minimum at $t=0$, is the same.) Exercise 19.47(4) classifies all six points with the Hessian.

"All partials zero" is necessary, not sufficient. In an answer, say "the only points at which $f$ can have a local extremum are …" and then classify.

 Show that $f(x_1,x_2)=x_1x_2$ has all partial derivatives zero at the origin but no extremum there.

$f_1=x_2$, $f_2=x_1$, both $0$ at $(0,0)$. On $x_1=x_2$, $f=x_1^2\gt0$; on $x_1=-x_2$, $f=-x_1^2\lt0$ (except at $0$). Every neighbourhood has values above and below $f(0,0)=0$: a saddle (example 19.29).
