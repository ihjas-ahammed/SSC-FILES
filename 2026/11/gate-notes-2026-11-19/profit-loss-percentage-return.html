1. Chapter 5: Percentage — Advanced Applications

Problem 34: Percentage Comparison

Condition: Two variables with values $x = 20$ and $y = 10$.

$$\text{First Number} = \left(\frac{100 + x}{100 + y} \times 100\right)\% \text{ of the second}$$

$$= \left(\frac{100 + 20}{100 + 10} \times 100\right)\% = \left(\frac{120}{110} \times 100\right)\% = 109\frac{1}{11}\%$$

Result: The first number is $9\frac{1}{11}\%$ more than the second number.

Problem 35: Reduction in Consumption

Rule: If price increases by $P\%$, reduction in consumption to keep expenditure constant:

$$\text{Reduction in Consumption} = \left(\frac{P}{100 + P} \times 100\right)\%$$

For $P = 15\%$:

$$= \left(\frac{15}{100 + 15} \times 100\right)\% = \frac{1500}{115}\% = 13\frac{1}{23}\%$$

Alternative Fraction Method:

$$\text{Price } \uparrow = 15\% = \frac{3}{20} = \frac{a}{b} \implies \text{Consumption } \downarrow = \left(\frac{a}{a+b} \times 100\right)\% = \left(\frac{3}{23} \times 100\right)\% = 13\frac{1}{23}\%$$

Problem 36: Increase in Consumption

Rule: If price decreases by $P\%$, increase in consumption to maintain expenditure:

$$\text{Increase in Consumption} = \left(\frac{P}{100 - P} \times 100\right)\%$$

For $P = 10\%$:

$$= \left(\frac{10}{100 - 10} \times 100\right)\% = \frac{100}{9}\% = 11\frac{1}{9}\%$$

Alternative Fraction Method:

$$\text{Price } \downarrow = 10\% = \frac{1}{10} = \frac{a}{b} \implies \text{Consumption } \uparrow = \left(\frac{a}{b-a} \times 100\right)\% = \left(\frac{1}{9} \times 100\right)\% = 11\frac{1}{9}\%$$

Problem 37 & 38: Successive Net Percentage Changes

Formula: $\text{Net \% change} = \left(x + y + \frac{xy}{100}\right)\%$

  • Problem 37: $x = +20, y = -20 \implies 20 - 20 - \frac{20 \times 20}{100} = -4\%$ (Decrease of 4%).
  • Problem 38: $x = -50, y = +50 \implies -50 + 50 - \frac{50 \times 50}{100} = -25\%$ (Loss of 25%).
    Rule: If an amount increases by $x\%$ and then decreases by $x\%$, net change is always a loss: $\left(\frac{x}{10}\right)^2\% = \left(\frac{50}{10}\right)^2\% = 25\% \text{ loss}$.

Problem 39 & 40: Population Decay & Discounts

  • Problem 39: Successive drops of 20% and 25%: $$\text{Net change} = \left(-20 - 25 + \frac{(-20)(-25)}{100}\right)\% = -45 + 5 = -40\% \quad (\text{Decrease by } 40\%)$$
  • Problem 40: Successive discounts of 20% and 20%: $$\text{Equivalent Discount} = 20 + 20 - \frac{20 \times 20}{100} = 36\%$$ Given: Difference $(36\% - 35\%) = 1\% = \text{₹}22$. $$\text{Total Bill Amount} = 22 \times 100 = \text{₹}2200$$

Problem 41 & 42: Price Adjustment & Discount

  • Problem 41: $x = +25, y = -12$: $$\text{Net change} = \left(25 - 12 - \frac{25 \times 12}{100}\right)\% = 13 - 3 = +10\% \quad (\text{Profit of } 10\%)$$
  • Problem 42: Successive discounts of 20% and 10%: $$\text{Equivalent Single Discount} = 20 + 10 - \frac{20 \times 10}{100} = 28\%$$

2. Chapter 9: Profit and Loss — Principles & Shortcuts

2.1 Fundamental Definitions

Transactions involve buying and selling goods. The technical terms are defined below:

  • Cost Price (C.P.): The purchase price of an article plus overhead expenses (transport, repair, tax).
  • Selling Price (S.P.): The price at which an article is sold to a customer.
  • Profit (Gain): Occurs when Selling Price is greater than Cost Price: $\text{Profit} = \text{S.P.} - \text{C.P.}$
  • Loss: Occurs when Cost Price is greater than Selling Price: $\text{Loss} = \text{C.P.} - \text{S.P.}$
Important Rule: Profit percentage and Loss percentage are always computed on the Cost Price (C.P.) unless explicitly stated otherwise.

2.2 Basic Formulae

01 Profit % and Loss %
$$\text{Gain \%} = \left(\frac{\text{Gain} \times 100}{\text{C.P.}}\right)$$ $$\text{Loss \%} = \left(\frac{\text{Loss} \times 100}{\text{C.P.}}\right)$$
02 & 03 When Gain % is Given
$$\text{C.P.} = \left(\frac{100}{100 + \text{Gain \%}}\right) \times \text{S.P.}$$ $$\text{S.P.} = \left(\frac{100 + \text{Gain \%}}{100}\right) \times \text{C.P.}$$
04 & 05 When Loss % is Given
$$\text{C.P.} = \left(\frac{100}{100 - \text{Loss \%}}\right) \times \text{S.P.}$$ $$\text{S.P.} = \left(\frac{100 - \text{Loss \%}}{100}\right) \times \text{C.P.}$$
Illustrations 1 to 6 (Summary)
  • • $\text{C.P.} = \text{₹}200, \text{S.P.} = \text{₹}250 \implies \text{Gain} = \text{₹}50 \implies \text{Gain \%} = \frac{50}{200} \times 100 = 25\%$
  • • $\text{C.P.} = \text{₹}750, \text{S.P.} = \text{₹}675 \implies \text{Loss} = \text{₹}75 \implies \text{Loss \%} = \frac{75}{750} \times 100 = 10\%$
  • • $\text{C.P.} = \text{₹}4500, \text{Gain} = 8\% \implies \text{S.P.} = \frac{108}{100} \times 4500 = \text{₹}4860$
  • • $\text{S.P.} = \text{₹}7200, \text{Loss} = 10\% \implies \text{C.P.} = \frac{100}{90} \times 7200 = \text{₹}8000$
  • • $\text{S.P.} = \text{₹}99, \text{Gain} = 12.5\% = \frac{25}{2}\% \implies \text{C.P.} = \frac{100}{100 + 12.5} \times 99 = \text{₹}88$

2.3 Advanced Shortcut Methods (01 to 13)

Method 01 Cross-Multiplication for Quantity vs Price

If a person buys $x$ items for ₹$y$ and sells $z$ items for ₹$w$, the overall profit or loss percentage is:

$$\text{\% Gain or Loss} = \left(\frac{xw}{zy} - 1\right) \times 100\%$$

Rule: The downward cross product $xw$ is in the numerator. The upward cross product $zy$ is in the denominator. Positive result indicates profit; negative result indicates loss.

Illustration 7: 11 oranges bought for ₹10; 10 oranges sold for ₹11. $$\% \text{ Gain} = \left(\frac{11 \times 11}{10 \times 10} - 1\right) \times 100\% = \left(\frac{121 - 100}{100}\right) \times 100\% = 21\%$$
Illustration 8: 12 apples bought for ₹12; 15 apples sold for ₹12. $$\% \text{ Gain/Loss} = \left(\frac{12 \times 12}{15 \times 12} - 1\right) \times 100\% = \left(\frac{144 - 180}{180}\right) \times 100\% = -20\% \implies 20\% \text{ Loss}$$
Method 02 Cost Price of $m$ Articles = Selling Price of $n$ Articles
$$\text{\% Gain or Loss} = \left(\frac{m - n}{n}\right) \times 100\%$$

If $m > n$, there is a gain. If $m < n$, there is a loss.

Illustration 9 (Faulty Weight): Professes to sell at cost price, but gives 800 gm instead of 1000 gm. $$\text{Gain \%} = \left(\frac{1000 - 800}{800}\right) \times 100\% = \frac{200}{800} \times 100\% = 25\%$$
Illustration 10: Selling price of 12 articles equals cost price of 18 articles ($m=18, n=12$). $$\text{Gain \%} = \left(\frac{18 - 12}{12}\right) \times 100\% = \frac{6}{12} \times 100\% = 50\%$$
Method 03 Relation Between Two Selling Prices

If selling price $\text{S.P.}_1$ gives $x\%$ and selling price $\text{S.P.}_2$ gives $y\%$, then:

$$\frac{\text{S.P.}_1}{100 + x} = \frac{\text{S.P.}_2}{100 + y} = \frac{\text{C.P.}}{100}$$
Illustration 11: Selling a radio for ₹1536 causes a loss of 20% ($x = -20$). Find profit/loss percentage if sold for ₹2000 ($\text{S.P.}_2 = 2000$). $$\frac{1536}{100 - 20} = \frac{2000}{100 + y} \implies \frac{1536}{80} = \frac{2000}{100 + y} \implies 100 + y = \frac{2000 \times 80}{1536} = 104\frac{1}{6}$$ $$y = 4\frac{1}{6}\% \quad (\text{Gain of } 4\frac{1}{6}\%)$$
Method 04 & 05 Successive Chains of Sale ($A \to B \to C$)

If $A$ sells to $B$ at $m\%$ profit/loss and $B$ sells to $C$ at $n\%$ profit/loss, and $C$ pays ₹$z$:

$$\text{Initial Cost Price for } A = \left[\frac{100^2 \times z}{(100 + m)(100 + n)}\right]$$ $$\text{Resultant Profit/Loss \%} = \left(m + n + \frac{mn}{100}\right)\%$$
Illustration 12: $m = +10\%, n = +5\%, z = \text{₹}462$. $$\text{C.P. for Mohit} = \frac{100 \times 100 \times 462}{(100 + 10)(100 + 5)} = \frac{4620000}{110 \times 105} = \text{₹}400$$
Illustration 13: $m = +17\%, n = -25\%, z = \text{₹}1053$. $$\text{C.P. for A} = \frac{100 \times 100 \times 1053}{117 \times 75} = \text{₹}1200$$
Method 06 & 07 Two Articles Sold at the Same Selling Price

When two articles have equal Selling Price: First sold at $x\%$ profit/loss, second sold at $y\%$ profit/loss:

$$\text{Overall \% Profit or Loss} = \left[\frac{100(x + y) + 2xy}{(100 + x) + (100 + y)}\right]\%$$
Special Symmetric Case (Method 07): If one article is sold at $+x\%$ and the other at $-x\%$, there is always a net loss: $$\text{Overall Loss \%} = \left(\frac{x}{10}\right)^2\% = \frac{x^2}{100}\%$$
Illustration 16: S.P. = ₹24000 each. $x = +20, y = -15$. $$\text{Net \%} = \frac{100(20 - 15) + 2(20)(-15)}{(120) + (85)} = \frac{500 - 600}{205} = -\frac{100}{205}\% = -\frac{20}{41}\% \quad (\text{Loss})$$
Illustration 17: S.P. = ₹990 each. $x = 10\%$ gain, $y = 10\%$ loss. $$\text{Overall Loss \%} = \left(\frac{10}{10}\right)^2\% = 1\% \text{ loss}$$
Method 08 & 09 Dishonest Merchant and Faulty Measurements
$$\frac{100 + g}{100 + x} = \frac{\text{True Measure}}{\text{Faulty Measure}}$$ $$\text{Overall Gain \% with } y\% \text{ less measure and } x\% \text{ markup} = \left(\frac{y + x}{100 - y} \times 100\right)\%$$
Illustration 18: Sells at cost price ($x=0$), meter rod measures 95 cm instead of 100 cm. $$\frac{100 + g}{100} = \frac{100}{95} \implies g = \frac{10000}{95} - 100 = 5\frac{5}{19}\%$$
Illustration 20: Sells at 44% loss ($x = -44$), uses 30% less weight ($y = 30$). $$\text{Net \%} = \left(\frac{30 - 44}{100 - 30} \times 100\right)\% = \frac{-14}{70} \times 100\% = -20\% \quad (\text{Loss of } 20\%)$$
Method 10 Two Items Purchased Together for Total Cost ₹$A$ and Sold at Same S.P.

Item 1 sold at $l\%$ loss, Item 2 sold at $g\%$ gain:

$$\text{Cost Price of Item Sold at Loss} = \frac{A(100 + g\%)}{(100 - l\%) + (100 + g\%)}$$ $$\text{Cost Price of Item Sold at Gain} = \frac{A(100 - l\%)}{(100 - l\%) + (100 + g\%)}$$
Illustration 21: Total cost $A = \text{₹}410$, $l = 20\%$, $g = 25\%$. $$\text{C.P. at loss} = \frac{410 \times 125}{80 + 125} = \frac{410 \times 125}{205} = \text{₹}250$$ $$\text{C.P. at gain} = \frac{410 \times 80}{80 + 125} = \frac{410 \times 80}{205} = \text{₹}160$$
Method 11 & 12 Successive Discounts

Single discount equivalent to two successive discounts $m\%$ and $n\%$:

$$\text{Equivalent Discount (2 items)} = \left(m + n - \frac{mn}{100}\right)\%$$

Single discount equivalent to three successive discounts $l\%$, $m\%$, and $n\%$:

$$\text{Equivalent Discount (3 items)} = \left[(l + m + n) - \frac{lm + ln + mn}{100} + \frac{lmn}{10000}\right]\%$$
Illustration 22: 10% and 20%: $$D = 10 + 20 - \frac{200}{100} = 28\%$$
Illustration 23: 10%, 20%, and 30%: $$D = (10 + 20 + 30) - \frac{200 + 300 + 600}{100} + \frac{6000}{10000} = 60 - 11 + 0.6 = 49.6\%$$
Method 13 Selling Price with Discount vs Normal Cost Price

Sold at ₹$z$ after discount $d\%$. If no discount given, profit would be $p\%$:

$$\text{Cost Price (C.P.)} = \frac{100^2 \times z}{(100 - d)(100 + p)}$$
Illustration 25: $z = \text{₹}266, d = 5\%, p = 12\%$. $$\text{C.P.} = \frac{100 \times 100 \times 266}{(100 - 5)(100 + 12)} = \frac{2660000}{95 \times 112} = \text{₹}250$$

7. Chapter 17: Simple Interest — Core Fundamentals

7.1 Technical Terminology

  • Principal ($P$): The money lent or borrowed for a specified period.
  • Interest ($I$): Additional payment given by the borrower to the lender for using the capital.
  • Rate ($R$): The interest charged per ₹100 of principal per annum (% p.a.).
  • Time ($T$): The duration for which the principal amount is utilized (in years).
  • Amount ($A$): The sum of Principal and Interest: $$A = P + I$$

7.2 Fundamental Formulae

01 Simple Interest Calculation
$$I = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$$
02 Determining Principal
$$P = \frac{100 \times I}{R \times T}$$
Illustration 1: Direct Calculation of Interest

Problem: Find simple interest on ₹5200 for 2 years at 6% per annum.

Values: $P = \text{₹}5200, T = 2 \text{ years}, R = 6\%$

$$I = \frac{5200 \times 6 \times 2}{100} = 52 \times 12 = \text{₹}624$$
Illustration 2: Determination of Principal

Problem: A man earns ₹450 interest in 2 years at 12% p.a. Find the sum invested.

Values: $I = \text{₹}450, T = 2 \text{ years}, R = 12\%$

$$P = \frac{450 \times 100}{12 \times 2} = \frac{45000}{24} = \text{₹}1875$$
