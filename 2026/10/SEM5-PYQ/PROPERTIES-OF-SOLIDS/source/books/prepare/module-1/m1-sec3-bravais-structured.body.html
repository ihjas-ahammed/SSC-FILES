1.7 Types of lattices: Bravais lattices and crystal systems (structured notes)

Source: book-sources/bs-1-crystal-structure.html, Puri and Babbar pp. 10–15. Equation 1.5 is cited by its book number. Tables 1.1 and 1.2 are reproduced from the book.

Before you start

  • Point groups (Section 1.6) constrain which lattices are possible.
  • Lattice parameters: $a$, $b$, $c$, $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$.
  • Sharing of lattice points between cells (recall below).
Plus Two recall: sharing a quantity among cells

Recall. A quantity held by $k$ identical objects is counted as $1/k$ for each.

Re-derive.

  1. In a stack of cubes, a corner point touches 8 cubes; its weight per cube is $1/8$.
  2. A face point touches 2 cubes; its weight per cube is $1/2$.
  3. A point inside a cube belongs to that cube alone; its weight is 1.

Saved. Weights $1/8$, $1/2$ and $1$ in Eq. (1.5).

1.7.3 Effective number of lattice points

$$N = N_i + \frac{N_f}{2} + \frac{N_c}{8} \tag{1.5}$$

Results: sc $N=1$; bcc $N=2$; fcc $N=4$ (with $N_c=8$ for each, $N_i=1$ for bcc, $N_f=6$ for fcc).

1.7.4 Why only fourteen

The orthorhombic system has four lattices, the cubic three and the tetragonal two. The extra cells are not new arrangements: two face-centred tetragonal lattices placed side by side give a body-centred tetragonal lattice (Fig. 1.12), which is already listed.

Result: fourteen Bravais lattices

Five in two dimensions, fourteen in three. Every crystal structure is one of these lattices with a basis.

Summary

  • 5 two-dimensional and 14 three-dimensional Bravais lattices.
  • Eq. (1.5) gives the effective lattice points per cell.
