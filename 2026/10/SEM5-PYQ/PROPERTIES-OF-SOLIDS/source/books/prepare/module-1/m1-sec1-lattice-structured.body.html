  • Triple product and cell volume (Plus Two recall below).
Plus Two recall: area and volume from vector products

Recall. The cross product $\mathbf a\times\mathbf b$ has magnitude $ab\sin\theta$, the area of the parallelogram formed by $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$.

Re-derive.

  1. Parallelogram with sides $a$, $b$ and angle $\theta$: base $a$, height $b\sin\theta$, area $ab\sin\theta=|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|$.
  2. Parallelepiped with third edge $\mathbf c$: volume = base area × height, height $= |\mathbf c\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}|$ with $\hat{\mathbf n}=(\mathbf a\times\mathbf b)/|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|$.
  3. Multiply: $V=|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|\,|\mathbf c\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}|=|\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)|$.

Saved. $V=|\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)|$ (book §1.3, not numbered).

1.1 Definition of a lattice

A point lattice (space lattice) is an infinite array of imaginary points in three-dimensional space in which every point has identical surroundings. The lattice has the same appearance seen from any of its points. This holds only if the points form a repeating pattern unit, moved by a translation operation.

1.2 Translation vectors

The translation operation is

$$\mathbf T = n_1\mathbf a + n_2\mathbf b + n_3\mathbf c \tag{1.1}$$

where $n_1$, $n_2$, $n_3$ are arbitrary integers and $\mathbf a$, $\mathbf b$, $\mathbf c$ are the fundamental translation vectors. The relation between two lattice points is

$$\mathbf r' = \mathbf r + \mathbf T = \mathbf r + n_1\mathbf a + n_2\mathbf b + n_3\mathbf c \tag{1.2}$$

A perfect lattice satisfies Eq. (1.2) for every integer choice. An imperfect lattice does not admit such vectors.

Result 1: primitive and non-primitive vectors

Vectors whose translation operation has integral coefficients are primitive. Vectors that need non-integral coefficients, for example $\mathbf R'=\mathbf R+\tfrac12\mathbf a_2+\tfrac12\mathbf b_2$, are non-primitive. The book prefers an orthogonal, shortest set (Fig. 1.1).

1.3 Unit cell

A unit cell is a parallelogram (2D) or parallelepiped (3D) built from the translation vectors. Repetition of its translation generates the full lattice. A cell is primitive when its lattice points lie at its corners only, so that its effective count is one. A non-primitive cell has points at other positions inside or on its faces, so its effective count is greater than one (Fig. 1.2).

{{FIG:m1-s1-cells}}

The Wigner–Seitz cell is constructed by drawing the bisectors of the lines from a lattice point to its neighbours; the smallest enclosed volume is primitive (Fig. 1.3).

{{FIG:m1-s1-ws}}

The conventional unit cell is chosen for convenience; it is the primitive cell of least volume, unless a non-primitive cell shows higher symmetry.

1.4 Basis and crystal structure

A basis is an atom or group of atoms placed identically at every lattice point. The combination is the crystal structure:

Space lattice + Basis → Crystal structure

{{FIG:m1-s1-basis}}

A lattice is mathematical; a crystal structure is physical. Fourteen lattices suffice because the bases vary.

Result 2: effective lattice points

A lattice point at a corner is shared by eight cells (weight 1/8); at a face centre by two (1/2); a point inside belongs wholly to the cell (weight 1). Eq. (1.5) gives the count, checked in §1.7.

Summary

  • Translations (Eqs. 1.1 and 1.2) define the lattice.
  • Primitive cells have one effective lattice point; non-primitive cells have more.
  • Crystal structure = lattice + basis.
