9.6 Ferroelectricity

Ferroelectricity is spontaneous polarisation, the electrical analogue of spontaneous magnetisation. Centres of positive and negative charge do not coincide in the absence of a field. The polarisation against field forms a hysteresis loop. Above the Curie point $T_C$ the crystal is paraelectric and the dielectric constant falls sharply.

Examples are $\mathrm{BaTiO_3}$ and Rochelle salt. Order–disorder ferroelectrics (for example $\mathrm{KH_2PO_4}$, $\mathrm{RbH_2PO_4}$) transform by ionic ordering, and displacive ones ($\mathrm{BaTiO_3}$, $\mathrm{KNbO_3}$, $\mathrm{LiTaO_3}$) by the displacement of one sublattice.

In $\mathrm{BaTiO_3}$ above about 380 K the cell is cubic, with $\mathrm{Ba^{2+}}$ at the corners, $\mathrm{O^{2-}}$ at the face centres and $\mathrm{Ti^{4+}}$ at the body centre (Fig. 9.5). Below $T_C$ the Ti and Ba ions move up and the O ions move down, the structure becomes tetragonal, and a spontaneous polarisation appears.

{{FIG:m4-f95}}
$$\varepsilon_r=\frac{1+\dfrac{2}{3\varepsilon_o}\sum_jN_j\alpha_j}{1-\dfrac{1}{3\varepsilon_o}\sum_jN_j\alpha_j}\tag{9.33}$$

The dielectric constant diverges when

$$\sum_jN_j\alpha_j=3\varepsilon_o\tag{9.34}$$

This is the polarisation catastrophe: the local field grows faster than the restoring force, and higher-order forces limit the displacement. Writing $\tfrac{1}{3\varepsilon_o}\sum_jN_j\alpha_j=1-3s$ with $s\ll1$:

$$\frac{1}{3\varepsilon_o}\sum_jN_j\alpha_j=1-3s\tag{9.35}$$
$$\varepsilon_r=\frac{1}{s}-2\cong\frac{1}{s}\tag{9.36}$$
$$s\cong\frac{T-T_C}{\xi}\tag{9.37}$$
$$\varepsilon_r\cong\frac{\xi}{T-T_C}\tag{9.38}$$

This matches the paraelectric state qualitatively.

9.7 Piezoelectricity

A stress induces polarisation in a piezoelectric crystal, and an electric field strains it (quartz, Rochelle salt, tourmaline). In one-dimensional notation:

$$\left.\begin{aligned}P&=Zd+\varepsilon_oE\chi\\e&=Zs+Ed\end{aligned}\right.\tag{9.39}$$

Strains are very small: in quartz about $10^{-8}$ for $10^4$ V/m. Piezoelectricity needs the absence of a centre of inversion (Fig. 9.6). All ferroelectrics are piezoelectric; not all piezoelectrics are ferroelectric ($\mathrm{BaTiO_3}$ and quartz are the examples). The devices are transducers: gramophone pick-ups, microphones, strain gauges and ultrasonic generators.
