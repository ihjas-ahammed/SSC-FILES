Before you start

  • Capacitance with a dielectric, $C=KC_0$ (Plus Two recall below).
  • Electric dipole moment $p=qd$.
Plus Two recall: a dielectric in a capacitor

Recall. A dielectric of constant $K$ between capacitor plates gives $C=KC_0$.

Re-derive.

  1. Polarisation creates bound charges that oppose the free-charge field.
  2. The resultant field is therefore smaller than the applied field.
  3. For fixed charge, $C$ rises by the factor $K$.

Saved. Eq. (9.1) and Eq. (9.5).

9.1 Polarisation and susceptibility

The polarisation field opposes the applied field, so

$$\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{E}_o+\mathbf{E}_p\tag{9.1}$$
{{FIG:m4-f91}}
$$\mathbf{P}=\varepsilon_o\chi_e\mathbf{E}\tag{9.2}$$

where $\chi_e$ is the electric susceptibility.

9.2 The local field

The local field at an atom with cubic symmetry is given by the Lorentz relation:

$$\mathbf{E}_{loc}=\mathbf{E}_o+\mathbf{E}_p+\frac{\mathbf{P}}{3\varepsilon_o}=\mathbf{E}+\frac{\mathbf{P}}{3\varepsilon_o}\tag{9.3}$$

The extra term is the field of the other atoms' polarisation. Its form depends on specimen shape.

9.3 Dielectric constant and polarisability

$$\mathbf{D}=\varepsilon_o\varepsilon_r\mathbf{E}=\varepsilon_o\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{P}\tag{9.4}$$
$$\varepsilon_r=\frac{\varepsilon_o\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{P}}{\varepsilon_o\mathbf{E}}=1+\chi_e\tag{9.5}$$

The polarisability of an atom is

$$p=\alpha E_{loc}\tag{9.6}$$

Polarisability is atomic; the dielectric constant is macroscopic. For $N$ identical atoms per unit volume:

$$P=Np=N\alpha E_{loc}\tag{9.7}$$
$$P=\sum_jN_j\alpha_jE_{loc}(j)\tag{9.8}$$

With cubic symmetry, (9.3) and (9.7) give

$$P=N\alpha\left(E+\frac{P}{3\varepsilon_o}\right)\tag{9.9}$$

which yields

$$\chi_e=\frac{P}{\varepsilon_oE}=\frac{N\alpha/\varepsilon_o}{1-\dfrac{N\alpha}{3\varepsilon_o}}\tag{9.10}$$
$$\varepsilon_r=\frac{1+\dfrac{2N\alpha}{3\varepsilon_o}}{1-\dfrac{N\alpha}{3\varepsilon_o}}\tag{9.11}$$
$$\frac{\varepsilon_r-1}{\varepsilon_r+2}=\frac{N\alpha}{3\varepsilon_o}\tag{9.12}$$

This is the Clausius–Mossotti relation. In general form:

$$\frac{\varepsilon_r-1}{\varepsilon_r+2}=\frac{1}{3\varepsilon_o}\sum_jN_j\alpha_j\tag{9.13}$$
