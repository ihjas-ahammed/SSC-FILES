9.4 Sources of polarisability

Electronic, ionic and dipolar (orientational) polarisability make up the net value. Their relative weight depends on the material and on the frequency.

9.4.1 Electronic polarisability

Electronic polarisation is the displacement of the electron cloud relative to the nucleus (Fig. 9.2). At optical frequencies the dielectric constant is mainly electronic. With $\varepsilon_r=n^2$:

{{FIG:m4-f92}}
$$\frac{n^2-1}{n^2+2}=\frac{1}{3\varepsilon_o}\sum_jN_j\alpha_j\quad\text{(electronic)}\tag{9.14}$$

Classical resonance and static polarisability:

$$\omega_o=\sqrt{\beta/m}\tag{9.15}$$
$$-eE_{loc}=\beta x=m\omega_o^2x\tag{9.16}$$
$$\alpha_e=\frac{p}{E_{loc}}=-\frac{ex}{E_{loc}}=\frac{e^2}{m\omega_o^2}\tag{9.17}$$
$$m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}+m\omega_o^2x=-eE_{loc}\sin\omega t\tag{9.18}$$
$$m(-\omega^2+\omega_o^2)x_o=-eE_{loc}\tag{9.19}$$
$$\alpha_e=\frac{p_o}{E_{loc}}=\frac{e^2/m}{\omega_o^2-\omega^2}\tag{9.20}$$
$$\alpha_e=\frac{e^2}{m}\sum_j\frac{f_{ij}}{\omega_{ij}^2-\omega^2}\tag{9.21}$$

9.4.2 Ionic polarisability

$$\beta\Delta x\cong eE\tag{9.22}$$
$$\alpha_i=\frac{p}{E}=\frac{e\Delta x}{E}\cong\frac{e^2}{\beta}\tag{9.23}$$

For $\beta\simeq20$ N/m, $\alpha_i\simeq10^{-39}$ F m$^2$. NaCl: $\varepsilon_r\simeq5.6$ at low frequency, 2.25 at optical frequencies.

9.4.3 Dipolar polarisability

A polar molecule has a permanent dipole. The energy at angle $\theta$ (Fig. 9.3) is

{{FIG:m4-f93}}
$$U=-\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{E}=-pE\cos\theta\tag{9.24}$$
$$\exp\left(-\frac{U}{kT}\right)d\Omega=\exp\left(\frac{pE\cos\theta}{kT}\right)2\pi\sin\theta\,d\theta\tag{9.25}$$
$$2\pi p\exp\left(\frac{pE\cos\theta}{kT}\right)\cos\theta\sin\theta\,d\theta\tag{9.26}$$
$$\bar p=\frac{P}{N}=\frac{p\int_0^\pi\exp\left(\frac{pE\cos\theta}{kT}\right)\cos\theta\sin\theta\,d\theta}{\int_0^\pi\exp\left(\frac{pE\cos\theta}{kT}\right)\sin\theta\,d\theta}\tag{9.27}$$
$$\frac{\bar p}{p}=\coth x-\frac{1}{x}=L(x)\tag{9.28}$$
$$L(x)\simeq\frac{x}{3}=\frac{pE}{3kT}\tag{9.29}$$
$$P=N\bar p=\frac{Np^2E}{3kT}\tag{9.30}$$
$$\alpha_d=\frac{\bar p}{E}=\frac{p^2}{3kT}\tag{9.31}$$

9.5 Frequency dependence of the total polarisability

$$\alpha=\alpha_e+\alpha_i+\alpha_d\tag{9.32}$$
{{FIG:m4-f94}}

The total falls with frequency. Relaxation times are longest for dipolar, then ionic, then electronic processes, so the dipolar part disappears first.

Worked Example 9.1

NaCl with $n=1.5$, $\varepsilon_r=5.6$. The static and optical Clausius–Mossotti relations are

$$\frac{\varepsilon_r-1}{\varepsilon_r+2}=\frac{N(\alpha_e+\alpha_i)}{3\varepsilon_0}\tag{9.40}$$
$$\frac{n^2-1}{n^2+2}=\frac{N\alpha_e}{3\varepsilon_0}\tag{9.41}$$

Dividing (9.41) by (9.40):

$$\left(\frac{n^2-1}{n^2+2}\right)\left(\frac{\varepsilon_r+2}{\varepsilon_r-1}\right)=\frac{\alpha_e}{\alpha_e+\alpha_i}\tag{9.42}$$

The ionic share is $[1-(9.42)]\times100=51.4\%$.
