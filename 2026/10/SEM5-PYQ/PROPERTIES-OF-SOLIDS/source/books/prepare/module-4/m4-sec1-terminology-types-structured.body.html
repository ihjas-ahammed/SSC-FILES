8.1 Magnetic terminology

The magnetisation $\mathbf{M}$ is the magnetic moment per unit volume. The susceptibility is

$$\chi=M/H\tag{8.1}$$

$\chi$ is a tensor in general and a scalar in isotropic media. It is dimensionless in SI units. The molar susceptibility $\chi_m$ refers to a gram molecule.

The flux density $\mathbf{B}$ in the medium is

$$\mathbf{B}=\mu_0(\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{M})\tag{8.2}$$
$$\mathbf{B}=\mu_0(1+\chi)\mathbf{H}\tag{8.3}$$
$$\mathbf{B}=\mu\mathbf{H}\tag{8.4}$$
$$\mu=\mu_0\mu_r\tag{8.5}$$
$$\mathbf{B}=\mu_0\mu_r\mathbf{H}\tag{8.6}$$
$$\mu_r=1+\chi\tag{8.7}$$

In free space $\chi=0$, $\mu_r=1$ and $\mathbf{B}=\mu_0\mathbf{H}$ (8.8).

$$\mathbf{B}=\mu_0\mathbf{H}\tag{8.8}$$

8.2 Types of magnetism

Magnetism in solids arises from the orbital and spin motion of electrons, and to a smaller extent from nuclear spins. The main contribution is the spin of unpaired valence electrons, which gives permanent electronic moments. The total moment depends on the number of unpaired electrons in each atom and on the relative orientation of neighbouring moments. Five classes are recognised: diamagnetism (induced, opposing the field, very weak), paramagnetism (permanent moments, aligned with the field, weak), ferromagnetism (parallel alignment of neighbours, very strong), antiferromagnetism (antiparallel and equal, so no net moment) and ferrimagnetism (antiparallel and unequal, so a net moment). Above the critical temperature all materials are paramagnetic.
