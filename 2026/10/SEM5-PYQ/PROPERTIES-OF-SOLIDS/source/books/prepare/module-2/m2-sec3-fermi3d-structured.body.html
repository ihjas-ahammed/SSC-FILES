Before you start

  • Volume of a sphere; counting states in $k$-space.
  • Pressure from energy: $P=-\partial E/\partial V$; bulk modulus $B=-V\partial P/\partial V$.
Plus Two recall: volume of a sphere

Recall. $V_{\text{sphere}}=\tfrac43\pi k^3$.

Re-derive.

  1. Each allowed $\mathbf k$ occupies $(2\pi/L)^3$.
  2. Number of $\mathbf k$-points in the sphere: $\tfrac43\pi k^3/(2\pi/L)^3$.
  3. Times 2 for spin: $\tfrac{L^3}{3\pi^2}k^3$ (Eq. 5.34).

Saved. Eq. (5.34).

5.2.2 Three-dimensional box

Schrödinger equation (5.19) in a cube of edge $L$; standing-wave solution (5.20), with travelling-wave form $\psi_{\mathbf k}=Ae^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}$ (5.21). Periodic boundary conditions (5.23) fix

$$k_x=\frac{2\pi n_x}{L},\qquad k_y=\frac{2\pi n_y}{L},\qquad k_z=\frac{2\pi n_z}{L}\tag{5.24}$$

with $k^2=2mE_k/\hbar^2$ (5.22) and $E_k=(\hbar^2/2m)(k_x^2+k_y^2+k_z^2)$ (5.25). Normalised wave function (5.27): $\psi_{\mathbf k}=L^{-3/2}e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}$. The state is labelled by $k_x,k_y,k_z,m_s$.

{{FIG:m2-s3-kspace}}

5.2.2.1 Filling of levels; Fermi sphere

At 0 K the occupied states fill a sphere of radius $k_F$ (Fig. 5.8). Counting (5.34): $\dfrac{L^3}{3\pi^2}k_F^3=N$. Then

$$k_F=\left(\frac{3\pi^2N}{V}\right)^{1/3}\tag{5.35}$$
$$E_F=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left(\frac{3\pi^2N}{V}\right)^{2/3}\tag{5.36}$$
$$N=\frac{V}{3\pi^2}\left(\frac{2mE_F}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}\tag{5.37}$$
$$v_F=\frac{\hbar}{m}\left(\frac{3\pi^2N}{V}\right)^{1/3}\tag{5.38}$$
{{FIG:m2-s3-occ3d}}

5.2.2.2 Density of states

From (5.39) and (5.37), $D(E)$ is parabolic in $E^{1/2}$:

$$D(E)=\frac{V}{2\pi^2}\left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}E^{1/2}\tag{5.40}$$
{{FIG:m2-s3-dos3d}}
$$D(E_F)=\frac{dN}{dE_F}=\frac{V}{2\pi^2}\left(\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}\right)^{3/2}E_F^{1/2}\tag{5.41}$$
$$\frac{dN}{N}=\frac32\frac{dE_F}{E_F}\tag{5.42}$$

Hence $D(E_F)$ is of the order of $N/E_F$.

5.2.2.3 Fermi function

$$N(E)=D(E)f(E)\tag{5.43}$$
$$N(E)\,dE=D(E)f(E)\,dE\tag{5.44}$$
$$f(E)=\frac{1}{\exp\!\left[\dfrac{E-E_F}{k_BT}\right]+1}\tag{5.28}$$
{{FIG:m2-s3-fermi}}

At 0 K: step function (5.29). At $E_F$: $f=\tfrac12$. Shift of $E_F$ with $T$ (5.30): $E_F\cong E_{F_0}[1-(\pi^2/12)(k_BT/E_{F_0})^2]$. Only $\sim k_BT$ near $E_F$ is affected.

$$E_F\cong E_{F_0}\left[1-\frac{\pi^2}{12}\left(\frac{k_BT}{E_{F_0}}\right)^2\right]\tag{5.30}$$

5.2.2.4 Parameters at 0 K

$$E_{F0}=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}(3\pi^2n)^{2/3}\tag{5.47}$$

Average kinetic energy: $E_0=\dfrac1N\int_0^{E_{F0}}E\,D(E)\,dE=\tfrac35E_{F0}$.

$$E_0=\tfrac35E_{F0}$$

5.3 Applications

5.3.1 Electronic specific heat

Classical: $E=\tfrac32Nk_BT$, $C_V=\tfrac32Nk_B$ (5.49–5.50), about $3$ cal g-atom$^{-1}$K$^{-1}$ electronic, contrary to observation (electronic part at most 1% at room temperature). Quantum: only electrons within $k_BT$ of $E_F$ contribute, a fraction $T/T_F$; the printed result is

$$C_V\cong\frac32Nk_B\left(\frac{T}{T_F}\right)\tag{5.51}$$

Proportional to $T$; zero at $T=0$; with $T=300$ K and $T_F=3000$ K, about 0.01 of the classical value.

5.3.2 Examples 5.1 and 5.2 (book)

Example 5.1: sodium, $r=1.86$ Å, bcc, $N/V=2.52\times10^{28}$ m$^{-3}$, $E_{F0}=3.11$ eV. Example 5.2: $P=\tfrac25NE_{F0}/V$ (5.53) and the pressure–volume law (5.52); $B=\tfrac53P$.

Result

3D gas: $k_F$ (5.35), $E_F$ (5.36), $D(E)$ (5.40), $E_0=\tfrac35E_F$, $C_V\cong\tfrac32Nk_B(T/T_F)$ (5.51).

Summary

  • (5.35)–(5.40) Fermi sphere and density of states; (5.28), (5.30) occupation; (5.51) specific heat.
