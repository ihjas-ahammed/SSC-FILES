Section B: 2 marks each, answer all
Curie–Weiss law

Total polarisability

Paramagnetism

Show that for $x\ll1$ the Langevin function is $L(x)\cong x/3$, and hence derive the Curie law $\chi_{para}=C/T$. (4 marks)

Origin of permanent moment and g-factor

Local field

Piezoelectricity

Section D: 10 marks each, answer any ONE
Weiss theory
15

Clausius–Mossotti relation and sources
16

Derive the Clausius–Mossotti relation $\dfrac{\varepsilon_r-1}{\varepsilon_r+2}=\dfrac{N\alpha}{3\varepsilon_o}$ from the local field. Explain the three sources of polarisability and how their contributions change with frequency. (10 marks)

Ferroelectricity
17

MODULE IV: MODEL ANSWERS
For checking your work. Each answer shows the key step and the result.

Section A. 1. $M=\chi H$ (book Eq. 8.1). 2. $\mu_r=1+\chi$ (book Eq. 8.7). 3. $\chi_{para}=C/T$ with $C=\mu_0N\mu^2/3k$ (book Eqs. 8.33, 8.34). 4. $M_s=NgJ\mu_B$ (book Eq. 8.49).

Section B. 5. $\chi=C/(T-T_C)$ with $C=\mu_0T_C/\lambda$ (book Eqs. 8.64, 8.65). 6. $T_N/\theta=(\beta-\alpha)/(\beta+\alpha)$ (book Eq. 8.82). 7. $(\varepsilon_r-1)/(\varepsilon_r+2)=N\alpha/3\varepsilon_o$ (book Eq. 9.12). 8. $\alpha=\alpha_e+\alpha_i+\alpha_d$ (electronic, ionic, dipolar; book Eq. 9.32).

Section C. 9. The orbital frequency changes by $eB/2m$ (Larmor). The induced moment is $\mu_a=-(Ze^2B/4m)\langle\rho^2\rangle$; with $\langle r^2\rangle=\tfrac32\langle\rho^2\rangle$ and $M=N\mu_a$ this gives Eq. 8.20 (book §8.3.1). 10. $L(x)=\coth x-1/x\cong x/3$ for small $x$ (Eq. 8.32). With $M\cong N\mu^2B/3kT$, $\chi=\mu_0N\mu^2/3kT=C/T$ (Eqs. 8.33, 8.34). 11. Origin: spin and orbital motion of unpaired electrons (book §8.2). $g=1+\dfrac{J(J+1)+S(S+1)-L(L+1)}{2J(J+1)}$ (Eq. 8.37); $g=2$ for pure spin. 12. Antiferromagnet: equal and opposite sublattice moments, cancelling (no net moment). Ferrimagnet: unequal opposite moments, so a net moment. Exchange integral negative in both, with AB interaction dominant (book §8.6, §8.7). 13. The applied field $\mathbf{E}_o$ plus the polarisation field $\mathbf{E}_p=-\mathbf{P}/3\varepsilon_o$ inside a cubic site, plus the field of the other polarised atoms (book Eq. 9.3 and §9.2). 14. Piezoelectricity: stress gives a dipole (Eq. 9.39). Needs no centre of inversion (Fig. 9.6). Quartz is piezoelectric but not ferroelectric (book §9.7).

Section D. 15. Molecular field $B_E=\lambda M$ (Eq. 8.52); $x=gJ\mu_B(B+\lambda M)/kT$ (Eq. 8.62); $\chi=C/(T-T_C)$ (Eq. 8.64), with $T_C$ from Eq. 8.66. 16. Eq. (9.10) and (9.12) from $P=N\alpha(E+P/3\varepsilon_o)$ (Eq. 9.9). Sources: electronic (resonance at $\omega_o$, $\alpha_e=e^2/m(\omega_o^2-\omega^2)$), ionic ($e^2/\beta$), dipolar ($p^2/3kT$); dipolar disappears first at high frequency (book §9.4, §9.5). 17. Cubic phase: centres of charge coincide. Tetragonal phase: Ti and Ba move up, O moves down, giving spontaneous polarisation (book §9.6, Fig. 9.5). Polarisation catastrophe when $\sum N_j\alpha_j=3\varepsilon_o$ (Eq. 9.34). Near $T_C$, $\varepsilon_r\cong\xi/(T-T_C)$ (Eq. 9.38).
