1What conditions must be satisfied to obtain a sustained interference pattern? Ghatak §14.3 Coherence, 14.5–14.6 [206–207] (sources with a constant phase difference, footnote on incoherent sources); §14.4. Not set out as a numbered list in Ghatak. Old paper: N21 Q13, N22 Q13. TheoryPartly: p. 206 in no cut; p. 207 in unit 7M2 Q5 ✓ Coherent sources (constant phase difference), same λ, nearly equal amplitudes, narrow sources close together, screen far away, same state of polarisation.
2Explain polarization of light. How does it prove that light is transverse? Book 2 §20.1–20.2, pp. 480–481 [1–2]. TheoryYes: unit 17Not as a question Restricting the vibrations to one plane. A longitudinal wave is the same in every direction perpendicular to its travel, so it cannot be polarised. Light can be (crossed polaroids or crystals cut it off), so its vibrations are transverse. Book 2 p. 481 [2].
3How does a zone plate differ from a convex lens? Ghatak §20.3 The Zone-Plate, 20.5 [330]: f = r₁²/λ, eq. (20.20), foci at f, f/3, f/5 … TheoryYes: unit 15M3 Q22 ✓ The zone plate works by diffraction and the lens by refraction. The zone plate has many foci and f ∝ 1/λ (red light focuses nearer, the opposite of a lens). Its image is less bright, and it has an extra phase difference.
4Differentiate between Fresnel and Fraunhofer diffraction. Ghatak §18.1, 18.4 [280] ("two categories"); §20.1, 20.1 [326]. TheoryYes: unit 12 (p. 280); §20.1 (p. 326) not in unit 15M3 Q1 ✓ Fresnel: source or screen at a finite distance, curved wavefronts, no lenses. Fraunhofer: both at infinity (lenses used), plane wavefronts, simple to analyse.
5Why do soap bubbles and oil films show beautiful colors? Ghatak §15.9 Colors of Thin Films, 15.18 [238]. TheoryYes: unit 11M2 Q17 ✓ Interference of light reflected from the top and bottom surfaces: 2nt cos r = (m+½)λ is bright for a different λ at each thickness and angle in white light.
6Distinguish between plane, circularly, elliptically polarized and unpolarized light. Book 2 §20.3 and §20.5.1–20.5.4, pp. 482–487 [3–8]. TheoryYes: unit 17M4 (PYQ N20 Q23) ✓ The path traced by the tip of E: a line; a circle (equal amplitudes, phase π/2); an ellipse; random and changing quickly (unpolarised).
7Describe how quarter wave and half wave plates are made. Book 2 §20.19.1–20.19.2, pp. 515–516 [36–37], eqs. (20.49), (20.51). TheoryYes: unit 21M4 Q18 (half-wave only) A doubly refracting plate cut with its optic axis parallel to the faces. Thickness d = λ/4(μ₀−μₑ) for a quarter-wave plate and λ/2(μ₀−μₑ) for a half-wave plate.
8What is optical path? Condition for constructive interference in terms of optical path difference. Ghatak §3.1–3.2, 3.3–3.5 [56–58] (optical path = Σ nᵢlᵢ); condition: §14.2, eq. (14.3), 14.3 [204]. TheoryYes: units 1 and 6M1 Q1 ✓ L = ∫n dl = c × time. Phase difference = (2π/λ₀)Δ. Constructive when Δ = mλ₀.
9Explain paraxial rays and the paraxial approximation. Ghatak §4.2, 4.2 [80]. TheoryYes: unit 2M1 Q8 ✓ Rays close to the axis making small angles: sin θ ≈ tan θ ≈ θ, cos θ ≈ 1. All such rays from a point meet at one image point.
10Obtain the Gaussian formula for a spherical refracting surface separating media n₁, n₂. Ghatak §4.2.2, eq. (4.5), 4.2–4.3 [80–81]. Old paper: N23 Q20 (2019 admission). TheoryYes: unit 2M1 (single spherical surface) ✓ n₂/v − n₁/u = (n₂−n₁)/R, from Fermat (or Snell) with the paraxial approximation and the Cartesian sign convention.

TheoryYes: unit 15M3 Q36, Q22 ✓ "Variable" means f depends on λ (f = r₁²/λ) and that there are many foci f/(2p−1). Prove the lens formula from the zone radii.

15Discuss Huygens' theory of double refraction in uniaxial crystals. Book 2 §20.11.1, p. 498 [19]; §20.14 Huygens' construction, pp. 504–507 [25–28]. TheoryYes: unit 20M4 Q15, Q41 ✓ O-wavelet is a sphere and the E-wavelet a spheroid; they touch along the optic axis. Negative crystal: oblate spheroid, outside. Positive crystal: prolate spheroid, inside. Draw the constructions for the different optic-axis orientations.
