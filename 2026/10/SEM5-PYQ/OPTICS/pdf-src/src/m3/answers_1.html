1PYQ N25 Q5

Fresnel vs Fraunhofer diffraction.

Know firstT1P2
FresnelSource and/or screen at finite distances from the aperture; incident wavefronts are spherical or cylindrical; no lenses needed; the pattern depends on the distances. FraunhoferSource and screen effectively at infinity; plane wavefronts fall on the aperture (realised with two convex lenses); the pattern is in the focal plane of the second lens.

N20 Q4 (fill in the blank: "source or screen or both at finite distances"): Fresnel.

2

Huygens–Fresnel principle.

Know firstT1P2

Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets, and the wavelets from different points mutually interfere. The disturbance at any point beyond is obtained by superposing (with their phases) all the secondary wavelets reaching that point.

3

Interference vs diffraction.

Know firstT1T5

Both are superposition of waves; there is no fundamental difference. Interference refers to superposition of waves from a few separate coherent sources (e.g. two slits), giving fringes of equal width and nearly equal intensity. Diffraction refers to superposition of wavelets from a continuous set of points of the same wavefront (one aperture), giving a pattern with a bright central maximum and rapidly weakening, unequal side bands.

4

Effect of slit width and wavelength.

Know firstT3

The minima are at $b\sin\theta = m\lambda$, so the central maximum has angular half-width $\lambda/b$. (a) A narrower slit (smaller $b$) gives a wider pattern, with the central maximum spreading out (and getting fainter). (b) A longer wavelength also widens the pattern: red light gives a wider pattern than blue.

5

Positions of secondary maxima.

Know firstT3M4

Setting $dI/d\beta = 0$ for $I = I_0\sin^2\beta/\beta^2$ gives $\sin\beta(\beta - \tan\beta) = 0$. The maxima satisfy $\tan\beta = \beta$, whose roots are $\beta = 1.43\pi, 2.46\pi, 3.47\pi, \ldots$, slightly less than $1.5\pi, 2.5\pi, \ldots$. They are not midway because the factor $1/\beta^2$ falls off with β, pulling each maximum towards the centre. Intensities are about 4.7%, 1.6%, 0.8% of $I_0$.

6

Sound bends round buildings, light does not.

Know firstT1

The spreading by diffraction is of angular size about $\lambda/b$, where $b$ is the size of the opening or obstacle. Sound wavelengths (about 1 m) are comparable with doors and buildings, so diffraction is large. Light wavelengths (about $5\times10^{-7}$ m) are millions of times smaller than everyday objects, so the bending is negligible and light appears to travel in straight lines.

7PYQ N19 Q7 · adapted

Circular aperture gives concentric rings?

Know firstT4

True. Fraunhofer diffraction by a circular aperture gives a bright central disc (Airy disc) surrounded by alternate dark and bright concentric rings of rapidly decreasing intensity. The first dark ring is at $\sin\theta = 1.22\lambda/D$.

8PYQ N19 Q12

Loudspeaker diameter for directionality.

Know firstT4T1

Sound from the speaker spreads by diffraction over an angle $\theta \approx 1.22\lambda/D$ ($D$ = diameter). To get a narrow, directional beam we need small θ, so the diameter should be large compared with the wavelength of the sound. (That is why high-frequency tweeters, with short λ, can be small, while directional speakers for low frequencies are large.)

9

Missing orders.

Know firstT5

In the double-slit pattern, interference maxima occur at $d\sin\theta = m\lambda$ and diffraction minima at $b\sin\theta = p\lambda$. If both hold for the same θ, the interference maximum falls on a diffraction zero and is absent: a missing order. Condition: $\dfrac db = \dfrac mp$. E.g. $d = 2b$: orders 2, 4, 6, … are missing.

10PYQ N22 Q10 · adapted

Principal maximum sharper as N increases?

Know firstT6T7

True. The angular half-width of a principal maximum is $\Delta\theta = \dfrac{\lambda}{Nd\cos\theta}$, which decreases as N increases, while the peak intensity grows as $N^2$. (So the N19 Q6 statement "sharper as N decreases" is false.)

11

Minima and secondary maxima between principal maxima; N² intensity.

Know firstT6

Minima occur at $N\gamma = p\pi$ with $p \ne 0, N, 2N, \ldots$, so between two adjacent principal maxima there are $N-1$ minima and hence $N-2$ secondary maxima. At a principal maximum ($d\sin\theta = m\lambda$) the waves from all N slits arrive in phase, so the amplitudes add to $N$ times one slit's; intensity ∝ amplitude² ∝ $N^2$.

12PYQ N20 Q15

Explain a diffraction grating.

Know firstT8

A diffraction grating is a large number of equidistant, parallel, narrow slits of equal width, usually made by ruling fine lines (several thousand per cm) on glass. The distance $d$ between corresponding points of neighbouring slits is the grating element (= 1 ÷ number of lines per unit length). Light of wavelength λ gives sharp principal maxima in directions $d\sin\theta = m\lambda$, so a grating disperses light into spectra of different orders and is used to measure wavelengths.

13PYQ N19 Q9 · adapted

Larger grating element, larger dispersion?

Know firstT8

False. Angular dispersion $\dfrac{d\theta}{d\lambda} = \dfrac{m}{d\cos\theta}$ is inversely proportional to the grating element $d$. A larger $d$ (fewer lines per cm) gives smaller dispersion.

14PYQ N22 Q15

Overlapping spectra.

Know firstT8

Since $d\sin\theta = m\lambda$, the m-th order of a wavelength $\lambda_2$ falls at the same angle as the (m+1)-th order of $\lambda_1$ if $m\lambda_2 = (m+1)\lambda_1$. Then spectra of adjacent orders overlap. For visible light (400–700 nm) the first and second orders do not overlap (2 × 400 = 800 nm > 700 nm), but the second and third do (3 × 400 = 1200 < 2 × 700 = 1400 nm). Filters are used to remove the unwanted order.

15PYQ N20 Q7 · adapted

Grating spectra purer than prism spectra?

Know firstT8T9

True. A grating with many lines gives very sharp principal maxima (half-width $\lambda/Nd\cos\theta$) and high resolving power ($mN$), so the spectral lines are narrow and well separated; the dispersion is also nearly uniform (normal spectrum). A prism's spectrum depends on how $n$ varies with λ, is non-linear, and has much lower resolving power.

16PYQ N24 Q8

Equation for resolving power of a grating.

Know firstT9
$$R = \frac{\lambda}{d\lambda} = mN$$

where $m$ is the order of the spectrum and $N$ the total number of lines illuminated. (N22 Q5 fill-in: the same equation.)

17

Rayleigh's criterion.

Know firstT9T4

Two spectral lines (or two point objects) of equal intensity are just resolved when the principal maximum of one falls exactly on the first minimum of the other. Then the combined intensity curve shows a dip (to about 81% for slit patterns) between the two peaks, and the eye sees two lines.

Difference in areas of nth and (n−1)th zones.

Know firstT10
$r_n^2 = \left(d+\frac{n\lambda}{2}\right)^2 - d^2 = n\lambda d + \frac{n^2\lambda^2}{4}$
Area of zone n: $A_n = \pi(r_n^2 - r_{n-1}^2) = \pi\lambda d + \dfrac{\pi\lambda^2}{4}(2n-1)$
$A_n - A_{n-1} = \dfrac{\pi\lambda^2}{4}\times2 = \dfrac{\pi\lambda^2}{2}$

20

Whole wavefront = half the first zone.

Know firstT10

The resultant amplitude is $u = u_1 - u_2 + u_3 - \cdots$, the $u_n$ decreasing slowly. Write $u = \frac{u_1}{2} + \left(\frac{u_1}2 - u_2 + \frac{u_3}2\right) + \left(\frac{u_3}{2} - u_4 + \frac{u_5}{2}\right) + \cdots$. Each $u_n$ is very nearly the average of its neighbours, so each bracket is nearly zero, and the last term is negligible. Hence $u \approx u_1/2$: the whole wavefront produces half the amplitude (a quarter of the intensity) of the first zone alone.

21

Bright centre of a disc's shadow.

Know firstT11T10

A small opaque disc covering the first p half-period zones (as seen from a point P on the axis) leaves the rest uncovered. Their resultant is $u_{p+1} - u_{p+2} + \cdots \approx \frac{u_{p+1}}{2}$, which is not zero; for a small disc $u_{p+1}$ is almost as large as $u_1$. So the centre of the shadow is bright (Poisson's spot), as Arago observed.

22

Zone plate vs convex lens.

Know firstT12
SimilarityBoth focus light and obey $\frac1a+\frac1b=\frac1f$. PrincipleZone plate: diffraction. Lens: refraction. FociZone plate: many ($f$, $f/3$, $f/5$, …). Lens: one. ColourZone plate $f = r_1^2/\lambda$: shorter for red. Lens: shorter for violet. BrightnessZone plate wastes half the light (blocked zones).
23

Intensity at the edge of the geometrical shadow.

Know firstT13

It is one quarter of the unobstructed intensity, $I_0/4$. At the edge ($v_0 = 0$) exactly half of the wavefront (one half of the Cornu spiral, from the centre O to $Z^+$) is effective, so the amplitude is half the unobstructed amplitude (chord OZ⁺ = ½ chord Z⁻Z⁺), and intensity ∝ amplitude² gives $I_0/4$.

24

Cornu spiral and its end points.

Know firstT13M3

The Cornu spiral is the curve traced by the point $(C(v), S(v))$, where $C(v) = \int_0^v\cos\frac{\pi u^2}{2}du$ and $S(v) = \int_0^v\sin\frac{\pi u^2}{2}du$ are Fresnel's integrals. It is a double spiral through the origin. Its end points $Z^+(\frac12, \frac12)$ and $Z^-(-\frac12, -\frac12)$ correspond to $v = +\infty$ and $v = -\infty$, i.e. the contributions of strips infinitely far on either side. The chord $Z^-Z^+$ represents the unobstructed wave; a chord from a point $v_0$ to $Z^+$ gives the amplitude behind a straight edge.
