1PYQ N24 Q10

Define polarization.

Know firstT1P1P2

Polarisation is the phenomenon by which the vibrations of a transverse wave (for light, the electric field vector) are restricted to a particular direction, or a definite pattern, in the plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation. Light in which the electric vector vibrates along a single fixed direction is called plane (linearly) polarised.

2

Plane of vibration vs plane of polarisation.

Know firstT1

The plane of vibration is the plane containing the direction of vibration of the electric vector and the direction of propagation. The plane of polarisation is the plane perpendicular to the plane of vibration, also containing the direction of propagation (it contains the magnetic vector).

3

Why light but not sound can be polarised.

Know firstP1T1

Polarisation means selecting one direction of vibration perpendicular to the direction of travel, which is possible only for transverse waves. Light is transverse (E ⟂ direction of propagation), so it can be polarised. Sound in air is longitudinal (vibrations along the direction of travel), so there is no transverse direction to select.

4

Unpolarised light and its representation.

Know firstT1

In unpolarised light the electric vector vibrates in all directions perpendicular to the direction of propagation, changing randomly and very rapidly (about $10^8$ times a second), with no preferred direction. It is represented by a star of arrows in the transverse plane, or, in side view, by equal numbers of short lines (vibrations in the page) and dots (vibrations perpendicular to the page).

5

State Malus' law.

Know firstT2

When completely plane polarised light falls on an analyser, the intensity of the transmitted light is proportional to the square of the cosine of the angle θ between the plane of vibration of the incident light and the pass axis of the analyser: $I = I_0\cos^2\theta$.

7

Polaroid and its principle.

Know firstT3

A Polaroid is a thin sheet that produces plane polarised light. It contains long-chain molecules (polyvinyl alcohol with iodine) stretched so that they are aligned. It works by dichroism (selective absorption): the component of $\vec E$ parallel to the chains is absorbed because electrons can move along the chains; the perpendicular component is transmitted. The emergent light is plane polarised, along the pass axis (perpendicular to the chains).

8

Polarisation by scattering at 90°.

Know firstT3P2

The electric field of the incident light makes the electrons of the molecules oscillate in the plane perpendicular to the beam. An oscillating charge radiates nothing along its line of oscillation. Seen at 90° to the beam, the oscillation component along the line of sight sends nothing to the observer; only the component perpendicular to both the beam and the line of sight is seen. So the scattered light is plane polarised.

9PYQ N20 Q17

Brewster's law; Brewster window.

Know firstT4

Brewster's law: the tangent of the polarising angle equals the refractive index of the reflecting medium: $\tan i_B = n$. At this angle the reflected light is completely plane polarised (vibrations perpendicular to the plane of incidence) and the reflected and refracted rays are at right angles.

Brewster window: a glass plate set at the Brewster angle at the ends of a gas laser tube. Light vibrating in the plane of incidence passes through with no reflection loss, so the laser output is plane polarised and losses are minimised.

11PYQ N19 Q8 · adapted

Are both O and E rays plane polarised?

Know firstT5

True. In double refraction both the ordinary and extraordinary rays are plane polarised, and their planes of vibration are perpendicular to each other: the E-ray vibrates in the principal section and the O-ray perpendicular to it. (Check: viewed through an analyser, the two images become dark alternately every 90°.)

12

Optic axis; uniaxial and biaxial.

Know firstT5

The optic axis is a direction in a doubly refracting crystal along which the ordinary and extraordinary rays travel with the same velocity, so a ray along it is not doubly refracted. Crystals with one such direction (calcite, quartz, tourmaline) are uniaxial; those with two (mica, topaz) are biaxial.

13PYQ N22 Q9 · adapted

Negative crystal: is v_o < v_e?

Know firstT5T6

True. In a negative crystal (calcite) $\mu_e < \mu_o$, so the E-ray travels faster than the O-ray in all directions except along the optic axis, where both are equal. The E-wavelet (ellipsoid) lies outside the O-wavelet (sphere), touching it on the optic axis.

14

Principal section.

Know firstT5

A principal section of a crystal is a plane containing the optic axis and perpendicular to a pair of opposite faces. When the plane of incidence is a principal section, the E-ray vibrates in the principal section and the O-ray vibrates perpendicular to it.

15PYQ N24 Q9

Huygens' explanation of double refraction.

Know firstT6P3

According to Huygens, every point of a wavefront entering a doubly refracting crystal gives rise to two secondary wavelets: a spherical wavelet for the ordinary wave (same speed in all directions) and an ellipsoidal wavelet (ellipsoid of revolution about the optic axis) for the extraordinary wave. The two touch along the optic axis. The common tangent surfaces to the spheres and to the ellipsoids give the O and E wavefronts, so two refracted rays are produced.

16

Optic axis perpendicular to the faces, normal incidence.

Know firstT6

Only one image is seen: there is no double refraction. The light travels along the optic axis, where the spherical and ellipsoidal wavelets touch, so the O and E wavefronts coincide and both waves travel with the same speed $c/\mu_o$ in the same direction.

17

Elimination of the O-ray in a Nicol prism.

Know firstT7P3

The O-ray travels from calcite ($\mu_o = 1.658$) to Canada balsam ($\mu = 1.55$), i.e. from denser to rarer. Its angle of incidence at the balsam layer is greater than the critical angle, so it is totally internally reflected to the blackened side and absorbed. The E-ray ($\mu_e = 1.486 < 1.55$) cannot undergo total internal reflection and is transmitted.

$\sin C = \dfrac{1.55}{1.658} = 0.935$, so $C = 69.2^\circ$
C ≈ 69°
18PYQ N20 Q16

What is a half-wave plate?

Know firstT9

A half-wave plate is a plate of doubly refracting crystal, cut with its optic axis parallel to its faces, of such thickness that it introduces a path difference of λ/2 (phase difference π) between the O and E waves: $d = \dfrac{\lambda}{2|\mu_o - \mu_e|}$. It turns plane polarised light at angle φ to its axis into plane polarised light at −φ (a rotation of 2φ), and reverses the sense of circularly polarised light.

19

Quarter-wave plate and its thickness.

Know firstT9

A quarter-wave plate is a doubly refracting plate, optic axis parallel to its faces, that introduces a path difference of λ/4 (phase difference π/2) between the O and E waves. From $(\mu_o-\mu_e)d = \lambda/4$:

$$d = \frac{\lambda}{4|\mu_o-\mu_e|}$$

It converts plane polarised light into circularly or elliptically polarised light, and vice versa.

20

Plane polarised at 45° on a QWP and on a HWP.

Know firstT9T8

The incident vibration splits into equal O and E components ($E_0\cos45^\circ = E_0\sin45^\circ$). QWP: they emerge with phase difference π/2 and equal amplitudes, so the light is circularly polarised. HWP: phase difference π, so the light is plane polarised, with the plane rotated through 2 × 45° = 90°.

21

Nicol prism vs Polaroid.

Know firstT7T3
PrincipleNicol: double refraction + total internal reflection. Polaroid: selective absorption (dichroism). QualityNicol: almost perfect polarisation. Polaroid: slightly imperfect, a little coloured. Size, costNicol: small aperture, costly (calcite). Polaroid: large sheets, cheap. FieldNicol: narrow (about 28°). Polaroid: wide.
22

Right vs left circular light.

Know firstT8

In circularly polarised light the tip of $\vec E$ moves on a circle. If, looking towards the oncoming light, it rotates clockwise, the light is right circularly polarised; anticlockwise, left circularly polarised. With $E_x = a\cos\omega t$, $E_y = a\cos(\omega t - \delta)$: δ = π/2 gives right and δ = 3π/2 (−π/2) gives left (Ghatak's convention). A half-wave plate changes one into the other.

23PYQ N25 Q7

Optical activity.

Know firstT12

Optical activity is the rotation of the plane of vibration of plane polarised light as it passes through certain substances (quartz along its optic axis, sugar solution, turpentine). Substances rotating it clockwise (looking towards the source) are dextro-rotatory; anticlockwise, laevo-rotatory. The rotation is proportional to the thickness (path length) and, for solutions, to the concentration; it depends on wavelength and temperature. Fresnel explained it by the different speeds of right and left circular components.

24PYQ N20 Q5

Define specific rotation.

Know firstT12

Specific rotation of an optically active substance, at a given temperature and wavelength, is the rotation produced by a 1 decimetre length of its solution of concentration 1 g/cm³:

$$[\alpha]_\lambda^T = \frac{\theta}{l\,c}\quad(l \text{ in dm},\ c\text{ in g/cm}^3)$$
