UNIT 3 · GHATAK §4.4–4.5, 4.7

The thin lens

Two curved surfaces, very close together. Apply T7 twice.

T11

Thin lens formula and lens-maker's formula

Where $\frac1v-\frac1u=\frac1f$ and $\frac1f=(n-1)(\frac1{R_1}-\frac1{R_2})$ come from.

Result · definition of a thin lens (PYQ N24 Q1) A lens is a piece of transparent material bounded by two spherical surfaces. It is thin when its thickness is negligible compared with the object distance, the image distance and the radii of curvature. Then all distances can be measured from one point, the centre of the lens.

Signs of $R_1$, $R_2$ (light from the left)

Biconvex$R_1 > 0$, $R_2 < 0$ Biconcave$R_1 < 0$, $R_2 > 0$ Meniscusboth the same sign (both + or both −) Plano-convexflat side has $R = \infty$, so $1/R = 0$

Rule: R is + if that surface's centre is on the right (P4).

{{fig:thin_lens_two_surfaces}}

Lens of index $n_2$ in a medium of index $n_1$. Object O at distance $u$.

Surface 1 alone (from $n_1$ into $n_2$): $\dfrac{n_2}{v'} - \dfrac{n_1}{u} = \dfrac{n_2-n_1}{R_1}$T7 formula. $v'$ is where surface 1 alone would form the image $I_1$ (Ghatak 4.8).
Surface 2 (from $n_2$ back into $n_1$), object $I_1$ at $v'$: $\dfrac{n_1}{v} - \dfrac{n_2}{v'} = \dfrac{n_1-n_2}{R_2}$Same T7 formula, with three swaps: its object distance "u" is $v'$; the light now comes from $n_2$ and goes into $n_1$; its radius is $R_2$. The lens is thin, so $v'$ is measured from the same point (Ghatak 4.9).
Add the two equations: $\dfrac{n_1}{v} - \dfrac{n_1}{u} = (n_2 - n_1)\left(\dfrac1{R_1} - \dfrac1{R_2}\right)$The $\frac{n_2}{v'}$ terms cancel, so the middle image disappears.
$\dfrac1v - \dfrac1u = (n-1)\left(\dfrac1{R_1}-\dfrac1{R_2}\right)$,   where $n = \dfrac{n_2}{n_1}$Divide by $n_1$. $n$ is the index of the lens relative to its surroundings (Ghatak 4.10).
Result · thin lens (Ghatak 4.11, 4.12) $$\frac1v - \frac1u = \frac1f,\qquad \frac1f = (n-1)\left(\frac1{R_1}-\frac1{R_2}\right)$$

The second formula is the lens-maker's formula. For a lens in air, $n$ is just the glass index.

Worked example (PYQ N25 Q13 type)

Biconvex lens, $n=1.5$, radii 50 cm and 100 cm, in air.

$R_1 = +50$, $R_2 = -100$Biconvex signs.
$\dfrac1f = 0.5\left(\dfrac1{50} + \dfrac1{100}\right) = 0.5\times0.03 = 0.015$, so $f = 66.7$ cmNote $-\frac{1}{R_2} = -\frac{1}{-100} = +\frac1{100}$.
Real object, image wanted 60 cm away: since $60 < f$, the image must be on the same side as the object (virtual), so $v = -60$A converging lens with a real object gives a real image only beyond $f$ (P3 table).
$\dfrac1u = \dfrac1v - \dfrac1f = -\dfrac1{60} - 0.015 = -0.03167$, so $u = -31.6$ cmObject 31.6 cm in front of the lens: inside $F_1$, magnifier case. ✓
Exam trapLosing the sign in $\left(\frac1{R_1}-\frac1{R_2}\right)$. For a biconvex lens the two terms add.

T12

Principal foci and focal length

The two special points of a lens, and what changes when the lens is put in water.

{{fig:principal_foci}}
Result · definitions (PYQ N24 Q2)

First principal focus $F_1$: the point on the axis such that rays starting from it (or heading towards it) come out of the lens parallel to the axis. Its image is at infinity.

Second principal focus $F_2$: the point where rays coming in parallel to the axis meet after the lens (or seem to come from, for a diverging lens).

Focal lengths: $f_1$ = distance of $F_1$ from the lens, $f_2$ = distance of $F_2$, both with signs.

Lens with different media on the two sides (Ghatak §4.5)

Medium $n_1$ on the left, lens $n_2$, medium $n_3$ on the right. Adding the two surface equations as in T11 gives:

$\dfrac{n_3}{v} - \dfrac{n_1}{u} = \dfrac{n_2-n_1}{R_1} + \dfrac{n_3-n_2}{R_2}$Ghatak 4.14. Call the right-hand side $D$.
$F_1$: put $v=\infty$, $u=f_1$:   $f_1 = -\dfrac{n_1}{D}$Ghatak 4.15.
$F_2$: put $u=-\infty$, $v=f_2$:   $f_2 = \dfrac{n_3}{D}$Ghatak 4.16.
Same medium both sides ($n_3 = n_1$):   $f_2 = -f_1 = f$Ghatak 4.13. So for a lens in air $F_1$ and $F_2$ are the same distance $f$ from the lens, on opposite sides.
{{fig:concave_foci}}
Converging or diverging? $f>0$ (so $f_2>0$, $f_1<0$): converging. $f<0$: diverging. In air, a biconvex glass lens has $n-1>0$ and $\frac1{R_1}-\frac1{R_2}>0$, so it converges.

Glass lens in water (PYQ N25 Q1)

{{fig:lens_in_water}}
Relative index $n = \dfrac{n_{glass}}{n_{water}} = \dfrac{1.5}{1.33} = 1.128$In T11, $n = n_2/n_1$: lens relative to its surroundings.
$n - 1 = 0.128 > 0$, and $\left(\frac1{R_1}-\frac1{R_2}\right) > 0$ for biconvex, so $f > 0$It is still converging.
$\dfrac{f_{water}}{f_{air}} = \dfrac{1.5-1}{1.128-1} = \dfrac{0.5}{0.128} \approx 3.9$The curvature factor cancels. The focal length becomes about 4 times longer, so the lens is much weaker.
Exam trapIf the lens is less dense than the surroundings ($n_{lens} < n_{medium}$, e.g. an air bubble in water, or glass of 1.5 in a liquid of 1.6), then $n<1$: a biconvex lens diverges and a biconcave one converges.
Remind me · power (Plus Two)$P = \dfrac1f$ with $f$ in metres; unit dioptre (D). Converging: $P>0$. Diverging: $P<0$. Example: $f=-25$ cm gives $P = 1/(-0.25) = -4$ D. (Not in Ghatak's sections, but useful.)

T13

Finding the image by drawing

Three easy rays. Use them to sketch every answer and to check your signs.

{{fig:ray_construction}}
Result · three rules (Ghatak §4.7)
  1. A ray parallel to the axis goes through $F_2$ (or seems to come from $F_2$ for a diverging lens).
  2. A ray through the centre of the lens goes straight on, undeviated.
  3. A ray through $F_1$ (or heading to $F_1$) comes out parallel to the axis.
Any two of them locate the top of the image.
Why the middle ray is straightNear its centre a thin lens is like a thin parallel slab of glass. A slab only shifts a ray sideways, and for a thin lens that shift is negligible.
Check yourself
Object inside $F_1$ of a convex lens: where do rays 1 and 2 meet?
They spread out after the lens and do not meet. Extended backwards they meet on the object's side: a virtual, upright, bigger image.

In the model paper: Q19, Q38.

UNIT 4 · GHATAK §4.6–4.7

Newton's formula and magnification

T14

Newton's formula

The lens formula again, with distances measured from the foci instead of the lens.

{{fig:newton}} {{fig:newton_signs}}

Object of height $h$ is a distance $|x_1|$ to the left of $F_1$. Image of height $h'$ (below the axis) is $|x_2|$ to the right of $F_2$. The lens is in air, so both focal distances are $f$.

Step A: two pairs of similar triangles (sizes only)

Blue pair, meeting at $F_1$: $\dfrac{|h|}{|x_1|} = \dfrac{|h'|}{f}$The ray through $F_1$. One triangle has the object as one side, the other has the lens (height $|h'|$, because the ray then goes parallel at the image's height).
Green pair, meeting at $F_2$: $\dfrac{|h|}{f} = \dfrac{|h'|}{|x_2|}$The parallel ray: it leaves the lens at height $|h|$ and passes through $F_2$ to the image top.
Both give $\dfrac{|h'|}{|h|}$:   $\dfrac{f}{|x_1|} = \dfrac{|x_2|}{f}$   so   $|x_1|\,|x_2| = f^2$Set the two expressions for the size ratio equal.

Step B: signs

Object left of $F_1$: $x_1 < 0$. Image right of $F_2$: $x_2 > 0$Coordinates measured from each focus, with the usual sign rule.
$x_1\,x_2 = -f^2$   (general form: $x_1x_2 = f_1f_2$)The product of a negative and a positive number. With $f_1 = -f$, $f_2 = f$: $f_1f_2 = -f^2$ (Ghatak 4.19, 4.20).
Result · Newton's formula $$x_1\,x_2 = f_1 f_2 = -f^2$$

(lens in air, Cartesian signs)

In sizes only (as in many guides, and your old notes): $|x_1|\,|x_2| = f^2$. If a question writes $x_1x_2 = f^2$, it means sizes. Say so in one line, then continue.

What it tells you$x_1$ and $x_2$ have opposite signs. If the object is to the left of $F_1$, the image is to the right of $F_2$. Move the object closer to $F_1$ ($|x_1|$ smaller) and the image runs away ($|x_2|$ larger).
Check with numbers: $f = 75$ cm, object at $u=-100$ cm. Lens formula: $\frac1v = \frac1{75} - \frac1{100}$, so $v = +300$ cmGhatak problem 4.1.
$x_1 = u - f_1 = -100 - (-75) = -25$,   $x_2 = v - f_2 = 300 - 75 = +225$Distance from the focus = (distance from the lens) − (focus position).
$x_1x_2 = -25\times225 = -5625 = -(75)^2$ ✓Newton agrees with the lens formula.
Exam trap$x_1$ and $x_2$ are measured from the foci, not from the lens.

In the model paper: Q36, Q21.

T15

Lateral magnification

How big is the image, and is it upside down?

Result · definition (PYQ N24 Q3) Lateral (transverse) magnification is the ratio of the image height to the object height, both measured perpendicular to the axis with signs: $$m = \frac{h'}{h}$$ $m>0$: upright (erect). $m<0$: inverted. $|m|>1$: bigger. $|m|<1$: smaller.

For one refracting surface (Ghatak 4.23)

{{fig:magnification_surface}}
Ray from the object top to the pole P. Angles: $\theta_1 \approx \dfrac{h}{-u}$,   $\theta_2 \approx \dfrac{-h'}{v}$Small angles (M1). $-u$ and $-h'$ are positive sizes, because $u<0$ and the image is inverted.
$n_1\theta_1 = n_2\theta_2$:   $n_1\dfrac{h}{-u} = n_2\dfrac{-h'}{v}$Snell at P (where the normal is the axis itself), small angles.
$m = \dfrac{h'}{h} = \dfrac{n_1 v}{n_2 u}$Rearrange. The minus signs cancel.

For a thin lens: multiply the two surfaces (Ghatak 4.24)

$m_1 = \dfrac{n_1 v'}{n_2 u}$,   $m_2 = \dfrac{n_2 v}{n_1 v'}$Surface formula, used twice. For surface 2 the media are swapped and its object is at $v'$.
$m = m_1 m_2 = \dfrac vu$The final image is $m_2$ times the middle image, which is $m_1$ times the object. $v'$, $n_1$, $n_2$ all cancel.
Using Newton: $m = -\dfrac{f_1}{x_1} = -\dfrac{x_2}{f_2}$, i.e. $m = \dfrac{f}{x_1} = -\dfrac{x_2}{f}$ in airFrom the similar triangles of T14, with signs (Ghatak 4.21).
MirrorPut $n_2 = -n_1$ in $m = \frac{n_1v}{n_2u}$: $m = -\dfrac vu$. That is the Plus Two mirror formula.
Result · all magnification formulas
Surface$m = \dfrac{n_1 v}{n_2 u}$ Thin lens$m = \dfrac vu = \dfrac f{x_1} = -\dfrac{x_2}f$ Mirror$m = -\dfrac vu$
Bonus: longitudinal magnification How much the image moves along the axis when the object moves a little. Differentiate $\frac1v - \frac1u = \frac1f$: $\;-\frac{dv}{v^2} + \frac{du}{u^2} = 0$, so $\dfrac{dv}{du} = \dfrac{v^2}{u^2} = m^2$. It is always positive: the image moves the same way as the object.
Check yourself
1. $f = 75$ cm, $u=-100$, $v = +300$. Find $m$ in two ways.
$m = v/u = -3$. Also $m = f/x_1 = 75/(-25) = -3$. ✓ Inverted, 3 times bigger.
2. $f=+20$ cm, object at $u=-12$ cm. Image and $m$?
$\frac1v = \frac1{20}-\frac1{12} = -\frac1{30}$, so $v=-30$ cm, and $m = \frac{-30}{-12} = +2.5$: virtual, upright, 2.5 times bigger (a magnifier).
