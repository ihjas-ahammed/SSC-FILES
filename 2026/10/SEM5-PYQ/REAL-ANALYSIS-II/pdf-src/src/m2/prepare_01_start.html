One idea for the whole module

The derivative is a slope that is the limit of chord slopes

Take a point c on a graph. Join it to a nearby point x by a straight line (a chord). Its slope is the difference quotient $\dfrac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}$. Slide x towards c. If these slopes settle down to one number, that number is the derivative $f'(c)$.

Said another way: near c, the graph is best approximated by a straight line of slope $f'(c)$, and the error shrinks faster than the distance $|x-c|$ does.

The chain that carries the module

Everything after the definition hangs on one chain:

interior maximum ⇒ slope 0 (T10) ⇒ Rolle (T11) ⇒ Mean Value Theorem (T12) ⇒ constant, increasing, extrema, inequalities, roots, Darboux (T13–T24).

The Mean Value Theorem says that a smooth arc from A to B has a point where the tangent is parallel to the chord AB. Whenever you know something about $f'$ everywhere on an interval, the MVT turns it into something about $f$.

The whole module in one story

The idea

1. The derivative at c is the limit of chord slopes $\frac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}$; where it exists, f is continuous, and the usual rules (sum, product, quotient, chain) follow from limit laws and Carathéodory's trick (T01–T06).

2. Some functions are differentiable but badly behaved: $|x|$ has a corner, and $x^2\sin(1/x)$ is differentiable everywhere with a derivative that is not continuous (T07–T09).

3. At a peak or valley inside an interval the slope is zero (T10); with the Max–Min Theorem this gives Rolle (T11) and then the Mean Value Theorem (T12).

4. From the MVT: zero derivative means constant, positive derivative means increasing, and a sign change of $f'$ marks an extremum (T13–T15); also roots, estimates and inequalities (T16–T21).

5. Derivatives cannot jump: Darboux says $f'$ takes every value between two of its values, so signum is not a derivative of anything (T22–T24).

The hypotheses to check every time

For Rolle (T11) and the MVT (T12)
