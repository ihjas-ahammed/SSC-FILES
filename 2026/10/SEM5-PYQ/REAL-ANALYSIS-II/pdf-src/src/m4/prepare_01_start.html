One idea for the whole module

The ε-tube

A sequence of functions is a film: f1, f2, f3, … are frames, each a whole graph.

Pointwise: stand at one x and watch the height fn(x) settle down. Every x settles, but some may take much longer than others.

Uniform: draw a thin tube round the final graph f. From some frame on, the whole graph of fn lies inside the tube, however thin the tube.

Every question in this module asks: which of these two happens, and what does it buy us?

The whole module in one story

The idea

1. Fix x and let n run: that gives the pointwise limit f(x). Work it out case by case (T1, T2).

2. Ask for one K that works for all x at once: that is uniform convergence. To show it fails, find points xn where fn stays far from f (T3, T4).

3. Measure the worst gap with the uniform norm ‖fn − f‖. Uniform ⇔ worst gap → 0. Find the worst gap with calculus or a bound (T5–T9).

4. The Cauchy criterion tests uniform convergence without knowing f (T10).

5. Pointwise limits can destroy continuity and spoil integrals; uniform limits of continuous functions stay continuous (T11–T15).
