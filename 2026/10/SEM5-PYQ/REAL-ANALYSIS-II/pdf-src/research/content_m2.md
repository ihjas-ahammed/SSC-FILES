## 2. PREREQUISITES P## AND MATHS TOOLS M##
P01 Plus Two: derivative as slope of tangent / rate of change; difference quotient $\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$; tangent line $y-f(c)=f'(c)(x-c)$. Gap: Plus Two uses $h$, B uses $x\to c$ ($h=x-c$, same thing).
P02 Plus Two rules (assumed known, quote in exam): sum, product, quotient, chain; $D x^n=nx^{n-1}$, $\sin'=\cos$, $\cos'=-\sin$, $\tan'=\sec^2$, $(e^x)'=e^x$, $(\ln x)'=1/x$, $(x^r)'=rx^{r-1}$. Teach: derive nothing new, use freely; B proves rules for the sake of rigour.
P03 Graph shapes: $x^2,x^3,|x|,\operatorname{sgn}x,1/x,\sqrt x,x^{1/3},\sin x,e^x,\ln x$ (for the figures).
P04 Real Analysis I limits: (4.1) limit of a function at a cluster point, $\varepsilon$-$\delta$; one-sided limits; (Thm 4.2.4) limit laws; (Thm 4.2.6) order: $f\ge0$ near c and $\lim f$ exists => $\lim f\ge0$; (Thm 4.2.9) local sign: $\lim f=L>0$ => $f>0$ on a punctured neighbourhood; (Thm 4.1.8) sequential criterion; squeeze.
P05 Sequences: $x_n\to c$, $x_n\ne c$; special sequences $1/n$, $1/(n\pi)$, $1/(2n\pi)$, $1/((2n+1)\pi)$; limits of $n\{\cdot\}$.
P06 Continuity (Module 1): $f$ continuous at $c$ iff $\lim_{x\to c}f(x)=f(c)$; sums/products/composites of continuous functions are continuous; continuity of $|x|$, $\sin$, polynomials.
P07 Module 1 big theorems: Max-Min Thm 5.3.4 (continuous on $[a,b]$ attains max and min), Boundedness 5.3.2, Bolzano IVT 5.3.7, location of roots 5.3.5; Lipschitz Def 5.4.4.
P08 Intervals/neighbourhoods: interior point, $V_\delta(c)=(c-\delta,c+\delta)$, open vs closed interval; "interior point" excludes endpoints.
P09 Inequalities and absolute value: $|ab|=|a||b|$, $|a|<\varepsilon\iff-\varepsilon<a<\varepsilon$, triangle inequality, sign rules for products, strict vs non-strict.
P10 Piecewise-defined functions, evaluating at joins; sgn function $\operatorname{sgn}x=1,0,-1$.
P11 Trig values: $|\sin|\le1$, $|\cos|\le1$, $\sin n\pi=0$, $\cos2n\pi=1$, $\cos(2n+1)\pi=-1$.
P12 Exponential/logarithm: $e^0=1$, $e^x>1$ for $x>0$, $e^x<1$ for $x<0$, increasing; $\ln1=0$, $\ln$ increasing.
P13 Quadratic skills: roots, discriminant $b^2-4ac$ (sign of a quadratic that never vanishes), sign chart of product $x(x-1)(x-2)$.
M01 Limit of a difference quotient: write $\frac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}$, simplify until the factor $x-c$ cancels, then substitute $x=c$. Micro: $x^2$: $\frac{x^2-c^2}{x-c}=x+c\to2c$.
M02 Factorising: $x^n-c^n=(x-c)(x^{n-1}+x^{n-2}c+\cdots+c^{n-1})$; rationalising $\sqrt x-\sqrt c=\frac{x-c}{\sqrt x+\sqrt c}$; common denominator for $\frac1x-\frac1c=\frac{c-x}{xc}$. Micro: $\frac{\sqrt x-\sqrt c}{x-c}=\frac1{\sqrt x+\sqrt c}\to\frac1{2\sqrt c}$.
M03 One-sided limits and one-sided derivatives: $f'(c)$ exists iff left and right difference-quotient limits exist and are equal. Micro: $|x|/x$: $+1$ from the right, $-1$ from the left. Always compute both sides at a join of a piecewise function.
M04 Algebra of limits + squeeze: $\lim(fg)=\lim f\cdot\lim g$; "bounded times zero is zero": $|x\sin(1/x)|\le|x|\to0$. Never apply the product law when one factor has no limit without a squeeze.
M05 Sign analysis of $f'$: solve $f'=0$, mark the points on a number line, test a point in each interval, write sign pattern; then "increasing/decreasing on $[\cdot,\cdot]$" (include endpoints: B's definition is non-strict).
M06 Inequality from MVT: (i) write $f(b)-f(a)=f'(c)(b-a)$; (ii) bound $f'(c)$ using $a<c<b$ (monotone derivative or $|\cdot|\le1$); (iii) multiply by $b-a>0$ keeping direction; (iv) strict if the bound on $f'(c)$ is strict; (v) treat $x=a$ and the other side separately.
M07 Non-existence of a limit via two sequences: find $x_n\to c$, $y_n\to c$ with $f(x_n)\to A$, $f(y_n)\to B$, $A\ne B$.
M08 Composite bookkeeping: name inner $f$ and outer $g$; compute $g'$ at $f(x)$ (not at $x$) then multiply by $f'(x)$. Micro: $\sqrt{x^2-2x+5}$: $f=x^2-2x+5$, $g=\sqrt y$.
M09 Defining a function by cases and checking continuity at the join: $\varphi(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}$ for $x\ne c$, $\varphi(c)=f'(c)$: the whole point is whether the two descriptions meet (limit equals chosen value).
M10 Reading "there exists $c$" statements and proof by contradiction; sign of product $(x-c)\cdot q$ with $q>0$.

## 3. PROOF SKELETONS S## (faithful to Bartle; WHY after each step; [10] = 10-mark, [6] = 6-mark, [3] = short)
S01 [6] Thm 6.1.2 differentiable => continuous (K02)
 1 For $x\ne c$: $f(x)-f(c)=\frac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}\,(x-c)$. WHY: multiply and divide by $x-c\ne0$ (algebra).
 2 Take $\lim_{x\to c}$: product of limits $f'(c)\cdot0=0$. WHY: limit theorem 4.2.4, the first factor has limit $f'(c)$ (hypothesis), the second limit $0$.
 3 So $\lim f(x)=f(c)$. WHY: $\lim(f(x)-f(c))=0$.
 4 Hence $f$ continuous at $c$. WHY: definition 5.1.1. Add: converse false, $|x|$ at 0: quotient $|x|/x=\pm1$ one-sided, limit absent (C5).
S02 [6] Thm 6.1.3(c),(d) product, quotient (K03); (a),(b) are one-liners.
 (a)(b) $\frac{(\alpha f)(x)-(\alpha f)(c)}{x-c}=\alpha\frac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}$; sum splits the quotient. WHY: limit laws.
 (c) 1 For $x\ne c$: $\frac{f(x)g(x)-f(c)g(c)}{x-c}=\frac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}g(x)+f(c)\frac{g(x)-g(c)}{x-c}$. WHY: add and subtract $f(c)g(x)$ (the key trick). 2 $g$ continuous at $c$ (S01) so $g(x)\to g(c)$. WHY: the first term's second factor needs a limit. 3 Limit laws give $f'(c)g(c)+f(c)g'(c)$. WHY: Thm 4.2.4.
 (d) 1 $g$ continuous at $c$ and $g(c)\ne0$ => $g\ne0$ on a neighbourhood $J$ of $c$. WHY: Thm 4.2.9 (so $f/g$ is defined near $c$). 2 For $x\in J$, $x\ne c$: $\frac{f(x)/g(x)-f(c)/g(c)}{x-c}=\frac1{g(x)g(c)}\Big[\frac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}g(c)-f(c)\frac{g(x)-g(c)}{x-c}\Big]$. WHY: common denominator, add and subtract $f(c)g(c)$. 3 Let $x\to c$ using continuity of $g$: $\frac{f'(c)g(c)-f(c)g'(c)}{g(c)^2}$. WHY: limit laws.
S03 [3] Cor 6.1.4 (K04): induction on $n$. Base $n=1$ trivial; step: $(f_1\cdots f_{n+1})'=((f_1\cdots f_n)f_{n+1})'$, apply product rule and hypothesis. WHY: product rule is the case $n=2$. Special case all $f_i=f$: $(f^n)'=n f^{n-1}f'$; $f(x)=x$: $Dx^n=nx^{n-1}$.

WHY: this is the only candidate (the difference quotient). 2 $\varphi$ continuous at $c$: $\lim_{x\to c}\varphi(x)=f'(c)=\varphi(c)$. WHY: definition of $f'(c)$. 3 $f(x)-f(c)=\varphi(x)(x-c)$: for $x\ne c$ multiply; for $x=c$ both sides $0$. WHY: check every $x\in I$, including $c$.
 (<=) 1 Given $\varphi$ continuous at $c$ with (10). Divide by $x-c\ne0$: $\frac{f(x)-f(c)}{x-c}=\varphi(x)$. WHY: $x\ne c$. 2 $\lim_{x\to c}\varphi(x)=\varphi(c)$. WHY: continuity at $c$. 3 So $f'(c)$ exists and equals $\varphi(c)$. WHY: definition 6.1.1 (limit of the quotient exists).
 Illustration: $x^3-c^3=(x^2+cx+c^2)(x-c)$, $\varphi(x)=x^2+cx+c^2$ continuous, $f'(c)=3c^2$.

WHY: Caratheodory (=>). 2 With $d=f(c)$: $g$ differentiable at $d$ => $\exists\psi$ on $I$, continuous at $d$, $g(y)-g(d)=\psi(y)(y-d)$, $\psi(d)=g'(d)$. Pedagogy misconception lines verified: "f'(c)>0 => f increasing near c" false (E14, $g(x)=x+2x^2\sin(1/x)$; pedagogy's $x/2+x^2\sin(1/x)$ also works: $f'(x)=\frac12+2x\sin(1/x)-\cos(1/x)$ equals $-\frac12$ at $x=\frac1{2n\pi}$) [OK]. "f' continuous automatically" false (C6) [OK]. "xi is the midpoint/unique" false (E11, E12) [OK].
New traps:
N1 Naive chain rule divides by $f(x)-f(c)$: undefined if $f(x)=f(c)$ at points arbitrarily close to $c$ (e.g. $x^2\sin(1/x)$ at 0). Fix: Caratheodory.
N2 Calculational rules at a point where an inner piece is undefined: $x^2\sin(1/x)$ at 0 must be done from the definition.
N3 Piecewise functions at the join: first check continuity (CALC-24 Q13: $1\ne4$), then compare one-sided derivatives (E29b: 3 vs 1). Differentiable => continuous, never conversely.
N4 Differentiable at ONE point only: $f=x^2$ on $\mathbb Q$, $0$ off $\mathbb Q$ (Ex 6.1.4): $f'(0)=0$; discontinuous elsewhere so differentiable only at 0.
N5 $f'=0$ does not give constancy on a non-interval: $0$ on $(0,1)$, $1$ on $(2,3)$.
N6 $f'>0$ on a non-interval does not give increasing: $f(x)=-1/x$ on $\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$, $f'=1/x^2>0$ but $f(-1)=1>f(1)=-1$.
N7 Endpoints are not interior: Interior Extremum / Rolle need interior; extremum of $x$ on $[0,1]$ at endpoints with $f'=1$.
N8 Critical point is only a candidate: $x^3$ at 0 ($f'=0$ no extremum); $|x|$ at 0 ($f'$ absent, still min). "Critical points" in CALC-24 Q10 include points where $f'$ is undefined (none here).
N9 First Derivative Test converse false (E15): extremum without sign-stable $f'$ on either side.
N10 Darboux converse false/continuity: $f'$ has IVP but need not be continuous (C6); derivatives cannot have jump discontinuities (sgn not a derivative); a function with IVP need not be a derivative in general (not needed, do not claim).
N11 Quotient rule requires $g(c)\ne0$; product rule is not $(fg)'=f'g'$; derivative of $|f|$ at a zero of $f$ needs $f'(c)=0$.
N12 MVT conclusion "$f(b)-f(a)=f'(c)(b-a)$" holds for SOME $c$; inequalities require a bound on $f'$ over the whole open interval; strict inequality needs a strict bound.
N13 MVT for $\sin$: $|\sin x-\sin y|\le|x-y|$ is the Lipschitz statement, not $<$ (equality at $x=y$; strict for $x\ne y$ is true but needs more).
N14 Increasing in Bartle is non-strict (constant function is both increasing and decreasing).
N15 "Rolle proof uses Max-Min thm 5.3.4": forgetting closed bounded interval breaks it (open interval/non-continuous).
N16 Bartle uses $\operatorname{Arcsin}$ etc (practicum only).
