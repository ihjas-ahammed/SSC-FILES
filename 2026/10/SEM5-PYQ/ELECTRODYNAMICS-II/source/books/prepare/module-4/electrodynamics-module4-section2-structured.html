12.3 Growth and decay of charge in a C-R circuit

Source: book-sources/m4-s2-cr-lc.html, Murugeshan pp. 315–317 (printed). Syllabus items 15 and 16. Equations (a)(1)–(a)(3) and (b)(1)–(b)(3) are given in full.

Prerequisites

  • $Q=CV$ and the p.d. across a capacitor (pre-physics P3).
  • First-order equations solved by separation of variables (pre-math M4).

1. Charging

Recall (Plus Two) $Q=CV$, so the p.d. across the capacitor is $Q/C$. The emf is shared between the capacitor and the resistor in series.

A capacitor $C$ and a resistance $R$ are connected to an emf $E$ through a key (Fig. 12.6). $Q$ denotes the charge on $C$ at time $t$, and $I=dQ/dt$.

$$E = \frac{Q}{C} + RI\tag{12.3(a)(1)}$$

2. Final charge

At the maximum charge $Q_0$, $dQ/dt=0$ and $E=Q_0/C$.

3. Variables separated

$$\begin{aligned}(Q_0 - Q) &= CR\frac{dQ}{dt}\\ \frac{dQ}{Q_0 - Q} &= \frac{dt}{CR}\end{aligned}\tag{12.3(a)(2)}$$

4. Solution

With $Q=0$ at $t=0$,

$$Q = Q_0\left(1 - e^{-t/CR}\right)\tag{12.3(a)(3)}$$

The time constant is $CR$. At $t=CR$ the charge is $0.632\,Q_0$.

5. Discharging

With $E=0$ and the capacitor charged to $Q_0$,

$$R\frac{dQ}{dt} + \frac{Q}{C} = 0\tag{12.3(b)(1)}$$
$$Q = Q_0 e^{-t/CR}\tag{12.3(b)(2)}$$
$$\begin{aligned}I &= \frac{dQ}{dt} = -\frac{Q_0}{CR}e^{-t/CR}\\ &= -\frac{Q}{CR}\end{aligned}\tag{12.3(b)(3)}$$

At $t=CR$, $Q = 0.368\,Q_0$; the time constant is also the time to fall to $1/e$.

6. Worked examples (printed pp. 316–317)

(1) With $R=2\ \text{M}\Omega$ and $t=0.5$ s for $Q/Q_0=3/4$, Eq. (a)(3) gives $C=0.18\ \mu$F. (2) For $C=0.1\ \mu$F and $R=10\ \text{M}\Omega$, the p.d. halves in $CR\ln 2=0.6931$ s.

12.4 Measurement of high resistance by leakage (background)

Background. Outside the Module 4 items (15–22). Kept because it applies the discharge law of §12.3. The book numbers no equations in this section. Source: book-sources/m4-s2-cr-lc.html, Murugeshan pp. 318–319 (printed).

1. Principle

A capacitor of capacitance $C$ and initial charge $Q_0$ discharges through a resistance $R$ for a time $t$. From §12.3 the remaining charge is $Q = Q_0e^{-t/CR}$, so

$$R = \frac{t}{C\log_e(Q_0/Q)} = \frac{t}{2.3026\,C\log_{10}(Q_0/Q)}$$

A high $R$ gives a slow discharge. Measuring $Q_0/Q$ then determines $R$.

2. Circuit and procedure

$C$ is a capacitor of known value, $R$ the high resistance, B.G. a ballistic galvanometer, $E$ a cell, and $K_1$, $K_2$, $K_3$ tap keys (Fig. 12.8).

(i) The capacitor is charged through $K_1$ and then discharged through the galvanometer, giving a throw $\theta_0\propto Q_0$. (ii) The capacitor is charged again, allowed to leak through $R$ for a time $t$, and then discharged through the galvanometer, giving a throw $\theta\propto Q$. Since $Q_0/Q = \theta_0/\theta$,

$$R = \frac{t}{2.3026\,C\log_{10}(\theta_0/\theta)}$$

3. Graph

$\log_{10}(\theta_0/\theta)$ plotted against $t$ is a straight line through the origin (Fig. 12.9). Its slope gives $t/\log_{10}(\theta_0/\theta)$, which with $C$ known gives $R$.

4. Worked examples (printed pp. 318–319)

(1) $C=2\ \mu$F, $R=100\ \text{M}\Omega$, $Q_0/Q=2$: $t=2.3026\,CR\log_{10}2=138.7$ s. (2) $C=0.2\ \mu$F, throws of 12 cm and 6 cm, $t=2$ s: $R=14.43\ \text{M}\Omega$.

12.5 Charging and discharging of a capacitor through an inductance

Source: book-sources/m4-s2-cr-lc.html, Murugeshan pp. 319–321 (printed). Syllabus items 15 and 16. Charging equations are tagged (i)(1)–(4) and discharge equations (ii)(1)–(4), following the book’s separate numbering. Section 12.6 onwards is not included.

Prerequisites

  • Charge and p.d. of a capacitor; self-inductance and back emf (pre-physics P2, P3).
  • Simple harmonic motion (pre-math H2).

1. Charging

Let $C$ and $L$ be joined to an emf $E$ through a key (Fig. 12.10), and $Q$ the charge on $C$ at time $t$.

$$L\frac{dI}{dt} + \frac{Q}{C} = E\tag{12.5(i)(1)}$$

With $I=dQ/dt$, and $Q_0=CE$, this becomes

$$\begin{aligned}L\frac{d^2Q}{dt^2} + \frac{Q}{C} &= E\\ \frac{d^2Q}{dt^2} + \frac{1}{LC}(Q - Q_0) &= 0\end{aligned}\tag{12.5(i)(2)}$$

which is simple harmonic motion with period

$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{LC}\tag{12.5(i)(3)}$$
$$\nu = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}\tag{12.5(i)(4)}$$

2. Discharge

With no external emf (Fig. 12.11),

$$\begin{aligned}\frac{Q}{C} + L\frac{dI}{dt} &= 0\\ \frac{d^2Q}{dt^2} + \frac{Q}{LC} &= 0\end{aligned}\tag{12.5(ii)(1)}$$

whose general solution, with $\omega=1/\sqrt{LC}$, is

$$Q = A\sin(\omega t+\theta)\tag{12.5(ii)(2)}$$

With $Q=Q_0$ at $t=0$ and $\theta=\pi/2$,

$$Q = Q_0\sin\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{LC}} + \frac{\pi}{2}\right)\tag{12.5(ii)(3)}$$
$$\nu = \frac{\omega}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}\tag{12.5(ii)(4)}$$

3. Energy

The energy alternates between the electric field of $C$ and the magnetic field of $L$.
