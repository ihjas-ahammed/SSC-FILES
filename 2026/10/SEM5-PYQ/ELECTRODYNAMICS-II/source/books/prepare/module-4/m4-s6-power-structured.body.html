13.5 Power in an AC circuit

Source: book-sources/m4-s6-power.html, Murugeshan pp. 347–351 (printed), with Examples 1–5. Syllabus item 22. §13.6 (choke coil) is outside this module; its heading only is shown. Equations (1) and (2) and the Example 2 equations are tagged with the book’s numbers.

Prerequisites

  • Power in a DC circuit (pre-physics P8).
  • Product-to-sum identities and the average of $\cos^2$ over a cycle (pre-math M5, M7).

1. Instantaneous power

Recall (Plus Two) $P=VI=I^2R$ for DC (Plus Two). For AC the average power carries the factor $\cos\phi$.
$$E\times I = \frac{1}{2}E_0I_0\left[\cos\phi - \cos(2\omega t - \phi)\right]\tag{13.5(1)}$$

2. Average power

$$P = \frac{1}{2}E_0I_0\cos\phi = E_{rms}I_{rms}\cos\phi\tag{13.5(2)}$$

The apparent power is $E_{rms}I_{rms}$. The power factor is $\cos\phi$, which equals the true power divided by the apparent power.

{{FIG:m4-s6-pf-triangle}}

3. Special cases

Purely resistive: $\cos\phi=1$. Purely inductive or purely capacitive: $\cos\phi=0$ and the true power is zero.

4. Wattless current

The current is wattless when no power is consumed. A pure inductor or capacitor, with no resistance, draws a wattless current; this is the principle of the choke coil. The component $I\cos\phi$ gives the power and $I\sin\phi$ does not.

5. Worked examples

Example 1 (printed p. 348): $\cos\phi=0.062$; power 0.00189 W. Example 2 (printed pp. 348–349): $R=16.7\ \Omega$, $L=37.3$ mH, from the equations tagged 13.5(ex2)(1)–(5). Example 3 (printed pp. 350–351): power component 0.20 A, wattless component 0.87 A. Example 4 (printed p. 351): wattmeter reading 514.5 W. Example 5 (printed p. 351): no power is dissipated in a pure inductor.

Example 2 equations

$$\frac{50^2}{[R^2+(100\pi)^2L^2]}R = 100\ \text{W}\tag{13.5(ex2)(1)}$$
$$\frac{50^2R}{[R^2+(200\pi)^2L^2]} = 50\ \text{W}\tag{13.5(ex2)(2)}$$
$$2500\,R = 100\left[R^2+10^4\pi^2L^2\right]\tag{13.5(ex2)(3)}$$
$$2500\,R = 50\left[R^2+4\times10^4\pi^2L^2\right]\tag{13.5(ex2)(4)}$$
$$\begin{aligned}R^2 &= 2\times10^4\times\pi^2L^2\\ R &= 100\sqrt2\,\pi L\end{aligned}\tag{13.5(ex2)(5)}$$
