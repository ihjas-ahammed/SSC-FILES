9.2.2 Monochromatic plane waves

Source: book-sources/m3-sec3-plane.html, Griffiths pp. 402–406. Equations 9.44–9.53, Table 9.1 and Example 9.2 in full. Problem 9.9 not worked. Figures redrawn.

Prerequisites

  • Complex exponentials, real part (§9.1.2).
  • Faraday’s law (Eq. 9.40, iii); dot and cross products (pre-math M8–M10).

1. Definitions

Monochromatic: a single frequency $\omega$. Plane wave: uniform over each plane perpendicular to the direction of travel $z$ (Fig. 9.9). Then

$$\tilde{\mathbf E}(z,t)=\tilde{\mathbf E}_0e^{i(kz-\omega t)},\qquad\tilde{\mathbf B}(z,t)=\tilde{\mathbf B}_0e^{i(kz-\omega t)}\tag{9.44}$$

with $\omega=ck$.

Table 9.1 (book), the electromagnetic spectrum, is reproduced in the study version.

2. Transversality and amplitude relations

Substituting Eq. 9.44 into $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=0$ and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$:

$$(\tilde E_0)_z=(\tilde B_0)_z=0\tag{9.45}$$

Faraday’s law then gives

$$-k(\tilde E_0)_y=\omega(\tilde B_0)_x,\qquad k(\tilde E_0)_x=\omega(\tilde B_0)_y\tag{9.46}$$
$$\tilde{\mathbf B}_0=\frac{k}{\omega}(\hat z\times\tilde{\mathbf E}_0)\tag{9.47}$$
Plus Two recall: transverse, in-phase fields

Recall. From Class 12: $\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf B$ are in phase, mutually perpendicular, and perpendicular to the direction of travel.

Re-derive.

  1. Take $\tilde{\mathbf E}_0=E_0\hat x$. By Eq. 9.47, $\tilde{\mathbf B}_0=(k/\omega)E_0(\hat z\times\hat x)=(k/\omega)E_0\hat y$.
  2. Faraday’s law gives $kE_0=\omega B_0$.
  3. Both fields carry the phase $kz-\omega t$, so they are in phase.

Saved. $B_0=\dfrac{k}{\omega}E_0=\dfrac{E_0}{c}$ Eq. 9.48.

Result 9.8: amplitude relation
$$B_0=\frac{k}{\omega}E_0=\frac{1}{c}E_0\tag{9.48}$$

3. Example 9.2

For $\mathbf E$ along $x$, $\mathbf B$ is along $y$. Real fields:

$$\begin{aligned}\mathbf E(z,t)&=E_0\cos(kz-\omega t+\delta)\hat x,\\ \mathbf B(z,t)&=\frac1cE_0\cos(kz-\omega t+\delta)\hat y\end{aligned}\tag{9.49}$$

Polarization is named by the direction of $\mathbf E$ (Fig. 9.10).

4. Propagation vector

For direction $\hat k$, the propagation vector $\mathbf k=k\hat k$ and the phase $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r$ (Fig. 9.11):

$$\tilde{\mathbf E}(\mathbf r,t)=\tilde E_0e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)}\hat n,\qquad\tilde{\mathbf B}(\mathbf r,t)=\frac1c\tilde E_0e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)}(\hat k\times\hat n)=\frac1c\hat k\times\tilde{\mathbf E}\tag{9.50}$$

Transversality: $\hat n\cdot\hat k=0$ Eq. 9.51. Real fields:

$$\mathbf E(\mathbf r,t)=E_0\cos(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t+\delta)\hat n\tag{9.52}$$
$$\mathbf B(\mathbf r,t)=\frac1cE_0\cos(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t+\delta)(\hat k\times\hat n)\tag{9.53}$$
