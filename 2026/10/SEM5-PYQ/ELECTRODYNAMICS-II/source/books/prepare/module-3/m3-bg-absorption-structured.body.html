9.4 Absorption and dispersion (background)

Source: book-sources/m3-sec4-oblique.html (p. 418), m3-bg-absorption.html, Griffiths pp. 418–430. Background, beyond the syllabus list. Equations 9.119–9.176 in full. Problems 9.20–9.27 listed, not worked.

Prerequisites

  • Complex wave numbers and the real part (§9.1.2).
  • Ohm’s law (Module 1 and Plus Two current electricity).

1. Conductors (§9.4.1)

$$\mathbf J_f=\sigma\mathbf E\tag{9.119}$$
$$\text{(i)}\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf E=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\rho_f,\qquad\text{(iii)}\ \nabla\times\mathbf E=-\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t},$$
$$\text{(ii)}\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,\qquad\text{(iv)}\ \nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu\sigma\mathbf E+\mu\epsilon\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t}\tag{9.120}$$

Continuity of free charge gives $\nabla\cdot\mathbf J_f=-\partial\rho_f/\partial t$ Eq. 9.121, and with Ohm’s and Gauss’s laws

$$\rho_f(t)=e^{-(\sigma/\epsilon)t}\rho_f(0)\tag{9.122}$$

so free charge relaxes to zero in $\tau=\epsilon/\sigma$ (footnote 20). Then $\rho_f=0$ and

$$\text{(i)}\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf E=0,\qquad\text{(iii)}\ \nabla\times\mathbf E=-\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t},$$
$$\text{(ii)}\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,\qquad\text{(iv)}\ \nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu\epsilon\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t}+\mu\sigma\mathbf E\tag{9.123}$$
Result 9.16: modified wave equations
$$\nabla^2\mathbf E=\mu\epsilon\frac{\partial^2\mathbf E}{\partial t^2}+\mu\sigma\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t},\qquad\nabla^2\mathbf B=\mu\epsilon\frac{\partial^2\mathbf B}{\partial t^2}+\mu\sigma\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t}\tag{9.124}$$

Plane-wave solutions $\tilde{\mathbf E}=\tilde{\mathbf E}_0e^{i(\tilde kz-\omega t)}$ Eq. 9.125 require $\tilde k^2=\mu\epsilon\omega^2+i\mu\sigma\omega$ Eq. 9.126. With $\tilde k=k+i\kappa$ Eq. 9.127:

$$k\equiv\omega\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon\mu}{2}}\left[\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon\omega}\right)^2}+1\right]^{1/2},\qquad\kappa\equiv\omega\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon\mu}{2}}\left[\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon\omega}\right)^2}-1\right]^{1/2}\tag{9.128}$$

Attenuated fields Eq. 9.129, skin depth $d=1/\kappa$ Eq. 9.130, and

$$\lambda=\frac{2\pi}{k},\qquad v=\frac{\omega}{k},\qquad n=\frac{ck}{\omega}\tag{9.131}$$

With $\mathbf E$ along $x$ Eq. 9.132, $\tilde{\mathbf B}=(\tilde k/\omega)\tilde E_0e^{-\kappa z}e^{i(kz-\omega t)}\hat y$ Eq. 9.133. Polar form $\tilde k=Ke^{i\phi}$ Eq. 9.134, with

$$K\equiv|\tilde k|=\omega\sqrt{\epsilon\mu\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon\omega}\right)^2}} \tag{9.135}$$
$$\phi\equiv\tan^{-1}(\kappa/k)\tag{9.136}$$

Hence the complex amplitudes are related by $B_0e^{i\delta_B}=(Ke^{i\phi}/\omega)E_0e^{i\delta_E}$ Eq. 9.137, and the phase difference and amplitude ratio are

$$\delta_B-\delta_E=\phi\tag{9.138}$$
$$\frac{B_0}{E_0}=\frac{K}{\omega}=\sqrt{\epsilon\mu\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon\omega}\right)^2}}\tag{9.139}$$
Result 9.17: real fields in a conductor
$$\mathbf E(z,t)=E_0e^{-\kappa z}\cos(kz-\omega t+\delta_E)\hat x,\qquad\mathbf B(z,t)=B_0e^{-\kappa z}\cos(kz-\omega t+\delta_E+\phi)\hat y\tag{9.140}$$

2. Reflection at a conducting surface (§9.4.2)

$$\text{(i)}\ \epsilon_1E_1^\perp-\epsilon_2E_2^\perp=\sigma_f,\qquad\text{(iii)}\ \mathbf E_1^\parallel-\mathbf E_2^\parallel=0,$$
$$\text{(ii)}\ B_1^\perp-B_2^\perp=0,\qquad\text{(iv)}\ \frac{1}{\mu_1}\mathbf B_1^\parallel-\frac{1}{\mu_2}\mathbf B_2^\parallel=\mathbf K_f\times\hat{\mathbf n}\tag{9.141}$$
$$\tilde{\mathbf E}_I=\tilde E_{0I}e^{i(k_1z-\omega t)}\hat x,\qquad\tilde{\mathbf B}_I=\frac{1}{v_1}\tilde E_{0I}e^{i(k_1z-\omega t)}\hat y\tag{9.142}$$
$$\tilde{\mathbf E}_R=\tilde E_{0R}e^{i(-k_1z-\omega t)}\hat x,\qquad\tilde{\mathbf B}_R=-\frac{1}{v_1}\tilde E_{0R}e^{i(-k_1z-\omega t)}\hat y\tag{9.143}$$
$$\tilde{\mathbf E}_T=\tilde E_{0T}e^{i(\tilde k_2z-\omega t)}\hat x,\qquad\tilde{\mathbf B}_T=\frac{\tilde k_2}{\omega}\tilde E_{0T}e^{i(\tilde k_2z-\omega t)}\hat y\tag{9.144}$$
$$\tilde E_{0I}+\tilde E_{0R}=\tilde E_{0T}\tag{9.145}$$
$$\frac{1}{\mu_1v_1}(\tilde E_{0I}-\tilde E_{0R})-\frac{\tilde k_2}{\mu_2\omega}\tilde E_{0T}=0\tag{9.146}$$
$$\tilde E_{0I}-\tilde E_{0R}=\tilde\beta\tilde E_{0T},\qquad\tilde\beta\equiv\frac{\mu_1v_1}{\mu_2\omega}\tilde k_2\tag{9.147}$$
$$\tilde E_{0R}=\left(\frac{1-\tilde\beta}{1+\tilde\beta}\right)\tilde E_{0I},\qquad\tilde E_{0T}=\left(\frac{2}{1+\tilde\beta}\right)\tilde E_{0I}\tag{9.149}$$

Here $\tilde\beta$ is defined by Eq. 9.148. For a perfect conductor, $\tilde E_{0R}=-\tilde E_{0I}$ and $\tilde E_{0T}=0$ Eq. 9.150. Problem 9.23 is outside these notes.

3. Dispersion (§9.4.3)

$$v=\frac{\omega}{k}\tag{9.151}$$
$$v_g=\frac{d\omega}{dk}\tag{9.152}$$

Model: spring force $-m\omega_0^2x$ Eq. 9.153, damping $-m\gamma\,dx/dt$ Eq. 9.154, driving $qE_0\cos\omega t$ Eq. 9.155, equation of motion Eq. 9.156. Complex steady state Eq. 9.157 with $\tilde x=\tilde x_0e^{-i\omega t}$ Eq. 9.158 gives

$$\tilde x_0=\frac{q/m}{\omega_0^2-\omega^2-i\gamma\omega}E_0\tag{9.159}$$
$$\tilde p(t)=q\tilde x(t)=\frac{q^2/m}{\omega_0^2-\omega^2-i\gamma\omega}E_0e^{-i\omega t}\tag{9.160}$$

Summing over electrons of each type with $N$ molecules per unit volume:

$$\tilde{\mathbf P}=\frac{Nq^2}{m}\left(\sum_j\frac{f_j}{\omega_j^2-\omega^2-i\gamma_j\omega}\right)\tilde{\mathbf E}\tag{9.161}$$
$$\tilde{\mathbf P}=\epsilon_0\tilde\chi_e\tilde{\mathbf E}\tag{9.162}$$
$$\tilde\epsilon_r=\frac{\tilde\epsilon}{\epsilon_0}=1+\frac{Nq^2}{m\epsilon_0}\sum_j\frac{f_j}{\omega_j^2-\omega^2-i\gamma_j\omega}\tag{9.163}$$
$$\nabla^2\tilde{\mathbf E}=\tilde\epsilon\mu_0\frac{\partial^2\tilde{\mathbf E}}{\partial t^2}\tag{9.164}$$
$$\tilde{\mathbf E}(z,t)=\tilde{\mathbf E}_0e^{i(\tilde kz-\omega t)}\tag{9.165}$$
$$\tilde k\equiv\sqrt{\tilde\epsilon\mu_0}\,\omega\tag{9.166}$$
$$\tilde k=k+i\kappa\tag{9.167}$$
$$\tilde{\mathbf E}(z,t)=\tilde{\mathbf E}_0e^{-\kappa z}e^{i(kz-\omega t)}\tag{9.168}$$
$$\alpha\equiv2\kappa\tag{9.169}$$
$$n=\frac{ck}{\omega}\tag{9.170}$$

For gases, $\sqrt{1+\varepsilon}\approx1+\tfrac12\varepsilon$ gives

$$\tilde k=\frac{\omega}{c}\sqrt{\tilde\epsilon_r}\approx\frac{\omega}{c}\left[1+\frac{Nq^2}{2m\epsilon_0}\sum_j\frac{f_j}{\omega_j^2-\omega^2-i\gamma_j\omega}\right]\tag{9.171}$$
$$n\approx1+\frac{Nq^2}{2m\epsilon_0}\sum_j\frac{f_j(\omega_j^2-\omega^2)}{(\omega_j^2-\omega^2)^2+\gamma_j^2\omega^2}\tag{9.172}$$
$$\alpha\approx\frac{Nq^2\omega^2}{m\epsilon_0c}\sum_j\frac{f_j\gamma_j}{(\omega_j^2-\omega^2)^2+\gamma_j^2\omega^2}\tag{9.173}$$

Normal dispersion (index rising with $\omega$) holds away from resonance; anomalous dispersion occurs near each resonance (Fig. 9.22). With damping neglected and $\omega<\omega_j$:

$$n=1+\frac{Nq^2}{2m\epsilon_0}\sum_j\frac{f_j}{\omega_j^2-\omega^2}\tag{9.174}$$
$$n=1+\left(\frac{Nq^2}{2m\epsilon_0}\sum_j\frac{f_j}{\omega_j^2}\right)+\omega^2\left(\frac{Nq^2}{2m\epsilon_0}\sum_j\frac{f_j}{\omega_j^4}\right)\tag{9.175}$$
Result 9.18: Cauchy’s formula
$$n=1+A\left(1+\frac{B}{\lambda^2}\right)\tag{9.176}$$

$A$ is the coefficient of refraction and $B$ the coefficient of dispersion. Problems 9.24–9.27 are outside these notes.
