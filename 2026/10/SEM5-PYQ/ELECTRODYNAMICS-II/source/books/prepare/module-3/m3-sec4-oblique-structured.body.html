9.3.3 Reflection and transmission at oblique incidence

Source: book-sources/m3-sec4-oblique.html, Griffiths pp. 411–418. Equations 9.90–9.118 in full; footnotes 17–19. Background (beyond the syllabus list). Problems 9.16–9.19 listed, not worked.

Prerequisites

  • Normal incidence and the interface conditions (§9.3.2).
  • Snell’s law (pre-physics P5); wave vectors and $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r$ (pre-math M10).

1. Waves

$$\tilde{\mathbf E}_I=\tilde{\mathbf E}_{0I}e^{i(\mathbf k_I\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)},\qquad\tilde{\mathbf B}_I=\frac{1}{v_1}(\hat k_I\times\tilde{\mathbf E}_I)\tag{9.90}$$
$$\tilde{\mathbf E}_R=\tilde{\mathbf E}_{0R}e^{i(\mathbf k_R\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)},\qquad\tilde{\mathbf B}_R=\frac{1}{v_1}(\hat k_R\times\tilde{\mathbf E}_R)\tag{9.91}$$
{{FIG:m3-sec4-oblique}}
$$\tilde{\mathbf E}_T=\tilde{\mathbf E}_{0T}e^{i(\mathbf k_T\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)},\qquad\tilde{\mathbf B}_T=\frac{1}{v_2}(\hat k_T\times\tilde{\mathbf E}_T)\tag{9.92}$$

Common frequency $\omega$ (footnote 17: linear media only). Wave numbers:

$$k_Iv_1=k_Rv_1=k_Tv_2=\omega,\qquad k_I=k_R=\frac{v_2}{v_1}k_T=\frac{n_1}{n_2}k_T\tag{9.93}$$

2. Matching exponentials

The matching condition Eq. 9.75 must hold for all $x$, $y$ and $t$. So the exponential factors must agree on the plane $z=0$:

$$\mathbf k_I\cdot\mathbf r=\mathbf k_R\cdot\mathbf r=\mathbf k_T\cdot\mathbf r\qquad\text{when } z=0\tag{9.95}$$
$$\begin{aligned}x(k_I)_x+y(k_I)_y&=x(k_R)_x+y(k_R)_y\\ &=x(k_T)_x+y(k_T)_y\end{aligned}\tag{9.96}$$

Separating the $x$ and $y$ coefficients:

$$(k_I)_y=(k_R)_y=(k_T)_y\tag{9.97}$$
$$(k_I)_x=(k_R)_x=(k_T)_x\tag{9.98}$$

3. Laws of geometrical optics

Result 9.13: the three laws

First law. The three wave vectors and the normal lie in one plane, the plane of incidence.

Second law. From Eq. 9.98, $k_I\sin\theta_I=k_R\sin\theta_R=k_T\sin\theta_T$ Eq. 9.99, so

$$\theta_I=\theta_R\tag{9.100}$$

Third law (Snell).

$$\frac{\sin\theta_T}{\sin\theta_I}=\frac{n_1}{n_2}\tag{9.101}$$

Only the general form of the boundary condition Eq. 9.94 was used. So these laws hold for any wave that crosses an interface, including water and sound waves.

4. Amplitude conditions

$$\epsilon_1(\tilde{\mathbf E}_{0I}+\tilde{\mathbf E}_{0R})_z=\epsilon_2(\tilde{\mathbf E}_{0T})_z,\qquad(\tilde{\mathbf B}_{0I}+\tilde{\mathbf B}_{0R})_z=(\tilde{\mathbf B}_{0T})_z\tag{9.102}$$

together with the $x$- and $y$-components of $(\tilde{\mathbf E}_{0I}+\tilde{\mathbf E}_{0R})$ and of $\frac1{\mu}(\tilde{\mathbf B}_{0I}+\tilde{\mathbf B}_{0R})$ (9.102, pairs of equations).

5. Polarization parallel to the plane of incidence

$$\epsilon_1(-\tilde E_{0I}\sin\theta_I+\tilde E_{0R}\sin\theta_R)=\epsilon_2(-\tilde E_{0T}\sin\theta_T)\tag{9.103}$$
$$\tilde E_{0I}\cos\theta_I+\tilde E_{0R}\cos\theta_R=\tilde E_{0T}\cos\theta_T\tag{9.104}$$
$$\frac{1}{\mu_1v_1}(\tilde E_{0I}-\tilde E_{0R})=\frac{1}{\mu_2v_2}\tilde E_{0T}\tag{9.105}$$

Using the laws above, Eq. 9.103 and Eq. 9.105 give

$$\tilde E_{0I}-\tilde E_{0R}=\beta\tilde E_{0T}\tag{9.106}$$
$$\beta\equiv\frac{\mu_1v_1}{\mu_2v_2}=\frac{\mu_1n_2}{\mu_2n_1}\tag{9.107}$$

and Eq. 9.104 gives

$$\tilde E_{0I}+\tilde E_{0R}=\alpha\tilde E_{0T}\tag{9.108}$$
$$\alpha\equiv\frac{\cos\theta_T}{\cos\theta_I}\tag{9.109}$$
Result 9.14: Fresnel equations, parallel polarization
$$\tilde E_{0R}=\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\right)\tilde E_{0I},\qquad\tilde E_{0T}=\left(\frac{2}{\alpha+\beta}\right)\tilde E_{0I}\tag{9.110}$$
$$\alpha=\frac{\sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta_T}}{\cos\theta_I}=\frac{\sqrt{1-[(n_1/n_2)\sin\theta_I]^2}}{\cos\theta_I}\tag{9.111}$$

Normal incidence gives $\alpha=1$ and recovers Eq. 9.83. Grazing incidence gives total reflection.

6. Brewster’s angle

The reflected amplitude vanishes when $\alpha=\beta$, which gives

$$\sin^2\theta_B=\frac{1-\beta^2}{(n_1/n_2)^2-\beta^2}\tag{9.112}$$

For $\mu_1\approx\mu_2$: $\tan\theta_B\approx n_2/n_1$ Eq. 9.113. The reflected beam is then polarized perpendicular to the plane of incidence (footnote 19).

7. Intensities and coefficients

$$I_I=\frac12\epsilon_1v_1E_{0I}^2\cos\theta_I\tag{9.114}$$
$$I_R=\frac12\epsilon_1v_1E_{0R}^2\cos\theta_R,\qquad I_T=\frac12\epsilon_2v_2E_{0T}^2\cos\theta_T\tag{9.115}$$
Result 9.15: coefficients (parallel polarization)
$$R=\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha+\beta}\right)^2\tag{9.116}$$
$$T=\alpha\beta\left(\frac{2}{\alpha+\beta}\right)^2\tag{9.117}$$
$$I_I=I_T+I_R\tag{9.118}$$

Hence $R+T=1$. $R$ vanishes and $T$ reaches 1 at Brewster’s angle.
