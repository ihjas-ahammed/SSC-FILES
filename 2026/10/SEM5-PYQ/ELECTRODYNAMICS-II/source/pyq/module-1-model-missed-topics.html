MODULE 1: MODEL ANSWERS
For checking your work. Each answer shows the key step and the result.

1 $\mathbf p=\alpha\mathbf E$; $\alpha=4\pi\varepsilon_0a^3$. 2 $\rho_b=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P$. 3 $\mathbf E=-\mathbf P/3\varepsilon_0$. 4 $\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf m\times\mathbf B$. 5 $\mathbf K_b=\mathbf M\times\hat{\mathbf n}$. 6 $\mathbf B=\mu_0(1+\chi_m)\mathbf H$.

7 Positive charges at one end and negative at the other: each internal head cancels the next tail, but the two ends are left uncancelled. 8 $D^\perp_{above}-D^\perp_{below}=\sigma_f$; $\mathbf D^\parallel_{above}-\mathbf D^\parallel_{below}=\mathbf P^\parallel_{above}-\mathbf P^\parallel_{below}$. 9 Each atom gives a loop current, but at the edge there is no neighbour to cancel it, so the loops add up to one surface current $K_b=M$. 10 $\nabla\times\mathbf H=\mathbf J_f$ but $\nabla\cdot\mathbf H=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf M\neq0$ at the ends of a bar magnet; H is found from symmetry and free current only where $\nabla\cdot\mathbf M=0$. 11 A uniform field gives $\mathbf F=0$ but $\boldsymbol\tau=\mathbf m\times\mathbf B$; in a non-uniform field $\mathbf F=\nabla(\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf B)\neq0$.

12 Tangent dipole, $\mathbf p\cdot\nabla$ along the tangent gives $F=\dfrac{Qp}{4\pi\varepsilon_0R^3}=8.99\times10^{-3}$ N (about 9.0 mN), tangential. 13 $\rho_b=-3k=-6\times10^{-6}$ C/m$^3$; $\sigma_b=kR=2\times10^{-7}$ C/m$^2$; total $=-4\pi kR^3+4\pi kR^3=0$. 14 $E=-P/3\varepsilon_0=-3.77\times10^4$ V/m (magnitude $3.77\times10^4$ V/m); $p=\tfrac43\pi R^3P=3.35\times10^{-11}$ C m. 15 $E=3E_0/(\varepsilon_r+2)=600$ V/m; $\mathbf P=\varepsilon_0\chi_e\mathbf E$ with $\chi_e=2$ gives $P=1.06\times10^{-8}$ C/m$^2$. 16 $B=\tfrac23\mu_0M=8.38\times10^{-3}$ T; $m=\tfrac43\pi R^3M=5.24$ A m$^2$. 17 $H=nI=2000$ A/m; $B=\mu_0(1+\chi_m)H=2.51\times10^{-3}$ T; $K_b=\chi_mH=0.04$ A/m.

18 Inside, $\rho_b$ is uniform and $\sigma_b=P\cos\theta$ on the surface; the potential is $V_{in}=\tfrac{P}{3\varepsilon_0}r\cos\theta$ and $\mathbf E_{in}=-\mathbf P/3\varepsilon_0$. Outside, $V=\tfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\tfrac{\mathbf p\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}}{r^2}$ with $\mathbf p=\tfrac43\pi R^3\mathbf P$. 19 Gauss gives $\mathbf D=\dfrac{Q}{4\pi r^2}\hat{\mathbf r}$ everywhere; $\mathbf E=\dfrac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon r^2}\hat{\mathbf r}$ for $a<r<b$. Potential at the centre $V=\dfrac{Q}{4\pi}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon_0 b}+\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon a}-\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon b}\Big)$. 20 Larmor: an electron orbit speeds up or slows down in B, so $\Delta v=eRB/2m_e$ and $\Delta\mathbf m=-\tfrac12e\,\Delta v\,R\,\hat z$, opposite to B for both senses of orbit. Diamagnetism acts on all atoms, but paramagnetic torques on unpaired spins are much larger, so it is weaker.
