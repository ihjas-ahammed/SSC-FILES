MODULE 4: MODEL ANSWERS
For checking your work. Each answer shows the key step and the result.

Section A. A1 $I=I_0 e^{-Rt/L}$ (Murugeshan §12.2, Eq. 2). A2 $\nu=1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})$ (§12.5, Eq. 4). A3 $E_0=NBA\omega$ (§13.1). A4 False: in a pure inductor the current lags the emf by $\pi/2$ (§13.2(ii)). A5 $Z=\sqrt{R^2+1/(\omega C)^2}$, from $Z=R+1/(j\omega C)$ (§13.2(v)). A6 $Z_L=j\omega L$ (§13.2 j operator).

Section B. B7 Mean over half a cycle $I_{mean}=2I_0/\pi=0.637\,I_0$; r.m.s. $I_{rms}=I_0/\sqrt2=0.707\,I_0$ (§13.1). B8 The current leads the emf by $\theta=\tan^{-1}\dfrac{1/(\omega C)}{R}$ (§13.2(v), Eq. 5). B9 $\nu_0=1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})$ (§13.4). B10 Both curves peak at $\nu_0$ with height $E_0/R$; the low-$R$ curve is taller and sharper (§13.3, Figs. 13.17–13.18). B11 $P=E_{rms}I_{rms}$, since $\cos\phi=1$ (§13.5, special case 1).

Section C. C12 Time constant $L/R=0.05$ s; steady current $I_0=12/10=1.2$ A; $I(0.1)=1.2(1-e^{-2})=1.2\times0.8647=1.04$ A (§12.1, Eq. 4). C13 $Q_0=CV=10^{-5}\times100=1.0\times10^{-3}$ C; $CR=10$ s; $Q(10\text{ s})=Q_0e^{-1}=3.68\times10^{-4}$ C; the charge falls to $1/e$ at $t=CR=10$ s (§12.3(b), Eq. 2). C14 $\nu_0=\dfrac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{(0.1)(4\times10^{-6})}}=251.6$ Hz, about 252 Hz (§13.4, Eq. 2). C15 $X_L=2\pi(50)(0.1)=31.42\ \Omega$; $Z=\sqrt{30^2+31.42^2}=43.44\ \Omega$; $\cos\phi=30/43.44=0.691$; $I=240/43.44=5.52$ A; $P=E\,I\cos\phi=916$ W (§13.5).

Section D. D16 $Z=R+j(\omega L-1/\omega C)$, so $|Z|=\sqrt{R^2+(\omega L-1/\omega C)^2}$ and $\tan\phi=(\omega L-1/\omega C)/R$. The current is $I=E/Z=I_0 e^{j(\omega t-\phi)}$ with $I_0=E_0/|Z|$. The amplitude is largest when $\omega L=1/\omega C$, that is $\omega=1/\sqrt{LC}$, where $|Z|=R$ and $I_0=E_0/R$ (§13.3, second method, Eqs. 2–5). D17 For the parallel branch $1/Z=1/(R+j\omega L)+j\omega C$. With $R\to0$, $1/Z=j(\omega C-1/\omega L)$, which is zero at $\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}$, so $Z$ is infinite there. The circuit rejects the current of its natural frequency, which is why it is called a rejector (§13.4).
