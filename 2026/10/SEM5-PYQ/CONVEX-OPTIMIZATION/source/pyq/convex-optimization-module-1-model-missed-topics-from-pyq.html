Section B: 2 marks each, answer all

4. (Unit 6). If $f$ is twice differentiable near $\boldsymbol{\xi}$, then for $\mathbf{x}$ near $\boldsymbol{\xi}$, $f(\mathbf{x}) = f(\boldsymbol{\xi}) + f'(\boldsymbol{\xi})(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\xi}) + \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\xi})^{T} f''(\boldsymbol{\eta})(\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\xi})$ for some $\boldsymbol{\eta}$ on the line segment joining $\mathbf{x}$ and $\boldsymbol{\xi}$.

5. (Unit 6). For $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}$ with the segment between them in the domain, there is $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ on the segment with $f(\mathbf{b}) - f(\mathbf{a}) = f'(\boldsymbol{\xi})(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})$.

Section C: 4 marks each, answer all

6. (Unit 5). $f_x = 3x^2 - 3y$, $f_y = -3x + 2y$; $f_{xx} = 6x$, $f_{xy} = f_{yx} = -3$, $f_{yy} = 2$. At $(1, 1)$: $H = \begin{pmatrix} 6 & -3 \\ -3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$. It is symmetric because $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$ (equality of mixed partials, since $f$ has continuous second order partials).

7. (Units 7, 8). $f_x = 2x + y - 3 = 0$ and $f_y = x + 2y = 0$ give $(x, y) = (2, -1)$. The Hessian is $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, with characteristic equation $(2 - \lambda)^2 - 1 = 0$, so $\lambda = 1$ and $\lambda = 3$. Both are positive, so $(2, -1)$ is a local minimum with value $f(2, -1) = -3$.

Section D: 10 marks, answer the question

8. (Units 4, 8). $f_x = 3x^2 - 3 = 0$ gives $x = \pm 1$; $f_y = 6y = 0$ gives $y = 0$. The stationary points are $(1, 0)$ and $(-1, 0)$. The Hessian is $\begin{pmatrix} 6x & 0 \\ 0 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$. At $(1, 0)$: $H = \operatorname{diag}(6, 6)$, eigenvalues $6, 6$, both positive; a local minimum with value $f(1, 0) = -2$. At $(-1, 0)$: $H = \operatorname{diag}(-6, 6)$, eigenvalues $-6$ and $6$ of opposite sign; a saddle point with value $f(-1, 0) = 2$.
