Key steps for checking your work

Section A. 1. $\operatorname{Ker}\phi = \phi^{-1}[\{e'\}] = \{x \in G \mid \phi(x) = e'\}$ (Definition 8.6). 2. The characteristic of $\mathbb{Z}_6 \times \mathbb{Z}_{10}$ is the least $n$ with $n \cdot 1 = 0$, which is $\operatorname{lcm}(6, 10) = 30$ (Definition 23.12; Theorem 23.14). 4. Yes. $\{0, 1\}$ is $\mathbb{Z}_2$, a field (Corollary 23.5).

Section B. 5. $\phi(x) = x \bmod 4$ on $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$ sends $x$ to the class of $x$ in $\mathbb{Z}_4$; the kernel is $\{0, 4, 8\} = \langle 4 \rangle$ (Definition 8.6). 6. $\phi(a + b) = \phi(a) + \phi(b)$ and $\phi(ab) = \phi(a)\phi(b)$ for all $a, b$ (Definition 22.9). 7. $\{1, 3, 7, 9\}$, the numbers relatively prime to $10$ (Theorem 23.3). 8. $\mathbb{Z}_2$ is commutative with unity $1 \neq 0$ and has no divisors of $0$: the only nonzero element is $1$, and $1 \cdot 1 = 1 \neq 0$ (Definition 23.7). 9. Yes. Since $5 \mid 10$, the value $x \bmod 5$ does not depend on the representative of $x$ in $\mathbb{Z}_{10}$, so $\phi$ is well defined. And $\phi(x + y) = \phi(x) + \phi(y)$ in $\mathbb{Z}_5$ (Definition 8.1).

Section C. 10. $\phi(xy^{-1}) = \phi(x)\phi(y)^{-1}$ by Theorem 8.5 (2). This equals $e'$ exactly when $\phi(x)\phi(y)^{-1} = e'$, that is when $\phi(x) = \phi(y)$. 11. For $r' \in R'$, $r' = \phi(r)$ for some $r$, and $\phi(1)\phi(r) = \phi(r) = \phi(r)\phi(1)$. So $\phi(1)$ is unity of $R'$ (Definition 22.9, onto). 12. $0 \in$ the set; the difference and the product of two elements are again of this form, since $(a + b\sqrt{2})(c + d\sqrt{2}) = (ac + 2bd) + (ad + bc)\sqrt{2}$ (Section 22, subring test). 13. $\operatorname{Ker}\phi$ is a subgroup (Theorem 8.5 (4)). For $g \in G$, $k \in \operatorname{Ker}\phi$: $\phi(gkg^{-1}) = \phi(g)e'\phi(g)^{-1} = e'$. So it is normal (Exercise 35 of Section 8). 14. Let $a \neq 0$. Multiplication by $a$ is one-to-one (cancellation, Theorem 23.6) and so onto (finite set). So some $b$ has $ab = 1$ (Theorem 23.11), and the ring is a field.

Section D. 15. Let $\phi : \mathbb{Z}_{12} \to \mathbb{Z}_4$ be reduction mod $4$; $\phi$ is a homomorphism with kernel $\langle 4 \rangle$ (Theorem 16.2 and Example 16.7). The map $\mu(x + \langle 4 \rangle) = \phi(x)$ is an isomorphism $\mathbb{Z}_{12}/\langle 4 \rangle \to \mathbb{Z}_4$ (First Isomorphism Theorem). 16. $\mathbb{Z}_{p^2}$ has $p^2$ elements. By Theorem 23.3 the units are the classes of numbers coprime to $p^2$, which are the $p^2 - p$ numbers not divisible by $p$. The non-units are the multiples of $p$, which are $p$ numbers (including $0$).
