20.18.1 SUPERPOSITION OF e-RAY AND O-RAY:
     Now we are in a position to understand what happens when e-ray and o- ray overlap on each
other after emerging from an anisotropic crystal plate. It is obvious that they cannot produce inter-
ference fringes as in a double slitexperiment, since the o-ray are not vibrating in the same plane. On
the other hand, they combine to produce different states of polarization depending upon their optical
path difference.
      1. When the optical path difference is 0 or an even or odd multiple of 2/2, the resultant light
         wave is linearly   polarized.
      2. When the optical path difference is λ/4, the resultant light wave is elliptically polarized.
      3. In the particular instance when the wave amplitudes are equal and the optical path difference
         is 2/4, the resultant light wave is circularly polarized.


 20.19. RETARDERS

     A retarder is a uniform plate of birefringent material whose optic axis lies in the plane of the
plate. Retarders are called quarter-wave plates, half-wave plates and full-wave plates depending on
their action. They divide the incident wave into two polarized waves that travel perpendicular to the
plate at different speeds. A phase retardation of one wave relative to the other is therefore introduced
as the waves cross the thickness d of the plate. They are used to produce circularly or elliptically

polarised light and to analyse polarised light into its elliptical components.
20.19.1 QUARTER WAVE PLATE

    A quarter wave plate is a thin plate of birefringent crystal having the optic axis parallel to its
refracting faces and its thickness adjusted such that it introduces a quarter-wave (7/4) path difference
(or a phase difference of 90°) between the e-ray and o-ray propagating through it.

 20.20. PRODUCTION OF ELLIPTICALLY POLARIZED LIGHT

     A quarter wave plate and a polarizer are the optical devices necessary to produce elliptically

     Unpolarized light is first converted to plane polarized light by allowing it to pass through a
polarizer (a polaroid sheet or a Nicol prism), as shown in Fig.20.47. The plane-polarized light is
then made incident on a quarter wave plate. The quarter wave plate or the polarizer is rotated such
that the electric vector E of plane polarized light wave makes an angle 0 (+45°) with the optic axis

are polarized in orthogonal planes.  They are in phase at the front face but progressively get out of
phase as they travel through the crystal. When they emerge out of the crystal they will have a path
difference of ./4 or a phase difference of 90°. When they combine, they produce elliptically polarized
light.

20.20.1 DETECTION OF ELLIPTICALLY POLARIZED LIGHT

    The light beam is allowed to pass through an analyzer (a polaroid sheet or a Nicol prism). If on
rotating the analyzing Polaroid sheet or Nicol, the intensity of the emerging beam varies from a
maximum to a minimum value, but never reaching zero, then the incident light is elliptically polarized.
