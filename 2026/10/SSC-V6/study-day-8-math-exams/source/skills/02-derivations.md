# Textbook proofs and derivations

Read the actual primary section and question. State its hypotheses explicitly.
Write complete mathematical arguments directly, including endpoint cases,
domains, quantifier order, bounds and conclusions. An answer that only quotes
the requested theorem is incomplete when the question asks for a proof.

Use info/math cards for step-by-step inequality, set-membership and logical
proofs. Give each step a purpose. Long equations, cases and tables can scroll.
Do not replace proofs with mnemonic word-completion questions.

An animated derivation is appropriate for a sequence of equalities. Each step
has one top-level equals sign, one move and a short mathematical reason.
Use 3–6 named phases and four decision checkpoints. The first step has no move;
later moves name the actual operation. Do not invent equations or alter a
statement just to meet the animation format.

Textbook specifics:

- Uniform-limit proofs: choose one approximating function before delta.
- Supremum estimates: use an epsilon buffer; strict pointwise bounds may have
  a nonstrict supremum or limiting bound.
- Uniform Cauchy: completeness first constructs a pointwise limit, then the
  shared index gives uniformity; prove boundedness of the limit separately.
- Riemann Squeeze: establish boundedness and common-mesh Cauchy control before
  assuming integrability of the function being squeezed.
- Coset proofs: preserve factor order and prove coverage/distinctness.
- Finite-index multiplication does not assume the groups themselves finite.
- Euler uses the multiplicative unit group, not the additive residue group.

Every original question has a source reference and a fully worked answer.
Interactive recall compares an eight-word-or-longer sketch, but does not replace
the complete model proof or self-marking rubric.
