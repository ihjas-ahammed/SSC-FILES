IRRATIONAL MULTIPLICATION We have seen that the length of the diagonal of a square cannot be expressed as a fractional multiple of the length of its side. In other words, if we take the side of a square as the unit of length, then its diagonal cannot be expressed as a fraction. We have also seen that this length is denoted by the symbol 2 . We then saw some other lengths like these and new numbers to denote them. (We will see another such number in the lesson, Circular Measures). Such numbers which are used to denote lenghts which cannot be expressed as fractions of a unit are called irrational numbers. We have also seen how joining lengths denoted by irrational numbers leads to the definition of addition of such numbers. We now see how computation of areas lead to the operation of multiplication of such numbers.
