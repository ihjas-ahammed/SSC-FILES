Curved surface A rectangular sheet of paper or metal can be rolled into a cylinder; on the other hand, a hollow cylinder, open at both ends, can be cut and spread out into a rectangle:

The area of this rectangle is called the curved surface area of the cylinder. The length of one side of this rectangle is the height of the cylinder. The other side is the base circle straightened, so that its length is the circumference of this circle. The curved surface area is the product of these lengths: The curved surface area of a cylinder is the product of its base circumference and its height The base circumference is π times the base diameter, isn't it? So, the curved surface area of a cylinder of base radius 3 centimetres and height 5 centimetres is π × 6 × 5 = 30π square centimetres. If this is a closed cylinder, to get the total surface area, we must add to this, the areas of the two circles at the ends; that is, 30π + (2 × π × 32) = 48π square centimetres.
