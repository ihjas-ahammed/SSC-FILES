Note that in all these, we join the ends of the arc to a point on the circle, outside the arc.
We can readily see that if we join the ends of an arc to a point within the arc, then we
won't get half the central angle :

So all that we have seen so far can be combined to a general principle :
If the ends of an arc of a circle are joined to a point on the circle, which is not a point
on the arc itself, then the angle so made is half the central angle of the arc

The case of a semicircle is worth special mention :
If the ends of a semicircle are joined to another point on the circle, the angle made
is a right angle

This can be shortened like this:
The angle in a semicircle is a right angle

Segments of a circle
Any two points on a circle splits the circle into two arcs :

Each of these arcs may be called the alternate arc of the other . Any point on the circle
is either on one of these arcs or its alternate arc . So , the relation between the central
angle of an arc and the angle made by joining the ends of the arc to a point on the circle
can be stated like this
The angle made by joining the ends of an arc of a circle to any point on the alternate
arc is half the central angle of the arc

This can put in another way. A chord joining two points
on a circle splits the entire circular region into two parts.
Such parts of a circle are called segments of the circle. Of
the two segments into which a chord cuts a circle, each
may be called the alternate segment of the other.
So the result about the angles in a circle can be stated in
terms of segments also, instead of arcs.
