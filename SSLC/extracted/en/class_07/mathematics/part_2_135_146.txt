Any right triangle can be seen as half a rectangle like this. How do we compute its
area then?
• The width and height of the rectangle are the perpendicular sides of the 
triangle
• The area of the rectangle is the product of its width and height
• The area of the right triangle is half the area of the rectangle
Putting all these together, we can calculate the area of a right triangle without
imagining it as half a rectangle every time:
The area of a right triangle is half the product of its perpendicular sides

Changing the lengths does not alter the method of finding areas. So we can make a
general statement about computing the area of a triangle:
The area of any triangle is half the product of one of the sides and the height from
this side to the opposite vertex

If we name the side used to compute the area, the base of the triangle and the height
to the opposite vertex, the altitude, then this can be shortened a bit:
The area of any triangle is half the product of the base and the altitude
