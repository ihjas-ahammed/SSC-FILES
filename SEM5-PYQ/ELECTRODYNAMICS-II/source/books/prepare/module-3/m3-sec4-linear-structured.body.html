9.3.1–9.3.2 Propagation in linear media; normal incidence

Source: book-sources/m3-sec4-linear.html and m3-sec4-oblique.html (Eqs. 9.87–9.89), Griffiths pp. 408–412. Equations 9.66–9.89 in full; footnotes 13–16; Problems 9.14–9.15 described.

Prerequisites

  • Vacuum Maxwell equations (Eq. 9.40) and plane-wave relations Eq. 9.46–Eq. 9.48.
  • Interface conditions (Eq. 7.74, given as 9.75).
  • Permittivity and permeability of matter (pre-physics P12, P13).

1. Maxwell’s equations in linear media

$$\text{(i)}\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf D=0,\qquad\text{(iii)}\ \nabla\times\mathbf E=-\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t},$$
$$\text{(ii)}\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,\qquad\text{(iv)}\ \nabla\times\mathbf H=\frac{\partial\mathbf D}{\partial t}\tag{9.66}$$

Linear constitutive relations: $\mathbf D=\epsilon\mathbf E$, $\mathbf H=\mathbf B/\mu$ Eq. 9.67. For a homogeneous medium:

$$\text{(i)}\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf E=0,\qquad\text{(iii)}\ \nabla\times\mathbf E=-\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t},$$
$$\text{(ii)}\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,\qquad\text{(iv)}\ \nabla\times\mathbf B=\mu\epsilon\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t}\tag{9.68}$$

2. Speed and index

Plus Two recall: refractive index

Recall. From Class 12: $n=c/v$.

Re-derive.

  1. Replacing $\mu_0\epsilon_0$ by $\mu\epsilon$ in the vacuum wave equation gives $v^2=1/\mu\epsilon$.
  2. Writing $v=c/n$, $n=c/v=\sqrt{\mu\epsilon/\mu_0\epsilon_0}$.

Saved. $v=c/n$ Eq. 9.69.

$$v=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon\mu}}=\frac{c}{n},\qquad n\equiv\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon\mu}{\epsilon_0\mu_0}}\tag{9.69}$$
$$n\approx\sqrt{\epsilon_r}\tag{9.71}$$

Index of refraction Eq. 9.70 and the dielectric constant $\epsilon_r$ (Eq. 4.34). Since $\epsilon_r>1$, light is slower in matter. Footnote 13: the induced dipole fields combine with the original field into a single wave with a new speed (the Ewald–Oseen theorem); transparency follows. Footnote 14: $\epsilon_r$ may depend on frequency, so static values give the wrong $n$ (water: 8.9 static, 1.33 for visible light).

3. Energy and flux

$$u=\frac12\left(\epsilon E^2+\frac1\mu B^2\right)\tag{9.72}$$
$$\mathbf S=\frac1\mu(\mathbf E\times\mathbf B)\tag{9.73}$$

Plane wave: $\omega=kv$ Eq. 9.11, $B_0=E_0/v$ (9.48 with $c\to v$), and

Result 9.11: intensity in a medium
$$I=\frac12\epsilon vE_0^2\tag{9.74}$$

4. Interface conditions

$$\text{(i)}\ \epsilon_1E_1^\perp=\epsilon_2E_2^\perp,\qquad\text{(iii)}\ \mathbf E_1^\parallel=\mathbf E_2^\parallel,$$
$$\text{(ii)}\ B_1^\perp=B_2^\perp,\qquad\text{(iv)}\ \frac{1}{\mu_1}\mathbf B_1^\parallel=\frac{1}{\mu_2}\mathbf B_2^\parallel\tag{9.75}$$

5. Normal incidence

Interface in the $xy$ plane. Incident, reflected and transmitted waves (Fig. 9.13):

$$\tilde{\mathbf E}_I=\tilde E_{0I}e^{i(k_1z-\omega t)}\hat x,\qquad\tilde{\mathbf B}_I=\frac1{v_1}\tilde E_{0I}e^{i(k_1z-\omega t)}\hat y\tag{9.76}$$
{{FIG:m3-sec4-normal}}
$$\tilde{\mathbf E}_R=\tilde E_{0R}e^{i(-k_1z-\omega t)}\hat x,\qquad\tilde{\mathbf B}_R=-\frac1{v_1}\tilde E_{0R}e^{i(-k_1z-\omega t)}\hat y\tag{9.77}$$
$$\tilde{\mathbf E}_T=\tilde E_{0T}e^{i(k_2z-\omega t)}\hat x,\qquad\tilde{\mathbf B}_T=\frac1{v_2}\tilde E_{0T}e^{i(k_2z-\omega t)}\hat y\tag{9.78}$$

Minus sign in $\tilde{\mathbf B}_R$: required by Eq. 9.50. Conditions (iii) and (iv) at $z=0$:

$$\tilde E_{0I}+\tilde E_{0R}=\tilde E_{0T}\tag{9.79}$$
$$\frac1{\mu_1}\left(\frac1{v_1}\tilde E_{0I}-\frac1{v_1}\tilde E_{0R}\right)=\frac1{\mu_2}\frac1{v_2}\tilde E_{0T}\tag{9.80}$$

Writing $\tilde E_{0I}-\tilde E_{0R}=\beta\tilde E_{0T}$ Eq. 9.81, $\beta\equiv\mu_1v_1/\mu_2v_2=\mu_1n_2/\mu_2n_1$ Eq. 9.82. Solving:

Result 9.12: normal-incidence amplitudes
$$\tilde E_{0R}=\left(\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}\right)\tilde E_{0I},\qquad\tilde E_{0T}=\left(\frac{2}{1+\beta}\right)\tilde E_{0I}\tag{9.83}$$
$$\tilde E_{0R}=\left(\frac{v_2-v_1}{v_2+v_1}\right)\tilde E_{0I},\qquad\tilde E_{0T}=\left(\frac{2v_2}{v_2+v_1}\right)\tilde E_{0I}\tag{9.84}$$

Real amplitudes Eq. 9.85, and in terms of indices Eq. 9.86:

$$E_{0R}=\left|\frac{v_2-v_1}{v_2+v_1}\right|E_{0I},\qquad E_{0T}=\left(\frac{2v_2}{v_2+v_1}\right)E_{0I}\tag{9.85}$$
$$E_{0R}=\left|\frac{n_1-n_2}{n_1+n_2}\right|E_{0I},\qquad E_{0T}=\left(\frac{2n_1}{n_1+n_2}\right)E_{0I}\tag{9.86}$$

6. Coefficients

$$R=\left(\frac{n_1-n_2}{n_1+n_2}\right)^2\tag{9.87}$$
$$T=\frac{4n_1n_2}{(n_1+n_2)^2}\tag{9.88}$$
$$R+T=1\tag{9.89}$$

Check: air to glass gives $R=0.04$, $T=0.96$. Problems 9.14 and 9.15 are outside these notes.
