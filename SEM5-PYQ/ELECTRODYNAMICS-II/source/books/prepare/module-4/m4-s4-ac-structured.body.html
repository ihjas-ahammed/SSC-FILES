13.2 Alternating current circuit theory

Source: book-sources/m4-s4-ac-circuits.html, Murugeshan pp. 334–337 (printed). Syllabus items 18, 19 and 20. The parallel circuit, §13.2(vi), is in Section 5 with §13.4. Equations are tagged with the book’s numbers.

Prerequisites

  • Phase of a sinusoid (pre-physics H2).
  • Complex numbers and the phasor (pre-math B1, B2).

1. Pure resistance (i)

Recall (Plus Two) Ohm’s law $V=IR$ (Plus Two) gives $I=E/R$ in complex form. The current is in phase with the emf.

For $E=E_0e^{j\omega t}$ across $R$ (Fig. 13.4), $I = E/R = I_0e^{j\omega t}$ with $I_0=E_0/R$. The current is in phase with the voltage (Fig. 13.5).

{{FIG:m4-s4-resistor}}

2. Pure inductance (ii)

$$I = \frac{E}{j\omega L} = \frac{E_0}{\omega L}e^{j(\omega t-\pi/2)}\tag{13.2(ii)(1)}$$
$$I = I_0\sin\left(\omega t - \frac{\pi}{2}\right)\tag{13.2(ii)(2)}$$

The current lags the emf by $\pi/2$. The impedance is $j\omega L$.

{{FIG:m4-s4-inductor}}

3. Pure capacitance (iii)

From $q/C=E$, $I=dq/dt=j\omega CE$, so $I = I_0 e^{j(\omega t+\pi/2)}$ with $I_0 = E_0/(1/\omega C)$. The current leads the emf by $\pi/2$. The impedance is $1/(j\omega C)$.

{{FIG:m4-s4-capacitor}}

4. Inductance and resistance in series (iv)

$$I = \frac{E}{R + j\omega L}\tag{13.2(iv)(1)}$$
$$I = \frac{E}{Z}\tag{13.2(iv)(2)}$$
$$Z = R + j\omega L\tag{13.2(iv)(3)}$$

With $\tan\theta=\omega L/R$, $Z = \sqrt{R^2+\omega^2L^2}\,e^{j\theta}$, and

$$I = \frac{E_0}{\sqrt{R^2+\omega^2L^2}}\,e^{j(\omega t-\theta)}\tag{13.2(iv)(4)}$$
$$I = I_0e^{j(\omega t-\theta)}\tag{13.2(iv)(5)}$$
$$I_0 = \frac{E_0}{\sqrt{R^2+\omega^2L^2}}\tag{13.2(iv)(6)}$$

The current lags the emf by $\theta$.

{{FIG:m4-s4-rl-series}} {{FIG:m4-s4-rl-graph}}

5. Capacitance and resistance in series (v)

$$I\left(R+\frac{1}{j\omega C}\right) = E = E_0e^{j\omega t}\tag{13.2(v)(1)}$$
$$I = \frac{E}{R + 1/(j\omega C)}\tag{13.2(v)(2)}$$
$$I = \frac{E}{Z}\tag{13.2(v)(3)}$$
$$I = \frac{E}{R - j/(\omega C)} = \frac{E_0e^{j\omega t}}{\sqrt{R^2 + 1/(\omega C)^2}}\,e^{-j\theta}\tag{13.2(v)(4)}$$
$$\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1/\omega C}{R}\right)\tag{13.2(v)(5)}$$
$$I = \frac{E_0}{\sqrt{R^2+(1/\omega C)^2}}\,e^{j(\omega t+\theta)}\tag{13.2(v)(6)}$$

The current leads the voltage by $\theta$.

{{FIG:m4-s4-rc-series}} {{FIG:m4-s4-rc-graph}}

6. The j operator

$j$ rotates a phasor $90^\circ$ anticlockwise and $-j$ rotates it $90^\circ$ clockwise. The total emf is $E = E_R + jE_L - jE_C$. The complex impedance $Z=R+jX$ has resistance $R$ and effective reactance $X=X_L-X_C$.
