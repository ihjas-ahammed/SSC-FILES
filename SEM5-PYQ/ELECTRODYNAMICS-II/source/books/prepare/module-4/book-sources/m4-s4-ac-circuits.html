13.2 ALTERNATING CURRENT (A.C.) CIRCUIT THEORY

(i) A.C. Circuit Containing Resistance only. Suppose an alternating sinusoidal voltage

$$E = E_0 e^{j\omega t}$$

is applied across a pure resistance $R$ (Fig. 13.4). The current through the resistance at any instant $t$ is,

$$I = \frac{E}{R} = \frac{E_0 e^{j\omega t}}{R} = I_0 e^{j\omega t}$$

Here,

$$I_0 = \frac{E_0}{R}$$

Actual value of current $=$ Imaginary part of $I_0 e^{j\omega t} = I_0\sin\omega t$.

$$\therefore\quad I = I_0\sin\omega t$$

Hence the current is in phase with the driving voltage (Fig. 13.5).

(ii) A.C. Circuit Containing Inductance only. Let an alternating voltage

$$E = E_0 e^{j\omega t}$$

be applied across an inductance $L$ (Fig. 13.6). Let $I$ be the instantaneous current and $dI/dt$ be the rate of change of current with time through the inductance.

The voltage induced across the inductance $= L\dfrac{dI}{dt}$.

$$L\frac{dI}{dt} = E_0 e^{j\omega t}\quad\text{or}\quad dI = \frac{E_0}{L}e^{j\omega t}\,dt$$
$$\text{Integrating,}\quad I = \frac{E_0}{j\omega L}e^{j\omega t} = \frac{E}{j\omega L}\qquad\therefore\quad I = \frac{E_0}{\omega L}e^{j(\omega t - \pi/2)}\qquad(1)$$

Let $I_0 = \dfrac{E_0}{\omega L}$ be the maximum current. Then,

$$I = I_0 e^{j(\omega t - \pi/2)}$$

(iii) A.C. Circuit Containing Capacitance only. Let an alternating voltage,

$$E = E_0 e^{j\omega t}$$

be applied across the plates of a capacitor of capacitance $C$ (Fig. 13.8). Let $q$ be the charge at any instant across the plates of the capacitor. The p.d. across the plates at any instant must be equal to the applied emf at that instant. Thus

$$\frac{q}{C} = E_0 e^{j\omega t}$$

The current through the circuit is given by,

$$I = \frac{dq}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}\left(CE_0 e^{j\omega t}\right) = j\omega C E_0 e^{j\omega t}$$
$$I = \frac{E_0}{1/\omega C}e^{j(\omega t + \pi/2)} = I_0 e^{j(\omega t + \pi/2)}$$

Here, $I_0 = \dfrac{E_0}{1/\omega C}$ is the peak value of alternating current.

The actual current in the circuit is

$$I = I_0\sin\left(\omega t + \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$$

In a pure capacitor, the current leads the emf by $\pi/2$ (Fig. 13.9).

(iv) A.C. Circuit Containing Inductance and Resistance in Series. Let an alternating emf $E = E_0 e^{j\omega t}$ be applied to a circuit having an inductance $L$ and a non-inductive resistance $R$ in series (Fig. 13.10).

The potential drop across the inductance is $V_L = j\omega L I$. The potential drop across resistance is $V_R = RI$. Here, $I$ is the current at any instant $t$.

$$\therefore\quad E = j\omega L I + RI$$

Current in the circuit,

$$I = \frac{E}{R + j\omega L}\qquad(1)$$
$$\text{But}\quad I = \frac{E}{Z}\qquad(2)$$

Impedance of $R$–$L$ circuit,

$$Z = R + j\omega L\qquad(3)$$
$$\therefore\quad I = \frac{E_0 e^{j\omega t}}{\sqrt{(R^2 + \omega^2 L^2)}\,e^{j\theta}}\qquad\left(\text{where}\ \tan\theta = \frac{\omega L}{R}\right)$$
$$= \frac{E_0}{\sqrt{(R^2 + \omega^2 L^2)}}e^{j(\omega t - \theta)}\qquad(4)$$
$$= I_0 e^{j(\omega t - \theta)}\qquad(5)$$
$$\text{Here,}\quad I_0 = \frac{E_0}{\sqrt{R^2 + \omega^2 L^2}}\qquad(6)$$

It represents the peak value of the current through the circuit. The impedance $Z$ of the circuit is given by the term $\sqrt{R^2 + \omega^2 L^2}$.

The current lags in phase behind the emf by an angle $\theta = \tan^{-1}\dfrac{\omega L}{R}$.

The variation of instantaneous values of emf and current with time are represented graphically in Fig. 13.11.

(v) A.C. Circuit Containing Capacitance and Resistance in Series. Let an alternating voltage $E = E_0 e^{j\omega t}$ be applied to a circuit containing a resistance $R$ and a capacitor $C$ in series (Fig. 13.12). Let $I$ be the current through the circuit and $q$, the charge on the plates of the capacitor at any instant. Then,

$$I\left(R + \frac{1}{j\omega C}\right) = E = E_0 e^{j\omega t}\qquad(1)$$
$$I = \frac{E}{R + \dfrac{1}{j\omega C}}\qquad(2)$$
$$\text{But}\quad I = \frac{E}{Z}\qquad(3)$$

Impedance of series $R$–$C$ circuit,

$$Z = R + \frac{1}{j\omega C} = R - \frac{j}{\omega C}$$
$$I = \frac{E}{R - \dfrac{j}{\omega C}} = \frac{E_0 e^{j\omega t}}{\sqrt{\left[R^2 + \dfrac{1}{\omega^2 C^2}\right]}}\,e^{-j\theta}\qquad(4)$$
$$\text{Here,}\quad \theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1/\omega C}{R}\right)\qquad(5)$$
$$\therefore\quad I = \frac{E_0}{\sqrt{\{R^2 + (1/\omega C)^2\}}}\,e^{j(\omega t + \theta)}\qquad(6)$$

The current in this circuit thus leads the applied voltage by an angle $\theta$ (Fig. 13.13).

The impedance of series $R$–$C$ circuit, $Z = \sqrt{\left[R^2 + (1/\omega C)^2\right]}$.

The actual current in the circuit is $I = I_0\sin(\omega t + \theta)$.
