12.1 GROWTH OF CURRENT IN A CIRCUIT CONTAINING A RESISTANCE AND INDUCTANCE

Consider a circuit having an inductance $L$ and a resistance $R$ connected in series to a cell of steady emf $E$ (Fig. 12.1). When the key $K$ is pressed, there is a gradual growth of current in the circuit from zero to maximum value $I_0$. Let $I$ be the instantaneous current at any instant.

FIG. 12.1: circuit with R, L, cell E and key K in series

Then, the induced back emf $\varepsilon = -L\dfrac{dI}{dt}$.

$$E = RI + L\frac{dI}{dt}\qquad(1)$$

When the current reaches the maximum value $I_0$, the back emf is $L\dfrac{dI}{dt}=0$. Hence

$$E = RI_0\qquad(2)$$

Substituting this value for $E$ in Eq. (1),

$$RI_0 = RI + L\frac{dI}{dt}\quad\text{or}\quad R(I_0 - I) = L\frac{dI}{dt}$$
$$\frac{dI}{I_0 - I} = \frac{R}{L}\,dt$$

Integrating,

$$-\log(I_0 - I) = \frac{R}{L}t + C\qquad(3)$$

where $C$ is the constant of integration. When $t = 0$, $I = 0$, so $-\log_e I_0 = C$. Substituting this value of $C$ in Eq. (3),

$$-\log(I_0 - I) = \frac{R}{L}t - \log_e I_0\quad\text{or}\quad \log_e(I_0 - I) - \log_e I_0 = -\frac{R}{L}t$$
$$\log_e\frac{(I_0 - I)}{I_0} = -\frac{R}{L}t$$
$$\frac{I_0 - I}{I_0} = e^{-(R/L)t}\quad\text{or}\quad 1 - \frac{I}{I_0} = e^{-(R/L)t}$$
$$\therefore\quad I = I_0\left(1 - e^{-(R/L)t}\right)\qquad(4)$$

Eq. (4) gives the value of the instantaneous current in the $LR$ circuit. The quantity $(L/R)$ is called the time constant of the circuit.

$$\text{If}\quad \frac{L}{R} = t,\quad I = I_0\left(1 - e^{-1}\right) = I_0\left(1 - \frac{1}{e}\right) = 0.632\,I_0$$

Thus, the time constant $L/R$ of a L-R circuit is the time taken by the current to grow from zero to 0.632 times the steady maximum value of current in the circuit. Similarly, when $t = 2L/R,\ 3L/R\ \ldots$, the value of current will be 0.8647, 0.9502, $\ldots$ of the final maximum current.
