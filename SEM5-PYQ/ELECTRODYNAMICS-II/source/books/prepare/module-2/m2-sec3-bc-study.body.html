7.3.6 Boundary conditions

Source: book-sources/m2-sec3-bc.html, Griffiths pp. 345–347. Equations 7.69–7.74 are included.

Before you start

  • Integral forms of Maxwell’s equations (Eq. 7.65).
  • Gauss’s law for a closed surface; Faraday’s law for a loop.

1. Integral forms

Fields are discontinuous at a boundary between two media, or at a surface that carries charge $\sigma$ or current $\mathbf K$. The integral forms of Eq. 7.65 give the jumps:

  • (i) $\oint_{\mathcal S}\mathbf D\cdot d\mathbf a=Q_{f,\text{enc}}$ and (ii) $\oint_{\mathcal S}\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf a=0$, for any closed surface $\mathcal S$;
  • (iii) $\oint_{\mathcal P}\mathbf E\cdot d\mathbf l=-\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathcal S}\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf a$ and (iv) $\oint_{\mathcal P}\mathbf H\cdot d\mathbf l=I_{\text{enc}}+\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathcal S}\mathbf D\cdot d\mathbf a$, for any surface $\mathcal S$ bounded by the loop $\mathcal P$.

2. Normal components: a pillbox

Plus Two recall: Gauss’s law for a thin sheet of charge

Recall. From Class 12: the field either side of a sheet of charge $\sigma$ has a normal component jump of $\sigma/\epsilon_0$.

Re-derive.

  1. Draw a thin cylinder (pillbox) with faces above and below the surface, area $a$, and thickness $d\to0$.
  2. Apply (i): the flux out of the pillbox is $\mathbf D_{\text{above}}\cdot\mathbf a-\mathbf D_{\text{below}}\cdot\mathbf a$ (the sides vanish as $d\to0$).
  3. The enclosed free charge is $\sigma_f\,a$ (area $a$ times the surface density).

Saved. Eq. 7.69.

{{FIG:m2-sec3-pillbox}}

Applying (i) to a thin pillbox extending slightly into the material on each side gives $\mathbf D_{\text{above}}\cdot\mathbf a-\mathbf D_{\text{below}}\cdot\mathbf a=\sigma_f\,d$, where $\mathbf a$ points up. As $d\to0$ the edges and any volume charge contribute nothing:

Result 3.6: normal components
$$D^\perp_{\text{above}}-D^\perp_{\text{below}}=\sigma_f \tag{7.69}$$
$$B^\perp_{\text{above}}-B^\perp_{\text{below}}=0 \tag{7.70}$$

(7.70) follows by the same argument from (ii).

3. Tangential components: a thin Amperian loop

A very thin loop straddling the surface gives $\mathbf E_{\text{above}}\cdot\mathbf l-\mathbf E_{\text{below}}\cdot\mathbf l=-\dfrac{d}{dt}\int\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf a$. The flux through the thin loop vanishes as its width goes to zero, so the tangential $\mathbf E$ is continuous:

Result 3.7: tangential E
$$\mathbf E^\parallel_{\text{above}}-\mathbf E^\parallel_{\text{below}}=\mathbf 0 \tag{7.71}$$

For (iv), with $\hat{\mathbf n}$ the unit normal pointing up, $I_{f,\text{enc}}=\mathbf K_f\cdot(\hat{\mathbf n}\times\mathbf l)=(\mathbf K_f\times\hat{\mathbf n})\cdot\mathbf l$. Only a surface current contributes, since a volume current gives zero in the thin limit (Fig. 7.50):

{{FIG:m2-sec3-surface-loop}}
Result 3.8: tangential H
$$\mathbf H^\parallel_{\text{above}}-\mathbf H^\parallel_{\text{below}}=\mathbf K_f\times\hat{\mathbf n} \tag{7.72}$$

So the parallel components of $\mathbf H$ jump by an amount proportional to the free surface current.

4. Linear media

Result 3.9
$$\epsilon_{\text{above}}E^\perp_{\text{above}}-\epsilon_{\text{below}}E^\perp_{\text{below}}=\sigma_f,\qquad B^\perp_{\text{above}}-B^\perp_{\text{below}}=0 \tag{7.73}$$
$$E^\parallel_{\text{above}}-E^\parallel_{\text{below}}=0,\qquad\frac{1}{\mu_{\text{above}}}B^\parallel_{\text{above}}-\frac{1}{\mu_{\text{below}}}B^\parallel_{\text{below}}=\mathbf K_f\times\hat{\mathbf n}$$

With no free charge or current at the interface (Eq. 7.74), the right-hand sides are zero. These conditions give reflection and refraction (Chapter 9).
