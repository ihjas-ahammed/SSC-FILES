MODULE 2: MODEL ANSWERS
For checking your work. Each answer shows the key step and the result.

Section A. A1 $vBl$. A2 False: its line integral round a closed loop is not zero. A3 $\nabla\times\vec E=-\partial\vec B/\partial t$. A4 $\vec J=\sigma\vec E$. A5 $\vec J_d=\varepsilon_0\,\partial\vec E/\partial t$. A6 $u=\tfrac12\vec H\cdot\vec B$.

Section B. B1 The magnetic force $q\vec v\times\vec B$ pushes positive charges along the bar, so an emf $\oint(\vec v\times\vec B)\cdot d\vec l$ appears. B2 Static-charge fields are conservative ($\nabla\times\vec E=0$); the induced field has $\nabla\times\vec E=-\partial\vec B/\partial t\neq0$. B3 Through the gap $I=0$, so $\mu_0 I$ alone gives zero; Maxwell added $\mu_0\varepsilon_0\,d\Phi_E/dt$, which fixes it. B4 Lenz: the induced emf opposes the change in flux. B5 $\oint\vec B\cdot d\vec l=\mu_0 I_{enc}+\mu_0\varepsilon_0\,\frac{d}{dt}\int\vec E\cdot d\vec a$.

Section C. C1 emf $=Blv=0.4\times0.5\times2=0.4$ V; $I=0.4/0.2=2$ A; $F=BIl=0.4\times2\times0.5=0.4$ N; power $=Fv=0.8$ W $=I^2R$. C2 $C=\varepsilon_0 A/d=8.85\times10^{-11}$ F; conduction current $I=C\,dV/dt=4.43\,\mu$A; displacement current between plates is the same, $4.43\,\mu$A. C3 $B=\mu_0 nI=1.257\times10^{-3}$ T; $u=B^2/2\mu_0=0.628$ J/m$^3$; volume $=\pi r^2 l=6.28\times10^{-4}$ m$^3$; $U=3.95\times10^{-4}$ J (check: $\tfrac12LI^2$ with $L=1.97\times10^{-4}$ H). C4 Inside, $E=\tfrac{r}{2}\,dB/dt=1.0$ mV/m at $r=2$ cm; outside, $E=\dfrac{R^2}{2r}\,dB/dt=1.56$ mV/m at $r=8$ cm.

Section D. D1 Gauss in $\vec E$ with $\rho=\rho_b+\rho_f$ and $\rho_b=-\nabla\cdot\vec P$ gives $\nabla\cdot\vec D=\rho_f$, with $\vec D=\varepsilon_0\vec E+\vec P$. Ampère–Maxwell with $\vec J=\vec J_f+\nabla\times\vec M+\partial\vec P/\partial t$ gives $\nabla\times\vec H=\vec J_f+\partial\vec D/\partial t$, with $\vec H=\vec B/\mu_0-\vec M$. Boundary conditions: $D^\perp_{above}-D^\perp_{below}=\sigma_f$ and $\vec H^\parallel_{above}-\vec H^\parallel_{below}=\vec K_f\times\hat n$. D2 Transformer emf comes from $\partial\vec B/\partial t$ with the loop fixed; motional emf from $\vec v\times\vec B$ with the loop moving. Both follow from $\oint\vec E_{\rm loop}\cdot d\vec l$ in the frame of the loop, and the flux rule $\mathcal E=-d\Phi/dt$ covers both.
