MODULE 3: MODEL ANSWERS
For checking your work. Each answer shows the key step and the result.

Section A. A1 $\lambda=2\pi/k$. A2 $v=\nu\lambda$. A3 True: a circularly polarised wave has equal components, $90^\circ$ apart in phase. A4 $\hat n=\cos\theta\,\hat x+\sin\theta\,\hat y$ (Eq. 9.38). A5 $n=\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu/\varepsilon_0\mu_0}=c/v$. A6 $P=I/c$ (Eq. 9.65 for the absorber).

Section B. B7 $\tilde f=\tilde A e^{i(kz-\omega t)}$ with $\tilde A=Ae^{i\delta}$; the physical wave is $f=\mathrm{Re}(\tilde f)$ (Eqs. 9.16–9.18). B8 $\langle u\rangle=\tfrac12\varepsilon_0E_0^2$, since the average of $\cos^2$ over a cycle is $\tfrac12$ (Eq. 9.61). B9 $v=c/n$; $B_0=E_0/v=nE_0/c$ (Eqs. 9.48, 9.69, 9.74). B10 $R=\left(\frac{n_1-n_2}{n_1+n_2}\right)^2=0.04$, and $T=1-R=0.96$ (Eqs. 9.87, 9.88). B11 $\tan\theta_B=n_2/n_1=1.5$, so $\theta_B\approx56.3^\circ$ (Eq. 9.113).

Section C. C12 $v=c/n=2.0\times10^8$ m/s; vacuum wavelength $\lambda_0=c/\nu=6.0\times10^{-7}$ m; wavelength in glass $\lambda=v/\nu=4.0\times10^{-7}$ m. C13 Absorber: $P=I/c=1300/3.0\times10^8=4.3\times10^{-6}$ Pa. Reflector: $P=2I/c=8.7\times10^{-6}$ Pa. C14 $B_0=E_0/c=3.3\times10^{-7}$ T; $I=\tfrac12c\varepsilon_0E_0^2=13.3$ W/m$^2$; $\langle u\rangle=I/c=4.4\times10^{-8}$ J/m$^3$. C15 Reflection angle $=40^\circ$. Refraction: $\sin\theta_T=\sin40^\circ/1.50=0.429$, so $\theta_T\approx25.4^\circ$.

Section D. D16 Curl of Faraday’s law and the Ampère–Maxwell law with $\nabla\cdot\vec E=0$ give $\nabla^2\vec E=\mu\varepsilon\,\partial^2\vec E/\partial t^2$, so $v=1/\sqrt{\varepsilon\mu}=c/n$ (Eqs. 9.68–9.69). For a plane wave, $\nabla\times\vec E=-\partial\vec B/\partial t$ gives $kE_0=\omega B_0$, so $B_0=E_0/v$. The average Poynting vector is $\langle S\rangle=E_0^2/(2\mu v)=\tfrac12v\varepsilon E_0^2$, using $1/\mu=v^2\varepsilon$ (Eqs. 9.72–9.74). D17 Matching the exponential factors at the boundary gives $\theta_I=\theta_R$ and $\sin\theta_I/\sin\theta_T=n_2/n_1$ (Eqs. 9.100–9.101). For polarisation in the plane of incidence the Fresnel reflection amplitude has $\alpha=\beta$ at $\theta_B$, with $\sin^2\theta_B=(1-\beta^2)/((n_1/n_2)^2-\beta^2)$; for $\mu_1\approx\mu_2$, $\tan\theta_B=n_2/n_1$ (Eqs. 9.110–9.113). The reflected beam at $\theta_B$ is polarised perpendicular to the plane of incidence (footnote 19).
