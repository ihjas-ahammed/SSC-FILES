MODULE III: MODEL ANSWERS
For checking your work. Each answer shows the key step and the result.

Section A. 1. An intrinsic semiconductor is pure, with $n=p=n_i$. An extrinsic semiconductor is doped with donors or acceptors, so one carrier type dominates (book §7.1 to §7.3). 2. $np=n_i^2$ (book Eq. 7.34). 3. $v_d=\mu\mathcal{E}$ (book Eq. 7.3). 4. $\sigma=en_i(\mu_n+\mu_p)$ (book Eq. 7.9).

Section B. 5. $E_F=E_i=(E_c+E_v)/2$ (book Eq. 7.31). 6. $n=N_c\exp[-(E_c-E_F)/kT]$ (book Eq. 7.20). 7. $\Delta E=E_c-E_d$ (book §7.6a; Eq. 7.44). 8. $p\cong N_a^-$ (book Eq. 7.46).

Section C. 9. $J=nev_d$ and $v_d=\mu\mathcal{E}$ with $\mu=e\tau/m$ give $J=(ne^2\tau/m)\mathcal{E}$; compare with $J=\sigma\mathcal{E}$ (book Eqs. 7.3 to 7.6). 10. Use $n=N_d^+$ (Eq. 7.38) and $N_d^+=N_d\exp[-(E_F-E_d)/kT]$ (Eq. 7.40) with $n=N_c\exp[-(E_c-E_F)/kT]$ (Eq. 7.20); take logarithms (Eq. 7.41). 11. $np=n_i^2=N_cN_v\exp(-E_g/kT)$ (Eqs. 7.32 to 7.34), a constant at fixed $T$. Its value depends only on $E_g$ and $T$ for an intrinsic material. 12. Equate $N_c\exp[-(E_c-E_F)/kT]=N_v\exp[-(E_F-E_v)/kT]$ (Eqs. 7.20, 7.26), take logarithms, and use $N_c$, $N_v$ (Eqs. 7.21, 7.27) (Eq. 7.30). 13. Rises with temperature across the ionisation region to $E_F$ near $E_d$, then falls towards $E_i$ as $T$ rises; the formula (7.41) fails at $T=0$ and at very high $T$ (book §7.6a, Fig. 7.8). 14. From $\sigma=CT^{3/2}\exp(-E_g/2kT)$ (Eq. 7.11), $\ln\sigma=-(E_g/2k)(1/T)+\tfrac32\ln T+$ const (Eq. 7.12). The slope is $-E_g/2k$, so $E_g=-2k\times$slope (book Fig. 7.5).

Section D. 15. Integrate $dn=D(E)f(E)dE$ with $D$ and $f$ (Eqs. 7.13 to 7.18), use $kT\ll E-E_F$ and $x=(E-E_c)/kT$ (book §7.5.1); $n=N_c\exp[-(E_c-E_F)/kT]$ with $N_c=2(2\pi m_n^*kT/h^2)^{3/2}$ (Eqs. 7.19 to 7.21). 16. Integrate $dp=D(E)[1-f(E)]dE$ over $E17. Ionisation region: $\sigma$ rises as donors ionise (point A to B). Saturation region: $\sigma$ falls with the mobility (B to C). Intrinsic region: $\sigma$ rises as $n_i$ grows (C to D). Middle region: $n\cong N_d^+$ (Eq. 7.38), so $\sigma=en\mu_n\cong eN_d\mu_n$ (Eq. 7.45).
