7.6(a) $n$-type semiconductor

At 0 K donor levels are occupied; with rising $T$, donors ionise. The Fermi level lies between $E_d$ and $E_c$ (Fig. 7.7).

{{FIG:m3-f77}}

For $E_F$ a few $kT$ below $E_c$, $n=N_c\exp[-(E_c-E_F)/kT]$ (7.20). Charge balance:

$$n=N_d^++p\tag{7.37}$$

Neglecting thermal holes,

$$n=N_d^+\tag{7.38}$$

so the minority carriers follow from $np=n_i^2$:

$$p=\frac{n_i^2}{N_d^+}\tag{7.39}$$

With

$$N_d^+=N_d\exp\left[-\left(\frac{E_F-E_d}{kT}\right)\right]\tag{7.40}$$

taking logarithms gives

$$E_F=\frac{E_d+E_c}{2}+\frac{kT}{2}\ln\left(\frac{N_d}{N_c}\right)\tag{7.41}$$

Valid at moderate temperatures only. It fails at $T=0$ K ($N_c\propto T^{3/2}$) and at very high $T$. As $T$ rises, $E_F$ falls through $E_d$ to $E_i$ (Fig. 7.8).

{{FIG:m3-f78}}
$$n=n_i\exp\left(\frac{E_F-E_i}{kT}\right)\tag{7.42}$$
$$n=(N_dN_c)^{1/2}\exp\left(\frac{E_d-E_c}{2kT}\right)\tag{7.43}$$
$$n=(N_dN_c)^{1/2}\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta E}{2kT}\right)\tag{7.44}$$

$\Delta E=E_c-E_d$ is the donor ionisation energy. At moderate $T$, $n\propto\sqrt{N_d}$.

$$\sigma=en\mu_n\cong eN_d^+\mu_n\tag{7.45}$$

Fig. 7.9 (n-type Ge): (i) from about 10 K (A), $\sigma$ rises as donors ionise, peaking at B (about 50 K) when all donors are ionised; (ii) from B to about room temperature (C), $\sigma$ falls because the mobility falls; (iii) from C to D, $\sigma$ rises sharply as the intrinsic conductivity grows faster than the mobility falls.

{{FIG:m3-f79}}

7.6(b) $p$-type semiconductor

Acceptors $E_a$ lie above $E_v$. Some become ionised by taking valence electrons, creating holes. Charge balance $p=n+N_a^-$ with $n\ll N_a^-$ gives

$$p\cong N_a^-\tag{7.46}$$

where

$$N_a^-=\frac{N_a}{1+\exp\left(\dfrac{E_a-E_F}{kT}\right)}\tag{7.47}$$

For $E_a-E_F\gg kT$,

$$N_a^-\cong N_a\exp\left[\frac{E_F-E_a}{kT}\right]\tag{7.48}$$

Equating with (7.26):

{{FIG:m3-f710}}
$$E_F=\frac{E_a+E_v}{2}-\frac{kT}{2}\ln\left(\frac{N_a}{N_v}\right)\tag{7.49}$$

Valid at moderate $T$. $E_F$ rises with $T$ towards $E_i$ (Fig. 7.8). With (7.26) and (7.28):

$$p=n_i\exp\left(\frac{E_i-E_F}{kT}\right)\tag{7.50}$$
$$\sigma_p=ep\mu_p\cong eN_a^-\mu_p\tag{7.51}$$

The conductivity grows with either impurity concentration. For $10^{20}$ to $10^{22}\ \mathrm{m}^{-3}$ dopant, $\rho\approx10^{-3}$ to $10^{-1}$ ohm-m. Degenerate semiconductors ($10^{23}$ to $10^{24}\ \mathrm{m}^{-3}$) approach metallic conductivity.

7.6(c) Mixed semiconductor

$$N_d^++p=N_a^-+n\tag{7.52}$$

For $N_d^+=N_a^-$, $p=n=n_i$ (intrinsic behaviour). For $N_d\neq N_a$ the sample is $n$-type or $p$-type according to the larger concentration.

Example 7.1

Intrinsic Si at 300 K: $\mu_n=0.135$, $\mu_p=0.048$ m$^2$/V-s, $n_i=1.5\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{m}^{-3}$. $\sigma_i=en_i(\mu_n+\mu_p)=4.39\times10^{-4}\ (\Omega\text{-m})^{-1}$. After doping with $10^{23}$ P atoms per m$^3$: $n\cong10^{23}\ \mathrm{m}^{-3}$, $p=n_i^2/n=2.25\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{m}^{-3}$, $\sigma=en\mu_n=21.6\times10^{2}\ (\Omega\text{-m})^{-1}$, and $E_F-E_i=kT\ln(n/n_i)=0.406$ eV (Fig. 7.11).

{{FIG:m3-f711}}

Example 7.2

Intrinsic GaAs: $\mu_n=0.85$, $\mu_p=0.04$ m$^2$/V-s; $m_n^*=0.068\,m_0$, $m_p^*=0.5\,m_0$; $E_g=1.43$ eV at 300 K. From (7.36), $n_i=1.94\times10^{12}\ \mathrm{m}^{-3}$. Then $\sigma=en_i(\mu_n+\mu_p)=2.76\times10^{-7}\ (\Omega\text{-m})^{-1}$.

Example 7.3

For intrinsic material, $\sigma=CT^{3/2}\exp(-E_g/2kT)$ with $C=2e\mu(2\pi k/h^2)^{3/2}(m_n^*m_p^*)^{3/4}$. With $\sigma_1/\sigma_2=(T_1/T_2)^{3/2}\exp\{(E_g/2k)(1/T_2-1/T_1)\}$, $T_1=293$ K, $\rho_1=4.5$ ohm-m, $T_2=305$ K, $\rho_2=2.0$ ohm-m, the book gives $E_g=1.54\times10^{-19}$ J $=0.96$ eV.
