1. Divisors of zero and cancellation

Example 23.1. In $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$, $x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3)$ and $(2)(6) = (3)(4) = 0$, so the equation has the roots $2, 3, 6, 11$.

Definition 23.2 Nonzero $a, b$ in a ring $R$ with $ab = 0$ are divisors of $0$ (or $0$ divisors).

In $\mathbb{Z}_{12}$ the divisors of $0$ are $2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10$. A unit is never a divisor of $0$.

Theorem 23.3 For $m \in \mathbb{Z}_n$, either $m = 0$; or $\gcd(m, n) = 1$ and $m$ is a unit; or $\gcd(m, n) \ne 1$ and $m$ is a $0$ divisor.

Proof. If $d = \gcd(m, n) \ne 1$ and $m \ne 0$, then $m \cdot (n/d) = (m/d)n = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_n$, with $m$ and $n/d$ nonzero. If $\gcd(m, n) = 1$, write $an + bm = 1$, $b = nq + r$, $0 \le r \le n - 1$; then $rm = 1 - n(a + qm) = 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_n$. ◆

Example 23.4. In $\mathbb{Z}_{20}$ the units are $1, 3, 7, 9, 11, 13, 17, 19$ and the $0$ divisors are $2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16, 18$.

Corollary 23.5 If $p$ is prime, every nonzero element of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is a unit; $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is a field with no divisors of $0$.

Proof. Apply Theorem 23.3 with $\gcd(m, p) = 1$ for $0 < m \le p - 1$. ■

Theorem 23.6 Cancellation holds in a ring $R$ if and only if $R$ has no divisors of $0$.

Proof. If $ab = 0$ with $a \ne 0$, cancellation applied to $ab = a0$ gives $b = 0$. Conversely, if $ab = ac$ with $a \ne 0$ and no divisors of $0$, then $a(b - c) = 0$ gives $b = c$. ◆

In a ring with no divisors of $0$, $ax = b$ with $a \ne 0$ has at most one solution. In a commutative ring $b/a$ denotes $a^{-1}b = ba^{-1}$.

2. Integral domains

Definition 23.7 An integral domain is a commutative ring with unity $1 \ne 0$ and no divisors of $0$.

Example 23.8. $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}_p$ ($p$ prime) are integral domains; $\mathbb{Z}_n$ is not if $n$ is composite; a direct product $R \times S$ of nonzero rings is not, since $(r, 0)(0, s) = (0, 0)$.

Example 23.9. $\mathbb{Z}_2$ is an integral domain, but $M_2(\mathbb{Z}_2)$ has divisors of $0$: $\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix} = 0$. Every field is an integral domain.

Theorem 23.11 Every finite integral domain is a field.

Proof. For $a \ne 0$ the map $x \mapsto ax$ is one-to-one by cancellation, hence onto since $R$ is finite; so $ab = 1$ for some $b$. ■

3. Characteristic

Definition 23.12 The least positive $n$ with $n \cdot a = 0$ for all $a \in R$ is the characteristic of $R$; if none exists, $R$ has characteristic $0$.

Exercise 35 (named in the text after Definition 23.12). Show that the characteristic of an integral domain $D$ must be either $0$ or a prime $p$. [Hint: If the characteristic of $D$ is $mn$, consider $(m \cdot 1)(n \cdot 1)$ in $D$.]

Example 23.13. $\mathbb{Z}_n$ has characteristic $n$; $\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Q}, \mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}$ have characteristic $0$.

Theorem 23.14 In a ring with unity, the characteristic is $0$ if $n \cdot 1 \ne 0$ for all $n$, and otherwise the least $n$ with $n \cdot 1 = 0$.

Proof. If $n \cdot 1 = 0$, then $n \cdot a = a(n \cdot 1) = 0$ for all $a$. ◆
