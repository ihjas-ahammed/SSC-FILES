### P: facts you must already own (with a 1-line refresher)
- P01 Plus Two: definite integral as AREA under $y=f(x)$ for $f\ge0$, and as antiderivative difference $F(b)-F(a)$; $\int x^n=\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}$; $\int\sin=-\cos$. This module PROVES why both views agree (Riemann sums = area; FTC = antiderivative).
- P02 $\Sigma$ notation: $\sum_{i=1}^nc\,a_i=c\sum a_i$; $\sum(a_i+b_i)=\sum a_i+\sum b_i$; $\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-x_{i-1})=x_n-x_0$.
- P03 Sums: $1+2+\cdots+n=\frac{n(n+1)}2$ (so $1+\cdots+(n-1)=\frac{n(n-1)}2$); $\sum k^2=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6$; $\sum_{k\ge1}\frac1{2^k}=1$.
- P04 Real Analysis I: $\sup,\inf$; completeness; $\varepsilon$-characterisation $s=\sup A\iff$ upper bound and $\forall\varepsilon\ \exists a:\ a>s-\varepsilon$ (used for "$\le$ for every $\varepsilon$ $\Rightarrow$ $\le$").
- P05 Triangle inequality $|a+b|\le|a|+|b|$; reverse $|a+b|\ge|a|-|b|$; $|a|<\varepsilon\iff-\varepsilon<a<\varepsilon$; "$x\le y+\varepsilon\ \forall\varepsilon>0\Rightarrow x\le y$".
- P06 Cauchy sequences converge in $\mathbb R$ (B 3.5.5); a sequence with $|a_m-a_n|<1/n$ for $m>n$ is Cauchy.
- P07 Continuity facts (Module 1): continuous on closed bounded $[a,b]$ $\Rightarrow$ bounded (5.3.2), attains max and min (5.3.4); IVT/preservation of intervals (5.3.10: image of an interval is an interval); composition of continuous functions is continuous.
- P08 Uniform continuity theorem 5.4.3 (Module 1): $f$ continuous on $[a,b]\Rightarrow\forall\varepsilon\,\exists\delta$: $|u-v|<\delta\Rightarrow|f(u)-f(v)|<\varepsilon$ for ALL $u,v\in[a,b]$.
- P09 Step function (B 5.4.9): $[a,b]$ is a finite union of non-overlapping intervals on each of which the function is constant. Lipschitz (B 5.4.4): $|f(x)-f(u)|\le K|x-u|$.
- P10 Monotone functions have a countable set of discontinuities (B 5.6.4, quoted without proof in 7.3.13(b); you may cite it).
- P11 Module 2 derivative rules: differentiable $\Rightarrow$ continuous (6.1.2); product rule (6.1.3c); chain rule (6.1.6).
- P12 Mean Value Theorem (B 6.2.4, Module 2): $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$ $\Rightarrow\exists u\in(a,b)$: $f(b)-f(a)=f\,\prime(u)(b-a)$. This is the ONE place Module 2 enters (FTC1 proof).
- P13 Countable sets: can be listed $r_1,r_2,\dots$; $\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]$ is countable; $\mathbb N$ unbounded ($\exists n>1/\varepsilon$).
- P14 Dirichlet and Thomae functions (B 5.1.6(g),(h)): Dirichlet nowhere continuous; Thomae $h$ continuous exactly at irrationals of $[0,1]$.

### M: tools built from zero
- M01 Index shifting: $\sum_{i=1}^n(a_i-a_{i-1})=a_n-a_0$ (everything cancels) - the telescoping sum; example $\sum_{i=1}^n\tfrac12(x_i^2-x_{i-1}^2)=\tfrac12(x_n^2-x_0^2)$.
- M02 Partitions and mesh: $n$ points chosen in order; the norm is the LARGEST gap, not the number of pieces. Equal partition: $\lVert P\rVert=\frac{b-a}n$. Norm $\to0$ is stronger than $n\to\infty$ (F04).
- M03 Tags: any point of the subinterval, endpoints allowed (an endpoint can be the tag of two neighbours). Same partition + different tags = different sums, all within $\varepsilon$ of $L$ if integrable.
- M04 Bounding a sum: $|\sum a_ib_i|\le\sum|a_i||b_i|\le\max|a_i|\sum|b_i|$. Typical: $|S(f;\dot P)-S(g;\dot P)|\le\sup|f-g|\cdot(b-a)$ and, if $f=g$ except at one point $c$, the sums differ in at most TWO terms (c can be a tag of two intervals), each of size $\le|f(c)-g(c)|\,\lVert P\rVert$.
- M05 Telescoping with monotonicity/FTC: $F(b)-F(a)=\sum[F(x_i)-F(x_{i-1})]$; $\sum_{k}(f(x_k)-f(x_{k-1}))=f(b)-f(a)$.
- M06 Step functions/indicators: $\varphi_J$; $g=2\varphi_{[0,1]}+3\varphi_{(1,3]}$; integral = $\sum$ value $\times$ length.
- M07 Null-set trick: cover the $k$-th point by an interval of length $\varepsilon/2^k$; $\sum\varepsilon/2^k=\varepsilon$.
- M08 "$\varepsilon/2$ argument and min of $\delta$": each of two (or three) estimates holds when norm $<\delta_i$; take $\delta=\min\delta_i$; add errors to get $<\varepsilon$. Always say "since $\varepsilon$ is arbitrary".
- M09 Choosing $\delta$ explicitly: write the final estimate as $C\delta$ (e.g. $|S-8|\le5\delta$) then $\delta<\varepsilon/C$.
- M10 Splitting a Riemann sum by tags: $\dot P=\dot P_1\cup\dot P_2$ (tags in a bad set $E$ / elsewhere); the union of the subintervals with tags in $[c_1,c_2]$ lies between $[c_1+\lVert P\rVert,c_2-\lVert P\rVert]$ and $[c_1-\lVert P\rVert,c_2+\lVert P\rVert]$ (B Ex 7.1.4-5). Count: a finite bad set of $n$ points meets at most $2n$ subintervals.

### S-7.1.2 Uniqueness of the integral [3] (K04)
KNOW: $L_1,L_2$ both satisfy Def 7.1.1. WANT: $L_1=L_2$. TOOL: triangle inequality.
1. Fix $\varepsilon>0$; get $\delta_1$ for $L_1$ and $\delta_2$ for $L_2$, both with tolerance $\varepsilon/2$ -- WHY: the definition applies with any positive tolerance.
2. $\delta=\min\{\delta_1,\delta_2\}$; take ANY tagged partition $\dot P$ with $\lVert P\rVert<\delta$ -- WHY: one partition must serve both; such $\dot P$ exists (equal subintervals).
3. $|L_1-L_2|\le|L_1-S(f;\dot P)|+|S(f;\dot P)-L_2|<\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/2=\varepsilon$ -- WHY: add and subtract $S$.
4. $\varepsilon$ arbitrary, so $L_1=L_2$ -- WHY: a non-negative number below every $\varepsilon>0$ is $0$.

### S-7.1.3 Finite change [3] (K06)
KNOW: $g\in\mathcal R$, $f=g$ off one point $c$. WANT: $f\in\mathcal R$, same $\int$. TOOL: M04.
1. Finite case follows from one point by induction -- WHY: change the points one at a time.
2. $S(f;\dot P)-S(g;\dot P)$ has non-zero terms only where a tag equals $c$ -- WHY: elsewhere $f=g$.
3. $c$ is a tag of at most two subintervals (when it is a partition point), each of length $\le\lVert P\rVert$: $|S(f)-S(g)|\le2(|f(c)|+|g(c)|)\lVert P\rVert$ -- WHY: M04.
4. $\delta_1<\varepsilon/[4(|f(c)|+|g(c)|)]$ makes this $<\varepsilon/2$; $\delta_2$ makes $|S(g)-L|<\varepsilon/2$; $\delta=\min$ -- WHY: $\varepsilon/2$ argument. Conclude $|S(f)-L|<\varepsilon$.

### S-7.1.4 Computing from the definition (K05) [3 each]
(a) constant $k$: every $S=k\sum\Delta x_i=k(b-a)$ (telescoping, M01), so $|S-k(b-a)|=0<\varepsilon$ for any $\delta$ (take $\delta_\varepsilon=1$).
(b) $g=2$ on $[0,1]$, $3$ on $(1,3]$: split $\dot P=\dot P_1\cup\dot P_2$ by tag in $[0,1]$ or $(1,3]$ -- WHY: $g$ is constant on each; $U_1=\bigcup$ of intervals of $\dot P_1$ satisfies $[0,1-\delta]\subseteq U_1\subseteq[0,1+\delta]$ (M10) so $2(1-\delta)\le S_1\le2(1+\delta)$; likewise $3(2-\delta)\le S_2\le3(2+\delta)$; sum: $|S-8|\le5\delta<\varepsilon$ if $\delta=\varepsilon/10$. Answer 8.
(c) $h(x)=x$ on $[0,1]$: midpoint tags $q_i$ give $h(q_i)\Delta x_i=\tfrac12(x_i^2-x_{i-1}^2)$ so $S(h;\dot Q)=\tfrac12$ (telescoping); for any tags $|t_i-q_i|<\delta$ so $|S(h;\dot P)-\tfrac12|<\delta\sum\Delta x_i=\delta$; $\delta_\varepsilon=\varepsilon$. Answer $\tfrac12$.
(d) $G(1/n)=1/n$, $0$ elsewhere: $E=\{G\ge\varepsilon\}$ finite, $n$ points, $\delta=\varepsilon/(2n)$; tags in $E$ meet $\le2n$ intervals of length $<\delta$, $G\le1$: $<\varepsilon$; others $G<\varepsilon$, total length $\le1$: $<\varepsilon$; $0\le S<2\varepsilon$. $\int G=0$.

### S-7.1.5 Linearity and monotonicity [6] (K07)
1. $S(kf)=kS(f)$, $S(f+g)=S(f)+S(g)$, $f\le g\Rightarrow S(f)\le S(g)$ for every $\dot P$ -- WHY: Riemann sums are finite sums of values times positive lengths (P02).
2. (b) Given $\varepsilon$, $\delta=\min$ of the two $\delta_{\varepsilon/2}$; $|S(f+g)-(\int f+\int g)|\le|S(f)-\int f|+|S(g)-\int g|<\varepsilon$ -- WHY: triangle inequality; so $f+g\in\mathcal R$ with the stated integral.
3. (c) $\int f-\tfrac\varepsilon2<S(f)\le S(g)<\int g+\tfrac\varepsilon2$ so $\int f<\int g+\varepsilon$ for all $\varepsilon$ -- WHY: step 1 sandwiched between the two tolerance statements; hence $\int f\le\int g$ (P05). Only "$\le$", never "$<$".

### S-7.1.6 Integrable $\Rightarrow$ bounded [6] (K08)
KNOW: $f\in\mathcal R$, integral $L$, suppose unbounded. WANT: contradiction.
1. $\delta$ for $\varepsilon=1$: $\lVert P\rVert<\delta\Rightarrow|S(f;\dot P)|<|L|+1$ -- WHY: Def 7.1.1.
2. Fix a partition $Q$ with $\lVert Q\rVert<\delta$ -- WHY: only tags are free now.
3. $f$ is unbounded on some $[x_{k-1},x_k]$ -- WHY: if bounded on each of the finitely many pieces by $M_i$, it is bounded by $\max M_i$.
4. Tags $t_i=x_i$ ($i\ne k$); choose $t_k$ with $|f(t_k)|\Delta x_k>|L|+1+|\sum_{i\ne k}f(t_i)\Delta x_i|$ -- WHY: unbounded $f$ on that piece lets $|f(t_k)|$ be as large as we like.
5. $|S|\ge|f(t_k)|\Delta x_k-|\sum_{i\ne k}\cdots|>|L|+1$, contradiction (reverse triangle inequality). Hence bounded.

### S-7.1.7 Thomae is integrable [6, proof optional] (K09)
1. $\varepsilon>0$; $E=\{x:h(x)\ge\varepsilon/2\}$ is FINITE with $n$ points -- WHY: $h(m/n)=1/n\ge\varepsilon/2$ forces $n\le2/\varepsilon$, finitely many fractions. Example $\varepsilon/2=\tfrac15$: 11 points.
2. $\delta=\varepsilon/(4n)$; $\dot P$ with $\lVert P\rVert<\delta$; $\dot P_1$ = tags in $E$, $\dot P_2$ = other tags -- WHY: M10.
3. $\dot P_1$: at most $2n$ intervals, total length $\le2n\delta=\varepsilon/2$, $0<h\le1$: $S(h;\dot P_1)\le\varepsilon/2$.
4. $\dot P_2$: $h<\varepsilon/2$ and total length $\le1$: $S<\varepsilon/2$.
5. $0\le S(h;\dot P)<\varepsilon$ so $\int_0^1h=0$.

### S-7.2.1 Cauchy criterion [10] (K10)
(⇒) 1. $\eta_\varepsilon=\delta_{\varepsilon/2}$; both sums within $\varepsilon/2$ of $L$ -- triangle inequality gives $<\varepsilon$.
(⇐) 2. $\delta_n$ for tolerance $1/n$, and may assume $\delta_n\ge\delta_{n+1}$ (replace by running minimum) -- WHY: then a partition fine for $\delta_m$ is fine for all earlier $\delta_n$.
3. Choose $\dot P_n$ with $\lVert P_n\rVert<\delta_n$ -- WHY: one for each $n$ (equal subintervals).
4. $m>n$: $|S(\dot P_m)-S(\dot P_n)|<1/n$ -- WHY: both norms $<\delta_n$. So $(S(\dot P_n))$ is a Cauchy sequence.
5. It converges to some $A$ (B 3.5.5, P06); let $m\to\infty$: $|S(\dot P_n)-A|\le1/n$.
6. Given $\varepsilon$ choose $K>2/\varepsilon$. For $\lVert Q\rVert<\delta_K$: $|S(Q)-A|\le|S(Q)-S(\dot P_K)|+|S(\dot P_K)-A|<1/K+1/K<\varepsilon$ -- WHY: hypothesis for the pair $(Q,\dot P_K)$ and step 5. So $f\in\mathcal R$, $\int f=A$.

### S-7.2.2(b) Dirichlet not integrable [6, optional example] (K11)
1. Negate the criterion: $\exists\varepsilon_0>0$ with, for every $\eta>0$, tagged $\dot P,\dot Q$ of norm $<\eta$ and $|S(\dot P)-S(\dot Q)|\ge\varepsilon_0$ -- WHY: logical negation (quantifiers flip).
2. $\varepsilon_0=\tfrac12$. Take equal partition of norm $<\eta$; rational tags: $S=\sum1\cdot\Delta x_i=1$; irrational tags: $S=0$ -- WHY: density of rationals and irrationals in every interval.
3. Difference $=1\ge\tfrac12$, so not integrable. (Same for $h=x+1$ on $\mathbb Q$, $0$ elsewhere: $S\ge1$ vs $0$.)

### S-7.2.3 Squeeze theorem [10] (K12)
(⇒) $\alpha_\varepsilon=\omega_\varepsilon=f$.
(⇐) 1. $\delta_\varepsilon$ such that $\lVert P\rVert<\delta_\varepsilon\Rightarrow|S(\alpha)-\int\alpha|<\varepsilon$ and $|S(\omega)-\int\omega|<\varepsilon$ -- WHY: both in $\mathcal R$; min of two deltas.
2. $S(\alpha)\le S(f)\le S(\omega)$ -- WHY: $\alpha\le f\le\omega$ pointwise, sums of positive-length products. Hence $\int\alpha-\varepsilon<S(f;\dot P)<\int\omega+\varepsilon$.
3. Same for $\dot Q$; subtract: $|S(f;\dot P)-S(f;\dot Q)|<\int(\omega-\alpha)+2\varepsilon<3\varepsilon$ -- WHY: $\int\omega-\int\alpha=\int(\omega-\alpha)<\varepsilon$ by linearity.
4. Cauchy criterion (7.2.1) $\Rightarrow f\in\mathcal R$.

### S-7.2.4 / 7.2.5 Step functions [6] (K13, K14)
1. Lemma: $\varphi_{[c,d]}$: Ex 7.1.13 -- for $\lVert P\rVert<\delta$ the union of intervals with tag in $[c,d]$ contains $[c+\delta,d-\delta]$ and lies in $[c-\delta,d+\delta]$ (M10) so $d-c-2\delta\le S\le d-c+2\delta$; $\delta=\varepsilon/4$ (with $\alpha=1$).
2. $(c,d),[c,d),(c,d]$ differ from $[c,d]$ at $\le2$ points: 7.1.3 -- same integrability and integral $d-c$.
3. Any step function is $\sum_{j=1}^mk_j\varphi_{J_j}$ (B Ex 7.2.5: values $k_j$ on the intervals) -- WHY: pieces do not overlap.
4. Linearity (7.1.5) $\Rightarrow\varphi\in\mathcal R$, $\int\varphi=\sum k_j(d_j-c_j)$. Example: $2\cdot1+3\cdot2=8$.

### S-7.2.6 Squeeze applied to $h(x)=x$ [3] (K12 example)
$\alpha_n=\frac{k-1}n$, $\omega_n=\frac kn$ on $[\frac{k-1}n,\frac kn)$ ($k<n$), last piece $\frac{n-1}n$ and $1$: $\int\alpha_n=\frac1{n^2}(0+1+\cdots+(n-1))=\frac12(1-\frac1n)$, $\int\omega_n=\frac12(1+\frac1n)$, difference $\frac1n<\varepsilon$ for $n>1/\varepsilon$. Both are $\frac12$ in the limit, so $\int_0^1x\,dx=\frac12$.

### S-7.2.7 Continuous $\Rightarrow$ integrable [10] (K15)
KNOW: $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$. WANT: step functions squeezing $f$. TOOL: UCT + Max-Min + Squeeze.
1. UCT (5.4.3): $\exists\delta$: $|u-v|<\delta\Rightarrow|f(u)-f(v)|<\frac\varepsilon{b-a}$ -- WHY: continuity on a closed bounded interval is uniform; one $\delta$ for the whole interval is what lets us treat all pieces together.
2. Partition with $\lVert P\rVert<\delta$ -- WHY: so each piece is shorter than $\delta$.
3. Max-Min (5.3.4): $u_i,v_i\in I_i$ with $f(u_i)=\min_{I_i}f$, $f(v_i)=\max_{I_i}f$ -- WHY: $f$ continuous on the closed bounded $I_i$.
4. $\alpha(x)=f(u_i)$, $\omega(x)=f(v_i)$ on $[x_{i-1},x_i)$ (last piece closed) -- WHY: step functions are integrable (7.2.5) and $\alpha\le f\le\omega$.
5. $|v_i-u_i|\le\Delta x_i<\delta$ so $f(v_i)-f(u_i)<\frac\varepsilon{b-a}$; $\int(\omega-\alpha)=\sum(f(v_i)-f(u_i))\Delta x_i<\frac\varepsilon{b-a}\sum\Delta x_i=\varepsilon$ -- WHY: formula (5) for step functions.
6. Squeeze Theorem $\Rightarrow f\in\mathcal R$. (Where hypotheses were used: closed bounded -- steps 1,3; continuity -- 1,3.)

### S-7.2.8 Monotone $\Rightarrow$ integrable [10] (K16)
Increasing case (decreasing: apply to $-f$ and 7.1.5(a); or swap $\alpha,\omega$).
1. Equal partition $x_k=a+k\frac{b-a}n$ -- WHY: makes the telescoping clean.
2. On $I_k$ min $=f(x_{k-1})$, max $=f(x_k)$ -- WHY: $f$ increasing.
3. $\alpha=f(x_{k-1})$, $\omega=f(x_k)$ on $[x_{k-1},x_k)$, last piece closed with $f(x_{n-1})$, $f(x_n)$; $\alpha\le f\le\omega$.
4. $\int\alpha=\frac{b-a}n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f(x_k)$, $\int\omega=\frac{b-a}n\sum_{k=1}^nf(x_k)$ -- WHY: step-function integral.
5. Subtract: all middle terms cancel (telescoping, M01/M05): $\int(\omega-\alpha)=\frac{b-a}n(f(b)-f(a))$.
6. $n>(b-a)(f(b)-f(a))/\varepsilon$ makes it $<\varepsilon$; Squeeze. Example $x^2$, $[0,1]$, $n=4$: $0.46875-0.21875=0.25$.

### S-7.2.9 Additivity [10] (K17)
(⇐) 1. Pieces $f_1\in\mathcal R[a,c]$ ($L_1$), $f_2\in\mathcal R[c,b]$ ($L_2$); $\delta'$, $\delta''$ for $\varepsilon/3$ -- WHY: three equal errors at the end.
2. $M$ with $|f|\le M$ (7.1.6 on each piece); $\delta=\min\{\delta',\delta'',\varepsilon/(6M)\}$.
3. $\dot Q$ on $[a,b]$, $\lVert Q\rVert<\delta$. Case (i) $c$ a partition point: $S(Q)=S(Q_1)+S(Q_2)$ with norms $<\delta',\delta''$ -- WHY: Riemann sum is a sum over subintervals.
4. Case (ii) $c\in(x_{k-1},x_k)$: $\dot Q_1$ = intervals before $k$ plus $([x_{k-1},c],c)$; $\dot Q_2$ = $([c,x_k],c)$ plus intervals after -- WHY: restore partition points at $c$ with tag $c$.
5. $S(Q)-S(Q_1)-S(Q_2)=(f(t_k)-f(c))\Delta x_k$, absolute value $\le2M\delta\le\varepsilon/3$ -- WHY: only interval $k$ is changed.
6. $|S(Q)-(L_1+L_2)|<\varepsilon/3+\varepsilon/3+\varepsilon/3=\varepsilon$. So $f\in\mathcal R[a,b]$ and (6) holds (uniqueness gives the value $L_1+L_2$).
(⇒) 7. Cauchy $\eta_\varepsilon$ for $f$ on $[a,b]$; $\dot P_1,\dot Q_1$ of norm $<\eta$ on $[a,c]$; adjoin the SAME tagged partition of $[c,b]$ to both (norm $<\eta$ is kept by choosing it fine) -- WHY: the extra terms cancel.
8. $S(f_1;\dot P_1)-S(f_1;\dot Q_1)=S(f;\dot P)-S(f;\dot Q)$, $|\cdot|<\varepsilon$; Cauchy criterion gives $f_1\in\mathcal R[a,c]$. Same for $[c,b]$. Formula from (⇐).

### S-7.2.10 / 7.2.11 / 7.2.13 [3-6] (K18, K19)
- 7.2.10: $f\in\mathcal R[a,b]$, $[c,d]\subseteq[a,b]$. By 7.2.9 (⇒) at $c$: $f|_{[c,b]}\in\mathcal R$; again at $d$: $f|_{[c,d]}\in\mathcal R$. (If $c=a$ or $d=b$ skip that step.)
- 7.2.11 (no proof in B; give it): induction on $m$. $m=1$ trivial; $\int_a^bf=\int_a^{c_{m-1}}f+\int_{c_{m-1}}^bf$ (7.2.9 at $c_{m-1}$) and apply the induction hypothesis to the first term.
- 7.2.13: if two of $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ coincide, (8) is Def 7.2.12. Else $L(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\int_\alpha^\beta+\int_\beta^\gamma+\int_\gamma^\alpha$; (8)$\iff L=0$. 7.2.9 gives $L=0$ when $\alpha<\gamma<\beta$. Cyclic shifts leave $L$ unchanged; swapping two arguments changes sign (definition 7.2.12). Every ordering is reached, so $L=0$ always.

### S-7.3.1 FTC First Form [10] (K20)
(Case $E=\{a,b\}$; general $E$: split at the points of $E$ by 7.2.11, 7.2.10 and apply on each piece, since $F$ is continuous on the closed pieces.)
1. $f\in\mathcal R$: $\delta_\varepsilon$ with $|S(f;\dot P)-\int f|<\varepsilon$ for $\lVert P\rVert<\delta_\varepsilon$.
2. Partition $P$ with $\lVert P\rVert<\delta_\varepsilon$.
3. MVT on $[x_{i-1},x_i]$ ($F$ continuous on closed, differentiable inside, P12): $u_i\in(x_{i-1},x_i)$ with $F(x_i)-F(x_{i-1})=F\,\prime(u_i)\Delta x_i=f(u_i)\Delta x_i$ -- WHY: $u_i\notin E$ so $F\,\prime(u_i)=f(u_i)$.
4. Sum: $F(b)-F(a)=\sum f(u_i)\Delta x_i=S(f;\dot P_u)$ (telescoping, M05) -- WHY: $\dot P_u$ with tags $u_i$ is a legal tagged partition of the same norm.
5. $|F(b)-F(a)-\int f|<\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon$, so equality.
Hypotheses: (a) used in MVT; (b) used in step 3; (c) used in step 1. NOT automatic: $F$ differentiable everywhere does not give $f\in\mathcal R$ (C19).

### S-7.3.4 Indefinite integral is Lipschitz [3-6] (K22)
1. $w\le z$: $F(z)=F(w)+\int_w^zf$ (Additivity 7.2.9) -- WHY: $f$ integrable on $[a,w],[w,z]$ by 7.2.10.
2. $-M\le f\le M\Rightarrow-M(z-w)\le\int_w^zf\le M(z-w)$ (7.1.5c with constants, 7.1.4a).
3. $|F(z)-F(w)|\le M|z-w|$: Lipschitz, so continuous ($\delta=\varepsilon/M$).

### S-7.3.5 FTC Second Form [10] (K23)
KNOW: $f\in\mathcal R[a,b]$ continuous at $c$. WANT: $F\,\prime(c)=f(c)$.
1. Right derivative at $c\in[a,b)$. Given $\varepsilon$, $\eta$: $c\le x<c+\eta\Rightarrow f(c)-\varepsilon<f(x)<f(c)+\varepsilon$ -- WHY: continuity of $f$ AT $c$ (integrability alone is not enough).
2. $0<h<\eta$: $f$ is integrable on $[c,c+h]$ (7.2.10) and $F(c+h)-F(c)=\int_c^{c+h}f$ (additivity) -- WHY: the increment of $F$ is the area of the sliver.
3. $(f(c)-\varepsilon)h\le\int_c^{c+h}f\le(f(c)+\varepsilon)h$ (7.1.5c) -- WHY: step 1 on $[c,c+h]$.
4. Divide by $h>0$: $\left|\frac{F(c+h)-F(c)}h-f(c)\right|\le\varepsilon$. So the right derivative is $f(c)$.
5. Left derivative at $c\in(a,b]$ the same way with $h<0$; both equal, so $F\,\prime(c)=f(c)$.
Consequence 7.3.6: $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$ $\Rightarrow F\,\prime=f$ on $[a,b]$ (apply at every $c$). Example 7.3.7: if $f$ has a jump, $F$ has a corner there (C20).

### S-7.3.8 Substitution theorem [10] (K25) (B Exercise 17)
1. $F(u)=\int_{\varphi(\alpha)}^uf(x)\,dx$ for $u\in I$ (oriented integral 7.2.12) -- WHY: $I\supseteq\varphi(J)$ is an interval, $f$ continuous there.
2. $F\,\prime=f$ on $I$ (7.3.6) -- WHY: $f$ continuous.
3. $H(t)=F(\varphi(t))$; chain rule: $H\,\prime(t)=F\,\prime(\varphi(t))\varphi\,\prime(t)=f(\varphi(t))\varphi\,\prime(t)$ -- WHY: P11.
4. $H\,\prime$ is continuous (product/composition of continuous), so $\in\mathcal R[\alpha,\beta]$ (7.2.7).
5. FTC1 (7.3.1, $E=\varnothing$): $\int_\alpha^\beta f(\varphi(t))\varphi\,\prime(t)dt=H(\beta)-H(\alpha)=F(\varphi(\beta))-F(\varphi(\alpha))=F(\varphi(\beta))=\int_{\varphi(\alpha)}^{\varphi(\beta)}f$ -- WHY: $F(\varphi(\alpha))=0$.
Using it: say $x=\varphi(t)$, CHECK $\varphi\,\prime$ continuous on the closed interval, change the limits, integrate. $\int_1^4\frac{\sin\sqrt t}{\sqrt t}dt$: $\varphi=\sqrt t$, $f=2\sin x$, limits $1\to2$: $2(\cos1-\cos2)$.

### S-7.3.11 Rationals are a null set [3] (K26)
Enumerate $r_1,r_2,\dots$; $J_k=(r_k-\varepsilon/2^{k+1},r_k+\varepsilon/2^{k+1})$ of length $\varepsilon/2^k$; union covers all; total length $\sum\varepsilon/2^k=\varepsilon$ -- WHY: $\varepsilon$ arbitrary so null.

### S-7.3.14 Composition [6] (K28) (needs Lebesgue 7.3.12, quoted)
1. $f$ continuous at $u\Rightarrow\varphi\circ f$ continuous at $u$ -- WHY: composition of continuous functions.
2. Discontinuity set of $\varphi\circ f\subseteq$ that of $f$, a null set (Lebesgue, $f\in\mathcal R$); subsets of null sets are null.
3. $\varphi\circ f$ bounded (continuous $\varphi$ on closed $[c,d]$) -- WHY: Lebesgue requires bounded.
4. Lebesgue $\Rightarrow\varphi\circ f\in\mathcal R$. Continuity of $\varphi$ is needed (Thomae + $\varphi(0)=0,\varphi(y)=1$ else gives Dirichlet).

### S-7.3.15 $|f|$ [3-6] (K29)
$f$ bounded, $|f|\le M$ (7.1.6). $\varphi(t)=|t|$ continuous on $[-M,M]$: $|f|\in\mathcal R$ (Composition). $-|f|\le f\le|f|\Rightarrow-\int|f|\le\int f\le\int|f|$ (7.1.5c). $|f|\le M\Rightarrow\int|f|\le M(b-a)$ (constant integral 7.1.4a).

### S-7.3.16 Product [6] (K30)
$f^2,g^2,(f+g)^2\in\mathcal R$ (Composition with $\varphi(t)=t^2$ continuous on bounded intervals; bounds from 7.1.6). $fg=\tfrac12[(f+g)^2-f^2-g^2]$ -- WHY: expand. Linearity (7.1.5) $\Rightarrow fg\in\mathcal R$.

### S-7.3.17 Integration by parts [10] (K31)
1. $(FG)\,\prime=F\,\prime G+FG\,\prime=fG+Fg$ on $[a,b]$ (product rule) -- WHY: $F,G$ differentiable.
2. $F,G$ continuous (6.1.2), $f,g\in\mathcal R$: $fG$, $Fg\in\mathcal R$ (Product Thm 7.3.16) -- WHY: continuous functions are integrable.
3. $(FG)\,\prime=fG+Fg\in\mathcal R$ so FTC1: $[FG]_a^b=\int(fG+Fg)=\int fG+\int Fg$. Rearrange to (7).

### S-7.3.18 Taylor with integral remainder [10, proof optional] (K32)
KNOW: $f^{(n+1)}\in\mathcal R$. WANT: $f(b)=\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(b-a)^k+R_n$.
1. $R_n=\frac1{n!}\int_a^bf^{(n+1)}(t)(b-t)^ndt$. Parts with $F=f^{(n)}$ ($F\,\prime=f^{(n+1)}$), $G=\frac{(b-t)^n}{n!}$ ($g=G\,\prime=-\frac{(b-t)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}$) -- WHY: 7.3.17 needs $F,G$ differentiable, $F\,\prime,G\,\prime\in\mathcal R$.
2. $R_n=\left[\frac{f^{(n)}(t)(b-t)^n}{n!}\right]_a^b+\frac1{(n-1)!}\int_a^bf^{(n)}(t)(b-t)^{n-1}dt=-\frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}(b-a)^n+R_{n-1}$ -- WHY: boundary term is $0$ at $t=b$ and $-f^{(n)}(a)(b-a)^n/n!$ at $a$. [B prints $(b-a)^{n-1}$ in the integrand: misprint.]
3. Repeat: $R_{n-1}=-\frac{f^{(n-1)}(a)}{(n-1)!}(b-a)^{n-1}+R_{n-2}$, ..., down to $R_0=\int_a^bf\,\prime=f(b)-f(a)$ (FTC1).
4. Back-substitute: $f(b)=f(a)+R_0=f(a)+\frac{f\,\prime(a)}{1!}(b-a)+R_1=\cdots$. Intermediate integrability: $f^{(k)}$, $k\le n$, are differentiable hence continuous so each $f^{(k+1)}\in\mathcal R$ for $k+1\le n$.
Check: $e^x$, $a=0$, $b=1$, $n=1$: $R_1=\int_0^1e^t(1-t)dt=e-2$; $1+1+(e-2)=e$. With $n=2$: $R_2=e-\tfrac52=0.21828$.
