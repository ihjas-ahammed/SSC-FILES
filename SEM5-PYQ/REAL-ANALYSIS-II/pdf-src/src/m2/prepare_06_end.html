Your intuition will lie to you here"If $f'(0) > 0$, then f is increasing near 0." N10 says no. "A derivative must be continuous." C6 says no. "A discontinuous function is not a derivative." C10 says no: the right test is the intermediate value property.

Rule traps without a function: N1 (naive chain rule divides by $f(x) - f(c)$, which can be 0), N3 (piecewise: continuity first, then one-sided derivatives), N11 (quotient needs $g(c) \ne 0$; $(fg)' \ne f'g'$), N12 (MVT gives some c; inequalities need a bound over the whole interval), N14 ("increasing" is non-strict).

Which tool?

Mixed drill. Name the tool first, then check its hypotheses. Answers below.

  1. Find $f'(c)$ from the definition.
  2. Show f is differentiable at a join of two pieces.
  3. Show f is not differentiable at c.
  4. Differentiate a composite, or $|f|$.
  5. Find where f can have a maximum or minimum.
  6. Show an equation has exactly one real root.
  7. Find the c in "$f(b) - f(a) = f'(c)(b - a)$".
  8. Prove $e^x \ge 1 + x$, $\sin x \le x$, Bernoulli.
  9. Show f is increasing / find intervals of increase.
  10. Decide whether a critical point is a max, min or neither.
  11. Show two functions with equal derivatives differ by a constant.
  12. Estimate $\sqrt{105}$ or $\ln 1.1$ without a calculator.
  13. Show f is Lipschitz / uniformly continuous from f′.
  14. Show $f'$ must take a value between two given values.
  15. Show a given function is not a derivative.
  16. Prove $a^\alpha b^{1-\alpha} \le \alpha a + (1-\alpha)b$.
Answers
1. Difference quotient; factor $x - c$ (T01, M01, M02). 2. Continuity first, then compare one-sided quotients (T02, M03). 3. Show discontinuity (T02), or two different one-sided quotients, or an unbounded quotient (C5). 4. Chain rule, or $|f|' = \operatorname{sgn}(f)f'$ where $f \ne 0$ (T06, T07). 5. Interior Extremum Theorem: solve $f' = 0$, add points where f′ is undefined, add the ends (T10). 6. Sign change (Location of Roots) plus Rolle for uniqueness (T16). 7. MVT; solve $f'(c) = $ chord slope; reject c outside the interval (T12). 8. MVT on the right interval with a bound for f′ (T18 to T21, M06). 9. Sign of f′ on an interval, M05 (T14). 10. First Derivative Test: sign of f′ on each side (T15). 11. $h = f - g$ has $h' = 0$, so constant (T13). 12. MVT for $\sqrt{\ }$ or $\ln$ with bounds on c (T17). 13. $|f'| \le K$ ⇒ Lipschitz ⇒ uniformly continuous (T19; Module 1 T16). 14. Darboux (T23): prove both orderings. 15. A value skipped between two values: Darboux contradiction (T24). 16. $g = \alpha x - x^\alpha$ has absolute minimum at 1 (T21).

Theorem sheet

6.1.1 Derivative$f'(c) = \lim_{x \to c}\dfrac{f(x) - f(c)}{x - c}$, $c \in I$ (∀ε ∃δ: $0 < |x - c| < \delta$ ⇒ $|\text{quotient} - L| < \varepsilon$).
6.1.2 Differentiable ⇒ continuous$f'(c)$ exists ⇒ f continuous at c. Converse false: $|x|$.
6.1.3 · 6.1.4 Rulesf, g differentiable at c ⇒ $\alpha f$, $f + g$, $fg$, and $f/g$ (needs $g(c) \ne 0$): $(fg)' = f'g + fg'$, $(f/g)' = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}$. $Dx^n = nx^{n-1}$.
6.1.5 Carathéodoryf differentiable at c ⇔ ∃ φ on I, continuous at c, $f(x) - f(c) = \varphi(x)(x - c)$. Then $\varphi(c) = f'(c)$.
6.1.6 Chain Rulef differentiable at c, g differentiable at f(c) ⇒ $(g \circ f)'(c) = g'(f(c))f'(c)$.
6.2.1 Interior Extremum · 6.2.2Relative extremum at an interior c and $f'(c)$ exists ⇒ $f'(c) = 0$. If f continuous: $f'(c) = 0$ or does not exist.
6.2.3 Rollef continuous on [a, b], differentiable on (a, b), $f(a) = f(b) = 0$ ⇒ ∃ $c \in (a, b)$: $f'(c) = 0$.
6.2.4 MVTf continuous on [a, b], differentiable on (a, b) ⇒ ∃ $c \in (a, b)$: $f(b) - f(a) = f'(c)(b - a)$.
6.2.5 · 6.2.6 · 6.2.7$f' = 0$ on (a, b), f continuous on [a, b] ⇒ constant. $f' = g'$ ⇒ $f = g + C$. f differentiable on an interval: increasing ⇔ $f' \ge 0$; decreasing ⇔ $f' \le 0$; $f' > 0$ ⇒ strictly increasing.
6.2.8 First Derivative Testf continuous on [a, b], c interior, differentiable on (a, c) and (c, b). $f' \ge 0$ left, $\le 0$ right ⇒ relative max; reversed ⇒ relative min.
6.2.9 Rolle's consequences (T16, T17)Between two roots of f lies a root of f′; $f' \ne 0$ on an interval ⇒ at most one root. MVT with a bound on f′ gives numerical bounds.
6.2.10 Inequalities (T18 to T21)$e^x \ge 1 + x$; $\frac{x - 1}x < \ln x < x - 1$ ($x > 1$); $|\sin x - \sin y| \le |x - y|$; $|f'| \le K$ ⇒ $|f(x) - f(u)| \le K|x - u|$; $\alpha > 1$: $(1 + x)^\alpha \ge 1 + \alpha x$ ($x > -1$); $0 < \alpha < 1$: $a^\alpha b^{1-\alpha} \le \alpha a + (1 - \alpha)b$.
6.2.11 Local sign lemma$f'(c) > 0$ ⇒ $f(x) > f(c)$ for $c < x < c + \delta$. $f'(c) < 0$ ⇒ $f(x) > f(c)$ for $c - \delta < x < c$.
6.2.12 Darbouxf differentiable on [a, b], k between $f'(a)$ and $f'(b)$ (either order) ⇒ ∃ $c \in (a, b)$: $f'(c) = k$.
6.2.13 · Ex 6.2.12A function that skips a value between two of its values (sgn, Heaviside, $\lfloor x \rfloor$) is not a derivative.
Before quoting Rolle or the MVT, write these two lines
  • f is continuous on the closed [a, b](for Rolle also $f(a) = f(b)$)
  • f is differentiable on the open (a, b)$|x|$ on [−1, 1] and $1 - x^{2/3}$ on [−1, 1] fail here

Attacking a question

3 marksExact statement or one computation. Always name the theorem and check its hypotheses in one line. 6 marksStatement, Know/Want, skeleton with the key construction filled in (the gap function, $g = kx - f$, the interval for the MVT). 8 to 12 lines. 10 marksStatement word for word; numbered proof with theorem names (Max–Min, Interior Extremum, Rolle, MVT); where each hypothesis was used; counterexample for a dropped one. If stuck, write the skeleton anyway.
Five-minute final checkDid I say "continuous on [a, b], differentiable on (a, b)"? Did I keep the direction of "<" when multiplying by $b - a > 0$? Did I give strict or non-strict correctly? In Darboux, did I treat the second ordering? Is c really inside the open interval?

Bartle corrections and scope

Printed errors and gaps in Bartle & Sherbert, and what is outside this module.

Misprints and gaps in the textbook
  • Ex 6.1.7(b): the book assumes $f(x)\ne0$ and $f'(x)\ne0$. Only $f(x)\ne0$ is needed for $(1/f)'=-f'/f^2$.
  • 6.2.7 proof: the halves are labelled the wrong way. "$f'\ge0$ ⇒ increasing" is the half that needs the MVT; "increasing ⇒ $f'\ge0$" needs the order theorem for limits.
  • Darboux proof (6.2.12): only $f'(a)<k<f'(b)$ is proved. The case $f'(a)>k>f'(b)$ follows by applying the result to $-f$ and $-k$. Write that line in the exam.
  • Ex 6.2.10(c) refers to Example 6.1.10(c). It should be 6.1.10(d), the derivative of $x^r$.
  • Standard derivatives ($\sin$, $\cos$, $e^x$, $\ln x$, $x^r$) are quoted by Bartle as known and "proved later" (6.1.7(d), Section 8.3). In the exam, quote them as Plus Two facts.
  • CALC-24 Q13 (related paper): $f(x)=x^2$ for $x\le1$, $4\sqrt x$ for $x>1$ is not continuous at 1 ($1$ against $4$), so $f'(1)$ does not exist. The question hides this.
Not in the exam

Inverse functions (6.1.8–6.1.10), L'Hospital's rule (6.3) and Taylor's theorem with Lagrange remainder (6.4) are not part of this module. They are only mentioned where an example touches them.
