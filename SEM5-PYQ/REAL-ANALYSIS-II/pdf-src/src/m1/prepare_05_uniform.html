T13

Uniform continuity

The definition (PYQ MAT-22 Q1, MAT-21 Q2, old MCQs).

Definition 5.4.1 · say it word for wordLet $A \subseteq \mathbb R$ and let $f : A \to \mathbb R$. We say that f is uniformly continuous on A if for each $\varepsilon>0$ there is a $\delta(\varepsilon)>0$ such that if $x, u \in A$ are any numbers satisfying $|x - u| < \delta(\varepsilon)$, then $|f(x) - f(u)| < \varepsilon$. In plain words: one δ, chosen from ε alone, works at every point. Picture: a box of width 2δ and height 2ε slides along the whole graph and the curve leaves only through its sides.
{{fig:uc_sine}}
ResultUniformly continuous on A ⇒ continuous at every point of A (use the same δ at each point). The converse is false: $g(x) = 1/x$ on $(0, \infty)$ is continuous but not uniformly continuous.
{{fig:uc_recip}}
Why 1/x fails (Bartle's discussion)At a point u the best δ is about $u^2\varepsilon$ (Bartle uses $\delta = \min\{\frac u2, \frac{u^2\varepsilon}{2}\}$). As $u \to 0$ this shrinks to 0, so no single positive δ serves every u.
{{fig:uc_square}}
Exam trap"Uniform continuity fails only on unbounded sets." No: 1/x and sin(1/x) on the bounded set (0, 1) are not uniformly continuous. The trouble is an open end where f blows up or wiggles.

In the model paper: Q14 (PYQ MAT-22 Q1, also MAT-21 Q2, MAT-22 MCQ 13).

T14

Proving "not uniformly continuous"

Nonuniform Continuity Criteria 5.4.2 (PYQ MAT-21 Q16; MTS-22 MCQ 2).

5.4.2 Nonuniform Continuity CriteriaLet $A \subseteq \mathbb R$ and let $f : A \to \mathbb R$. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i) f is not uniformly continuous on A.

(ii) There exists an $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that for every $\delta>0$ there are points $x_\delta, u_\delta$ in A such that $|x_\delta - u_\delta| < \delta$ and $|f(x_\delta) - f(u_\delta)| \ge \varepsilon_0$.

(iii) There exists an $\varepsilon_0>0$ and two sequences $(x_n)$ and $(u_n)$ in A such that $\lim(x_n - u_n) = 0$ and $|f(x_n) - f(u_n)| \ge \varepsilon_0$ for all $n \in \mathbb N$.

Skeleton · use (iii)Take $\varepsilon_0 = $ ?, $x_n = $ ?, $u_n = $ ? in A. Then $x_n - u_n = $ ? $\to 0$ and $|f(x_n) - f(u_n)| = $ ? $\ge \varepsilon_0$ for all n. By 5.4.2, f is not uniformly continuous on A. ∎
The standard pairs

Why (i) ⇔ (iii) (Bartle leaves it to you)(i) ⇒ (ii) is the negation of Definition 5.4.1 (M3). (ii) ⇒ (iii): take δ = 1/n to get $x_n, u_n$ with $|x_n - u_n| < \frac1n$. (iii) ⇒ (i): if f were uniformly continuous, the δ for $\varepsilon_0$ would eventually exceed $|x_n - u_n|$, forcing $|f(x_n) - f(u_n)| < \varepsilon_0$: contradiction.

T15

The Uniform Continuity Theorem

Asked in three papers (PYQ MTS-26 Q24a, MAT-22 Q28, MAT-21 Q27).

Theorem 5.4.3 · Uniform Continuity TheoremLet I be a closed bounded interval and let $f : I \to \mathbb R$ be continuous on I. Then f is uniformly continuous on I.
Knowf continuous at every point of I = [a, b].Wantone δ(ε) for the whole of I.
Proof (contradiction, pattern M5)
Suppose f is not uniformly continuous on I. By 5.4.2 there are $\varepsilon_0>0$ and $x_n, u_n \in I$ with $|x_n - u_n| < 1/n$ and $|f(x_n) - f(u_n)| \ge \varepsilon_0$.Criterion (ii) with δ = 1/n.
I is bounded, so by BW a subsequence $x_{n_k} \to z$; I is closed, so $z \in I$.P7, order theorem.
$|u_{n_k} - z| \le |u_{n_k} - x_{n_k}| + |x_{n_k} - z| < \frac1{n_k} + |x_{n_k} - z| \to 0$, so $u_{n_k} \to z$ too.Triangle inequality: the partners follow.
f is continuous at z: $f(x_{n_k}) \to f(z)$ and $f(u_{n_k}) \to f(z)$.Sequential criterion.
So $|f(x_{n_k}) - f(u_{n_k})| \to 0$, contradicting $\ge \varepsilon_0$. Hence f is uniformly continuous. ∎Limit of a difference.
Hypotheses checklist
  • closed1/x on (0, 1]; sin(1/x) on (0, 1] C2 C3
  • boundedx² on [0, ∞) C1
  • continuousa jump: no δ works across it

T16

Lipschitz functions

A bound on every chord slope (PYQ MTS-26 Q3, MAT-21 Q28, MTS-25 Q2, MAT-22 Q15).

Definition 5.4.4 · say it word for wordLet $A \subseteq \mathbb R$ and let $f : A \to \mathbb R$. If there exists a constant $K>0$ such that $|f(x) - f(u)| \le K|x - u|$ for all $x, u \in A$, then f is said to be a Lipschitz function (or to satisfy a Lipschitz condition) on A. On an interval: every chord has slope between −K and K.
{{fig:lipschitz}}
Theorem 5.4.5If $f : A \to \mathbb R$ is a Lipschitz function, then f is uniformly continuous on A.
ProofGiven ε > 0 take $\delta = \varepsilon/K$. If $x, u \in A$ and $|x - u| < \delta$, then $|f(x) - f(u)| \le K|x - u| < K\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{K} = \varepsilon$. δ uses only ε and the constant K. ∎
Examples
$3x + 1$$|f(x) - f(u)| = 3|x - u|$: K = 3 $\sin x$ on ℝ$|\sin x - \sin u| \le |x - u|$: K = 1, so δ = ε $x^2$ on [0, b]$|x^2 - u^2| = |x + u||x - u| \le 2b|x - u|$: K = 2b $\frac1{1+x^2}$ on ℝ$|f(x) - f(u)| = \frac{|u - x||u + x|}{(1+x^2)(1+u^2)} \le |x - u|$, using $\frac{|x|}{1+x^2} \le \frac12$ (M1): K = 1
Result · uniformly continuous but not Lipschitz (PYQ MTS-25 Q2, MAT-22 Q15)$g(x) = \sqrt x$ on [0, 2] is uniformly continuous (5.4.3) but not Lipschitz: for any K, the point $x = \frac{1}{(K+1)^2}$ gives $\sqrt x = \frac1{K+1} > \frac{K}{(K+1)^2} = Kx$, so $|g(x) - g(0)| > K|x - 0|$.
{{fig:sqrt_fig}}
Sums and products of Lipschitz functions (old MCQ)If f, g are Lipschitz with constants $K_1, K_2$ then $f + g$ is Lipschitz with $K_1 + K_2$, and $cf$ with $|c|K_1$. But $f^2$ need not be: $f(x) = x$ on $[0,\infty)$ is Lipschitz, while $x^2$ has chord slopes $x + u$, unbounded. (Products are fine if f and g are also bounded.)

In the model paper: Q15 (PYQ MTS-26 Q3, also MAT-21 Q28a), Q16 (PYQ MTS-25 Q2, also MAT-22 Q15), Q17 (MTS-22 MCQ 3), Q18 (MTS-22 MCQ 4), Q31 (PYQ MAT-21 Q28b).

√x is uniformly continuous on [0, ∞)

Bartle 5.4.6(c) (PYQ MTS-26 Q24b, MAT-22 Q36b).

Proof 1 · Bartle's route
On $I = [0,2]$: g is continuous on a closed bounded interval, so uniformly continuous; let $\delta_I(\varepsilon)$ be its δ.Theorem 5.4.3.
On $J = [1,\infty)$: $|\sqrt x - \sqrt u| = \dfrac{|x - u|}{\sqrt x + \sqrt u} \le \frac12|x - u|$. Lipschitz with $K = \frac12$; $\delta_J = 2\varepsilon$.Conjugate (M1); $\sqrt x + \sqrt u \ge 2$ on J.
Put $\delta = \min\{1, \delta_I(\varepsilon), \delta_J(\varepsilon)\}$. If $|x - u| < \delta$, both points lie in I or both lie in J, so $|\sqrt x - \sqrt u| < \varepsilon$. ∎The "1" matters: if $x < 1 \le u$ and $|x - u| < 1$ then $u < 2$, so both are in [0, 2].
Proof 2 · one line (general knowledge)For $x \ge u \ge 0$: $(\sqrt u + \sqrt{x - u})^2 = x + 2\sqrt{u(x-u)} \ge x$, so $\sqrt x - \sqrt u \le \sqrt{x - u}$. Hence $|\sqrt x - \sqrt u| \le \sqrt{|x - u|}$ and $\delta = \varepsilon^2$ works on all of $[0,\infty)$. ∎

Result (Bartle Exercises 5.4.5–5.4.7)If f, g are uniformly continuous on A, so is $f + g$ (take $\delta = \min\{\delta_f(\varepsilon/2), \delta_g(\varepsilon/2)\}$). The product need not be; it is if f and g are also bounded on A.

Uniform continuity keeps Cauchy sequences Cauchy

Theorem 5.4.7 (PYQ MAT-21 Q36a, MTS-22 Q3).

Theorem 5.4.7If $f : A \to \mathbb R$ is uniformly continuous on a subset A of ℝ and if $(x_n)$ is a Cauchy sequence in A, then $(f(x_n))$ is a Cauchy sequence in ℝ.
Proof
Let ε > 0 and take δ = δ(ε) from uniform continuity.One δ for every pair of points.
$(x_n)$ is Cauchy: there is H with $|x_n - x_m| < \delta$ for $n, m > H$.Definition of Cauchy, used with δ (P8).
So $|f(x_n) - f(x_m)| < \varepsilon$ for $n, m > H$: $(f(x_n))$ is Cauchy. ∎Step 1 with $x = x_n$, $u = x_m$.
Mere continuity is not enough$f(x) = 1/x$ on (0, 1) is continuous; $x_n = \frac1{n+1}$ is Cauchy, but $f(x_n) = n + 1$ is unbounded, not Cauchy. (This also proves 1/x is not uniformly continuous on (0, 1).)

The Continuous Extension Theorem

A 7–10 mark question (PYQ MAT-22 Q29).

Theorem 5.4.8 · Continuous Extension TheoremA function f is uniformly continuous on the interval (a, b) if and only if it can be defined at the endpoints a and b such that the extended function is continuous on [a, b].
{{fig:extension}}
Proof

(⇐) The extension is continuous on the closed bounded [a, b], hence uniformly continuous there (5.4.3), hence on the smaller set (a, b) (the same δ works).

(⇒) We define f(a); f(b) is similar.

Let $x_n \in (a,b)$, $x_n \to a$. It converges, so it is Cauchy.P8.
By Theorem 5.4.7, $(f(x_n))$ is Cauchy, so it converges to some L.Uniform continuity is used here; Cauchy ⇒ convergent (3.5.5).
L does not depend on the sequence: if also $u_n \to a$, then $u_n - x_n \to 0$, so by uniform continuity $f(u_n) - f(x_n) \to 0$, and $f(u_n) \to L$.Pair argument (M6).
By the sequential criterion for limits, $\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = L$. Define $f(a) := L$; f is then continuous at a.P9, Remark 5.1.7 (T7).
Likewise at b. The extended f is continuous on [a, b]. ∎Interior points were already fine.
Using itsin(1/x) has no limit at 0, so it is not uniformly continuous on (0, b] C3. $x\sin(1/x)$ has limit 0 at 0, so it is uniformly continuous on (0, b].

Step functions and piecewise linear approximation

Bartle 5.4.9–5.4.13 (PYQ MAT-22 Q5, MAT-21 Q3, MTS-26 Q17).

Definition 5.4.9 · step functionA function $s : [a,b] \to \mathbb R$ is called a step function if [a, b] is the union of a finite number of nonoverlapping intervals $I_1, I_2, \ldots, I_n$ such that s is constant on each interval $I_k$.
Theorem 5.4.10Let I be a closed bounded interval and let $f : I \to \mathbb R$ be continuous on I. If ε > 0, then there exists a step function $s_\varepsilon : I \to \mathbb R$ such that $|f(x) - s_\varepsilon(x)| < \varepsilon$ for all $x \in I$.
{{fig:step_approx}}
Proof
f is uniformly continuous on I = [a, b]: take δ(ε).Theorem 5.4.3: the only place continuity enters.
Choose m with $h = (b-a)/m < \delta$. Cut I into $I_1 = [a, a + h]$, $I_k = (a + (k-1)h, a + kh]$.Archimedes; the pieces do not overlap.
Let $s_\varepsilon(x) = f(a + kh)$ for $x \in I_k$: a step function.Constant on each piece.
For $x \in I_k$, $|x - (a + kh)| \le h < \delta$, so $|f(x) - s_\varepsilon(x)| < \varepsilon$. ∎Uniform continuity.
Exam traph must be strictly less than δ. For f(x) = x on [0, 1], ε = 0.1 (so δ = 0.1): m = 10 gives h = 0.1 and fails at x = 0; m = 11 works (error ≤ 1/11).
Definition 5.4.12 and Theorem 5.4.13 · piecewise linearg is piecewise linear on [a, b] if [a, b] is a union of finitely many disjoint intervals on each of which g is linear. If f is continuous on a closed bounded I and ε > 0, there is a continuous piecewise linear $g_\varepsilon$ with $|f(x) - g_\varepsilon(x)| < \varepsilon$ for all $x \in I$.
{{fig:pl_approx}}
Proof idea (the step Bartle skips)Join the points $(x_k, f(x_k))$, $x_k = a + kh$, by chords. For x between $x_{k-1}$ and $x_k$, write $x = (1-\lambda)x_{k-1} + \lambda x_k$; then $g(x) = (1-\lambda)f(x_{k-1}) + \lambda f(x_k)$ and $$f(x) - g(x) = (1-\lambda)[f(x) - f(x_{k-1})] + \lambda[f(x) - f(x_k)],$$ where both brackets are less than ε in size. So $|f(x) - g(x)| < \varepsilon$.
Check yourself
$f(x) = x^2$ on [0, 1] with nodes 0, ¼, ½, ¾, 1. Largest error of the broken line?
On each piece of width h the chord of x² lies above it by at most $h^2/4$ (at the midpoint): $\frac{1}{64} \approx 0.0156$.
