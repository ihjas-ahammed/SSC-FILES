One idea for the whole module

You are handed ε; you hand back δ

Continuity is a game. An opponent draws a band of half-height ε round the value f(c). You must answer with a window of half-width δ round c so that the graph over the window stays in the band.

If you can answer every ε, f is continuous at c. If your δ can be chosen without looking at the point c, f is uniformly continuous.

On a closed bounded interval a continuous function behaves perfectly: it is bounded, reaches its max and min, takes every value in between, and is uniformly continuous. Most of this module proves exactly that.

The whole module in one story

The idea

1. Continuity at a point: ε–δ, or "every sequence $x_n \to c$ gives $f(x_n) \to f(c)$". One bad sequence proves discontinuity (T1–T7).

2. On a closed bounded interval [a, b] a continuous function is bounded and attains its maximum and minimum. The proofs use Bolzano–Weierstrass (T8, T9).

3. A sign change forces a root (bisection). So a continuous function takes every value between two of its values, and maps intervals to intervals (T10–T12).

4. Uniform continuity: one δ for the whole set. Continuous on [a, b] ⇒ uniformly continuous. Lipschitz functions are uniformly continuous (T13–T19).

5. Uniform continuity on (a, b) means the ends can be filled in, and lets us approximate f by steps, broken lines and polynomials (T20–T22).
