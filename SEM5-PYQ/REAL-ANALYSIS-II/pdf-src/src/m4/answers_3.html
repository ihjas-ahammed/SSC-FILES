Theorem 8.1.10. Let $(f_n)$ be a sequence of bounded functions on $A \subseteq \mathbb R$. Then $(f_n)$ converges uniformly on A to a bounded function f if and only if for each $\varepsilon>0$ there is $H(\varepsilon) \in \mathbb N$ such that $\lVert f_m - f_n\rVert_A \le \varepsilon$ for all $m, n \ge H(\varepsilon)$.

Proof (⇒). Suppose $f_n \to f$ uniformly. Let $\varepsilon>0$. By Lemma 8.1.8 there is $K = K(\varepsilon/2)$ with $\lVert f_n - f\rVert_A \le \varepsilon/2$ for $n \ge K$. For $m, n \ge K$ and every $x \in A$, $$|f_m(x) - f_n(x)| \le |f_m(x) - f(x)| + |f(x) - f_n(x)| \le \tfrac\varepsilon2 + \tfrac\varepsilon2 = \varepsilon.$$ Taking the supremum over x, $\lVert f_m - f_n\rVert_A \le \varepsilon$ for $m, n \ge H(\varepsilon) := K(\varepsilon/2)$.

Fix $x \in A$. For $m, n \ge H(\varepsilon)$: $|f_m(x) - f_n(x)| \le \lVert f_m - f_n\rVert_A \le \varepsilon$. So $(f_n(x))$ is a Cauchy sequence in ℝ.A value is at most the supremum.

Theorem 8.2.2. Let $(f_n)$ be a sequence of continuous functions on $A \subseteq \mathbb R$ converging uniformly on A to $f : A \to \mathbb R$. Then f is continuous on A.

Proof. Let $c \in A$ and $\varepsilon>0$.

By uniform convergence there is $H \in \mathbb N$ such that $|f_H(x) - f(x)| < \varepsilon/3$ for all $x \in A$.Definition 8.1.4 with ε/3; H independent of x.
For $x \in A$: $|f(x) - f(c)| \le |f(x) - f_H(x)| + |f_H(x) - f_H(c)| + |f_H(c) - f(c)| < \frac\varepsilon3 + |f_H(x) - f_H(c)| + \frac\varepsilon3$.Triangle inequality; step 1 at the two points x and c.
Since $f_H$ is continuous at c, there is $\delta>0$ with $|f_H(x) - f_H(c)| < \varepsilon/3$ whenever $x \in A$, $|x - c| < \delta$.Continuity of one function.
Hence $|f(x) - f(c)| < \varepsilon$ for $x \in A$, $|x - c| < \delta$. So f is continuous at c, and c was arbitrary. ∎Definition 5.1.1.

Deduction. Each $x^n$ is continuous on [0, 1], and $x^n \to g$ pointwise with g = 0 on [0, 1), g(1) = 1, which is discontinuous at 1. If the convergence were uniform, g would be continuous by the theorem. Hence $(x^n)$ does not converge uniformly on [0, 1].

Uniform ⇔ $\lVert f_n - f\rVert_A \to 0$; apply to $x/n$.

Know firstT6T5P3T9

Lemma 8.1.8. A sequence $(f_n)$ of bounded functions on $A \subseteq \mathbb R$ converges uniformly on A to f if and only if $\lVert f_n - f\rVert_A \to 0$.

(⇒) Let $\varepsilon>0$. By uniform convergence there is $K(\varepsilon)$ with $|f_n(x) - f(x)| \le \varepsilon$ for all $x \in A$ and $n \ge K(\varepsilon)$. Then ε is an upper bound of $\{|f_n(x) - f(x)| : x \in A\}$, so its supremum satisfies $\lVert f_n - f\rVert_A \le \varepsilon$ for $n \ge K(\varepsilon)$. As ε is arbitrary, $\lVert f_n - f\rVert_A \to 0$.

(⇐) (MAT-22 Q19) Let $\varepsilon>0$. There is $H(\varepsilon)$ with $\lVert f_n - f\rVert_A \le \varepsilon/2$ for $n \ge H(\varepsilon)$. For every $x \in A$, $|f_n(x) - f(x)| \le \lVert f_n - f\rVert_A \le \varepsilon/2 < \varepsilon$ when $n \ge H(\varepsilon)$. Since H depends only on ε, $f_n \to f$ uniformly on A. ∎

Application. On [0, 1], $\lVert x/n - 0\rVert = \sup_{0\le x\le1} x/n = 1/n \to 0$: uniform. On ℝ, $f_n - f = x/n$ is not bounded, so the norm is not a real number and the lemma does not apply directly. We show non-uniformity directly instead: $x_k = k$ gives $|f_k(k) - 0| = 1$ for all k (Lemma 8.1.5).

Marks: statement 1 · (⇒) 3 · (⇐) 3 · application 3.

$(x^n)$ on [0, 1], $[0, b]$, [0, 1).

Know firstT9T4T7T14

Limit. $g(x) = 0$ for $0 \le x < 1$, $g(1) = 1$.

On [0, 1]: not uniform. Three independent proofs:

  1. Points: $x_k = 2^{-1/k}$ gives $|x_k^k - g(x_k)| = \frac12$ for every k (Lemma 8.1.5, ε₀ = ½).
  2. Norm: $\lVert x^n - g\rVert_{[0,1]} = \sup_{0\le x<1} x^n = 1 \not\to 0$ (Lemma 8.1.8).
  3. Continuity: the $x^n$ are continuous, g is not (Theorem 8.2.2).

On $[0, b]$, $0<b<1$: uniform. $x^n$ increases in x, so $\lVert x^n - 0\rVert_{[0,b]} = b^n \to 0$.

On [0, 1): not uniform, even though the limit 0 is continuous there: $\sup_{0\le x<1} x^n = 1$ for every n (proof 1 also works, as $x_k \in [0,1)$).

Not uniform on [0, 1] or [0, 1); uniform on every [0, b] with b < 1.
