1PYQ N19 Q15

Principle of superposition.

Know firstT1P1

When two or more waves overlap at a point, the resultant displacement at that point at any instant is the algebraic (vector) sum of the displacements due to the individual waves: $\vec y = \vec y_1 + \vec y_2 + \cdots$. After crossing, each wave continues unaltered. Where the waves meet in phase the resultant is large (constructive interference); where they meet in opposite phase it is small or zero (destructive interference). This redistribution of light energy is interference.

N24 Q4 ("explain interference"): the same answer, with the definition: interference is the modification of intensity produced by the superposition of two or more coherent waves.

2PYQ N25 Q2

Do intensities obey superposition?

Know firstT1T4P2

No. The principle applies to displacements (amplitudes, electric fields). Intensity is proportional to the square of the amplitude, and $(a_1 + a_2)^2 \neq a_1^2 + a_2^2$. For coherent waves $I = I_1 + I_2 + 2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\cos\delta$, which can be up to $4I_0$ or as low as 0 for two equal waves. Intensities add simply ($I = I_1 + I_2$) only for incoherent waves, where the interference term averages to zero.

3PYQ N25 Q3

Incoherent waves of equal intensity I.

Know firstT2T4

$I_R = I + I + 2I\langle\cos\phi\rangle$. For incoherent waves the phase difference φ changes randomly about $10^{10}$ times a second, so its time average $\langle\cos\phi\rangle = 0$. Hence the resultant intensity is $I_R = 2I$, uniform everywhere: no interference fringes.

4PYQ N24 Q5

Coherence.

Know firstT2T5

Two waves are coherent if their phase difference remains constant in time. Light from an atom comes in wave trains of about $10^{-10}$ s, with random phase jumps between trains, so independent sources are incoherent; coherent sources are obtained by deriving two beams from a single source (division of wavefront or amplitude). Temporal coherence: the phase stays predictable along the beam for a coherence time $\tau_c$, i.e. a coherence length $L = c\tau_c \approx \lambda^2/\Delta\lambda$. Spatial coherence: points across the beam are in step; it requires a narrow source.

5PYQ N22 Q13

Conditions for sustained interference.

Know firstT2
  1. The sources must be coherent (derived from a single source).
  2. They must emit the same wavelength (monochromatic).
  3. Amplitudes equal or nearly equal (good contrast).
  4. Sources narrow and close together; screen far enough for visible fringe width ($\beta = \lambda D/d$).
  5. Same state of polarisation, and path difference less than the coherence length.
6PYQ N20 Q13

Division of wavefront and amplitude.

Know firstT2

Division of wavefront: the incident wavefront is divided into two parts by passing it through two narrow apertures, or by mirrors or prisms; the two parts, coming from different points of the same wavefront, are coherent and overlap (Young's double slit, Fresnel's biprism, Fresnel's two mirrors, Lloyd's mirror). Division of amplitude: the amplitude of the same wave is divided into two parts by partial reflection and partial transmission at a surface; the two beams travel different paths and recombine (thin films, Newton's rings, Michelson interferometer). Extended sources can be used with division of amplitude.

7

Coherence length.

Know firstT2

The coherence length is the length of an unbroken wave train (over which the phase is predictable): $L = c\tau_c$. Two beams split from one source interfere only if their path difference is less than L. For $\tau_c = 10^{-10}$ s: $L = 3\times10^8\times10^{-10} = 3\times10^{-2}$ m $= 3$ cm.

8PYQ N25 Q14 + N22 Q7

Bandwidth; screen distance.

Know firstT3M4

$\beta = \dfrac{\lambda D}{d} = \dfrac{500\times10^{-9}\times1}{0.5\times10^{-2}} = 1\times10^{-4}$ m $= 0.1$ mm.

False. β ∝ D: a screen close to the sources gives very narrow fringes that cannot be resolved, and the derivation also assumes D ≫ d. The screen must be far away.

9

Visibility.

Know firstT4

$V = \dfrac{I_{\max} - I_{\min}}{I_{\max} + I_{\min}} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{I_1I_2}}{I_1 + I_2}$. With $I_1 = 4I_2$: $I_{\max} = (2+1)^2I_2 = 9I_2$, $I_{\min} = (2-1)^2I_2 = I_2$, so $V = \dfrac{8}{10} = 0.8$.

10

Shape of the fringes.

Know firstT3

A fringe is the locus of points with constant path difference $S_2P - S_1P = \Delta$. Such points lie on a hyperboloid of revolution with S₁, S₂ as foci, so on a plane screen the fringes are hyperbolas, $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ (the central fringe Δ = 0 is a straight line). Near the centre of a distant screen ($y \ll D$) the hyperbolas are almost straight lines parallel to the slits.

11

White-light fringes.

Know firstT9

At the centre the path difference is zero for every wavelength, so all colours are bright there and the central fringe is white. On either side a few coloured fringes appear, violet-edged inside and red outside, since each colour has its own fringe width $\lambda D/d$ (where Δ is half the violet wavelength violet is cancelled; where it is half the red wavelength, red). Further out many wavelengths are bright and many dark at each point, so the fringes overlap into uniform white. The white central fringe locates the zero path difference.

12

Fresnel's two mirrors.

Know firstT7P5

The virtual sources S₁, S₂ are images of the same slit S in the two mirrors, so any phase change of S occurs in both at once: their phase difference is constant (coherent). If the mirrors are inclined at a small angle θ and S is at distance b from their line of intersection, S, S₁, S₂ lie on a circle of radius b and $\angle S_1MS_2 = 2\theta$, so $d = S_1S_2 = 2b\theta$.

13

Dark fringe at the edge of Lloyd's mirror.

Know firstT10T11

At the edge of the mirror the direct and reflected paths are equal, so a bright fringe is expected; it is observed to be dark. Hence the reflected wave has an additional phase change of π (path λ/2): light reflected from a denser medium (glass) suffers a phase change of π. All fringes of Lloyd's mirror are complementary to those of Young's arrangement.

14PYQ N19 Q5 · adapted

Optical path from the lower surface.

Know firstT12P4

Increases. The wave reflected from the lower surface travels twice through the film, an optical path $2nt\cos r$ ($2nt$ at normal incidence). Since optical path = n × geometric path, it increases with the refractive index n.

15PYQ N19 Q13

Thin film at normal incidence.

Know firstT12T11

The ray reflected from the upper surface (air → film) suffers a phase change π; the ray reflected from the lower surface (film → air) does not, and has travelled an extra optical path 2nt. Effective path difference $2nt \pm \lambda/2$. Reflected light: constructive $2nt = (m+\frac12)\lambda$; destructive $2nt = m\lambda$, m = 0, 1, 2, … (transmitted light: the reverse).

If $n_1 < n < n_3$ (film between a rarer and a denser medium), both reflections suffer π; the phase changes cancel and the conditions interchange: constructive $2nt = m\lambda$, destructive $2nt = (m+\frac12)\lambda$.

16PYQ N22 Q27

Least thickness of the soap film for brightness.

Know firstT12M4

Bright in reflection: $2nt = (m + \frac12)\lambda$. Least thickness, m = 0: $t = \dfrac{\lambda}{4n} = \dfrac{5839}{4\times1.42}$ Å.

$t_{\min} = 1028$ Å $\approx 1.03\times10^{-7}$ m
17PYQ N19 Q14

Colours of thin films.

Know firstT16T13

When white light falls on a thin film (soap bubble, oil on water), light reflected from the upper and lower surfaces interferes. The path difference $2nt\cos r$ depends on the thickness t and the angle r. For a given t and r, wavelengths satisfying $2nt\cos r = (m+\frac12)\lambda$ are strongly reflected and those satisfying $2nt\cos r = m\lambda$ are absent, so the film looks coloured. Since t and r vary over the film, different places show different colours.

N22 Q8 ("due to dispersion", T/F): False, the colours are due to interference. N22 Q14, N24 Q7: same answer.

18PYQ N19 Q17

No colours in thick plates.

Know firstT16T2

In a thick plate the path difference 2nt is many wavelengths. At any point, a large number of wavelengths spread over the visible spectrum satisfy the maximum condition and an equally large number the minimum condition; their mixture appears white. Moreover, the path difference exceeds the coherence length of ordinary white light, so the two reflected beams are not coherent. Hence no colours are seen.

19PYQ N22 Q4 · adapted

Non-reflecting coating.

Know firstT15

Reflection (loss of light by reflection at the lens surfaces). The film thickness $\lambda/4n_f$ is chosen for the middle of the spectrum (yellow-green); for red and violet the condition is not met, so they are partly reflected, giving the coated surface a purple (red + violet) tint.

20PYQ N20 Q10 · adapted

Fringes from two plane surfaces.

Know firstT17

False. Two perfectly plane surfaces inclined at a small angle φ form a wedge whose thickness increases uniformly with distance. The fringes are straight, parallel to the edge and equally spaced, with width $\beta = \lambda/2n\phi$, independent of the order.

21PYQ N19 Q3 · adapted

Central spot in Newton's rings.

Know firstT18T12

At the point of contact the air film thickness is zero, so the geometric path difference is zero. In reflected light one of the two rays (reflected at the glass plate, air → glass) suffers a phase change π: the waves cancel and the centre is dark. In transmitted light there is no such relative phase change and the centre is bright (N20 Q3).

22

Ring spacing; liquid film.

Know firstT18

The radius of the m-th dark ring is $r_m = \sqrt{m\lambda R}$, proportional to √m. So $r_{m+1} - r_m$ decreases as m increases: the rings get closer outward (the film thickness $t = r^2/2R$ grows faster and faster). With a liquid of index n, $r_m^2 = m\lambda R/n$: every ring contracts by the factor $1/\sqrt n$, and $n = D_{\text{air}}^2/D_{\text{liquid}}^2$.

23PYQ N25 Q4

Michelson: condition for constructive interference.

Know firstT20T13

$2d\cos\theta = (m + \frac12)\lambda$ for a bright fringe, where d = $x_1 - x_2$, the separation between mirror M₁ and the virtual image M₂′ of M₂ (difference of the arm lengths); θ = angle of the ray with the normal to the mirrors (θ = 0 at the centre of the field); m = order of the fringe (an integer). The extra ½ arises from the π phase change of one beam at the beam splitter. (If that phase change is ignored, as in many books: $2d\cos\theta = m\lambda$ for a bright fringe.)

24

Compensating plate.

Know firstT20

The beam going to M₁ passes through the glass plate G₁ three times, the beam to M₂ only once. A plate G₂ of the same material and thickness, placed parallel to G₁ in the other arm, equalises the glass paths. For monochromatic light the difference could be made up by moving M₁, but since n varies with λ, this is impossible for all wavelengths at once. G₂ is therefore essential for white-light fringes.
