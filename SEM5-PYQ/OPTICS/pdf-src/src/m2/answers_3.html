Coherent sources; resultant intensity; fringe width; incoherent sources.

Know firstT2T3T4M1P2

1. Coherent sources

Two sources are coherent if the phase difference between the waves they emit remains constant with time. Light from an ordinary source consists of wave trains about $10^{-10}$ s long with random phases, so two independent sources are incoherent. Coherent sources are produced from a single source by division of wavefront (Young's slits, biprism) or division of amplitude (thin films).

2. Path difference

{{fig:ydse}}

S₁, S₂ are separated by d; the screen is at distance D; P is at height x above the centre O.

$(S_2P)^2 - (S_1P)^2 = \left[D^2 + (x + \tfrac d2)^2\right] - \left[D^2 + (x - \tfrac d2)^2\right] = 2xd$
$S_2P - S_1P = \dfrac{2xd}{S_2P + S_1P} \approx \dfrac{xd}{D}$, since $S_1P + S_2P \approx 2D$ for $D \gg x, d$

3. Resultant intensity

$E_1 = E_{01}\cos\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}S_1P - \omega t\right)$, $\;E_2 = E_{02}\cos\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}S_2P - \omega t\right)$; by superposition $E = E_1 + E_2$
$I = K\langle E^2\rangle = K\left[\langle E_1^2\rangle + \langle E_2^2\rangle + 2\langle E_1E_2\rangle\right]$
$2E_1E_2 = E_{01}E_{02}\left[\cos\delta + \cos\left(2\omega t - \tfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}(S_1P + S_2P)\right)\right]$, $\;\delta = \tfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}(S_2P - S_1P)$
Time averages: $\langle\cos^2\rangle = \frac12$; the term in $2\omega t$ averages to zero
$I = I_1 + I_2 + 2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\cos\delta$, with $I_1 = \frac12KE_{01}^2$, $I_2 = \frac12KE_{02}^2$

4. Maxima, minima, fringe width

  • Maxima: $\delta = 2m\pi$, i.e. $S_2P - S_1P = m\lambda$; $I_{\max} = (\sqrt{I_1} + \sqrt{I_2})^2$.
  • Minima: $\delta = (2m+1)\pi$, i.e. path difference $(m + \frac12)\lambda$; $I_{\min} = (\sqrt{I_1} - \sqrt{I_2})^2$.
  • Bright fringes at $x_m = \dfrac{m\lambda D}{d}$, dark at $\left(m + \frac12\right)\dfrac{\lambda D}{d}$. Fringe width $\beta = x_{m+1} - x_m = \dfrac{\lambda D}{d}$, independent of m: the fringes are equally spaced.
  • Equal intensities: $I = 2I_0(1 + \cos\delta) = 4I_0\cos^2\dfrac\delta2$, varying between $4I_0$ and 0.
{{fig:fringes_cos2}}

The average intensity is $2I_0 = I_1 + I_2$: energy is redistributed, not created or destroyed.

5. Incoherent sources

If the sources are incoherent, δ varies randomly and rapidly, $\langle\cos\delta\rangle = 0$, and $I = I_1 + I_2$ (= $2I_0$ for equal sources), uniform everywhere: no fringes.

Marks: coherent sources 1 · path difference 2 · intensity derivation 4 · max/min, β 2 · incoherent 1.

40PYQ N25 Q20also N24 Q14

Maxima and minima for a parallel thin film; cosine law.

Know firstT13T12T11P4
{{fig:cosine_law}}

A film of index $n_2$ and thickness d lies in a medium of index $n_1$. A ray incident at angle θ at B is partly reflected along BC and partly refracted (angle θ′) to D, reflected, and emerges at F parallel to BC. FC is drawn perpendicular to BC.

1. Path difference (cosine law)

$\Delta = n_2(BD + DF) - n_1\,BC$Beyond the wavefront FC both rays travel equal paths.
B′ is the image of B in the lower surface, so $BD = B'D$ and $B'$, D, F are collinear: $BD + DF = B'F$
$\angle CFB = \theta$, so $BC = BF\sin\theta$. With BK ⟂ B′F, $KF = BF\sin\theta'$
Snell's law $n_1\sin\theta = n_2\sin\theta'$ gives $n_1BC = n_2KF$
$\Delta = n_2(B'F - KF) = n_2B'K = n_2\,BB'\cos\theta' = 2n_2d\cos\theta'$
$\Delta = 2n_2d\cos\theta'$ (cosine law)

2. Reflected light

For a film in air ($n_2 = n > n_1$), the ray reflected at B (rare → dense) suffers a phase change π; the ray reflected at D (dense → rare) does not (Stokes relations). The effective path difference is $2nd\cos\theta' - \lambda/2$.

Maxima$2nd\cos\theta' = (m + \frac12)\lambda$ Minima$2nd\cos\theta' = m\lambda$

3. Transmitted light

The two transmitted rays (one directly through, one after two internal reflections at D and F, both dense → rare) have the same path difference $2nd\cos\theta'$ but no relative phase change.

Maxima$2nd\cos\theta' = m\lambda$ Minima$2nd\cos\theta' = (m + \frac12)\lambda$

The reflected and transmitted systems are complementary: a thickness that is bright in reflection is dark in transmission, as required by conservation of energy.

4. Contrast

The two main reflected rays have nearly equal amplitudes (≈ 0.15a each for n = 1.36), so the reflected fringes have good contrast (minima nearly dark). The transmitted rays are very unequal (≈ 0.98a and 0.02a), so the transmitted fringes have poor contrast.

5. Remarks

  • If the film lies between media with $n_1 < n_2 < n_3$, both reflections have π and the conditions interchange (used in non-reflecting films).
  • Thick films give no fringes (path difference beyond the coherence length).

Marks: diagram 1 · cosine law 4 · reflected 2 · transmitted 2 · remarks 1.

41PYQ N22 Q32

Newton's rings in reflected light; determination of λ.

Know firstT18T12P6
{{fig:newton_rings}}

1. Arrangement

A plano-convex lens L of large radius of curvature R is placed, convex side down, on a plane glass plate P. Light from a sodium lamp is rendered parallel and reflected down onto the arrangement by a glass plate G at 45°. Light reflected from the top and bottom of the air film enters a travelling microscope above G. Concentric dark and bright rings are seen around the point of contact.

2. Theory

{{fig:newton_geom}}
At distance r from the point of contact the air film thickness t satisfies $R^2 = r^2 + (R - t)^2$, so $r^2 = 2Rt - t^2 \approx 2Rt$ (t ≪ R)
Normal incidence on an air film (n = 1): path difference 2t. Reflection at the lower surface (air → glass) adds π
Dark rings: $2t = m\lambda$ ⟹ $r_m^2 = m\lambda R$, $D_m^2 = 4m\lambda R$; so $r \propto \sqrt m$ and the rings crowd outward
Bright rings: $2t = (m + \frac12)\lambda$ ⟹ $r^2 = (m + \frac12)\lambda R$

t is constant on circles about the contact point ⟹ circular fringes (of equal thickness). At the centre t = 0 and only the π remains ⟹ dark spot.

3. Determination of λ

  1. Focus the microscope on the rings. Set the cross-wire tangential to, say, the 20th dark ring on the left, and move across reading the positions of rings 20, 18, …, 4 on the left and 4, …, 20 on the right.
  2. The diameter of each ring = difference of its left and right readings.
  3. $D_{m+p}^2 - D_m^2 = 4p\lambda R$, so
$\lambda = \dfrac{D_{m+p}^2 - D_m^2}{4pR}$
  1. R is found with a spherometer. Alternatively, plot $D_m^2$ against m: the slope is $4\lambda R$.

Using the difference $D_{m+p}^2 - D_m^2$ removes the error due to imperfect contact (a dust particle or elastic deformation adds a constant to $D^2$) and the need to know the absolute order of a ring.

Extension: with a liquid of index n in the gap, $D_m^2 = 4m\lambda R/n$, so $n = D_{\text{air}}^2/D_{\text{liquid}}^2$.

Marks: arrangement and diagram 2 · theory of radii 4 · centre dark, ring spacing 1 · determination of λ 3.

42PYQ N20 Q32also N19 Q21

Michelson interferometer; standardisation of the metre; circular, straight, white-light fringes.

Know firstT20T14T17T9
{{fig:michelson}}

1. Construction and working

Light from a broad source S falls on G₁ (half-silvered on the back, at 45°), which divides it into a beam to mirror M₁ and a beam to mirror M₂ (division of amplitude). M₁, M₂ are perpendicular; M₁ moves on a micrometer screw, M₂ has tilt screws. The returning beams recombine at G₁ and interfere in the telescope T. G₂, identical to G₁, is the compensating plate.

2. Theory; measuring λ (N19 Q21)

The observer sees M₁ and the virtual image M₂′ of M₂ formed by G₁. The arrangement is equivalent to an air film of thickness d between M₁ and M₂′. The path difference for a ray at angle θ is $2d\cos\theta$; including the π phase change at G₁, dark fringes satisfy $2d\cos\theta = m\lambda$.

At the centre (θ = 0): $2d = m\lambda$
M₁ is moved by $d_0$ and N fringes are counted crossing the centre: $2(d - d_0) = (m - N)\lambda$
Subtracting: $\lambda = \dfrac{2d_0}{N}$ (each fringe corresponds to a mirror movement of λ/2)

Example: N = 1000 for $d_0 = 0.290$ mm gives λ = 5800 Å.

3. Standardisation of the metre

Michelson and Benoit compared the standard metre with the red cadmium line (λ = 6438.4696 Å). Nine etalons (pairs of parallel mirrors), 10 cm, 5 cm, … down to about 0.39 mm, were made. The wavelengths in the shortest were counted as fringes while M₁ moved from its front to its back mirror (white-light fringes marking each position). Each etalon was compared with the next longer one, and the 10 cm etalon was stepped ten times along the metre bar.

1 m = 1 553 164.13 wavelengths of the Cd red line (to about 1 part in 10⁹)

4. Types of fringes

  • Circular fringes: M₁ parallel to M₂′, monochromatic extended source. Parallel air film: the path difference $2d\cos\theta$ depends only on θ, so fringes of equal inclination, concentric rings seen at infinity. As d decreases, rings collapse into the centre and become broader; at d = 0 the field is uniform.
  • Straight fringes: M₁ and M₂′ slightly inclined, forming a thin air wedge, with d small. Fringes of equal thickness, straight and parallel to the line of intersection, localised near the mirrors. For larger d they become curved (convex towards the thin edge).
  • White-light fringes: with white light, the wedge must have d ≈ 0 (paths equal within a few wavelengths), since each wavelength has its own fringe width. A central achromatic (dark, because of the π phase change) fringe is seen, with a few coloured fringes on each side; beyond, the colours overlap into white. They locate the position of zero path difference exactly (used in the metre experiment). The compensating plate is essential for them.
