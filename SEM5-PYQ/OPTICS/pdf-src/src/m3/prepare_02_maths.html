M1

Adding many arrows (phasors)

The picture behind sinβ/β and sinNγ/sinγ.

The idea

Each wave arriving at a point is an arrow: length = its amplitude, angle = its phase. Waves add like arrows placed head to tail.

If N equal arrows (length $a$) are each turned a bit more than the last (by the same angle φ), they bend into part of a regular polygon. The total arrow is the chord from the first tail to the last head.

{{fig:phasor_chain}}
The polygon sits on a circle of radius $r$. One arrow (a short chord): $a = 2r\sin\dfrac{\phi}{2}$A chord that subtends angle φ at the centre has length $2r\sin(\phi/2)$.
The whole chain subtends $N\phi$, so the total: $R = 2r\sin\dfrac{N\phi}{2}$
Divide: $\;R = a\,\dfrac{\sin(N\phi/2)}{\sin(\phi/2)}$The famous N-slit factor. When φ = 0 the arrows are in a line and $R = Na$.

Infinitely many tiny arrows

{{fig:slit_arc}}

Now let N become huge and each arrow tiny, with the total length $A$ fixed. The polygon becomes a smooth arc of length $A$. If the arc turns through a total angle $2\beta$, the chord is

$$R = A\,\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}$$

(the arc's radius is $A/2\beta$, and a chord across angle $2\beta$ is $2\cdot\frac{A}{2\beta}\sin\beta$). When the arc closes into a full circle ($2\beta = 2\pi$) the chord is zero: darkness.

M2

Small angles, limits and units

Tools for "what happens at θ = 0" and for grating numericals.

Result

For small θ in radians: $\sin\theta\approx\tan\theta\approx\theta$.

$\dfrac{\sin x}{x} \to 1$ as $x\to0$. Check: $x = 0.1$: $\sin 0.1/0.1 = 0.9983$.

$\dfrac{\sin N\gamma}{\sin\gamma} \to \pm N$ as $\gamma\to m\pi$ (both top and bottom → 0; near there $\sin N\gamma \approx N\cdot$small and $\sin\gamma\approx$ small).

M3

Complex numbers as arrows

Only needed to read Ghatak's straight-edge and N-slit algebra.

The idea $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$ is simply an arrow of length 1 at angle θ ($x$-part $\cos\theta$, $y$-part $\sin\theta$). Multiplying by $e^{i\phi}$ turns an arrow by φ. The length of $p + iq$ is $\sqrt{p^2+q^2}$, so its square is $p^2 + q^2$ (that is the intensity).
$e^{i0} + e^{i\pi} = 1 + (-1) = 0$Two opposite arrows cancel.
$\left|\dfrac{1-i}{2}\right|^2 = \dfrac{1^2 + 1^2}{4} = \dfrac12$Used in the straight-edge result.
Sum of a geometric series: $1 + r + r^2 + \cdots + r^{N-1} = \dfrac{1-r^N}{1-r}$With $r = e^{i\phi}$ this sum of arrows gives the $\sin(N\phi/2)/\sin(\phi/2)$ of M1 again.
M4

Solving tan β = β with a graph

Where are the weak bright bands of a single slit?

The ideaSome equations cannot be solved with algebra. Draw both sides as graphs, $y = \tan\beta$ and $y = \beta$, and read off where they cross. The straight line $y = \beta$ cuts each branch of $\tan\beta$ just before it shoots up at $\frac{3\pi}2, \frac{5\pi}2, \ldots$
ResultCrossings at $\beta = 0,\ 1.43\pi,\ 2.46\pi,\ 3.47\pi,\ \ldots$: slightly less than $1.5\pi, 2.5\pi, 3.5\pi$.
