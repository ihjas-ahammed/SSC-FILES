39PYQ N20 Q33

Fraunhofer diffraction by a single slit: maxima, minima, intensity diagram.

Know firstT2T3T1M1M4

1. Arrangement

Monochromatic light from a slit source S at the focus of lens L₁ becomes a plane wave falling normally on a narrow slit AB of width $b$. Lens L₂ collects the light diffracted at each angle θ to a point P of its focal plane (the screen).

{{fig:single_slit_geom}}

2. Theory

Divide the slit into $n$ equal strips of width Δ ($b = n\Delta$), each a source of amplitude $a$
Path difference between neighbouring strips in direction θ: $\Delta\sin\theta$; phase difference $\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\Delta\sin\theta$
Resultant (sum of n equal vectors each turned by φ): $E_\theta = a\dfrac{\sin(n\phi/2)}{\sin(\phi/2)}$
Let $n\to\infty$, $na = A$: $\frac{n\phi}{2} = \frac{\pi b\sin\theta}{\lambda} = \beta$, $\sin\frac\phi2\approx\frac{\beta}{n}$, so $E_\theta = A\dfrac{\sin\beta}{\beta}$
$I = I_0\dfrac{\sin^2\beta}{\beta^2}$, $\beta = \dfrac{\pi b\sin\theta}{\lambda}$

3. Principal maximum

At θ = 0, β = 0 and $\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\to1$: $I = I_0$, the central (principal) maximum. All wavelets arrive in phase.

4. Minima

$I = 0$ when $\sin\beta = 0$, β ≠ 0: $\beta = \pm m\pi$, i.e.

$$b\sin\theta = \pm m\lambda,\qquad m = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$$

(Physically, at $b\sin\theta = \lambda$ the slit divides into two halves whose corresponding wavelets differ by λ/2 and cancel in pairs.)

5. Secondary maxima

$\dfrac{dI}{d\beta} = I_0\dfrac{2\sin\beta(\beta\cos\beta - \sin\beta)}{\beta^3} = 0$
$\sin\beta \ne 0$ ⇒ $\tan\beta = \beta$; graphical solution: $\beta = \pm1.43\pi, \pm2.46\pi, \pm3.47\pi, \ldots$
Intensities: $I_0\left(\frac{\sin1.43\pi}{1.43\pi}\right)^2 = 0.047I_0$, then $0.016I_0$, $0.008I_0$
{{fig:tan_beta}}

6. Intensity distribution

{{fig:single_slit_pattern}}

The pattern is a bright central band of angular half-width $\lambda/b$ (twice as wide as the others), flanked by much weaker secondary bands, symmetric about the centre, with intensities in the ratio $1 : \frac{1}{21} : \frac{1}{61} : \frac{1}{121}$ approximately. The width on the screen is $2f\lambda/b$: a narrower slit or a longer wavelength gives a wider pattern.

40PYQ N25 Q21

Intensity distribution for N identical slits.

Know firstT6T2T5M1M3

1. Set-up

N parallel slits, each of width $b$, with spacing (grating element) $d$, illuminated normally by a plane wave; diffracted light at angle θ is focused by a lens.

2. Field from each slit

Each slit alone gives $A\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\cos(\omega t - \beta)$ with $\beta = \frac{\pi b\sin\theta}{\lambda}$. Corresponding points of neighbouring slits differ in path by $d\sin\theta$, phase $\Phi = \frac{2\pi d\sin\theta}{\lambda} = 2\gamma$.

3. Summing

$E = A\frac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\cos(\omega t - \beta - 2j\gamma)$
The sum of N equal vectors each turned by $2\gamma$ has amplitude $\dfrac{\sin N\gamma}{\sin\gamma}$ (polygon of vectors / geometric series)
$E = A\dfrac{\sin\beta}{\beta}\dfrac{\sin N\gamma}{\sin\gamma}\cos[\omega t - \beta - (N-1)\gamma]$
$I = I_0\dfrac{\sin^2\beta}{\beta^2}\cdot\dfrac{\sin^2N\gamma}{\sin^2\gamma}$

The first factor is the single-slit diffraction envelope; the second is the interference of N waves.

4. Principal maxima

When $\gamma = m\pi$ ($d\sin\theta = m\lambda$), $\frac{\sin N\gamma}{\sin\gamma}\to\pm N$, so $I = N^2I_0\frac{\sin^2\beta}{\beta^2}$: very intense maxima, whose positions do not depend on N.

5. Minima and secondary maxima

$I = 0$ when $\sin N\gamma = 0$ but $\sin\gamma \ne 0$: $N\gamma = p\pi$, $p \ne 0, N, 2N, \ldots$, i.e. $d\sin\theta = \frac{\lambda}{N}, \frac{2\lambda}{N}, \ldots, \frac{(N-1)\lambda}{N}, \frac{(N+1)\lambda}{N}, \ldots$. Between adjacent principal maxima there are $N-1$ minima and $N-2$ feeble secondary maxima.

{{fig:nslit_pattern}}

6. Width and missing orders

The half-width of a principal maximum is $\frac{\lambda}{Nd\cos\theta}$: it becomes sharper as N increases. A principal maximum is missing if it falls at a zero of the envelope, $b\sin\theta = p\lambda$, i.e. if $d/b = m/p$.

7. Special cases

N = 1: single slit. N = 2: $\frac{\sin2\gamma}{\sin\gamma} = 2\cos\gamma$, giving $4I_0\frac{\sin^2\beta}{\beta^2}\cos^2\gamma$ (double slit). Large N: diffraction grating.

41PYQ N22 Q34

Rectilinear propagation from half-period zones.

Know firstT10T11

1. The problem

Light is a wave, yet it appears to travel in straight lines and to cast sharp shadows. Fresnel explained this with half-period zones.

2. Half-period zones

{{fig:half_period_zones}}

For a plane wavefront and a point P at distance $b$, spheres of radii $b + n\lambda/2$ about P divide the wavefront into zones of radii $r_n = \sqrt{n\lambda b}$ and equal areas $\pi\lambda b$. Waves from adjacent zones reach P with phase difference π.

3. Resultant at P

$u = u_1 - u_2 + u_3 - u_4 + \cdots$, with $u_n$ slowly decreasing (obliquity and distance)
$u = \frac{u_1}{2} + \left(\frac{u_1}{2} - u_2 + \frac{u_3}{2}\right) + \left(\frac{u_3}{2} - u_4 + \frac{u_5}{2}\right) + \cdots \approx \frac{u_1}{2}$

4. Rectilinear propagation

The effect at P is thus half that of the first zone. The outer zones cancel each other in pairs; effectively only a small region around the foot O of the perpendicular from P matters. For $b = 50$ cm and λ = $5\times10^{-5}$ cm, $r_1 = \sqrt{\lambda b} = 0.05$ cm: the light reaching P comes, in effect, from a disc only half a millimetre across around the straight line OP. So light appears to travel along the straight line from the source to P.

5. Shadows

If an obstacle much larger than the first few zones is placed between the source and P, covering the region around OP, P receives practically no light: a sharp shadow. If P lies in the illuminated region, far from the edge of the obstacle, the first few zones around OP are unobstructed and P receives the normal intensity $u_1/2$. Only near the edge of the geometrical shadow, within a few zone-widths ($\sqrt{\lambda b}$, a fraction of a millimetre), does the light deviate: there diffraction fringes appear.

6. When it fails

If the obstacle or aperture is comparable with the zone size (very small holes, discs, narrow slits), the straight-line picture fails: a small hole gives a bright or dark centre depending on the number of zones it exposes, and a small disc gives a bright spot at the centre of its shadow.

7. Conclusion

Because λ is so small, the effective zones are tiny, so in everyday conditions light behaves as if it travels in straight lines. Rectilinear propagation is thus a consequence of the wave theory.

Fresnel diffraction at a straight edge; Fresnel integrals; Cornu spiral.

Know firstT13M3T10

1. Arrangement

A plane wave falls on an opaque straight edge (the edge along the ξ-axis, blocking one side). A screen at distance $d$ is observed at a point P at height $y$ from the edge of the geometrical shadow.

2. Amplitude at P

Distance from a point $(\xi, \eta)$ of the open wavefront to P: $r \approx d + \dfrac{\xi^2 + (\eta - y)^2}{2d}$
Summing the wavelets: $u(P) \propto \displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi\int_{0}^{\infty}d\eta\;e^{ik[\xi^2 + (\eta-y)^2]/2d}$
Substitute $u = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda d}}\,\xi$, $v = \sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda d}}(\eta - y)$; the exponent becomes $\frac{i\pi}{2}(u^2 + v^2)$, and the lower limit of $v$ is $v_0 = -\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda d}}\,y$
$u(P) = \dfrac{1-i}{2}\,u_0\left\{\left[\tfrac12 - C(v_0)\right] + i\left[\tfrac12 - S(v_0)\right]\right\}$, with $C(v) = \int_0^v\cos\frac{\pi t^2}{2}dt$, $S(v) = \int_0^v\sin\frac{\pi t^2}{2}dt$

3. Intensity

Since $\left|\frac{1-i}{2}\right|^2 = \frac12$:

$$I(P) = \frac{I_0}{2}\left\{\left[\tfrac12 - C(v_0)\right]^2 + \left[\tfrac12 - S(v_0)\right]^2\right\}$$

where $I_0$ is the unobstructed intensity (check: $v_0 \to -\infty$, $C, S \to -\frac12$, giving $I = I_0$).

4. Cornu spiral

{{fig:cornu}}

The points $(C(v), S(v))$ form the Cornu spiral, winding into $Z^+(\frac12, \frac12)$ and $Z^-(-\frac12, -\frac12)$. The amplitude at P is proportional to the length of the chord from the spiral point $v_0$ to $Z^+$, and the intensity to its square.

5. The pattern

{{fig:straight_edge}}
  • Edge of the shadow ($y = 0$, $v_0 = 0$): chord OZ⁺ is half of Z⁻Z⁺, so $I = I_0/4$.
  • Geometrical shadow ($v_0 > 0$): as $v_0$ increases, the point moves into the upper curl and the chord shrinks steadily: the intensity falls smoothly to zero, without fringes.
  • Illuminated region ($v_0 < 0$): the point moves round the lower curl, so the chord alternately grows and shrinks: fringes. Maxima at $|v_0| = 1.22, 2.34, 3.08$ ($1.37I_0, 1.20I_0, 1.15I_0$), minima at 1.87, 2.74, … ($0.78I_0$, $0.84I_0$). They get closer together and the intensity settles at $I_0$.
  • Positions on the screen: $y = |v_0|\sqrt{\lambda d/2}$.

Marks: set-up 1 · integrals and amplitude 3 · Cornu spiral 2 · intensity at edge, shadow and lit region with graph 4.
