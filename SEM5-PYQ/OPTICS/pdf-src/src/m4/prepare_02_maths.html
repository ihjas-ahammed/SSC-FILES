M1

Splitting an arrow into two parts

Components: the single most useful tool in this module.

Remind me · Class 10 trigonometry In a right triangle with hypotenuse $E$ and angle θ: the side next to θ is $E\cos\theta$, the side opposite θ is $E\sin\theta$.
The idea Any arrow $E$ can be replaced by two perpendicular arrows that add up to it: one along a chosen line ($E\cos\theta$) and one across it ($E\sin\theta$), where θ is the angle between the arrow and the line. Nothing is lost: $(E\cos\theta)^2 + (E\sin\theta)^2 = E^2$.

M3

Two vibrations, a phase difference, and an ellipse

The maths behind circular and elliptical light (T8).

Remind me · Plus One SHM and conic sections

Simple harmonic motion: $x = a\cos\omega t$. The point moves back and forth between $-a$ and $+a$.

Circle of radius $a$: $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$. Ellipse with half-axes $a$ (along $x$) and $b$ (along $y$): $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$.

The idea A point is pushed along $x$ by $x = a\cos\omega t$ and along $y$ by $y = b\cos(\omega t - \delta)$ at the same time. What path does it trace? Get rid of $t$ and you get the path. For $\delta = \pi/2$: $y = b\cos(\omega t - \tfrac\pi2) = b\sin\omega t$ (because $\cos(A - 90^\circ) = \sin A$). Then $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = \cos^2\omega t + \sin^2\omega t = 1$: an ellipse (a circle if $a = b$).
