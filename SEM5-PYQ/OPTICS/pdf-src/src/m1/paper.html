How this paper works

A real paper has 12 short, 7 paragraph and 2 essay questions. This paper has twice as many (24 + 14 + 4), all from Module 1. Together they test every concept of the module, and no concept is tested twice.

Section B · Paragraph / problemAnswer all questions in about half a page to one page. Each carries 5 marks. Ceiling 60.
25.
Derive the condition for image plane with respect to a refracting surface.
PYQ N19 Q18
26.
Use Fermat's principle to determine the mirror equation for an object point at a distance less than $R/2$ from a concave mirror of radius of curvature $R$.
PYQ N20 Q25
27.
A biconvex lens of refractive index 1.5 has radii of curvatures of 50 cm and 100 cm. Find the distance to the object if the image has to be formed at 60 cm from the lens.
PYQ N25 Q13
28.
Using Fermat's principle, derive the laws of reflection for a ray reflected at a plane mirror.
29.
Using Fermat's principle, derive Snell's law of refraction.
30.
Explain why equal optical paths from an object point to an image point mean a perfect image. Hence show that a spherical refracting surface forms a perfect image only for paraxial rays.
31.
Using Fermat's principle, obtain the condition for a virtual image formed by refraction, and explain why the refracted rays appear to come from that point.
32.
Light from air is incident on a glass surface ($n=1.5$). Find the position and nature of the image when (a) the surface is convex towards the light, $R = 10$ cm, and the object is 30 cm in front of it; (b) the same surface receives a beam converging towards a point 20 cm behind it; (c) the surface is concave towards the light, $R = 10$ cm, and the object is 30 cm in front of it.
33.
Derive the mirror equation $\frac1u+\frac1v=\frac2R$ for a concave mirror from the law of reflection, using the paraxial approximation.
34.
(a) Using the single-surface formula, show that a coin at depth $d$ in a liquid of refractive index $n$ appears at depth $d/n$ when viewed from above. (b) Parallel light falls on the flat face of a glass hemisphere ($n = 1.5$, radius 20 cm). Where is it focused?
35.
An object is 80 cm in front of a concave mirror of radius of curvature 60 cm. (a) Find the position, magnification and nature of the image. (b) Where must the object be placed so that the image falls on the object itself? (c) Repeat (a) for an object 20 cm from the mirror.
36.
Derive Newton's formula for a thin lens.
37.
A biconvex lens ($n = 1.5$) has $R_1 = 100$ cm and $R_2 = 60$ cm in air. An object 1 cm tall is 100 cm in front of it. Find the focal length, the image position and its height. Find $x_1$ and $x_2$ and verify Newton's formula.
38.
An object is placed 12 cm in front of a convex lens of focal length 20 cm. Locate the image by ray construction, then verify by calculation. State the nature and magnification of the image.
Section C · EssayAnswer any two, in about two pages each. Each carries 10 marks.
39.
State Fermat's principle. Using it, obtain the formula for refraction at a single spherical surface (optical path method). Show whether the optical path from the object to a point on the axis is a maximum or a minimum, depending on where the point lies relative to the image.
40.
Obtain the thin lens formula and the lens-maker's formula by considering refraction at the two surfaces of a lens. State the assumptions made, and explain when a lens converges and when it diverges.
41.
Define the first and second principal foci of a thin lens. Obtain expressions for the two focal lengths when the media on the two sides of the lens are different, and show that $f_2 = -f_1$ when they are the same. What happens when a biconvex lens is placed in a medium denser than itself?
42.
Define lateral magnification. Derive it for (a) a single refracting surface, and (b) a thin lens, by treating the lens as two surfaces. Express the magnification of the lens in terms of $f$ and the distances $x_1$, $x_2$ from the foci, and illustrate with a numerical example.
