# Rules for 10-Mark Questions (Physical Optics / Optics, CU BSc Physics)

Derived from the PYQs in `source/`.

## Where 10-mark questions appear

- Only in **Section E (Essays)** of the 80-mark papers: Nov 2019, Nov 2020, Nov 2022 (Q32 to Q35 each year).
- Every Section E question carries 10 marks. The student answers **2 of 4**, so a student prepares 4 questions and writes 2.
- The 60-mark papers (Nov 2024, Nov 2025) have no 10-mark questions. Their longest questions carry 5 marks.
- The printed instruction is "Answer in about two pages", so a 10-mark answer is roughly two pages of text, diagrams included.

## Question wording

- Each question opens with one action verb:
  - **Describe / Explain / Discuss**: conceptual account with theory (most Section E questions).
  - **Derive / Obtain / Deduce**: mathematical derivation (5 of the 12 Section E questions in 2019, 2020 and 2022).
  - **Find / Calculate**: positions, values or results, typically after a derivation.
  - **Distinguish**: compare two cases (2020 Q34).
- Many questions have two parts, for example "explain X, and how is it used to determine Y" (2022 Q32, 2020 Q32) or "derive the expression, and find the positions of maxima and minima" (2019 Q33, 2022 Q33).
- Keywords that request a figure: "with necessary theory and diagram" (2020 Q35), "draw a diagram" (2020 Q33). Shorter questions use "neat diagram" (2019 Q21, 4 marks). Assume a labelled diagram is expected whenever a ray path, a setup or an optical element is involved.
- Application or use is asked in about half the questions: "how is it used to standardize the meter" (2020 Q32), "give any one application" (2020 Q35), "how can we use this to determine the wavelength" (2022 Q32).

## Content rules

- Each 10-mark question covers **one topic**, taken from one unit of the syllabus. Topics are not mixed across units.
- The question tests understanding plus derivation or application, not recall of a single definition.
- Only content from the named unit is used. The question does not need results from another module.

## Typical mark distribution (inferred, not printed)

For a 10-mark essay, the examiner's allocation is about:

| Part | Marks (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Principle, statement or theory | 3 to 4 |
| Derivation or diagram | 3 to 4 |
| Application, result or numerical value | 2 to 3 |

## Rules for a 10-mark practice session (four questions, total 10 marks)

When a 10-mark session is set from a unit, follow these rules:

1. Set exactly four questions whose marks add up to 10. Each question is 2 or 3 marks.
2. Each question covers one topic from the unit's syllabus. Together the four questions cover all the topics listed for the unit.
3. Each question starts with one of the verbs above (state, define, derive, explain, find).
4. At least one question requires a derivation. At least one question requires a labelled diagram.
5. Use only the notation and sign convention of the reference book (here, Ghatak, chapters 3 and 4). Do not use results from later chapters.
6. Every question must be answerable from the reference pages supplied for the unit.
7. The question text must state all the given values it needs, and must not give away the answer.
