Determination of Velocity of Sound in Air

1. Aim & Apparatus

Aim: To determine the velocity of sound in air using a Cathode Ray Oscilloscope (CRO) by the phase difference method.

Apparatus Required:

  • CRO (Cathode Ray Oscilloscope).
  • Piezoelectric Buzzer (Source of sound).
  • Function Generator (To drive the buzzer, range 2000Hz - 6000Hz).
  • MAX4466 Electret Microphone with Amplifier (Receiver).
  • DC Power Supply (3.3V or 5V for the microphone).

Note: The MAX4466 is a dedicated amplifier chip designed for microphones with adjustable gain to fill the CRO screen.

2. Principle

Sound waves travel from the source (Buzzer) to the receiver (Microphone) over a distance d. The signals from both are displayed on the CRO.

As the distance d changes, the phase difference between the two signals changes. When the two signals show a phase difference of 180° (Mirror images/Antiphase), the distance between source and receiver is exactly half a wavelength (λ/2).

d=λ2⟹λ=2d
Since Velocity V=νλ, substituting λ: V=2νd

Where: V = Velocity (m/s), ν = Frequency (Hz), d = Distance (m).

3. Circuit Connections

Make connections as per the diagram below. Ensure common ground between all components.

  • Source: Connect Buzzer to Function Generator and CRO Channel 1 (X-Channel).
  • Receiver: Connect MAX4466 OUT to CRO Channel 2. Connect VCC to 5V and GND to common ground.

4. Procedure

  1. Switch on the Function Generator and set a frequency ν (e.g., 3000 Hz).
  2. Observe two waveforms on the CRO: one from the source (Buzzer) and one from the receiver (Mic).
  3. Slowly move the microphone away from the buzzer to increase the distance d.
  4. Stop moving when the two waveforms become mirror images of each other (180° phase difference). This creates a shape like () on the screen.
  5. Measure the distance d between the buzzer and the mic using a scale.
  6. Record ν and d in the observations table.
  7. Repeat for different frequencies.
