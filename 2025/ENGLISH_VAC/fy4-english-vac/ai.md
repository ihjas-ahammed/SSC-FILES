──────────────────────────────
I – INTRODUCTION LESSON
──────────────────────────────
Slide 0: Hook slide
         – Evocative image (canvas art) + provocative question
         – e.g. "What happens when following rules sets you free?" (for Gardiner)
Slide 1: Author / Director context
         – Who are they? When? Why does their background matter?
         – Include key biographical facts relevant to the text's themes
Slide 2: Genre & Form overview
         – What kind of text is this? (essay, poem, short story, film, policy doc)
         – What conventions should you look for?
Slide 3: Pre-reading MCQ 1
         – Activate prior knowledge; no right/wrong pressure
         – e.g. "Which best describes liberty: (a) doing what you want (b) doing what's fair..."
Slide 4: Pre-reading MCQ 2
         – Prediction or conceptual challenge
         – e.g. "What do you think the 'rule of the road' is a metaphor for?"

──────────────────────────────
Tx – TEXT LESSON (Essays / Prose / Policy Docs)
──────────────────────────────
Each passage block (paragraph group or section):
Slide 0: Passage displayed clearly
         – Key phrases highlighted by theme tags: [argument] [example] [irony] [contrast]
Slide 1: Annotation challenge
         – "What is the author's purpose in this line?" (MCQ)
Slide 2: Comprehension MCQ
         – Direct question about the passage's content
Slide 3: Inference MCQ
         – "What does the author imply here?"
Slide 4: Canvas visual
         – Argument map, concept diagram, or timeline for this section
Slide 5: Fill-in-the-blank
         – Key idea from this passage with a word removed
Slide 6 (if applicable): Connection MCQ
         – "Which theme from the module does this passage connect to?"

──────────────────────────────
Tx – TEXT LESSON (Short Stories)
──────────────────────────────
Divide story into: Opening / Rising Action / Climax / Resolution
Each segment gets:
Slide 0: Scene/segment displayed with key dialogue or description
Slide 1: Character focus MCQ
         – "What does this moment reveal about [character]?"
Slide 2: Literary device MCQ
         – Identify irony, foreshadowing, symbol, etc.
Slide 3: Canvas visual
         – Character web or scene illustration
Slide 4: Comprehension MCQ
         – "What happens next?" or "Why did X do Y?"
Slide 5: Thematic reading MCQ
         – "Which theme does this scene advance most strongly?"

──────────────────────────────
Tx – TEXT LESSON (Poetry)
──────────────────────────────
Each stanza or couplet group:
Slide 0: Stanza text with line numbers
Slide 1: Paraphrase challenge
         – "Restate this stanza in plain English" → reveal model paraphrase
Slide 2: Poetic device MCQ
         – Identify device: metaphor, personification, enjambment, etc.
Slide 3: Tone & mood MCQ
         – What is the emotional register here?
Slide 4: Canvas visual
         – Mood board or imagery illustration for this stanza
Slide 5: Thematic connection MCQ
         – "This stanza is mainly about: (a)... (b)... (c)..."

──────────────────────────────
Tx – TEXT LESSON (Films)
──────────────────────────────
Divide into key scenes (3–5 per film):
Slide 0: Scene description (text + canvas sketch of key visual)
         – Include setting, characters present, what happens
Slide 1: Comprehension MCQ
         – "What is happening in this scene?"
Slide 2: Cinematic technique MCQ
         – Identify technique: lighting, framing, music, symbolism, montage
Slide 3: Character analysis MCQ
         – "What does this scene reveal about [character]'s inner world?"
Slide 4: Thematic reading MCQ
         – Connect scene to module theme

──────────────────────────────
V – VOCABULARY LESSON
──────────────────────────────
Group 4–5 words per lesson (from the text, exam-relevant):
For each word:
Slide 0: Word in context — full sentence from text shown; MCQ on meaning
Slide 1: Connotation challenge — positive / negative / neutral? Why? (MCQ)
Slide 2: Usage — fill in blank with correct word from a given set
Slide 3: Word family — related noun / verb / adjective forms (label exercise)
(Create multiple V lessons if text has many key words)

──────────────────────────────
Q – QUESTIONS LESSON (Exam-Aligned)
──────────────────────────────
Mirrors the ENG4FV109 model question paper pattern:

Section A style (2 marks, ≤3 sentences):
Slide 0: Question displayed — "Try it first"
         – Attempt space (or MCQ outline of what a good answer includes)
Slide 1: Model answer reveal — clean, 2–3 sentence answer
         – Examiner tip: what keywords are expected?

Section B style (6 marks, ~100 words):
Slide 0: Question displayed
         – Guided outline: "Your answer should cover: point 1, point 2, point 3"
Slide 1: Paragraph-builder
         – User selects the best opening sentence (MCQ)
Slide 2: Model answer reveal — ~100-word structured response with annotations

Section C style (10 marks, ~200 words):
Slide 0: Essay question displayed
         – Structured plan: Intro → Argument 1 → Argument 2 → Argument 3 → Conclusion
Slide 1: Thesis MCQ — "Which is the strongest thesis statement for this question?"
Slide 2: Evidence matching — match quotes/examples to the argument they support
Slide 3: Model essay outline with key phrases (NOT a full essay — trains planning)

──────────────────────────────
R – REFLECTION LESSON
──────────────────────────────
Slide 0: Personal response prompt
         – Open question, no right/wrong: "Do you agree with the author's view?"
         – Canvas space for thinking (not graded)
Slide 1: Real-world connection
         – "Where do you see this theme in today's world?" (MCQ examples given)
Slide 2: Debate prompt MCQ
         – Two contrasting views on the text's central argument; which is stronger?
Slide 3: Cross-text connection MCQ
         – "Which other text in this module makes a similar/opposing point?"
Slide 4: Creative mini-challenge
         – "Rewrite one line/moment from a different character's perspective" (guided)

──────────────────────────────
SS – SECTION SUMMARY UNIT
──────────────────────────────
One lesson per text in the section.
Each lesson:
Slide 0: Text card — title, author/director, one-sentence summary
Slide 1: Three key themes MCQ — which themes appear in this text?
Slide 2: Key quote identification MCQ — "Which text does this line come from?"
Slide 3: Character/speaker recall MCQ
Slide 4: Compare MCQ — "How does this text connect to the other in this section?"

──────────────────────────────
MS – MODULE SUMMARY UNIT
──────────────────────────────
One lesson per section in the module.
Each lesson:
Slide 0: Module theme overview — "What unifies all texts in this module?"
Slide 1: Text-to-theme mapping
         – Match each text to its primary theme (interactive drag or MCQ)
Slide 2: Quote attribution MCQ
         – "Identify which text this quote/scene belongs to"
Slide 3: Essay planning slide
         – Module-level essay question with structured outline prompt
Slide 4: Final comprehensive MCQ
         – Cross-text understanding question

From Make It Stick / How We Learn:
  – Testing effect: every lesson has at least one retrieval question before revealing information
  – Interleaving: SS and MS units mix texts together rather than reviewing one at a time
  – Spaced repetition: key quotes and terms appear in multiple unit types (Tx, V, Q, SS)

From Ultralearning / Peak:
  – Direct practice: Q lessons mirror exact exam format (Section A/B/C structure)
  – Feedback loops: every MCQ and fill-in reveals the correct answer with explanation

From A Mind for Numbers / Learning How to Learn:
  – Chunking: text broken into meaningful segments, not arbitrary lengths
  – Elaborative interrogation: "Why does the author use this word?" forces deeper processing

From The Gamification of Learning (Kapp):
  – Guided discovery over passive reading: user "earns" each next piece of information
  – Interactivity makes learning feel like solving puzzles, not memorizing
