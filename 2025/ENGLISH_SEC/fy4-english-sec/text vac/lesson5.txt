Unit 5 The Self-Unseeing Thomas Hardy

Introduction Thomas Hardy was a renowned English novelist and poet of the
Victorian and early 20th-century period. He is best known for his vivid
portrayal of rural life and tragic characters struggling against social and
natural forces. Hardy’s work bridges the transition from Victorian realism to
modernist themes.

Hardy’s literary career began with novels, the most famous being Tess of the
d'Urbervilles (1891), Far from the Madding Crowd (1874), The Mayor of
Casterbridge (1886), and Jude the Obscure (1895). These novels are set in the
fictional region of Wessex, a reimagined southwest England, and explore themes
like class conflict, fate, sexuality, and the constraints of social norms. His
bleak yet beautiful depiction of human suffering challenged Victorian ideals and
led to critical controversy—especially with Jude the Obscure, after which he
stopped writing novels and turned to poetry. As a poet, Hardy produced over 900
poems, notable collections being Wessex Poems (1898) and Moments of Vision
(1917). His contributions to both fiction and poetry established him as a major
literary figure whose work continues to influence writers and critics today.

"The Self-Unseeing" by Thomas Hardy is a touching poem that contemplates memory,
loss, and the gentle beauty of the bygone days. In just a handful of stanzas,
Hardy reflects on a memory from his childhood residence, bringing to life the
warmth of family affection and modest pleasures. The poem illustrates how
individuals frequently overlook the importance of experiences while they are
occurring, but only gaining awareness of their significance later on.

Text Here is the ancient floor, Footworn and hollowed and thin, Here was the
former door Where the dead feet walked in.

She sat here in her chair, Smiling into the fire; He who played stood there,
Bowing it higher and higher.

Childlike, I danced in a dream; Blessings emblazoned that day; Everything glowed
with a gleam; Yet we were looking away!

Glossary Emblaze: conspicuously inscribe or display a design on Gleam: shine
brightly Footworn: worn out by footsteps (over a long period of time)
