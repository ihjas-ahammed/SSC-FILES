Electron and hole mobilities in Si are 0.135 and 0.048 $\mathrm{m}^{2}$/V·s. Find the conductivity of intrinsic Si at 300 K if $n_i=1.5\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$. The sample is then doped with $10^{23}$ phosphorus atoms per $\mathrm{m}^{3}$. Find the equilibrium hole concentration, the conductivity and the position of $E_F$ relative to the intrinsic level.

$\sigma_i=en_i(\mu_n+\mu_p)$; doped: $n\approx N_d$, $p=n_i^2/n$, $\sigma\approx en\mu_n$; $n=n_ie^{(E_F-E_i)/kT}$.

$\sigma_i=1.6\times10^{-19}\times1.5\times10^{16}\times(0.135+0.048)=4.39\times10^{-4}\ (\Omega\,\mathrm{m})^{-1}$.

Doped: $n=N_d=10^{23}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$; $p=\dfrac{(1.5\times10^{16})^2}{10^{23}}=2.25\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$.

$\sigma=1.6\times10^{-19}\times10^{23}\times0.135=2.16\times10^{3}\ (\Omega\,\mathrm{m})^{-1}$.

$E_F-E_i=kT\ln\dfrac{n}{n_i}=8.62\times10^{-5}\times300\times\ln\dfrac{10^{23}}{1.5\times10^{16}}=0.406$ eV above the intrinsic level.

The same Si is doped instead with $10^{22}$ boron atoms per $\mathrm{m}^{3}$. Find $p$, $n$, $\sigma$ and $E_i-E_F$.

In intrinsic GaAs, $\mu_n=0.85$, $\mu_p=0.04$ $\mathrm{m}^{2}$/V·s, $m_n^*=0.068m_0$, $m_p^*=0.5m_0$, and $E_g=1.43$ eV at 300 K. Find $n_i$ and the conductivity.

$n_i=2\left(\dfrac{2\pi kT}{h^2}\right)^{3/2}(m_n^*m_p^*)^{3/4}e^{-E_g/2kT}$.

$\left(\dfrac{2\pi\times1.38\times10^{-23}\times300}{(6.63\times10^{-34})^2}\right)^{3/2}$ and $(0.068\times0.5)^{3/4}(9.1\times10^{-31})^{3/2}$, with $e^{-1.43/(2\times8.62\times10^{-5}\times300)}$, give $n_i=1.94\times10^{12}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$.

$\sigma=en_i(\mu_n+\mu_p)=1.6\times10^{-19}\times1.94\times10^{12}\times0.89=2.76\times10^{-7}\ (\Omega\,\mathrm{m})^{-1}$.

The resistivity of an intrinsic semiconductor is 4.5 $Ω$ m at 20 °C and 2.0 $Ω$ m at 32 °C. Find the band gap.

$\sigma=CT^{3/2}e^{-E_g/2kT}$, so $\dfrac{\sigma_2}{\sigma_1}=\dfrac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}=\left(\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}\right)^{3/2}\exp\!\left[\dfrac{E_g}{2k}\left(\dfrac1{T_1}-\dfrac1{T_2}\right)\right]$.

$T_1=293$ K, $T_2=305$ K. Take logs: $\dfrac{E_g}{2k}\left(\dfrac1{293}-\dfrac1{305}\right)=\ln\dfrac{4.5}{2.0}+\dfrac32\ln\dfrac{293}{305}$.

$\dfrac{E_g}{2\times1.38\times10^{-23}}\times1.343\times10^{-4}=0.811-0.060=0.751$, so $E_g=1.54\times10^{-19}$ J $=0.96$ eV.

Si at 300 K has $N_c=2.8\times10^{25}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$. It is doped with $N_d=10^{22}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$ (all ionised). How far below $E_c$ is $E_F$?

$n=N_ce^{-(E_c-E_F)/kT}$ with $n=N_d$, so $E_c-E_F=kT\ln(N_c/N_d)$.

$E_c-E_F=0.0259\times\ln(2.8\times10^{3})=0.0259\times7.94=0.21$ eV.

By how much does $E_F$ move if the doping is raised tenfold? ($kT\ln10=0.06$ eV closer to $E_c$.)

The conductivity of intrinsic Si is $4.17\times10^{-5}$ and $4\times10^{-4}\ (\Omega\,\mathrm m)^{-1}$ at 0 °C and 27 °C. Find the average band gap.

$\sigma=CT^{3/2}e^{-E_g/2kT}$ (mobilities taken constant), so $\ln\dfrac{\sigma_2}{\sigma_1}=\dfrac32\ln\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}+\dfrac{E_g}{2k}\left(\dfrac1{T_1}-\dfrac1{T_2}\right)$.

$T_1=273$ K, $T_2=300$ K. $\ln\dfrac{4\times10^{-4}}{4.17\times10^{-5}}=\ln9.59=2.261$; $\dfrac32\ln\dfrac{300}{273}=0.141$.

$\dfrac1{273}-\dfrac1{300}=3.30\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$, so $\dfrac{E_g}{2k}=\dfrac{2.261-0.141}{3.30\times10^{-4}}=6.43\times10^{3}$ K.

$N_d=\dfrac{\sigma}{e\mu_n}=\dfrac{39}{1.6\times10^{-19}\times0.39}=6.25\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$.

The conductivity of a semiconductor changes when the electron concentration is varied (by moving the impurity level). Show that it passes through a minimum when $n=n_i\sqrt{\mu_p/\mu_n}$, and find the minimum value.

$\sigma=e(n\mu_n+p\mu_p)$ with $p=n_i^2/n$ (mass action). Differentiate with respect to $n$.

$\sigma(n)=e\left(n\mu_n+\dfrac{n_i^2}{n}\mu_p\right)$. $\dfrac{d\sigma}{dn}=e\left(\mu_n-\dfrac{n_i^2\mu_p}{n^2}\right)=0\Rightarrow n=n_i\sqrt{\dfrac{\mu_p}{\mu_n}}$.

$\dfrac{d^2\sigma}{dn^2}=\dfrac{2en_i^2\mu_p}{n^3}>0$: a minimum.

There $p=\dfrac{n_i^2}{n}=n_i\sqrt{\dfrac{\mu_n}{\mu_p}}$, so $\sigma_{\min}=e\left(n_i\sqrt{\mu_p\mu_n}+n_i\sqrt{\mu_n\mu_p}\right)=2en_i\sqrt{\mu_n\mu_p}$.

In intrinsic Ge, $\mu_n=0.39$ and $\mu_p=0.19\ \mathrm{m^2V^{-1}s^{-1}}$, $E_g=0.67$ eV, $m_n^*=0.55m_0$, $m_p^*=0.37m_0$. Find $n_i$ and $\sigma$ at 300 K. How many donors per $\mathrm{m}^{3}$ raise the conductivity by a factor of $10^4$?

$n_i=2\left(\dfrac{2\pi m_0kT}{h^2}\right)^{3/2}\left(\dfrac{m_n^*m_p^*}{m_0^2}\right)^{3/4}e^{-E_g/2kT}$. Useful number: $2\left(\dfrac{2\pi m_0kT}{h^2}\right)^{3/2}=2.51\times10^{25}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$ at 300 K.

$(0.55\times0.37)^{3/4}=0.2035^{3/4}=0.303$. $\dfrac{E_g}{2kT}=\dfrac{0.67}{2\times0.02585}=12.96$, $e^{-12.96}=2.35\times10^{-6}$.

$n_i=2.51\times10^{25}\times0.303\times2.35\times10^{-6}=1.8\times10^{19}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}$.

In an n-type semiconductor the Fermi level lies 0.5 eV below the conduction band. If the donor concentration is tripled, find the new position of the Fermi level, given $kT=0.03$ eV.

The usual treatment uses the n-type result for moderate temperatures: $E_F=\dfrac{E_c+E_d}{2}+\dfrac{kT}{2}\ln\dfrac{N_d}{N_c}$. Only the last term changes.

$\Delta E_F=\dfrac{kT}{2}\ln3=\dfrac{0.03}{2}\times1.099=0.016$ eV upward.

New position: $0.5-0.016\approx0.48$ eV below $E_c$.

If instead every donor is ionised ($n=N_d$, so $E_c-E_F=kT\ln(N_c/N_d)$), the shift doubles to $kT\ln3=0.033$ eV and the answer is 0.47 eV. Say which model you use: the usual printed answer, 0.48 eV, is the half-shift one.

The band gaps of diamond and silicon are 5.4 and 1.1 eV. Estimate the temperature at which diamond has the same conductivity as Si at 27 °C.

Take $\sigma\propto e^{-E_g/2kT}$ with similar mobilities and prefactors, so equal conductivity needs equal exponents.

$\dfrac{5.4}{2kT}=\dfrac{1.1}{2k\times300}\Rightarrow T=300\times\dfrac{5.4}{1.1}=1473$ K $\approx1200$ °C.
