Intrinsic: pure Si or Ge. Carriers come only from thermally broken bonds, so $n=p=n_i$, small and strongly temperature dependent. $E_F$ near mid-gap.

Extrinsic: doped (1 in $10^6$–$10^8$). Group V donors (P, As, Sb) give n-type: donor level 0.01 eV (Ge) / 0.03 eV (Si) below $E_c$, electrons majority. Group III acceptors (B, Al, Ga, In) give p-type, holes majority. Mass action $np=n_i^2$ still holds; $E_F$ moves towards $E_c$ (n) or $E_v$ (p).

Drift velocity: the extra average velocity a carrier gains in a field, between collisions: $v_d=\mu E$. Mobility $\mu$: drift velocity per unit field ($\mathrm{m^2V^{-1}s^{-1}}$). $J=e(n\mu_n+p\mu_p)E$, so $\sigma=e(n\mu_n+p\mu_p)$; intrinsic $\sigma_i=en_i(\mu_n+\mu_p)$.

At a given temperature $np=n_i^2=N_cN_ve^{-E_g/kT}$, independent of doping and of the position of $E_F$ (it cancels in the product). Raising $n$ by doping lowers $p$ in proportion.

Orbital motion and spin of electrons (current loops), plus a negligible nuclear contribution ($\sim1/1836$). Filled shells cancel in pairs, so a permanent moment needs unpaired electrons (transition and rare-earth ions). Unit: $\mu_B=e\hbar/2m=9.27\times10^{-24}\ \mathrm{A\,m^2}$; $\mu=g\mu_B\sqrt{J(J+1)}$.

The field actually acting on an atom: the applied field plus the fields of all other polarised atoms, not its own. For cubic symmetry $E_{\text{loc}}=E+P/3\varepsilon_0$, larger than the macroscopic field.

Ferroelectric: spontaneous, reversible polarisation with a $P$–$E$ hysteresis loop and a Curie point ($\mathrm{BaTiO_{3}}$). Piezoelectric: polarisation produced by stress (and strain by a field), needs only the absence of a centre of inversion (quartz). Every ferroelectric is piezoelectric; not every piezoelectric is ferroelectric.

Set $n=p$: $N_ce^{-(E_c-E_F)/kT}=N_ve^{-(E_F-E_v)/kT}$. Take logs: $E_F=\dfrac{E_c+E_v}{2}+\dfrac{kT}{2}\ln\dfrac{N_v}{N_c}=\dfrac{E_c+E_v}{2}+\dfrac{3kT}{4}\ln\dfrac{m_p^*}{m_n^*}$. At 0 K (or if $m_p^*=m_n^*$), $E_F$ is exactly at mid-gap; normally $m_p^*>m_n^*$ and $E_F$ rises slightly with $T$ (about 0.01 eV for Si at 300 K). $\sigma\propto T^{3/2}e^{-E_g/2kT}$: $\dfrac{E_g}{2k}\left(\dfrac1{293}-\dfrac1{305}\right)=\ln\dfrac{4.5}{2.0}+\dfrac32\ln\dfrac{293}{305}=0.751$, so $E_g=0.96$ eV.

Force balance $m\omega^2\rho=m\omega_0^2\rho-e\rho\omega B$ gives $\omega\approx\omega_0-eB/2m$ (Larmor). Extra current $I=-e^2B/4\pi m$, moment $\mu=I\pi\rho^2=-e^2B\rho^2/4m$; $Z$ electrons with $\langle\rho^2\rangle=\tfrac23\langle r^2\rangle$: $\mu=-Ze^2B\langle r^2\rangle/6m$; $\chi=-\mu_0NZe^2\langle r^2\rangle/6m$: negative, independent of $T$.

$\dfrac{\alpha_e}{\alpha_e+\alpha_i}=\dfrac{(n^2-1)/(n^2+2)}{(\varepsilon_r-1)/(\varepsilon_r+2)}=\dfrac{0.294}{0.605}=0.486$; ionic share 51.4%.

$dn=D(E)f(E)dE$, $D(E)=\frac{4\pi}{h^3}(2m_n^*)^{3/2}(E-E_c)^{1/2}$, $f\approx e^{-(E-E_F)/kT}$. Integrate from $E_c$ with $x=(E-E_c)/kT$, $\int_0^\infty x^{1/2}e^{-x}dx=\sqrt\pi/2$: $n=N_ce^{-(E_c-E_F)/kT}$, $N_c=2(2\pi m_n^*kT/h^2)^{3/2}$. Likewise $p=N_ve^{-(E_F-E_v)/kT}$. Product: $np=N_cN_ve^{-E_g/kT}=n_i^2$, so $n_i=2(2\pi kT/h^2)^{3/2}(m_n^*m_p^*)^{3/4}e^{-E_g/2kT}$. (Full working in the Module III derivations.)

$E=-\mu B\cos\theta$, $dn\propto e^{\mu B\cos\theta/kT}2\pi\sin\theta\,d\theta$; $M=N\mu\langle\cos\theta\rangle=N\mu L(x)$, $L=\coth x-1/x$, $x=\mu B/kT$. For $x\ll1$, $L\approx x/3$: $\chi=\mu_0N\mu^2/3kT=C/T$ (Curie). Weiss: molecular field $B_E=\lambda M$, so $M=\frac{N\mu^2}{3kT}(B+\lambda M)$, giving $\chi=C/(T-T_c)$, $T_c=\lambda C/\mu_0$ (Curie–Weiss). Below $T_c$, $M\ne0$ at $B=0$: spontaneous magnetisation (graphical solution); domains explain unmagnetised iron and hysteresis; the field is the exchange interaction.
