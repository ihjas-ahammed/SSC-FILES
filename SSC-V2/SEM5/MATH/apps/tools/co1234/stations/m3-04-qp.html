Climb the ladder LP ⊂ QP ⊂ QCQP ⊂ SOCP. A QP has a convex quadratic objective over a polyhedron; a QCQP also has convex quadratic constraints; an SOCP has constraints $\|A_i\mathbf x+\mathbf b_i\|_2\le\mathbf c_i^T\mathbf x+d_i$ and contains all of these. QP and QCQP (§4.4)

QP: minimise $\tfrac12\mathbf x^TP\mathbf x+\mathbf q^T\mathbf x+r$ s.t. $G\mathbf x\preceq\mathbf h$, $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$, with $P\in\mathbf S^n_+$. QCQP: minimise $\tfrac12\mathbf x^TP_0\mathbf x+\mathbf q_0^T\mathbf x+r_0$ s.t. $\tfrac12\mathbf x^TP_i\mathbf x+\mathbf q_i^T\mathbf x+r_i\le0$, $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$, all $P_i\succeq0$. The feasible set of a QCQP is an intersection of ellipsoids (when $P_i\succ0$) and an affine set.

Examples (§4.4.1). Least squares $\|A\mathbf x-\mathbf b\|_2^2=\mathbf x^TA^TA\mathbf x-2\mathbf b^TA\mathbf x+\mathbf b^T\mathbf b$, solution $A^\dagger\mathbf b$; with bounds $l_i\le x_i\le u_i$ it is a QP without closed form. Distance between polyhedra: minimise $\|\mathbf x_1-\mathbf x_2\|_2^2$ s.t. $A_1\mathbf x_1\preceq\mathbf b_1$, $A_2\mathbf x_2\preceq\mathbf b_2$. Bounding variance: for a distribution $\mathbf p$ restricted to a polyhedron, $\mathbf{var}(f)=\sum p_if_i^2-(\sum p_if_i)^2$ is a concave quadratic in $\mathbf p$, so maximising it is a convex QP. LP with random cost: minimise $\bar{\mathbf c}^T\mathbf x+\gamma\mathbf x^T\Sigma\mathbf x$ (risk-sensitive cost). Markowitz portfolio: minimise $\mathbf x^T\Sigma\mathbf x$ s.t. $\bar{\mathbf p}^T\mathbf x\ge r_{\min}$, $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf x=1$, $\mathbf x\succeq0$.

Second-order cone programming (§4.4.2)

SOCP: minimise $\mathbf f^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $\|A_i\mathbf x+\mathbf b_i\|_2\le\mathbf c_i^T\mathbf x+d_i$, $F\mathbf x=\mathbf g$. Each constraint says $(A_i\mathbf x+\mathbf b_i,\ \mathbf c_i^T\mathbf x+d_i)$ lies in the second-order cone. $A_i=0$ gives an LP constraint; $\mathbf c_i=0$ gives a QCQP constraint (square both sides).

Robust LP. Uncertain $\mathbf a_i$ in ellipsoids:

In the stochastic version, $\mathbf a_i\sim\mathcal N(\bar{\mathbf a}_i,\Sigma_i)$ and we ask $\mathbf{prob}(\mathbf a_i^T\mathbf x\le b_i)\ge\eta$. Since $\mathbf a_i^T\mathbf x\sim\mathcal N(\bar{\mathbf a}_i^T\mathbf x,\ \mathbf x^T\Sigma_i\mathbf x)$, this is $\bar{\mathbf a}_i^T\mathbf x+\Phi^{-1}(\eta)\|\Sigma_i^{1/2}\mathbf x\|_2\le b_i$: an SOC constraint when $\eta\ge\tfrac12$. Example 4.8: portfolio with a loss-risk constraint $\mathbf{prob}(r\le\alpha)\le\beta$ becomes an SOCP.

Minimal surface (discretised area $\sum\|(f_{i+1,j}-f_{ij},\ f_{i,j+1}-f_{ij},\ h)\|_2$) is another SOCP example.

$\|A\mathbf x+\mathbf b\|_2\le\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+d$ must not be squared when $\mathbf c\ne\mathbf 0$: $\|A\mathbf x+\mathbf b\|_2^2\le(\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+d)^2$ is not convex and loses the sign condition $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+d\ge0$.

 Show $x^2/y\le t$ with $y\gt0$ is an SOC constraint (exercise 4.26).

$x^2\le ty$, $t,y\ge0$ ⇔ $\left\|\begin{pmatrix}2x\\t-y\end{pmatrix}\right\|_2\le t+y$: square to get $4x^2+(t-y)^2\le(t+y)^2$, i.e. $x^2\le ty$.

 Is a QP with $P\not\succeq0$ a convex problem?

No: the objective is not convex, and the problem is NP-hard in general.
