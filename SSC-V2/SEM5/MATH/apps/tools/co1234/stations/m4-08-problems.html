Chapter 5 exercises on the examined sections (§5.1–5.5, 5.8, 5.9): computing duals, weak duality edge cases, KKT systems, and theorems of alternatives. Exercise 5.1 is worked in full as a derivation in §5.2. Dual problems and weak duality

Minimise $x^2+1$ s.t. $(x-2)(x-4)\le0$. (a) Feasible set, $p^\star$, $x^\star$. (b) Plot $f_0$ and $L$ for several $\lambda$; derive $g$. (c) State the dual, verify it is concave, find $d^\star$, $\lambda^\star$; does strong duality hold? (d) Sensitivity (not examined).

(a) $[2,4]$, $x^\star=2$, $p^\star=5$. (b) $L=(1+\lambda)x^2-6\lambda x+1+8\lambda$; $g(\lambda)=-\dfrac{9\lambda^2}{1+\lambda}+1+8\lambda$ for $\lambda\gt-1$ ($-\infty$ for $\lambda\le-1$). (c) Maximise $g(\lambda)$ s.t. $\lambda\ge0$; $g''(\lambda)=-18/(1+\lambda)^3\lt0$: concave. $g'=0$ gives $\lambda^\star=2$, $d^\star=5=p^\star$: strong duality (Slater: $x=3$ is strictly feasible). See the derivation "Exercise 5.1" and the sim.

Show: if $p^\star=-\infty$ then $d^\star=-\infty$; if $d^\star=\infty$ then $p^\star=\infty$.

For every $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0$, $\boldsymbol\nu$ and every feasible $\mathbf x$: $g(\boldsymbol\lambda,\boldsymbol\nu)\le f_0(\mathbf x)$. If $p^\star=-\infty$ there are feasible $\mathbf x$ with $f_0(\mathbf x)$ arbitrarily negative, so $g(\boldsymbol\lambda,\boldsymbol\nu)=-\infty$ for every $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0$: $d^\star=-\infty$. If $d^\star=\infty$, $g$ takes arbitrarily large values; a single feasible $\mathbf x$ would bound them by $f_0(\mathbf x)\lt\infty$. So no feasible point exists: $p^\star=\infty$.

Express the dual of minimise $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $f(\mathbf x)\le0$ ($\mathbf c\ne\mathbf 0$, $f$ not necessarily convex) in terms of $f^*$. Why is it convex?

$g(\lambda)=\inf_{\mathbf x}(\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+\lambda f(\mathbf x))$. For $\lambda=0$: $\inf\mathbf c^T\mathbf x=-\infty$. For $\lambda\gt0$: $g(\lambda)=-\lambda\sup_{\mathbf x}\big((-\mathbf c/\lambda)^T\mathbf x-f(\mathbf x)\big)=-\lambda f^*(-\mathbf c/\lambda)$. Dual: maximise $-\lambda f^*(-\mathbf c/\lambda)$ s.t. $\lambda\gt0$. Convex because $\lambda f^*(-\mathbf c/\lambda)$ is the perspective of the convex function $f^*$ (composed with $\mathbf y\mapsto-\mathbf y$), hence convex in $\lambda$, and we maximise its negative.

Find the dual of minimise $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $G\mathbf x\preceq\mathbf h$, $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$, making the implicit constraints explicit.

$L=\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+\boldsymbol\lambda^T(G\mathbf x-\mathbf h)+\boldsymbol\nu^T(A\mathbf x-\mathbf b)=(\mathbf c+G^T\boldsymbol\lambda+A^T\boldsymbol\nu)^T\mathbf x-\mathbf h^T\boldsymbol\lambda-\mathbf b^T\boldsymbol\nu$. Bounded below in $\mathbf x$ only if the coefficient vanishes. Dual: maximise $-\mathbf h^T\boldsymbol\lambda-\mathbf b^T\boldsymbol\nu$ s.t. $G^T\boldsymbol\lambda+A^T\boldsymbol\nu+\mathbf c=\mathbf 0$, $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq0$.

Show $\sup_{\mathbf z\in Z}\inf_{\mathbf w\in W}f(\mathbf w,\mathbf z)\le\inf_{\mathbf w\in W}\sup_{\mathbf z\in Z}f(\mathbf w,\mathbf z)$ for any $f$, $W$, $Z$.

For any $\tilde{\mathbf w}\in W$, $\tilde{\mathbf z}\in Z$: $\inf_{\mathbf w}f(\mathbf w,\tilde{\mathbf z})\le f(\tilde{\mathbf w},\tilde{\mathbf z})\le\sup_{\mathbf z}f(\tilde{\mathbf w},\mathbf z)$. The left end depends only on $\tilde{\mathbf z}$, the right end only on $\tilde{\mathbf w}$. Take the supremum over $\tilde{\mathbf z}$ of the left and the infimum over $\tilde{\mathbf w}$ of the right.

 KKT conditions

Minimise $\|A\mathbf x-\mathbf b\|_2^2$ s.t. $G\mathbf x=\mathbf h$, $\rank A=n$, $\rank G=p$. Give the KKT conditions and $\mathbf x^\star$, $\boldsymbol\nu^\star$.

KKT: $G\mathbf x^\star=\mathbf h$, $2A^T(A\mathbf x^\star-\mathbf b)+G^T\boldsymbol\nu^\star=\mathbf 0$. From the second, $\mathbf x^\star=(A^TA)^{-1}(A^T\mathbf b-\tfrac12G^T\boldsymbol\nu^\star)$. Substitute into $G\mathbf x^\star=\mathbf h$: $$\boldsymbol\nu^\star=2\big(G(A^TA)^{-1}G^T\big)^{-1}\big(G(A^TA)^{-1}A^T\mathbf b-\mathbf h\big).$$ ($G(A^TA)^{-1}G^T$ is invertible since $A^TA\succ0$ and $G$ has full row rank.) Convex with affine constraints, so KKT is necessary and sufficient.

Minimise $-3x_1^2+x_2^2+2x_3^2+2(x_1+x_2+x_3)$ s.t. $x_1^2+x_2^2+x_3^2=1$. Derive the KKT conditions, find all KKT pairs, and identify the optimum.

One quadratic constraint: strong duality holds (§5.2.4). The optimal $\nu$ must make $L$ convex in $\mathbf x$ (it must be minimised by $\mathbf x^\star$), i.e. $\nu\ge3$.

$L=(\nu-3)x_1^2+(1+\nu)x_2^2+(2+\nu)x_3^2+2(x_1+x_2+x_3)-\nu$. Stationarity: $x_1=\dfrac{1}{3-\nu}$, $x_2=-\dfrac1{1+\nu}$, $x_3=-\dfrac1{2+\nu}$; feasibility: $\dfrac1{(3-\nu)^2}+\dfrac1{(1+\nu)^2}+\dfrac1{(2+\nu)^2}=1$. This has four real roots (computed numerically): $\nu\approx-3.149$, $0.224$, $1.892$, $4.035$, giving objective values $4.647$, $-1.130$, $-1.592$, $-5.366$. Only $\nu=4.035\gt3$ makes $L$ convex in $\mathbf x$, so it is dual optimal, and $\mathbf x^\star\approx(-0.966,-0.199,-0.166)$, $p^\star\approx-5.366$ (the smallest of the four values, as it must be).

Solve with the KKT conditions.

$f_1=3-x_1-x_2\le0$, $f_2=x_2-1\le0$. Stationarity: $2x_1-\lambda_1=0$, $4x_2-\lambda_1+\lambda_2=0$. Try only $f_1$ active ($\lambda_2=0$): $x_1=\lambda_1/2$, $x_2=\lambda_1/4$, $x_1+x_2=3$ ⇒ $\lambda_1=4$, $\mathbf x=(2,1)$: then $f_2=0$, still feasible, $\lambda_2=0\ge0$. All conditions hold: $\mathbf x^\star=(2,1)$, $\boldsymbol\lambda^\star=(4,0)$, $p^\star=4+2=6$. (Convex, so KKT ⇒ optimal.)

 Alternatives and generalized inequalities

Derive from §5.8.2: $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ has a solution iff there is no $\mathbf y$ with $A^T\mathbf y=0$, $\mathbf b^T\mathbf y\ne0$.

Feasibility problem with only the affine constraint: $g(\boldsymbol\nu)=\inf_{\mathbf x}\boldsymbol\nu^T(A\mathbf x-\mathbf b)=-\mathbf b^T\boldsymbol\nu$ if $A^T\boldsymbol\nu=0$, $-\infty$ otherwise. The alternative system $g(\boldsymbol\nu)\gt0$ is $A^T\boldsymbol\nu=0$, $\mathbf b^T\boldsymbol\nu\lt0$. With only affine constraints strong duality holds, so these are strong alternatives. Replacing $\boldsymbol\nu$ by $-\boldsymbol\nu$ covers $\mathbf b^T\boldsymbol\nu\gt0$, so the alternative is $A^T\mathbf y=0$, $\mathbf b^T\mathbf y\ne0$.

$p_{ij}\ge0$, $P^T\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1$. Prove there is $\mathbf y\succeq0$, $\mathbf 1^T\mathbf y=1$, $P\mathbf y=\mathbf y$.

Write the system as $\begin{pmatrix}P-I\\\mathbf 1^T\end{pmatrix}\mathbf y=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf 0\\1\end{pmatrix}$, $\mathbf y\succeq0$. By Farkas it is solvable unless some $(\mathbf w,t)$ has $(P-I)^T\mathbf w+t\mathbf 1\succeq0$ and $t\lt0$, i.e. $(P^T\mathbf w)_j-w_j\ge-t\gt0$ for every $j$. Take $j$ with $w_j=\max_iw_i$: $(P^T\mathbf w)_j=\sum_ip_{ij}w_i\le w_j\sum_ip_{ij}=w_j$, so $(P^T\mathbf w)_j-w_j\le0$: contradiction. Hence an equilibrium distribution exists.

Find the dual of minimise $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $A\mathbf x\preceq_K\mathbf b$.

$L=\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+\boldsymbol\lambda^T(A\mathbf x-\mathbf b)$, $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq_{K^*}0$. $g(\boldsymbol\lambda)=-\mathbf b^T\boldsymbol\lambda$ if $A^T\boldsymbol\lambda+\mathbf c=0$, else $-\infty$. Dual: maximise $-\mathbf b^T\boldsymbol\lambda$ s.t. $A^T\boldsymbol\lambda+\mathbf c=0$, $\boldsymbol\lambda\succeq_{K^*}0$ — a conic problem in standard form.

Show the dual of minimise $\mathbf f^T\mathbf x$ s.t. $\|A_i\mathbf x+\mathbf b_i\|_2\le\mathbf c_i^T\mathbf x+d_i$ is maximise $\sum(\mathbf b_i^T\mathbf u_i-d_iv_i)$ s.t. $\sum(A_i^T\mathbf u_i-\mathbf c_iv_i)+\mathbf f=0$, $\|\mathbf u_i\|_2\le v_i$.

Each constraint says $-(A_i\mathbf x+\mathbf b_i,\ \mathbf c_i^T\mathbf x+d_i)\preceq_{K}0$ for the second-order cone $K$ (self-dual). Multiplier $(\mathbf u_i,v_i)\in K$, i.e. $\|\mathbf u_i\|_2\le v_i$. $L=\mathbf f^T\mathbf x-\sum_i\big(\mathbf u_i^T(A_i\mathbf x+\mathbf b_i)+v_i(\mathbf c_i^T\mathbf x+d_i)\big)$. Choosing the sign convention $\mathbf u_i\to-\mathbf u_i$ (the cone condition is unchanged), $L=\big(\mathbf f+\sum(A_i^T\mathbf u_i-\mathbf c_iv_i)\big)^T\mathbf x+\sum(\mathbf b_i^T\mathbf u_i-d_iv_i)$; bounded in $\mathbf x$ iff the bracket is zero, which gives the stated dual.
