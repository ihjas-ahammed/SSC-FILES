Build convex functions from convex pieces: nonnegative weighted sums, composition with an affine map, pointwise maximum and supremum, composition under monotonicity rules, partial minimisation over a convex set, and the perspective. Recognising these is how most convexity proofs are done. Weighted sums and affine composition (§3.2.1–3.2.2)

(1) $f=w_1f_1+\dots+w_mf_m$ with $w_i\ge0$, $f_i$ convex ⇒ convex (also integrals $\int w(y)f(\mathbf x,y)\,dy$, $w\ge0$). (2) $g(\mathbf x)=f(A\mathbf x+\mathbf b)$ with $f$ convex ⇒ $g$ convex (on $\{\mathbf x\mid A\mathbf x+\mathbf b\in\dom f\}$).

Proof of (1) in epigraph form: $\epi(wf)$ is the image of $\epi f$ under $(\mathbf x,t)\mapsto(\mathbf x,wt)$. Examples: $f(\mathbf x)=-\sum_i\log(b_i-\mathbf a_i^T\mathbf x)$ (log barrier) and $\|A\mathbf x+\mathbf b\|$ are convex.

Pointwise maximum and supremum (§3.2.3)

$f=\max\{f_1,f_2\}$ is convex if $f_1,f_2$ are: $f(\theta\mathbf x+(1-\theta)\mathbf y)\le\max\{\theta f_1(\mathbf x)+(1-\theta)f_1(\mathbf y),\theta f_2(\mathbf x)+(1-\theta)f_2(\mathbf y)\}\le\theta f(\mathbf x)+(1-\theta)f(\mathbf y)$. More generally $g(\mathbf x)=\sup_{\mathbf y\in\mathcal A}f(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$ is convex when $f(\cdot,\mathbf y)$ is convex for each $\mathbf y$: $\epi g=\bigcap_{\mathbf y}\epi f(\cdot,\mathbf y)$.

Example 3.5. Piecewise-linear $\max\{\mathbf a_1^T\mathbf x+b_1,\dots,\mathbf a_L^T\mathbf x+b_L\}$. Example 3.6. Sum of the $r$ largest components $f(\mathbf x)=x_{[1]}+\dots+x_{[r]}$ = max of all sums of $r$ distinct components. Example 3.7. Support function $S_C(\mathbf x)=\sup\{\mathbf x^T\mathbf y\mid\mathbf y\in C\}$ (a supremum of linear functions). Example 3.8. Distance to the farthest point $\sup_{\mathbf y\in C}\|\mathbf x-\mathbf y\|$. Example 3.9. Least-squares cost as a function of weights, $g(\mathbf w)=\inf_{\mathbf x}\sum w_i(\mathbf a_i^T\mathbf x-b_i)^2$, is concave (infimum of linear functions of $\mathbf w$). Example 3.10. $\lambda_{\max}(X)=\sup\{\mathbf y^TX\mathbf y\mid\|\mathbf y\|_2=1\}$ is convex on $\mathbf S^n$. Example 3.11. $\|X\|_2=\sup\{\mathbf u^TX\mathbf v\mid\|\mathbf u\|_2=\|\mathbf v\|_2=1\}$ is convex.

Representation. Every closed convex $f$ is the supremum of its affine under-estimators: $f(\mathbf x)=\sup\{g(\mathbf x)\mid g\text{ affine},\ g(\mathbf z)\le f(\mathbf z)\ \forall\mathbf z\}$.

Composition (§3.2.4)

$f(\mathbf x)=h(g(\mathbf x))$, $h:\mathbf R^k\to\mathbf R$, using the extended-value $\tilde h$. Scalar case $k=1$ (from $f''=h''(g)g'^2+h'(g)g''$):
• $h$ convex, $\tilde h$ nondecreasing, $g$ convex ⇒ $f$ convex.
• $h$ convex, $\tilde h$ nonincreasing, $g$ concave ⇒ $f$ convex.
• $h$ concave, $\tilde h$ nondecreasing, $g$ concave ⇒ $f$ concave.
• $h$ concave, $\tilde h$ nonincreasing, $g$ convex ⇒ $f$ concave.
Vector case: the same with "nondecreasing/nonincreasing in each argument" and each $g_i$ convex or concave accordingly.

Example 3.12 shows the extended-value monotonicity matters: $h(x)=x^2$ on $\dom h=[1,2]$ is nondecreasing on its domain, but $\tilde h$ is not; $g(x)=x^2$ gives $f=x^4$ on $[-\sqrt2,-1]\cup[1,\sqrt2]$, not convex.

Example 3.13. $g$ convex ⇒ $e^{g(\mathbf x)}$ convex. $g$ concave and positive ⇒ $\log g(\mathbf x)$ concave, $1/g(\mathbf x)$ convex. $g$ convex nonnegative, $p\ge1$ ⇒ $g(\mathbf x)^p$ convex. $g$ convex ⇒ $-\log(-g(\mathbf x))$ convex on $\{g\lt0\}$. Example 3.14. $g_i$ convex ⇒ $\log\sum e^{g_i(\mathbf x)}$ convex; $g_i$ concave positive ⇒ $\sum\log g_i$ concave; $g_i$ concave nonnegative, $0\le p\le1$ ⇒ $(\sum g_i^p)^{1/p}$ concave.

Minimisation (§3.2.5)

If $f(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$ is convex in $(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$ and $C$ is a nonempty convex set, then $g(\mathbf x)=\inf_{\mathbf y\in C}f(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$ is convex (where $g\gt-\infty$). Proof by epigraph: $\epi g$ is the projection of $\{(\mathbf x,\mathbf y,t)\mid f(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\le t,\ \mathbf y\in C\}$.

Example 3.16. $\mathbf{dist}(\mathbf x,S)=\inf_{\mathbf y\in S}\|\mathbf x-\mathbf y\|$ is convex if $S$ is convex. Example 3.17. $g(\mathbf x)=\inf\{h(\mathbf y)\mid A\mathbf y=\mathbf x\}$ is convex if $h$ is.

Partial maximisation of a jointly convex function preserves convexity; partial minimisation needs joint convexity in $(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$ and a convex $C$. Convex in each variable separately is not enough.

 Perspective of a function (§3.2.6)

$g(\mathbf x,t)=tf(\mathbf x/t)$, $\dom g=\{(\mathbf x,t)\mid\mathbf x/t\in\dom f,\ t\gt0\}$. If $f$ is convex, so is $g$: $(\mathbf x,t,s)\in\epi g\iff f(\mathbf x/t)\le s/t\iff(\mathbf x/t,s/t)\in\epi f$, so $\epi g$ is the inverse image of $\epi f$ under the perspective map.

Example 3.18. $f=\mathbf x^T\mathbf x$ ⇒ $g(\mathbf x,t)=\mathbf x^T\mathbf x/t$ convex. Example 3.19. $f=-\log x$ ⇒ $g(x,t)=t\log t-t\log x$ convex (relative entropy); hence the KL divergence $\sum u_i\log(u_i/v_i)-u_i+v_i$ is convex. Example 3.20. $g(\mathbf x)=(\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+d)f\big((A\mathbf x+\mathbf b)/(\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+d)\big)$ is convex on $\mathbf c^T\mathbf x+d\gt0$.

Show $f(\mathbf x)=\max_i(\mathbf a_i^T\mathbf x+b_i)+\|\mathbf x\|_2^2$ is convex.

A pointwise maximum of affine functions is convex; $\|\mathbf x\|_2^2$ is convex; their sum is convex.

 Is $e^{-x^2}$ convex? Use the composition rules.

The rules do not apply ($-x^2$ is concave and $e^u$ is increasing: that combination gives no conclusion). Directly, $f''=(4x^2-2)e^{-x^2}\lt0$ near $0$: not convex. (It is log-concave.)
