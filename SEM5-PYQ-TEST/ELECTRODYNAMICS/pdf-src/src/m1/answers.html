Q1Model · syllabus-based

How can an electrically neutral dielectric respond to a static electric field?

Know firstT1P1M1

Its positive and negative charge centres shift slightly relative to one another, or existing molecular dipoles turn. Charges remain bound rather than crossing the whole material.

Neutrality does not prevent polarization.

Q2Legacy PYQ N25 Q5 · expanded

Define atomic polarizability and give its SI unit.

Know firstT1P1M1

For a weak local field, $\mathbf p=\alpha\mathbf E_{\rm local}$. Thus $[\alpha]=(\mathrm{C\,m})/(\mathrm{V/m})=\mathrm{C\,m^2/V}$. The atom is isotropic in this scalar linear model; α describes an individual atom rather than the whole sample.

Polarizability is induced dipole moment per unit local field.

Q8Legacy PYQ N25 Q10 · expanded

What is the physical interpretation of bound charges? Include the case of uniform polarization.

Know firstT4P1M1

Polarization separates positive and negative charge within atoms or molecules, leaving unmatched dipole ends at boundaries and wherever the dipole density varies. These charges remain associated with the material, unlike mobile free charge. For uniform P, $\rho_b=0$ inside but $\sigma_b=P\cdot\hat n$ may remain at its surface.

Bound charges are the macroscopic result of displaced or aligned microscopic dipoles.

Q15Legacy PYQ N25 Q9

Distinguish paramagnets and diamagnets.

Know firstT8P1M1

Diamagnets have a weak induced response opposing the applied field, with $\chi_m<0$ and $\mu_r<1$. Paramagnets have pre-existing moments that partially align with the field, giving small $\chi_m>0$ and $\mu_r>1$. Thermal disorder competes with paramagnetic alignment.

Diamagnets oppose weakly; paramagnets align weakly.

Q16Model · syllabus-based

Why does a paramagnet have weak alignment rather than perfect alignment at ordinary temperature?

Know firstT8P1M1

Permanent atomic moments tend to align with an applied field, but thermal agitation disorders them. The resulting average alignment is small and normally decreases when temperature rises at fixed weak field.

Field alignment competes with thermal disorder.

Q17Legacy PYQ N25 Q11 · expanded

Define magnetic vector potential. How can it describe the field of a magnetized object?

The vector potential A is defined by $\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A$ and has units T m. In magnetostatics a localized magnetized object can be replaced by $\mathbf J_b=\nabla\times M$ and $\mathbf K_b=M\times\hat n$ in the usual current-source potential integrals. A is not unique: adding a gradient leaves its curl unchanged.

B is curl A; equivalent bound currents may be used as A sources.

Q18Legacy PYQ N25 Q18 · adapted marks

Discuss briefly the bound currents in a magnetized medium, emphasizing their physical interpretation.

Know firstT10P1M1

Microscopic magnetic dipoles can be modelled as current loops. Opposite currents on shared interior edges cancel for uniform M, leaving a surface current $K_b=M\times\hat n$. A spatially nonuniform magnetization can also leave $J_b=\nabla\times M$ in the bulk. These are equivalent macroscopic currents, not freely drifting conduction carriers.

Uncancelled microscopic loops produce bound surface and volume currents.

Q20Legacy PYQ N25 Q3 · expanded

Define magnetic susceptibility and relate it to relative permeability for a linear isotropic material.

Magnetic susceptibility measures magnetization per unit H in the linear regime.

Q21Model · syllabus-based

Explain remanence, coercivity and the role of domain behaviour in an M-H hysteresis loop.

Know firstT13P1M1
A ferromagnet contains domains with cooperative moment alignment.Why: Domains need not initially point in the same direction.
Applied H moves walls and rotates domains.Why: Favourable orientations grow.
After H returns to zero, pinning can leave nonzero M, called remanence.Why: The path depends on previous magnetic history.
A reverse H is needed to reduce M to zero; its magnitude on that branch is the coercivity.Why: The two intercepts describe different properties.
A narrow loop suits repeated cycling, whereas substantial coercivity helps a permanent magnet retain its state.Why: Applications depend on loss and resistance to demagnetization.
Remanence measures retained M; coercivity measures the required reverse H.

Q22Model · syllabus-based

Explain the effect of heating a ferromagnet through the Curie temperature, and distinguish the result from diamagnetism.

Know firstT13P1M1
Below the Curie temperature, interactions can maintain spontaneous alignment within domains.Why: Cooperative order survives thermal fluctuations.
Raising temperature increases disorder and reduces spontaneous magnetization.Why: Thermal agitation competes with ordering interactions.
Above the transition, spontaneous ferromagnetic order disappears and the material is paramagnetic.Why: It may still align weakly in an applied field.
This is not diamagnetism: a paramagnetic response is positive and comes from existing moments, whereas the diamagnetic contribution opposes the field.Why: The microscopic mechanisms differ.
Above its Curie temperature a ferromagnet loses spontaneous order and becomes paramagnetic.

Q37Legacy PYQ N25 Q17 · adapted marks

Starting from dipole potential, derive the surface and volume bound-charge representation of a polarized object and explain its physical meaning.

Know firstT4P1M1
Let $\mathcal R=\lvert\mathbf r-\mathbf r'\rvert$, where r is the observation point and r′ labels source points. A volume element has dipole moment $\mathbf P(\mathbf r')d\tau'$.Why: Polarization is moment density.
Its potential sums to $V=(4\pi\epsilon_0)^{-1}\int\mathbf P\cdot\nabla'(1/\mathcal R)\,d\tau'$.Why: The source-coordinate gradient equals $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal R}}/\mathcal R^2$.
Apply the product rule: $\mathbf P\cdot\nabla'(1/\mathcal R)=\nabla'\cdot(\mathbf P/\mathcal R)-(\nabla'\cdot\mathbf P)/\mathcal R$.Why: This moves the derivative from the distance factor to the polarization.
The divergence theorem converts the first volume integral to a surface integral.Why: It equates outward flux and integrated divergence.
Thus $V=(4\pi\epsilon_0)^{-1}[\oint\sigma_b\,da'/\mathcal R+\int\rho_b\,d\tau'/\mathcal R]$.Why: These are exactly the potential formulas for surface and volume charge.
Identify $\sigma_b=P\cdot\hat n$ and $\rho_b=-\nabla\cdot P$. Uniform interior P cancels there, but unmatched dipole ends remain on the surface.Why: This explains both the signs and the physical origin.
The polarized object has the same macroscopic field as its equivalent bound surface and volume charges.
