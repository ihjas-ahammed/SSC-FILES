Define the LR time constant, state its unit and explain its growth interpretation.

$\tau_L=L/R$; 63.2% rise in one time constant.

Q2Model · syllabus-based

Why does current in an ideal inductor remain continuous when a finite DC voltage is suddenly applied?

Know firstT1P1M1

The passive voltage is $v_L=L\,di/dt$. A finite voltage gives a finite slope, not an instantaneous finite jump. The initial current is therefore the pre-switch value even though the applied voltage changes abruptly.

Finite inductor voltage implies continuous current.

Q3Model · syllabus-based

During LR decay, how much magnetic energy remains when the current is half its initial value?

Know firstT2P1M1

$U_L=Li^2/2$. Therefore $U/U_0=(i/I_0)^2=(1/2)^2=1/4$. The remaining three quarters has been dissipated in the resistor for the ideal closed discharge path.

25% remains.

Q4Model · syllabus-based

Why must the LR decay formula specify a closed discharge path?

Know firstT2P1M1

The formula solves $L\dot i+Ri=0$ with a finite resistor path. If a switch simply opens an inductor carrying current, a large voltage can develop to maintain continuity, potentially producing arcing. That situation is not the assumed ideal R-L loop.

A defined resistor path is part of the exponential-decay model.

Q11Model · syllabus-based

At what coil orientations are flux and emf maximum in an ideal AC generator?

Know firstT6P1M1

Flux magnitude is maximum when the coil normal is parallel or antiparallel to B; its derivative and emf are then zero. Emf magnitude is maximum when the normal is perpendicular to B, where flux crosses zero fastest.

Flux and emf maxima are separated by a quarter-cycle.

A resistor dissipates positive mean power with power factor one.

Q13Model · syllabus-based

State the reactance and phase relation for an ideal inductor on sinusoidal AC.

Know firstT7P1M1

$X_L=\omega L$ and $Z_L=j\omega L$. Voltage leads current by 90°, or current lags voltage by 90°. Average power is zero because stored magnetic energy is returned each cycle.

Inductive current lags voltage by 90°.

$X_C=1/(\omega C)$ as a positive magnitude and $Z_C=-jX_C$. Current leads voltage by 90° because $i=C\,dv/dt$. Mean power is zero for an ideal capacitor.

Capacitive current leads voltage by 90°.

Q15Model · syllabus-based

What does multiplying a phasor by j do, and why is j used rather than i here?

Know firstT8P1M1

Since $j^2=-1$, multiplication by j rotates a complex-plane vector +90° without changing its magnitude. The letter j avoids conflict with the time-dependent current i. It represents phase mathematically rather than a second physical current.

j denotes a +90° phase rotation.

Q16Model · syllabus-based

Why can magnitudes of resistor and inductor voltages not be added directly in a series RL circuit?

Know firstT8P1M1

They differ in phase by 90°. With current as reference, $\widetilde V=V_R+jV_L$, so $V=\sqrt{V_R^2+V_L^2}$, not $V_R+V_L$. Their instantaneous values still obey Kirchhoff's law.

Add voltages as phasors, then take the magnitude.

At $\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}$, $Y=j(\omega C-1/\omega L)=0$. Thus source current is zero and input impedance tends to infinity. Opposite nonzero branch currents can circulate.

Ideal parallel resonance gives zero input current and infinite impedance.

Q18Model · syllabus-based

State the resonance condition and input impedance of a series LCR circuit.

Know firstT10P1M1

Resonance occurs when $\omega L=1/(\omega C)$, giving $\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}$. Reactive impedances cancel, so Z=R, current is in phase with voltage and its amplitude is maximal at fixed V.

At series resonance, Z=R.

Q19Model · syllabus-based

State whether a series LCR circuit is capacitive or inductive below and above its resonance frequency.

Know firstT10P1M1

Below resonance, $1/(\omega C)>\omega L$, so net reactance is negative and current leads. Above resonance, $\omega L>1/(\omega C)$, so net reactance is positive and current lags.

Below: capacitive. Above: inductive.

Q20Model · syllabus-based

Define power factor and distinguish real power from apparent power.

Know firstT11P1M1

Power factor is $\cos\phi$, where φ is the phase difference of voltage and current for a sinusoidal load. Apparent power is $S_{\rm app}=V_{\rm rms}I_{\rm rms}$ in VA; real mean power is $P=S_{\rm app}\cos\phi$ in W.

Only the in-phase contribution transfers net energy per cycle.

Derive average power for sinusoidal voltage and current with phase difference, then discuss pure R, L, C and series LCR at resonance.

Know firstT11P1M1
Let $v=V_0\cos\omega t$ and $i=I_0\cos(\omega t-\phi)$.Why: φ is the voltage lead over current.
Use $2\cos A\cos B=\cos(A-B)+\cos(A+B)$ to obtain $p=(V_0I_0/2)[\cos\phi+\cos(2\omega t-\phi)]$.Why: Power is the instantaneous product vi.
Average over a full cycle: the second cosine contributes zero.Why: It completes an integer number of cycles.
Since $V_{\rm rms}=V_0/\sqrt2$ and $I_{\rm rms}=I_0/\sqrt2$, $P=V_{\rm rms}I_{\rm rms}\cos\phi$.Why: Convert peaks to rms values.
For pure R, φ=0 and P=I²R; for pure L or C, φ=±π/2 and P=0.Why: Reactive elements return the energy they store in the ideal model.
At series LCR resonance φ=0, so all input mean power is dissipated in R, although reactive voltages and stored energies are nonzero.Why: Cancellation of reactances gives unity power factor.
Average AC power equals rms voltage × rms current × power factor.
